Route comparison

Two routes, planned in parallel so the choice is made on evidence. This document exists to be usable in both directions: to choose, and to defend that choice to an investor who prefers the other one.

The two

Route A, direct to parents. A parent buys a subscription. The company is an independent data controller. Distribution runs through creators and search.

Route B, through schools. A school or trust buys per pupil per year. The school is the controller and the company processes on its instructions. Distribution runs through a sales team and procurement cycles.

Recommendation

Route A, and the condition under which that holds is that the persona wedge is real. If named presets do not outperform unnamed archetypes on repeat use, Route A loses its distinguishing feature and becomes a thinner version of a free product, at which point Route B's higher evidence burden is worth paying because the buyer is buying outcomes rather than appeal.

RecommendationConditional. Test in document 11

The evidence for Route A is that the money moved to families: tutoring uptake is at 29% and rising, concentrated in Year 11 at 25% and London at 45%, while the school-organised channel fell to 20% of pupils and most schools cut their offer. Selling to the buyer who is withdrawing from the category is the harder path.

The second argument is feedback latency. Route A tells you within one cohort whether the wedge works. Route B tells you at renewal, by which time a year of build has been committed to a hypothesis.

The strongest case for Route B

Stated properly, because an investor may well prefer it and the case is not weak.

The regulatory position is materially better. As a processor acting on a school's instructions, the company is not the one answering the ICO for age assurance and lawful basis for profiling a child. In a year when the regulator fined two companies in one month for exactly that, moving the controller relationship to an institution that already has a lawful basis for processing pupil data is a real reduction in the risk the owner ranks as most likely to arrive without warning.

Retention is structural rather than earned. A school contract renews annually against a budget line. A parent cancels in July. If the product's weakness turns out to be seasonal churn, Route B solves it and Route A does not.

The evidence burden is an asset once paid. Efficacy evidence, a DPIA and a reference school are expensive to produce and expensive for a competitor to reproduce. Consumer traction is not.

It is the only route to the outcome data. Actual exam results, attached to actual usage, only exist inside institutions. Without them the accuracy and efficacy claims in documents 03 and 00 stay unproven indefinitely.

The honest counter to the recommendation: Route A can demonstrate that people will pay, and Route B can demonstrate that it works. Those are different questions, and only the second one is the business in ten years.

What is shared regardless

The style engine, the item bank and its verification, the rights machinery, the safety architecture and the kill switch are identical under both routes. Roughly eighty per cent of the design does not care who pays, which is why both are planned and only four documents diverge.

One thing must be built Route-A-shaped from the start even if Route B is chosen: the data model has to assume the company is a controller. Retrofitting controller obligations onto a processor-shaped system is expensive; the reverse is not.

PositionKeeps the choice cheap to reverse

What would have to be true

Neither route is choosable on what is currently known. Three things are missing and all three are cheap to obtain.

  • The cost per assured account, which sets the floor under Route A pricing and is currently unestimated.
  • Whether a school will contract at all for a tool whose distinguishing feature is pupil-selected personas. Six conversations would settle it.
  • Whether named presets outperform unnamed archetypes, which is the condition on the recommendation and cannot be known before launch.
