Figures are rendered, not generated

Diagrams are produced by deterministic rendering from a structured figure specification stored in the item bank, not by an image model. A number line, an axis pair, a labelled triangle and a function plot are all describable as data, and rendering them is a template.

Decision D2814 September 2026

Three constraints converge on this and any one of them would be sufficient.

  • Correctness. A generated image cannot be verified by the verifier. A figure rendered from validated data inherits the validation of the item, so visual_density in 02 can be a real style dimension without opening a hole in P2.
  • Cost. Image generation is the most expensive thing a turn could do. Rendering is effectively free, which is why the visual_density ceiling in 02 is a modest constraint rather than the dominant cost line.
  • Provenance. Verified The IT Amendment Rules 2026 require prominent labelling and permanent provenance metadata that cannot be stripped for synthetically generated information. A deterministically rendered figure carries its source item identifier in its metadata by construction. See 04.

What this costs: the figure vocabulary is finite, and a topic needing a figure type nobody has built is blocked on engineering rather than on content. The vocabulary needed for the Algebra strand is small, which is a further reason that strand is the launch scope.
