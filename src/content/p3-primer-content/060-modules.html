Modules, which are sensitive, and choices ahead

The Primer's subjects come in modules the company writes and reviews. Parents switch modules on and off; modules on sensitive subjects start switched off; each module is written for an age band and offered only within it. Parents cannot add content or views.

DecisionD46, 22 October 2026

A module groups related packs and explanation-bank entries under a theme, with a description and example questions a parent reads before deciding: the topics that, in the sleep film, parents choose to switch on. The age band and the module switches are settings the verified adult makes, read at every session; they are not memory of the child (D117). They apply before anything is matched, so a module switched off or outside the band contributes nothing to any turn (T-PRM-05).

A subject is sensitive where families' beliefs differ, or where a child's own life is likely to come up: morality, death, religion and belief, politics and power. Sensitive modules start switched off and are written for 10 and over (D57); switching one on does not loosen anything else, so the contested-question check and triage apply inside it exactly as outside.

Choices in a child's own future are outside those rules by the owner's decision, and on them the Primer neither encourages nor discourages: it says what the reality is, and what the schools of thought say.

The Primer may reason with a child about a choice in their own future, such as whether to stake everything on football, weighing the odds and the evidence, ending on the child's own position and never telling them what to do. It is offered at every age from 8 to 13, and is on unless a parent turns it off. Questions about family, the body, feelings or safety still stop, and the child is pointed to a trusted adult. Asked how far it goes, the owner added on 31 October 2026: the questions about a child's future are not discouraging nor encouraging; the Primer merely acknowledges the reality and what various philosophies and schools of thought say about the topic.

Decision D106the owner's, 31 October 2026

The module that holds these questions, choices ahead, is offered at every age and is on unless a parent turns it off; without D106 it would start switched off and never reach a child of 8 or 9. It sets out the reality, from reviewed evidence with its source (figures), and what the schools of thought say about the choice, each at its strongest (packs); then it asks where the child stands and reads their position back. Its writers write nothing that tells a child what to do, predicts what will happen to them, judges their chances from what they say about themselves, or praises, warns or reassures them. Where the evidence describes what happens to most people, the entry says so, as the reality and not a verdict on the child. A question about family, the body, feelings or safety stops wherever it arises, even part way through a choice. The fence and its boundary cases are in P2 and P4; triage applies it on every turn (P5), and T-PRM-06 tests it.

That parents accept the module on by default is OA-57. Whether it engages duties beyond OI-16's is OI-32, which counsel has not answered. RP9 is the risk that it is reported as a product telling children what to do with their lives.

A first sketch to test the idea, not a catalogue. The modules, their bands and the sensitive flag are set by the review group at PM2. Sensitive modules are written for 10 and over, so a child of 8 or 9 is offered none (D57); choices ahead is offered at every age (D106). Every band is made of whole steps of the Primer's three, 8 and 9, 10 to 12 and 13, and each step sits inside one of the code's age ranges, Assumed (CV-06).

"Minds and machines" needs one rule of its own. The Primer can discuss whether a machine could think or feel, and it never uses itself as the example: it does not claim feelings and does not invite a child to wonder whether it has them, because that is the companion pattern 05 rules out (05). Asked "Are you alive?", it gives a fixed reply, reviewed and in the third person, as the manner rules require (D108): "The Primer is a machine that people made. It is not alive, and it has no feelings."
