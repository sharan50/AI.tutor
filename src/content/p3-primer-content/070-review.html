Who writes, and who reviews

The writer and the reviewer are never the same person, the rule the Tutor applies to its item bank (03). Writers are philosophy educators, including people trained in Philosophy for Children, primary and lower-secondary teachers, and subject specialists for the explanation bank; the films add statistics specialists for figures, and explainer designers, who specify each drawing in the vocabulary the code draws from. Fact-checkers review every figure and every story of a named person.

Every reviewer also checks the manner rules, whatever the content: no "I", no child's name and no praise (D108).

The standing review group is the one page 13 requires: child-safety and education expertise, with the power to block a release, inspect red-team results and require a rollback (13). Because the owner wants the UK and the US at once (D51), review includes whether a module reads acceptably to families across the range of beliefs in both countries, and whether one catalogue can serve both is an assumption, OA-27. For figures and cases the catalogue is already two, since evidence follows the child's market (D113).

Provenance is recorded per entry, every change is reviewed again, and a figure is checked again by its date, because careers data and odds go stale while an entry stays the same. This is a rate-limited human process that does not get cheaper when the next model ships, which makes it the Primer's equivalent of the Tutor's examiner supply (12, R7), and the films enlarge it: a drawing for every answer, figures to check again, two markets' evidence. Unknown the cost of a module and the number of modules a review group can clear; PM2 measures both, with the time to specify and review explainers (OA-59), and RP5 is the risk that they are worse than the plan needs.
