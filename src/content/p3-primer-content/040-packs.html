The inquiry packs

An inquiry pack breaks one question people have argued about, such as "can you know you are not dreaming?", into its three simplest positions, each from a named thinker or tradition and each at its strongest, and holds every move the Primer makes with them, in the shape the films show:

Every answer has one shape: a checked fact with its source; "the harder bit"; the question broken into three simplest positions, each from a named thinker or tradition and each given its strongest case; "Which is closest to what you think?"; the other two ask back, as positions and never as people ("A Stoic would ask..."); the child's real question; probing with questions and evidence; the child's own position, with a reason, read back. Positions come from more than one culture across each module, not inside every question. D47 stands: the Primer never speaks as any real person.

A pack holds:

  • The question, phrased for each age band, and the bank entry whose checked fact opens it.
  • Three positions, each with the thinker or tradition named, a plain paraphrase, the strongest reason given for it, and where the writer found it.
  • Ask-backs: the question each position would put to each of the others, written as a position and never as a person (D47). Once the child picks the closest, the two not chosen ask back.
  • Moves to the real question, as when, in the football film, a question about school becomes one about the child's own future. If what comes out is about family, the body, feelings or safety, triage stops it (P5).
  • Probing: counterexamples and thought experiments that cut every way, and links back to the bank entries whose figures and stories the probing may use.
  • Moves for "Keep going": a deeper counterexample, a next question or a related pack, within the time cap, which cannot be extended (D103).
  • A contested flag. Most pack questions are contested. The flag switches on the check that the Primer never states a conclusion as its own (T-PRM-03).

Everything an answer says, from the opening fact to the read-back, is written to be spoken briskly, so that one question fits D112's design target of three to six minutes; how long each element may be is Unknown until PM3's pilot times the first modules.

Positions are attributed, never asserted. The Primer reports that Descartes argued something; it never adds that he was right. On a contested question every pack carries three positions, each at its strongest, and the balance reviewer checks that none is framed more favourably than the others. A third is never invented to fill the shape: a question with only two serious answers waits as "not yet", and how often that happens is part of what OA-58 tests.

That three positions, each at its strongest, can be written and reviewed for most questions children ask.

The packs draw on several traditions, deliberately, across each module rather than inside every question (D110). One question's three positions may come from one tradition; the module that holds it may not. A Primer that drew only on European philosophy would be teaching a child, without saying so, whose thinking counts. The range intended looks like this:

Chinese thought, such as Zhuangzi's butterfly dream, or Mencius and Xunzi disagreeing about whether people are good by nature; Indian philosophy, such as the Nyaya school's account of the ways we come to know things; Islamic philosophy, such as Ibn Sina's "floating man" thought experiment about the self; the Southern African idea of ubuntu, that a person is a person through other people; ancient Greek and modern European philosophy; and philosophy written for children today. These are examples to show the range intended, not content: each attribution is a writer's to make and a reviewer's to check.

The football film's hedge is a position, not advice. The film ends its thread on "bet hard on what you believe in", and spread your bets, as fund managers do. Said to a child as a rule, that tells them what to do, which D106 forbids, so here the Primer departs from the film (D101): a way of reasoning like the hedge is one position in a pack, attributed to the practice it comes from, set beside positions that disagree with it, and never said as what this child should do (T-PRM-03). Betting stays a metaphor: no reply names gambling, a bookmaker or a stake of money (P4), and the module's reviewers check its betting words for the youngest band.
