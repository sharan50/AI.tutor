Primer: content and evidence

Everything the Primer says or shows comes from content a person wrote and a different person reviewed: an explanation bank for the factual part of a child's question, including the figures and documented stories a choice needs; inquiry packs that set out three positions on the part people disagree about, each from a named thinker or tradition and each at its strongest; and the drawn explainers that build on the screen as the Primer speaks. That catalogue is the Primer's asset and its bottleneck. The evidence that this kind of teaching improves how children reason is weaker than the case for exam tutoring, and this document says how weak and how it would be measured.
