Figures, odds and named people

The explanation bank holds the evidence a choice needs: careers data, odds and the documented stories of named people, living ones included. A living person is described only by public, documented facts from reviewed sources, never their private life, never quoted beyond the source and never voiced. A figure whose source is itself an estimate is said with "roughly" and keeps its source; a figure no reviewed entry holds is never said, so D44 is unchanged. Evidence follows the child's market: UK figures and cases for a child in the UK, US ones in the US.

Decision D113from the films, confirmed by the owner, 31 October 2026

A choice in a child's own future needs evidence, and this is how the bank holds it without saying anything no reviewer has checked:

  • A figure keeps its source: the work, its date and the group it counts, so that a figure about boys in England is said as one. A figure about most people is said as that, never as this child's chances (P4).
  • "Roughly", where the source estimates. The fact-checker marks each figure as a count or an estimate; an estimate is written and spoken with "roughly", and a turn that drops it is not entailed by its entry, so it fails T-PRM-01.
  • Figures that count different groups are never set side by side as if they counted the same one.
  • Figures go stale. An entry holding one carries a date for checking it again, after which the figure is not said until it has been.
  • The child's market, which is the account's, decides the evidence. An entry with national data or cases is written for each market, and a child whose market has none gets "not yet", never another country's figure.
  • A named person appears only through public, documented facts from reviewed sources: nothing private, no quotation beyond the source, never their voice, and the Primer never speaks as them (D47). A story is told as what happened, never as a model or a forecast for the child. What the law requires of such stories (privacy, defamation, rights in a name or image, the Children's code) is OI-30, which counsel has not answered (P4).

The football film's odds show why the rules are needed. They come from three sources that count different groups: the publisher's description of Michael Calvin's No Hunger in Paradise, that only 180 of the 1.5 million boys who play organised youth football in England will become Premier League professionals, Assumed (CV-31); Sky Sports News's own analysis, that 91 per cent of academy footballers never play a single game as a professional, Assumed (CV-32); and the PFA's statement that over 99 per cent of those who sign for an academy aged 9 do not make it as professional footballers, Assumed (CV-33). The register treats the first and third as estimates, and the second gives no cohort or method, so an entry holding them would say "roughly", name each source and keep the three apart. They are the film's content, registered so that this page can say what the film shows; whether the bank uses them is for its reviewers. No US figure is registered, so what a child in the US would hear is Unknown.
