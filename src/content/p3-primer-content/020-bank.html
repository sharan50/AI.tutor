The explanation bank

The explanation bank answers the factual part of the questions children actually ask: about nature and the body, the sky and the sea, machines, history, words and numbers, and now people's lives and work, with the figures and stories a choice needs (figures). It is not a syllabus and it is not exam content; that is the Tutor's item bank.

Each entry holds:

  • The questions it answers, in the several ways children phrase them.
  • An explanation written for an age band, in short plain sentences, spoken as well as shown. Where one explanation cannot serve the whole of 8 to 13, there are versions for the code's age ranges within it, Assumed (CV-06) (P4).
  • What is not known, or where scientists disagree, said as plainly as what is known. A child should learn early that "we don't know yet" is an answer adults give.
  • Its figures, each with its source, and marked where the source estimates.
  • The harder bit: the question underneath, which the Primer turns to with those words, pointing at the inquiry pack that takes it up. Every entry has one, since every answer takes the whole shape (D110); until its pack is reviewed, the question gets "not yet" and joins the writing queue.
  • A drawn explainer, specified in advance and reviewed with the text (D111, D73; explainers). What one costs to make and review is Unknown.
  • Provenance: the writer, the reviewer, the reference works consulted, the date and the version; for a figure, the date by which it is checked again; and each real person it names, so that every entry naming one person can be pulled at once (P5).

Entries carry identifiers of the form EB-SCI-SLEEP-002. Under each answer a small grey line, "Based on: ...", names the reference works the entry drew on (D109), so that a parent can look the source up, as the Tutor's transcripts show the item-bank node behind each statement (01); the identifier stays in the entry's provenance, for reviewers.

When a child asks something the bank cannot answer, the question is separated from the child's account and rewritten in general terms, with names, places and anything about the child's own life removed, then counted and added to a writing queue ordered by how often children ask it. The queue never holds what a child typed or said, or any link back to the child: no recording and no transcript, since a spoken question's audio is thrown away at once (D102, ahead of OI-17). Who or what does the rewriting, and how it is checked, is set with the safeguarding policy (P4). A question leaves the queue when the catalogue answers it or the review group retires it. The queue is the content roadmap: the catalogue grows towards what children want to know, not towards what writers find interesting. The rate at which it can grow is set by reviewers, not writers, and is Unknown until PM2 times it.

Below the grounding floor, the affected module stops composing factual answers and serves the entry text verbatim or "not yet" until the cause is found. The floor is the Tutor's P2 floor, 99.5% of sampled turns, adopted as a design choice rather than derived. D21 carries over with it: grounding is reported per session, not per turn, and no claim is made that the Primer "does not make things up".
