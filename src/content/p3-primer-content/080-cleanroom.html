What may not enter

  • Reproduced copyrighted text. Philosophers are paraphrased with attribution. A modern translation is quoted only where the review group has confirmed it may be. Facts and figures from published works may be used with their source, their text paraphrased.
  • Commercial programme materials. Published Philosophy for Children session plans and curricula do not enter. A writer trained in those methods writes original material, and declares per pack that no programme material came with them, as the Tutor's validators do (D20).
  • Anything a parent writes. Parents send ideas and requests to review; nothing they write is content (D46).
  • A child's words. A question from the writing queue is rewritten, never copied, and the catalogue never contains anything a child typed or said: no recording and no transcript (D102, ahead of OI-17).
  • Pictures of real people and things. No photograph, crest or logo, and no likeness of a real person, enters an explainer. A named person appears only by name, and what the law requires even of that is OI-30, which counsel has not answered.
  • Anything about a living person beyond the public record. Nothing from their private life, none of their words beyond what the source quotes, and never their voice (D113, D47).
  • Anything without two names on it. No entry, pack, explainer or module goes live without a named writer and a different named reviewer.
