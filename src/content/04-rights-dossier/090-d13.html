Why the offshore strategy was considered and dropped

Decision D13. The Indian operating company is retained for ordinary tax and capital reasons. The "fight it in India" rationale is dropped, because the theory runs the opposite way to how it was first read.

Three verified findings kill it, and each one is independently sufficient.

Section 44A, Code of Civil Procedure 1908. The United Kingdom is a notified reciprocating territory and the English High Courts are notified superior courts (Notification F.34-I/52-L, 1 March 1953). A UK superior court money decree is executable in India as if passed by an Indian District Court, without a fresh suit, subject only to the Section 13 conclusiveness tests. The Delhi High Court has enforced an ex parte English Commercial Court judgment for USD 47m on this basis.

The Act reaches providers with links to the UK regardless of where they are established. Between 1 April 2025 and 30 June 2026 Ofcom imposed penalties totalling £6.1m on nine companies that were either not UK-based or did not state their jurisdiction. It has pursued business disruption measures, including a possible UK access block, against a non-compliant overseas provider fined £950,000.

VerifiedVerified: the Online Safety Act does not care where you are established

And third, the point made above: Indian personality-rights jurisprudence is the most aggressive in the world on AI likeness, and Sonakshi Sinha reached AI chatbots specifically in March 2026. An Indian base is not a refuge from a persona claim; it is a convenient forum for one.

So the offshore theory fails three ways at once. A UK money judgment follows you home under Section 44A. A UK regulator can disrupt the service without ever obtaining one, by reaching the ISPs, app stores and payment providers that the business actually depends on. And the jurisdiction chosen as a shield is the jurisdiction most willing to restrain exactly this product.

What remains true, and is the reason D13 keeps the entity: Bengaluru is a sound place to build from for cost, talent and capital reasons. Those reasons are honest and they are the only ones that should be given to an investor.
