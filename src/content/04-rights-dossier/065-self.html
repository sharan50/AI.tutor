The learner's own likeness

When the face and voice are the learner's own, the persona problem this dossier is built around mostly disappears, and a different one takes its place. For an archetype in the learner's own likeness, nobody else's identity is used, so in the vault's reading there is no endorsement to imply and no licence to take; whether that holds is Open OI-27. A named preset is different, and needs Schedule 4. The risk moves to misuse: a self-tutor made of someone who is not the learner.

The self-tutor (D59) lets a learner be taught by a tutor with their own face and voice, captured live in the product (D61) with their consent and a verified adult's (D62), and seen or heard by no one else (D60). It launches ahead of counsel's answers on OI-23 to OI-27 under D59's rule: it is withdrawn in a market only if counsel advises in writing that no form of it the design allows may launch there, and a conditional answer changes the design instead. That is recorded here as D34 records naming a board ahead of OI-3.

Of the routes by which a UK persona is protected (above), none has a third party to protect when the likeness is the learner's own and is shown only to them: there is no one whose endorsement could be implied, and no one else's performance in the clip. What remains is data protection, which is the subject of 05 and the question in OI-23. This reading has not been confirmed by counsel.

The misuse case: a self-tutor of someone else

A self-tutor made from a classmate's or a teacher's face and voice would be a virtual clone of a real person, the incident type behind Ofcom's letter (above), and the misuse of identity the Indian courts have restrained (below). It is R12, and D61 is the containment: a self-tutor is made only from a live capture in the product, with a liveness check, and no uploaded photograph, video or recording is accepted, so a likeness cannot be lifted from a video or a social media profile.

Two residues are stated rather than hidden. A person physically present at the camera can still be captured in the learner's place; how often live capture stops that is OA-39, measured by T-SLF-02 at M2. And D60 closes every path the product controls, but not a second device filming the screen.

A creator's style, in a learner's likeness

The creator licence grants a name and an image to label one preset (above). Delivering that preset in a learner's face and voice is a use the grant does not obviously cover, so it is asked for separately: Schedule 4 of the heads of terms, an opt-in a creator can refuse at signing or withdraw on its own at any time (D66). The creator's own face and voice are never used in a self-tutor, and D9 as amended keeps a creator's voice off the launch path. The label says whose style it is without implying that the creator appears. A creator who does not opt in is never offered in a self-tutor; every archetype is, whatever any creator decides.

Labelling and provenance

Every clip the self-tutor renders carries a visible label that it is synthetic, and provenance metadata (D64). That is a design rule, not an answer: what the law requires of synthetic audio and video of a real person shown only to that person is OI-26, and the answer can change the label's form, not whether there is one. The India section sets out why the question starts there.

OI-26: What labelling and provenance duties apply to synthetic audio and video of a real person shown only to that person: under India's IT Rules 2026, the operating company being in India, and in the UK and the US? Unblocks: the form of D64's label and metadata. Cost of getting it wrong: a launch feature out of compliance in the jurisdiction the company is built in.
