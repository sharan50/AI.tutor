What a UK persona right actually consists of

There is no statutory personality right in the UK. Protection of a creator's persona runs through: passing off for false endorsement (following Irvine v Talksport), trade marks, copyright in the underlying videos, performers' rights, and data protection insofar as a voice or image is personal data. This framing has not been confirmed by counsel for this fact pattern.

Three consequences for the design, and they are why D5, D8 and D9 are shaped as they are.

  • False endorsement is the live risk, not copying. Under D5 we ingest nothing, so copyright in the underlying videos and performers' rights are largely not engaged. What remains is the risk that a consumer believes the creator endorses, writes or checks the tutoring. That risk is managed by the on-screen label in 01, by the attribution change when a student adjusts an axis, and by an express non-endorsement term in the licence.
  • A licence does not remove the risk, it relocates it. A signed creator cannot easily complain, but a consumer misled about endorsement is a different matter, and so is a creator whose licence has ended. Hence the kill switch SLA and the post-termination deletion terms.
  • Voice is categorically worse than name and image. Hence D9: voice cloning is off the launch path entirely and becomes a separate opt-in limb.
