The creator licence

One page, because there is almost nothing in it. The creator licenses a name and a face to label a teaching style. They license nothing they have made.

Licence architecture at launch. Schedules 2 and 3 are drafted and unexercised; nothing in them is in force for any creator.
TermPosition at launch (D5)Why
ScopeNon-exclusive licence to use the creator's name and image solely to label and market one style preset inside the CINE product. No content licence. No voice. No adaptation right.D5. Makes it a one-page agreement instead of a content negotiation, and makes a departing creator cost a text file.
What we do not getNo right to their videos, transcripts, notes, worked examples, slides, or any other material. Nothing they have made is ingested, stored, indexed or passed to a model.The sentence a creator's own lawyer will look for.
TerritoryUnited Kingdom, plus any territory in which the product is subsequently sold, on notice.UK first, D1.
TermTwelve months, renewing for twelve months unless either party gives notice.Short enough that a creator is never locked in, long enough to be worth the fitting cost.
ExclusivityNone, in either direction. The creator may license the same rights to anyone, including a competitor.Exclusivity turns a one-page agreement into a negotiation and raises the price. D5's logic taken to its conclusion.
ApprovalThe creator is shown the twelve dimension values and the rendered style card before launch and may object to any of them. Repeatable once per term.A contractual right, not a courtesy. It is what makes "your style" a claim the creator has signed off.
Continuing review rightThe creator may at any time request a representative sample of real sessions run under their preset, and require any specific behaviour to be removed within 14 days.Makes the arrangement supervisable, not merely revocable. A creator whose only instrument is termination is more likely to use it.
Advertising and merchandisingPaid advertising built around the creator's name requires separate written consent per campaign. No merchandising of any kind.Organic description of availability inside the product is one thing; a paid campaign around someone's name is a different ask and should be asked separately.
Non-endorsementExpress term that the creator does not write, review, approve or endorse any tutoring content, and that the on-screen label will say so.Directly addresses the false endorsement risk above.
LabellingThe on-screen line is mandatory, specified in the agreement, and cannot be changed without the creator's consent.Puts the passing-off mitigation beyond unilateral marketing decisions.
TerminationEither party, at any time, for any reason or none, effective on notice. No cure period. No survival of the licence grant.D5 and D11. A creator who cannot leave instantly is a creator with a grievance and a platform.
On terminationThe kill switch SLA below. The parameter object and the style card are deleted; there is no residual right; the creator's name is removed everywhere; transcripts keep a neutral preset identifier only.See below.
FeeUnknown Not a design decision. Either a flat fee per term or a share of revenue on sessions using the preset; both structures are drafted. The choice and the number are commercial and are not invented here.Inventing a creator fee would put a plausible number into an investor conversation with nothing behind it.
Schedule 2 (D6)Exemplar conditioning. A content licence covering reproduction, adaptation and machine processing of specified material for a defined term. Drafted, unexercised.D6 requires the boundary to exist now so the fallback is not a rewrite under time pressure.
Schedule 3 (D9)Voice. A separate opt-in limb with its own consideration, its own term and its own deletion SLA. Drafted, unexercised.D9. Highest-risk signal on both the rights and regulatory side.

OI-6: What is the minimum viable creator licence for name and likeness only, and what does the exemplar-conditioning upgrade under D6 add to it? Unblocks: the first creator signature, and the D6 contingency. Cost of getting it wrong: either a licence too thin to permit what the product does, or one thick enough to require a negotiation per creator, which destroys the D5 economics.

OI-7: Which entity contracts with the parent, and what does that do to consumer jurisdiction, UK representative obligations and VAT? Unblocks: incorporation, the payment stack, and the terms of business. Cost of getting it wrong: a consumer contract unenforceable in the market it was written for, and a tax position discovered late.
