Naming a board: the convention, and a decision taken ahead of counsel

This is the one place in this vault where we act before counsel has answered. It is flagged as such rather than blended in, because the posture is different from everywhere else and a reader is entitled to see that.

Why waiting is not the neutral option. You cannot sell examination preparation without saying which examination. A product that will not name the board it prepares a student for is not a cautious version of this product; it is an unsellable one, because the parent's entire question is "is this the right one for my child's exam". Silence has a cost here, and it is not a smaller cost than the risk.

The basis we are proceeding on. Naming an exam board to describe what a product prepares a student for may be honest use under section 11(2) of the Trade Marks Act 1994, provided endorsement is not implied. That is counsel's likely starting point, not settled advice, and OI-3 remains open. Which limb of section 11(2) applies is itself part of the question, and nothing here should be read as settling it; the section has been amended and the choice between the descriptive limb and the limb covering use necessary to indicate intended purpose is exactly the sort of thing a guess would get wrong.

The convention

Permitted and forbidden wording. This is the whole of it, and it is not open to interpretation by whoever is writing a landing page that week.

What the risk actually is, and it is not litigation

Per the war-game, a board's fastest move is not a claim form; it is a trade mark complaint to an app store, which acts on a complaint rather than on the merits and can remove a product in days. So the mitigation is operational, not argumentative:

  • Every board-naming string is centralised in one place in the codebase and in the marketing site, so all of them change in a single edit. This is an engineering requirement, not a content guideline.
  • Rollback trigger: on receipt of any objection from any board, board naming reverts to DfE-subject-content wording within 24 hours everywhere we control, before any substantive reply is sent. Argue afterwards, from a compliant position.
  • Notice has to arrive somewhere. The same published address that receives creator kill-switch notices receives board objections, it is monitored on the same basis, and the 24 hours runs from receipt. A rollback trigger with no address and no monitoring duty is not a trigger; the kill switch is careful about this and so is this.
  • The counter-notice pack in the war-game covers this case as well as the copyright case.

What the 24 hours does not reach, stated because "a single edit" is not true of all of it. App store listing copy sits in a third-party review queue on somebody else's timetable, which is the same point the war-game makes about removals. Served ad creative, cached and archived pages, affiliate and press copy, and a creator's own video about the product are all outside our control entirely.

And the rollback changes the label, not the thing: board_alignment in item metadata and the per-board paper structural specification in 03 are unaffected by any wording change. An objection that reaches the product rather than the copy is a content question, not a naming one, and it is not contained by this.

Limits of the containmentthe honest version of "revert in 24 hours"

The exposure is analysed here only as trade mark, and that is a gap. Consumer protection law and the advertising standards regime both bite on consumer education copy that names an examination board, and a complaint to an advertising regulator is cheaper and faster for a complainant than anything in the war-game.

Unknown Neither regime has been analysed, neither is in the verified findings, and their current enforcement powers are not stated here because they would have to be written from memory. This is open item OI-12, and it should go to counsel with OI-3 rather than after it.

UnanalysedOI-12, consumer protection and advertising standards

Proceed with board naming under the convention above, ahead of counsel's answer on OI-3, accepting the risk on the basis that the exposure is a takedown rather than damages, that the rollback is 24 hours, and that the alternative is a product that cannot describe itself.

Note on the fallback: reverting to "DfE subject content" wording assumes that wording is itself available, which depends on OI-9, also unanswered. The rollback is to a less exposed position, not to a proven one. Rejected: waiting for OI-3. It would have cost the ability to market the product at all, for an unknown number of months, to remove a risk that is contained rather than eliminated by waiting. What it makes harder: if counsel comes back against the convention, marketing copy, app store listings and the product's own course naming all change at once, and any paid acquisition running at that moment is wasted. OI-3 stays open and stays first in the counsel instruction.
