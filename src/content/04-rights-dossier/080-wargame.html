The enforcement war-game

The courts are the slow, unlikely path. Payment processors and app stores are the fast, likely one, and they decide on a complaint rather than on a judgment. Plan for the chokepoint, not for the trial.

Who can do what, how fast, and what actually answers it.
ActorFirst realistic moveTimelineWhat answers it
An unhappy creatorNot a claim form. A post to their audience saying a company is using their name without permission, or with permission they now regret.Hours.The kill switch, exercised before responding, and the fact that the licence lets them leave instantly. The legal argument is irrelevant at this speed; the operational answer is everything. Rehearse it.
An exam boardA letter asserting copyright or trade mark, plus, in the worse case, a complaint to app stores.Days to weeks for the letter; the app store complaint acts faster.Per-item provenance records, the attestation trail, the similarity screening, and the validator engagement terms. The clean room is only a defence if it is documented at the time, which is why provenance is a field and not a policy.
App storesRemoval on a credible intellectual property complaint, with limited process and no adjudication of the merits.Days. Sometimes hours.A prepared counter-notice pack: provenance export, the clean-room policy, the licence register. Plus the structural answer: the product is web-first, so app store removal is damaging rather than fatal. This is the single most likely way the venture is interrupted.
Payment processorsFreeze or termination on brand-risk or category-risk grounds, often triggered by press rather than by a complainant.Days, and sometimes without warning.More than one processor from the start, a documented compliance posture, and a product that is not classifiable as adult or age-restricted content. Redundancy here is cheap insurance against an outage nobody can appeal.
OfcomInformation request, then enforcement. Verified Penalties are the higher of £18m or 10% of qualifying worldwide revenue, and where non-compliance continues Ofcom may seek court-ordered business disruption: UK ISPs blocking the service, app stores removing the app, payment providers ceasing to process.Months to enforcement; disruption measures follow non-compliance.The Online Safety Act scoping decision and its trigger list in 05, written down in advance with the evidence for it.
ICOAudit, then enforcement. Verified Fines to the higher of £17.5m or 4% of global annual turnover; the ICO has stated it will audit edtech providers.Months.The age assurance design, the lawful basis analysis, the DPIA, and the data model in 05.

The conclusion a lawyer should take from that table, and it is not the one a founder expects: the legal merits of our position have almost no bearing on the events most likely to stop us. An app store does not weigh the clean-room policy. A processor does not read the licence. What they respond to is a complaint and a risk assessment, and what answers them is a pre-assembled evidence pack and a second supplier. Both are cheap and both are listed at M1 in 11 Roadmap.
