The copyright line

Copyright protects expression, not facts and not curricula. Nobody owns the requirement that a student know Newton's second law, and GCSE and A-level subject content is set and published by the DfE. Verified Teaching to a published specification with wholly original items and explanations is not an act a board has standing to stop. Reproducing its papers, its mark schemes or its specification text is.

That line is the whole content strategy, so it is written as an operational rule rather than as a principle. The rule lives in 03 and is enforced by the provenance record on every item, the two-person rule, the per-item attestation, and the validator engagement terms that forbid a current examiner bringing board material into the work.

What exact wording may be used to state which specification a course follows, without implying endorsement? The starting point, and it is only that: Assumption naming an exam board to describe what a product prepares a student for may be honest descriptive use under section 11(2) of the Trade Marks Act 1994, provided endorsement is not implied. That is stated as counsel's likely starting point, not as settled advice, and no wording has been cleared. Unblocks: every piece of marketing copy and the product's own course naming. Cost of getting it wrong: a trade mark complaint that removes the product from app stores faster than it could be defended, per the war-game.
