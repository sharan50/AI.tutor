Why an AI can do this now, and why at eight to thirteen

The Primer is built around what current AI does well; the Tutor is built around what it does badly. The Tutor's case rests on general models being unreliable about correctness, so the item bank and the verifier are the product (06). The Primer's rests on the opposite: holding a patient conversation, following a child's reasoning and asking the next good question is what language models do best. The owner's view is that this makes the Primer the lower-risk of the two new ideas on whether it can work at all. Since 4 November 2026 it also trusts the model further than the Tutor does: for any question outside named living people and the sensitive modules (D127), it writes the answer live from sources it retrieves and has its explainer videos generated live, with automatic checks only (D122, D125). So a catalogue written in advance no longer sets how much it can answer. What limits it now is what the reviewed sources can support (OA-32), and whether it is right turns on what the automatic checks catch (OA-62, RP10).

That a child of 8 to 13 can hold a typed or spoken conversation on a screen long enough for an inquiry to work, and that this is something current AI does well. The youngest band is the least certain: a nine-year-old types slowly, is misheard more often and tires quickly. The two halves are tested at different times: whether a child can sustain the conversation is observed in a human-run pilot at PM3, before the Primer is built; whether an AI can lead it well is tested only when a built Primer runs at PM4.

AssumedOA-23, the owner's view

Speaking removes slow typing, which is why children may speak from launch (D102), and why the owner expects about three in four questions to be spoken Assumed (OA-61, D123). Counsel has cleared it in the UK and the US, as the owner reported on 4 November 2026 (D118). It adds two things typing did not need: recognising a young child's speech, which is least accurate for the youngest Assumed (CV-11), and taking turns aloud in real time. That children speak readily, and are recognised well enough that the conversation does not stall, is Assumed (OA-55), tested at PM3 and PM4. The films set a brisk pace: one question takes three to six minutes, a design target (D112). The session cap stays a design choice set from PM3's pilot, and how soon the spoken answer must begin is a threshold to be written down before PM4 (T-PRM-14), Unknown today. How long a child then waits for the first video is observed at PM4, and is Unknown too (OA-62).

The Primer still needs correctness. A child's why-question usually has a factual part before a philosophical one, and a confident wrong explanation to a nine-year-old is worse than none. So facts keep the purpose of the Tutor's rule (D44) in a new form: every factual sentence cites a source the Primer retrieved for the turn from a reviewed allow-list of reference sources, or a reviewed entry where one exists, and is entailed by it; a sentence no source supports is cut, and a turn left with nothing to say gets "not yet" (D125, T-PRM-01 in P3). A reviewed entry is used first wherever it exists. A figure whose source is itself an estimate is said with "roughly" and keeps its source; a figure no source supports is never said (D113, P3), so "roughly" is never a way round the rule. Facts and stories about named living people, and every sensitive module, come only from reviewed entries and packs, never from live generation (D127). The explainers are held to the same rule. Each is a video generated live from a script the Primer writes and checks, so its voice-over and on-screen words come from checked content; its frames pass an automatic image check before it plays; it is labelled on screen as made by AI, and it shows no real person and no child (D122, D126, P4). That supersedes D111, and D73's rule that a video is never generated live for a child. Conversation is where the model is trusted; facts, figures and videos are where it is checked, by machine before a child sees them and by people afterwards, in a sample (D128).

Eight is where the band starts, not where the ambition stops. Below about eight, the owner's view is that a screen alone cannot hold a child's attention or teach the physical side of reading and writing Assumed (OA-28), which is why Foundation puts no screen in front of the child at all (D93). From about eight, in the owner's judgement, a child can hold a conversation, and that is what the band rests on (D41); now that a child may speak, reading and typing no longer mark the floor. The band is a decision, not a finding: Decision D41 sets Foundation at 4 to 7, the Primer at 8 to 13 and the Tutor at 14 to 18.
