The tutors of history, and what the Primer takes from them

The ambition is an old one: a tutor for every child, of the kind that, in the two stories below, was kept for future rulers. One pair is Greek and one Indian; the Primer takes the form from them and nothing else.

Aristotle taught Alexander, beginning when Alexander was about thirteen. Chanakya, traditionally identified with Kautilya and credited with the Arthashastra, a treatise on government, became counsellor and adviser to Chandragupta, who founded the Mauryan empire; legend makes him a teacher at Taxila. Not every historian accepts the identification of Chanakya with Kautilya, and the dating of the text is disputed.

Two things follow, both constraints rather than decoration.

  • The form, not the relationship or the views: one teacher, one learner, and a conversation that follows the learner's questions, which the Primer tries to make ordinary. The historical tutors followed a learner over years and knew their life; the Primer remembers a child's past questions and views as summaries, and nothing of their life (D104). Aristotle and Chanakya appear in it, if at all, as named thinkers in an inquiry pack, beside others who disagreed with them, and across each module the positions come from more than one culture, though not inside every question (D110).
  • The ambition, never a real person's voice. Putting words in a real person's mouth misattributes views, and a simulated historical figure talking to a child is the companion pattern 05 forbids (05). It would make the most memorable demonstration, and it is ruled out.

The Primer names methods, never people: it may run a Socratic dialogue, a thought experiment or a story, and it never speaks as a real person.

DecisionD47, 22 October 2026

D47 is about whom the Primer speaks as, not whom it names: read with D44, it names a thinker whenever it attributes a position, and never speaks as one. The two positions a child did not choose ask back as positions, in the Primer's words and attributed, "A Stoic would ask...", never in a thinker's first person, and each ask-back rests on an inquiry-pack entry (D110, T-PRM-02). Real people may also appear as evidence: the bank may hold the documented stories of named people, living ones included, as the football film tells footballers' stories, using only public, documented facts from reviewed sources, never a private life, never quoted beyond the source and never voiced (D113, P4). What duties that brings, in the UK and the US, is Open OI-30.

The age in the first pair is worth noticing. If the account is right, Aristotle began with a thirteen-year-old Assumed (CV-02): the top of the Primer's band, not the bottom of Foundation's. The novel that gives the Primer its name makes a related point.

In The Diamond Age, Nell's Primer is voiced live by a human actor, Miranda, who becomes a mother figure to her; the novel leaves open how much of Nell's development comes from the machine and how much from Miranda.

The design takes that as a warning rather than a plan, and the films sharpen it, because this Primer speaks. Its voice is synthetic, no real person's, and carries no persona (D47). It never says "I", never uses the child's name and never praises (D108, tested by T-PRM-09), so that nothing in its manner stands in for a person. It forms no relationship with the child (D104), and it keeps the child's adults in the picture: they choose the subjects (D46) and see the questions and the child's views, as summaries (D48, D104).
