How it relates to the Tutor and to page 13

The Primer for 8 to 13 is a tablet of its own, so it cannot be a mode of the Tutor's app: page 13's gated mode, if it ever exists, is the Tutor's. Page 13, the Primer gateway, describes a Primer that grows out of the Tutor as a gated mode for its older students, once the Tutor has earned trust. Since D105 that cannot describe the Primer for 8 to 13, and D40's "may share one platform" means the back end: the pipeline, the checks and the kill switch that P5 reuses from the Tutor. Whether the three ideas become one product, a family's account that grows with a child, or stay three, turns on whether a child would move from one to the next, and nothing in the vault can yet say; it is set out in which idea goes first.

Page 13's principles and release gates bind the Primer all the same: agency over persuasion, epistemic humility, adult accountability, a bounded relationship and developmental fit (13), and the six gates an independent review must pass before release (13). Those gates are the Primer's milestone PM4.

Page 13's argument for older users, and the answer to it

Page 13 argued that the first Primer users should be older adolescents, who can say when they disagree, read their own transcript and opt out. At 8 to 13 those safeguards are supplied by design and by adults instead. Sensitive modules stay off until a parent switches them on (D46). Triage runs on every turn and stops any question about the child's family, body, feelings or safety (T-PRM-06); a choice in the child's own future gets the reality and the schools of thought, never a direction (D106). The Primer states no conclusion on a contested question (T-PRM-03). It remembers ideas, not lives, as summaries, with nothing word for word and no recording (D104). The child hears the summary that will be kept and can correct it, this band's version of reading one's own transcript (D117), and the parent sees the topics, the questions and those summaries (D48, superseded in part by D104 and D107).

What remains weaker is real. A nine-year-old is less able than a sixteen-year-old to notice being led, so T-PRM-04 has to be stricter, and the supervised pilot at PM4 is a condition of release. A child reasoning about their own future can be steered by a single sentence, which is why every reply in such a conversation is checked for direction (P4). The full treatment is in P4.

Nor does the Tutor's evidence transfer: the findings in 00 are about AI supporting the teaching of a subject, and the Primer has to earn its own at PM4.
