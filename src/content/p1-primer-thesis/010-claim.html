The Primer in four sentences

The Primer is a tutor for a child's why-questions, and it is its own tablet: one per child, with no browser, no other apps and no notifications. A child asks aloud or by typing, and the Primer speaks a short answer at once, at a high level, checked automatically before it is spoken; while it speaks, it writes and checks a script for each explainer, each on one specific concept, and has a video model generate the video from it, which plays once its checks pass. Every answer has one shape: facts, each cited to a source the child can see, retrieved for the turn from a reviewed allow-list, or to a reviewed entry, used first wherever one exists, with any sentence no source supports cut; then "the harder bit", the question underneath, set out as three positions from named thinkers or traditions, each at its strongest; then questions and evidence until the child can say where they stand, and why, which the Primer reads back. It never gives its own view where people disagree, never tells a child what to do, even about a choice in their own future, and never says "I", uses the child's name or praises; and an exchange closes with "Leave it there, or keep going?", so that the child decides when to stop.

DecisionD114, from the films, confirmed by the owner, 31 October 2026

The decision says where the case begins, not that it is true; it is how the owner's explainer film opens. That parents of 8 to 13 year olds find, most evenings, that they lack the time to answer their children's why-questions properly, and would pay for something that does, is the owner's view Assumed (OA-53), which PM1 asks parents. If it holds, it names what the Primer replaces: not a tutor's hour, as in the Tutor's case, but the answer at home that nobody had the time to give.

It replaces an answer, not a parent. The time argument can be read as a machine standing in for the adult who was too busy, and the design is built against that reading. The parent chooses the topics and sees the questions and the child's views (D46, D48); the Primer never says "I", never praises and forms no relationship with the child (D108, D104); and nothing in it asks a child to come back (D25). Whether parents hear the argument as help or as reproach is part of what asking them about OA-53 will show.

The Primer is narrower than "an AI that answers children's questions", deliberately. A general assistant already answers a child's question. As far as this vault knows, none is built to state no fact that a source on a reviewed list does not support, with the source on the screen, to withhold its own conclusion as a rule, to give a parent control of the subjects, to keep no relationship with the child and to run on a tablet with nothing else on it, all at once Assumed; the category has not been surveyed (below). Those refusals are the product.
