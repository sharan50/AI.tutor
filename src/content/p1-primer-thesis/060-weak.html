Where the case is weak

Eleven places, in the order a sceptical investor would reach them.

  1. Nobody yet knows why a parent would pay. The Tutor's strongest commercial argument is that a household already paying a human tutor before an exam is choosing between that hour and the product (00). The Primer has no exam and no deadline. The owner's answer is time: it replaces the answer at home that nobody had the time to give (D114). That is a hypothesis, not a finding Assumed (OA-53), and whether parents will pay at all is still OA-22, the Primer's most load-bearing assumption. Unknown the price, and what it would be compared with.
  2. The tablet has to be made, and bought. A parent buys hardware as well as a service (D105), and the comparison becomes a children's tablet, or an app on a tablet the family already owns. Whether parents will buy a dedicated tablet for one child, at a price that covers it, is Assumed (OA-54); what it costs to make, ship, support, repair and certify is Unknown; what a connected tablet sold for children must meet before sale, in the UK and the US, is Open OI-31; and a tablet that costs more than families will pay is RP7. It is also slower than software: at PM4 the Primer would run on a stand-in tablet locked to it, and the dedicated tablet is made only after PM4's evidence (P6).
  3. The reputational risk is larger than the Tutor's. An AI that discusses death, belief or right and wrong with a ten-year-old is a headline waiting for a writer, whatever the design says. Sensitive modules start switched off and the Primer never gives its own view, which reduces the risk without removing it (RP1, OA-26).
  4. Reasoning about a child's own future is on by default. The Primer may reason with a child about a choice in their own future, such as whether to stake everything on football, setting out the reality and what the schools of thought say, and neither encouraging nor discouraging (D106). It is on at every age from 8 to 13 unless a parent turns it off, the reverse of the sensitive modules, and an AI setting out to a ten-year-old the odds against becoming a footballer is a second headline, whatever the fence says (RP9). Whether parents accept it on by default is Assumed (OA-57); whether it engages duties beyond OI-16's is Open OI-32. Questions about family, the body, feelings or safety still stop, with a reply shown and never spoken (D115, P4).
  5. The bank may name living people. The football film tells the stories of named footballers, and the bank may hold such stories, within D113's limits (above). A wrong or unfair sentence about a real person, told to a child, is a different harm from a wrong sentence about sleep. What duties apply, from privacy and defamation law to any rights in a name or image and the Children's code, in both markets, is Open OI-30.
  6. A child's voice goes ahead of counsel. Children speak from launch (D102), though whether a child's voice, or anything derived from it, is biometric data is Open OI-17. The Primer proceeds ahead of that answer, as the self-tutor does on its own questions: the audio becomes text at once and is thrown away, and the microphone listens only while the talk key is held (D116). If counsel's answer is a categoric no, voice is withdrawn after launch from a tablet sold as one a child talks to (RP8).
  7. There is no proof that it works. The nearest human method, Philosophy for Children, was evaluated in the EEF's two trials: a smaller one found small positive effects, and a larger one found no evidence of an effect on attainment Assumed (P3, CV-05). "Thinks more critically" may be hard to measure at all (OA-24), and neither result may transfer to an AI-led version (OA-25). Nothing in this vault shows that an AI-led philosophical dialogue improves how children reason.
  8. The youngest children may not manage it. If a nine-year-old cannot sustain a typed or spoken inquiry, or is misheard often enough that the conversation stalls, the band starts later than designed and the market shrinks with it (OA-23, OA-55).
  9. A summary may misstate a child. What is kept of a conversation is the read-back the child hears at the close, in plain words, which the child can correct before it is kept; nothing is kept word for word (D104, D117). A summary that puts a view in a child's mouth, shown to a parent, is worse than no record. Whether children accept the read-back and parents understand it is Assumed (OA-56), tested at PM4.
  10. "Not yet" may dominate. Children ask more questions than any catalogue can answer at launch. A Primer that declines too often stops being asked (OA-32, RP5), and the catalogue is a rate-limited human process. The films make each entry slower and dearer: every answer needs a drawn explainer specified and reviewed, at a cost that is Unknown (D73), and three positions written at their strongest. Whether both can be done at the rate the catalogue needs is Assumed (OA-58, OA-59), measured at PM2.
  11. The category has not been surveyed. The register holds no survey of the products that already talk with children, free or paid, of the family settings the general assistants may now have, or of the children's tablets and AI devices a dedicated tablet would compete with. Until someone has read what they do and claim, the Primer's difference from them is asserted, not shown. That is a reading task at PM1.

One more weakness belongs to the market. Almost every user is under 13, and in the US, which the owner wants to enter alongside the UK (D51), AI chatbots that talk with children are under active regulatory scrutiny Assumed (P4, CV-10). Whether a design that refuses companionship keeps the Primer outside the new rules is Open OI-15, and a tablet that answers aloud looks more like a companion than text on a screen does; whether hearing a child is itself biometric processing is OI-17, above.
