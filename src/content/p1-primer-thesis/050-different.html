What makes it different from free AI study tools

The free study modes already answer children's questions patiently and Socratically; the Tutor's thesis concedes that this behaviour is now a free toggle (00). The Primer does not compete on being a better conversationalist. It competes on seven things a general assistant is not built to do, five of them refusals.

The middle column is reasoned, not surveyed; whether any assistant or children's device already offers some of these, family settings or a locked-down tablet for example, has not been checked.

Answers facts only from checked explanations, and says "not yet" otherwise; a figure no reviewed entry holds is never said, and an estimate is said with "roughly".Its value is breadth; refusing to answer is a cost to it, not a feature.D44, D113; P3
Never states its own conclusion on a contested question, and never tells a child what to do, even about their own future; every position is attributed to a named thinker or tradition.It is built to be helpful, and the helpful-seeming answer to "what should I think?", or "what should I do?", is an answer.T-PRM-02, T-PRM-03, T-PRM-06; P5
Draws each answer as it speaks it, with an explainer made in advance as part of a reviewed explanation and drawn by code from its specification, the same every time.Its pictures, where it makes them, are generated on request, and nobody has reviewed them before a child sees them.D73, D111; P3
Gives a parent control of the subjects, with sensitive subjects off until a parent switches them on, choices in the child's own future on until a parent switches them off, and no way for anyone to add their own views.Its content is not written and reviewed module by module, whatever settings a parent has over access.D46, D106; P4
Is not a companion. No persona and no feelings; it never says "I", never uses the child's name and never praises, and it remembers past questions and views as summaries, nothing of the child's life. Each exchange closes with "Leave it there, or keep going?", with leaving offered first; going on stays inside a cap that cannot be extended, and nothing prompts a child to come back.A consumer chat product is usually judged on use.D25, D47, D103, D104, D108; P4
Shows the parent what was asked: the topics, each question as the catalogue question its entries answer, and the child's views as the read-backs they accepted or corrected. Nothing is kept word for word, no audio is kept and no video is ever recorded, so there is no conversation to show; a flagged exchange goes only to a trained reviewer and is deleted when the review closes.Its default user is an adult.D48, D102, D104, D107, D117; P4
Is its own tablet, with nothing else on it: one per child, with no browser, no other apps, no notifications and no camera, and a microphone that listens only while the child holds the talk key.It is an app on a phone or tablet built for everything else, with a browser and other apps a tap away.D105, D116; P2, P5

Any of the software among these could be copied by a well-funded competitor in a quarter, and the tablet by anyone who already makes devices. What is harder to copy is the reviewed catalogue, written and checked by people, its drawings included, and whatever evidence accumulates from using it. That is the Tutor's argument about its item bank (00), with the same weakness: a bet that the catalogue stays worth more than it costs, for long enough.
