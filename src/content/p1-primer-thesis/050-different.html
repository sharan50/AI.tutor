What makes it different from free AI study tools

The free study modes already answer children's questions patiently and Socratically; the Tutor's thesis concedes that this behaviour is now a free toggle (00). The Primer does not compete on being a better conversationalist. It competes on eight things a general assistant is not built to do, five of them refusals.

The middle column is reasoned, not surveyed; whether any assistant or children's device already offers some of these, family settings or a locked-down tablet for example, has not been checked.

States no fact without a source the child can see. Every factual sentence cites a source retrieved for the turn from a reviewed allow-list, or a reviewed entry where one exists, and is entailed by it; a sentence no source supports is cut, and a turn left with nothing to say gets "not yet"; an estimate is said with "roughly".Its value is breadth: it answers from what it learned or from the open web, and a source, where it gives one, is not one anyone reviewed for a child.D113, D125; P3
Never states its own conclusion on a contested question, and never tells a child what to do, even about their own future; every position is attributed to a named thinker or tradition.It is built to be helpful, and the helpful-seeming answer to "what should I think?", or "what should I do?", is an answer.T-PRM-02, T-PRM-03, T-PRM-06; P5
Checks everything automatically before it reaches the child: the spoken answer, each explainer's script and each video's frames; a video that fails is not shown, and the spoken answer stands. Every video is labelled on screen as made by AI and shows no real person and no child. People review a sample afterwards, and everything a child or parent reports.Its checks, where it has them, are set for an adult user, and its pictures are made on request, not from a checked script.D122, D126, D128; P4, P3
Never generates live where a mistake would do most harm. Facts and stories about named living people, and every sensitive module, come only from reviewed entries and packs.It generates whatever it is asked, about anyone, with the same checks throughout.D113, D127; P4
Gives a parent control of the subjects, with sensitive subjects off until a parent switches them on, choices in the child's own future on until a parent switches them off, and no way for anyone to add their own views.Its subjects are not divided into modules a parent can switch, whatever settings a parent has over access.D46, D106; P4
Is not a companion. No persona and no feelings; it never says "I", never uses the child's name and never praises, and it remembers past questions and views as summaries, nothing of the child's life. Each exchange closes with "Leave it there, or keep going?", with leaving offered first; going on stays inside a cap that cannot be extended, and nothing prompts a child to come back.A consumer chat product is usually judged on use.D25, D47, D103, D104, D108; P4
Shows the parent what was asked: the topics, each question, and the child's views as the read-backs they accepted or corrected. A summary keeps the question in general terms: the catalogue question where a reviewed entry answers it, which holds none of the child's words, and otherwise the Primer's own rewrite, held to the read-back's checks: no run of the child's words longer than a short phrase, and no detail of their family, friends, body or feelings. Nothing is kept word for word, no audio is kept, no video of the child is ever recorded, and the scripts and videos the Primer makes hold none of the child's words, so there is no conversation to show. A flagged exchange's words go only to a trained reviewer and are deleted when the review closes. Once the reviewer has judged it safe to tell this parent, the record shows a notice that a question was flagged and is being handled, with no words, subject or reply; the reviewer decides whether to tell the parent more.Its default user is an adult.D48, D102, D104, D107, D117, D121; P4
Is its own tablet, with nothing else on it: one per child, with no browser, no other apps, no notifications and no camera, and a microphone that listens only while the child holds the talk key.It is an app on a phone or tablet built for everything else, with a browser and other apps a tap away.D105, D116; P2, P5

Any of the software among these could be copied by a well-funded competitor in a quarter, and the tablet by anyone who already makes devices. What is harder to copy is the reviewed part, kept by people: the allow-list of sources, the entries and packs for named living people and the sensitive modules, and the checks and red-team batteries tuned for children of 8 to 13, with whatever evidence accumulates from using them. That is the Tutor's argument about its item bank (00), with the same weakness, a bet that the reviewed part stays worth more than it costs, for long enough, and a new one: with most of what a child sees generated live, the reviewed part is a smaller share of the product than the Tutor's bank is of the Tutor.
