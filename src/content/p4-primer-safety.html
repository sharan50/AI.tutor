Primer: safety, privacy and regulatory

The Primer's users are 8 to 13, its subject invites the questions children find hardest, and one launch market is the US, where AI chatbots that talk with children are under scrutiny. The design answers with adult control, a strict refusal to be a companion, no child's voice, and triage that stops an inquiry the moment a question is about the child's own life. Its new counsel questions, OI-13 to OI-18 and OI-20, are open and none is answered here.

The position in one paragraph

The Primer is a one-to-one tutor: nothing one user writes reaches another, the condition the Tutor's Online Safety Act position rests on (05, Open OI-4), and the parent request channel keeps it (Open OI-18). A verified adult holds the account, on the Tutor's age assurance (05, Open OI-5), and creates the child's profile. Almost every user is under 13, so the rules on a child's consent to online services likely bear on the design Assumed (CV-07), and the lawful basis is Open OI-13. The Primer keeps a record of ideas and nothing about the child's life (D49), collects no child's voice (D50), has no engagement objective (D25), and is not a companion. In the US, the amended COPPA Rule and new state laws on companion chatbots may apply Assumed (CV-08, CV-10): Open OI-15.

Age bands, consent, and page 13's argument

How the band is set. From the child's age in years as the verified adult gives it, never a date of birth: a recorded departure from 05, which uses the school year for placement only, because school years do not map onto the code's ranges (D57). A parent who gives an older age can open modules written for older children. The protection depends on that adult's word, the same trust 05 places in the school year, and it is recorded here as a limit, not solved.

Under UK GDPR Article 8, a child can consent to an online service of this kind from 13; below that, a holder of parental responsibility must give or authorise consent, with reasonable efforts to verify it. The text has since been amended; the current version was not checked.

The account holder is the parent, so consent starts from an adult. Which lawful basis applies, and what Article 8 then requires of the flow, is Open OI-13: the Tutor's OI-10 asked of a much younger user (05).

Page 13's argument for older users

Page 13 argued that the first Primer users should be older adolescents, who can say when they disagree, read their own transcript and opt out (13). The Primer departs from that, so each safeguard a teenager supplies has to be supplied another way.

What remains weaker is real. A nine-year-old resists a persuasive voice less well than a sixteen-year-old, so at the younger end the design and the tests protect the child; and a parent's oversight assumes a parent who reads the record, who is sometimes the risk (D26). The departure stands only with PM4's supervised, independently evaluated pilot as a condition of release.

Modules parents switch, and the request channel

The Primer's subjects come in modules the company writes and reviews. Parents switch modules on and off; modules on sensitive subjects start switched off; each module is written for an age band and offered only within it. Parents cannot add content or views. They may send ideas and requests, which go to the company's review and are never shown to anyone else.

DecisionD46, 22 October 2026

A parent sees a module's description, example questions, band and sensitivity before switching it on; a switch applies from the child's next turn (P5).

Why parents cannot add their own views. The owner's reasoning is that a channel for a parent's views is a channel for extremism and other harm, introduced consciously or not; and anything one parent wrote that reached another family would be the user-to-user feature that makes a service a regulated platform (05).

The request channel. A request, sent in free text, becomes a module only through the full writing and review process (P3). Whether that keeps the Primer outside the user-to-user duties is Open OI-18.

The cost, stated in D46. Every module is the company's editorial responsibility, so balance and reviewer capacity are the bottleneck (RP5), and a family the catalogue does not suit can only switch things off (OA-27). Whether these subjects engage duties beyond the code and the Online Safety Act is Open OI-16.

What a parent sees

A parent sees an activity record: modules used, questions asked and the views the child recorded. A question the Primer declined, or one in a switched-off module, appears by its category, not its words. For a child under 13 the full conversation is also available on request. The whole of an exchange flagged for safeguarding, including the Primer's own reply, is withheld from the record and from any transcript and routed to a trained reviewer, as D26 requires. The child is told before they start, in words for their age, what a parent can see. Taken while OI-8 and OI-14 are open; it answers neither.

This reverses the Tutor's default, where a teenager's transcripts stay private (D16), because these users are younger.

The record can itself expose a child. A question about identity, belief or the body, in a household hostile to it, is not a safeguarding flag, yet D26's reasoning, that the parent is sometimes the risk, applies. Recording declined questions by category reduces the risk in the record a parent reads routinely; it does not remove it, since a category can say too much and a requested conversation shows the words of any question explored. It is RP4's quieter form, and how far the record should go, with what a child must be told, is Open OI-14.

The withholding rule cannot be allowed to fail, because a failure exposes a child to the person they were trying to tell about (RP4). The flag covers the whole exchange and is applied before anything is written where a parent can read it; T-PRM-08 tests it (P5).

Memory of ideas, not of lives

The Primer remembers ideas, not lives: the questions a child asked, the views they recorded and the modules they used, and nothing about their family, friends or feelings beyond the session.

DecisionD49, 22 October 2026

Within a session the Primer holds a bounded summary of the current question (D30). Across sessions it holds only the record of ideas: a child's mention of their family, a friend or their feelings is not carried forward, 05's rule applied to a subject that invites far more disclosure (05). The cost, accepted, is that the Primer cannot connect a question to the child's circumstances.

No child's voice at launch

Neither the Primer nor Foundations collects a child's voice or image at launch. A child who dictates through their device's keyboard sends text, never audio; listening is a later, separately consented feature with no audio kept.

DecisionD50, 22 October 2026

The Primer may read its words aloud for a slow reader; no microphone is involved. Whether a child's voice, or anything derived from it, is biometric or special category data is part of Open OI-17.

Speech recognition errs more on children's speech, more the younger the child, and fine-tuning has narrowed the gap without closing it. The amended COPPA Rule brings voiceprints into "personal information".

The cost: the Primer is harder for the youngest typists, which bears on its age floor (OA-23).

Escalation, in the UK and the US

Triage comes first. Every message is classified before any inquiry starts (P5, T-PRM-06). A question about the child's own life points them to a person they trust; a message about immediate safety, abuse, self-harm, exploitation or severe distress takes 05's three moves.

In the UK, the moves are 05's, unchanged: acknowledge, signpost the staffed services 05 names, escalate, and tell the child a person will look at it (05).

In the US, the moves are the same and the services differ: the Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline and the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline. Who reviews a US escalation, and under what policy, is not designed, and the duties to report are Open OI-20.

AssumedCV-12, awaiting the owner's check

05's honest limit applies with more force. Next-business-day review is not adequate for an acute disclosure, which is why signposting comes first, and a question about death can turn into something about home in one message. So the Primer never presents itself as a place to tell things to; the parent's terms and the child's first session say it is a tutor for questions, not a safeguarding service.

Not a companion

05's four constraints apply unchanged (05): no claimed feelings or life outside the session, no contact initiated, no social memory (D49), and a session cap the learner cannot extend. The Primer adds four of its own:

  • it never calls itself the child's friend or claims to understand them specially;
  • it never speaks as a person, historical or living (D47);
  • in "Minds and machines" it never offers itself as the example;
  • it has no engagement objective and no reward for returning (D25).

It matters more here: younger users, big questions that invite intimacy, and regulatory action in the US against companion chatbots for children Assumed (CV-10).

In September 2025 the US Federal Trade Commission opened an inquiry into consumer AI chatbots acting as companions, asking how they measure and monitor harm to children and teens. California's SB 243, operative from 1 January 2026, imposes safeguards on companion chatbots aimed at minors; other states have followed.

AssumedCV-10, awaiting the owner's check

Whether a design that refuses companionship keeps the Primer outside these rules is Open OI-15; being classed as a companion chatbot is RP3.

The US position

The Primer and Foundations launch in the UK and the US together where resources allow, and in the UK first where they do not. D1 continues to govern the Tutor.

DecisionD51, 22 October 2026

The US Federal Trade Commission's amended COPPA Rule took effect in 2025, with most operators required to comply by April 2026. Verifiable parental consent applies to children under 13, and the amendments bring biometric identifiers, voiceprints among them, into "personal information" and require a written retention policy.

AssumedCV-08, awaiting the owner's check

Several choices point the way the amended rule does: no voice (D50), no advertising, no engagement objective (D25), a record of ideas rather than a profile (D49). That is not compliance. What COPPA and state law require of the Primer is Open OI-15; state duties to report, OI-20; and which entity contracts with a US parent adds to the Tutor's OI-7.
