For counsel: twelve questions, none answered

No answer, no likely answer, and no range is offered for any of these. All are also unacted on except OI-3, where D34 proceeds under a fixed convention because waiting would mean a product that cannot describe itself. Supplying one would be worse than leaving the question open, because a plausible guess in a repository like this one gets quoted later as though it were advice. One, OI-3, we are proceeding ahead of under D34, and the row says so.

The pattern in this list

Worth noticing, because it shapes what M1 and PM1 should be: sixteen of the thirty-six assumptions are resolvable by reading or by asking, not by building or by advice. OA-2 is a few hours with three published policies. OA-5 is reading five sets of terms. OA-8 is one notified text. OA-13 is a tax reference. OA-16 is an hour with the Children's code. OA-17 is thirty phone calls. OA-10 is a landing page, and OA-35 the same page shown to US parents. OA-20 is Keeping Children Safe in Education and whatever supplier standard a school applies. OA-22, OA-26, OA-29 and OA-34 are conversations with parents; OA-28 and OA-33 are conversations with Reception and Year 1 teachers; OA-31 is reading published market data. The field research guide carries the questions for all of them.

Those sixteen include the most load-bearing assumption about the Tutor and the most load-bearing one about the Primer (OA-22), the one that decides whether Route B has a market, and the one most likely to end a school conversation in its first ten minutes. None of them requires a lawyer, an engineer or a product. They are in M1 and PM1 for that reason, and a plan that starts by building instead is choosing to pay the highest available price for the same information.
