D130 to D138: Foundation vision, the owner's decisions of 5 November 2026 and how they were applied

Taken on 5 November 2026, when the owner widened Foundation's scope and named it Foundation vision. D130 to D135 are the owner's: D130, D131 and D133 quote the owner's words, and D132, D134 and D135 record the option the owner chose. D136 to D138 were taken while applying them, and each is the owner's to keep or reverse. For Foundation's later phases and its home version they supersede D41's band and parts of D89 and D93, and for Foundation in every school they supersede D90's "or in the cloud"; each of those entries carries a note. None reopens D1 to D14, and none changes which idea goes first (D94). The brief that records them is FOUNDATION_VISION_BRIEF.md.

The owner's decisions

D130. It could be that we expand after a pilot and proof of concept, expand to all the use cases where there is a certain amount of fixed positioning. So like evaluating cooking skills, evaluating piano or other musical instruments. And we can talk about the expanded scope as like Foundation vision or something along those lines.
Driven by: the owner's thought, on 5 November 2026, that Foundation "could be even further improved, or rather increased in scope": what Foundation does for a pencil at a desk, a fixed camera could do for other skills practised in one place. Applied as: Foundation vision names Foundation's expanded scope, set out in F7: the same instrument, a camera that sees the hands and the work and never the face, for other skills practised at a fixed station, such as cooking at a counter and the piano or keyboard. It starts only after the first version's pilot and proof of concept (D136). What counts as a fixed station is a proposed rule for the owner to adopt or amend (F7). Rejected: a fourth idea of its own, which the owner's words do not make it; and skills practised on the move, which no fixed camera can follow. Makes harder: each skill needs its own catalogue of correct technique and common errors, written and reviewed by people qualified in it (F7); and whether one camera can judge any of them is untested (OA-64). F7
D131. Both School classes (a sell-to-school based model where Foundation is hosted on-prem) AND a D2C version for lessons and home practice that would be connected to a cloud based version of Foundation and feedback is through an app.
Driven by: the owner's answer when asked whom Foundation vision should serve. Applied as: two versions. In schools, Foundation is sold to the school, runs on the school's own servers (D135), and the teacher sees the class view, as now. At home, a home unit connects to Foundation's cloud service, and the feedback comes through an app (D134, D137). Rejected: the other options offered: schools only, at any school age or at 4 to 7; and any learner, adults included, which would take the vault beyond children. Makes harder: a second customer, families, beside schools, with a consumer sale and its support (OA-66); a camera in a family's home, whose lawful basis is open (OI-33); and frames of a child leaving the home for the cloud, to be read and discarded there (D97). Supersedes D89's "not to parents and not for home use" in part, for the home version only. The first version stays a school's alone. F7
D132. Foundation vision judges a skill by camera only: there is no microphone, and no data from the instrument.
Driven by: the owner's choice of "Camera only", of three options offered, when asked how Foundation vision should judge playing. Applied as: Foundation vision judges only what one camera can see: the hands, the fingering, the posture and the work. It does not hear an instrument's notes, timing, tone or tuning. Whether a camera above a keyboard can see which keys are pressed, and when, is untested (OA-64). D97's "no microphone" holds everywhere, at school and at home. Rejected: a digital keyboard's own note data alongside the camera; and a microphone, which would hear the voices in the room and need its own counsel question; OI-17 stays open for Foundation should a microphone ever be added. Makes harder: what matters most in playing, its sound, is outside Foundation's view, while practice apps that listen judge it, Assumed (CV-41), a risk RF6 records; so the instruments Foundation vision can serve are those where the hands and posture are much of what is taught. F7
D133. The version which we start to GTM with will be Reception to Year 2, then we will phase capability to expand to 4-18 + a parallel home D2C version which I will have to scope out in terms of what additional use cases we can apply. For the latter, the main examples that come to mind are musical instruments like piano and keyboard.
Driven by: the owner's answer when asked what ages Foundation vision should serve. Applied as: Foundation goes to market first as the classroom instrument for Reception to Year 2 that F1 to F6 describe. Later phases add capability for pupils of 4 to 18, one skill or age range at a time (D136). A home version, sold to families, runs in parallel with those phases. Which skills the home version covers is the owner's to scope; musical instruments, such as the piano and keyboard, are the owner's main examples. Nothing here changes which idea goes first (D94). Rejected: 4 to 18 from the start; 4 to 7 for good; and adult learners. Makes harder: pupils of 4 to 18 span all five of the Children's code's age ranges, Assumed (CV-06), and the older ones overlap the Primer's and the Tutor's ages; the home version's design waits on the owner's scoping; and two lines of work in parallel, after the first version, divide a small team (RF9). Supersedes D41's band for Foundation in its later phases. F7
D134. In the home version, the app shows its feedback to adults, and to a learner of 13 or over, who may hold the app themselves; a child under 13 is shown nothing.
Driven by: the owner's choice of "Adults, and learners 13+", of three options offered, when asked who sees the feedback in the home version's app. Applied as: the app belongs to a verified adult, who may add the child's own teacher (D137). A learner of 13 or over may hold the app, if their adult allows it (D137). For a child under 13 the app is the adult's alone, and the home unit shows the child nothing, as the desk unit does (D90). In schools, at every age, the pupil is shown nothing and the teacher acts (D93). No version rewards anyone: no stars, streaks, badges or confetti (D25). Rejected: adults only, at every age; and the learner too, at every age, which would put feedback on a screen before a child of four. Makes harder: the app must know whether its holder is an adult or a learner of 13 or over; if that is the verified adult's word, as for the Primer (D57), an adult who gives an older age gives a younger child the feedback, a limit recorded, not solved; a learner of 13 or over is still a child under the Children's code, Assumed (CV-06), and in the UK is old enough to consent to an online service themselves, Assumed (CV-07), which is part of OI-33; and the app's words must suit a teenager as well as an adult. Supersedes D93's "never shows the child anything" in part, for a learner of 13 or over in the home version only. F7
D135. Everything Foundation does in schools runs on the school's own servers, so no picture of a child leaves the building.
Driven by: the owner's words in D131, "a sell-to-school based model where Foundation is hosted on-prem", and the owner's choice of "Yes, all of Foundation" when asked whether that settles the classroom design too. Applied as: the processing service is a machine in the school, for the first version and for every later phase in schools. F5's open choice between the school's servers and the cloud is settled. Only the home version runs in Foundation's cloud service (D131, D137). Rejected: the cloud for schools, which would make support simpler; and on-prem for the new skills only. Makes harder: the school houses and powers the machine, and the company supports hardware it cannot reach (F5); what the machine costs, and how many desks one machine can serve, are Unknown until FM2 measures processing per desk; and every school is an installation on site. Supersedes D90's "or in the cloud" for schools. F5

Taken while applying them, the owner's to keep or reverse

D136. Nothing of Foundation vision is built before FM4 decides to build Foundation further. Each new skill, each new age range and the home version then passes its own bench test and its own observed pilot, as FM2 and FM3 require of the first version, before it is offered. Its cheap questions are asked at FM1, alongside Foundation's own.
Driven by: D130's "after a pilot and proof of concept", read as the first version's bench test (FM2, the proof of concept) and observed pilot (FM3); and D133's phases. Rejected: building a new skill alongside the first version, before the reader has met T-FND-04's threshold on a five-year-old's page; and one pilot for every skill, which would leave a skill nobody tested. Makes harder: every phase waits on its own evidence, and how long the ladder takes is Unknown; and a skill that fails its bench test is not offered, however much it is wanted. F7
D137. The home version keeps the desk unit's guarantees. The home unit is a camera on an arm or a stand, with no microphone, screen, speaker or light; an adult sets it up and aims it at the work, never at a face; its frames are read in Foundation's cloud service and discarded; the app belongs to a verified adult, who may add the child's own teacher; and what it reads is kept as text in the family's account, deleted when the adult asks or closes the account, and never used to infer a difficulty or to compare the child with others.
Driven by: D131, D132 and D134; D90 and D97, whose reasons hold in a home as in a classroom; and D98's second half. Rejected: the family's own phone or tablet as the camera, which has a microphone and a screen in front of the child; and keeping frames to check the feedback. Makes harder: a device to design, make, certify and sell, as the Primer's tablet is (OI-33); a family's home in a camera's view, where the face check must discard every member of the household, not only the child (RF8); a mount a family can still move; and whether the home unit could read its frames itself, so that none leaves the home, is for the home version's bench test to show. F7
D138. Foundation never supervises. It watches technique, never safety: where a skill can hurt, such as a knife or a hob, an adult in the room is responsible for the learner, at school or at home, and nothing Foundation shows or says suggests otherwise.
Driven by: D130's cooking; a camera that reads technique can neither see every danger nor stop one; and D93's rule that a person, not Foundation, is the one who acts. Rejected: alerts for danger, such as a hand near a blade, which would invite a school or a parent to rely on a camera for a child's safety. Makes harder: Foundation adds nothing to safety in a food room or a kitchen; and a home version that covers cooking, if the owner scopes one, needs an adult present at every session, which Foundation cannot check.
