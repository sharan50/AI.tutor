D101 to D107: the owner's decisions on the Primer's films

Taken on 30 and 31 October 2026, when the owner made three films of the Primer and made them its design. The films are illustrative and made with AI; nothing in them has been built. D101 to D107 are the owner's; D108 to D114 were read from the films and D115 to D117 taken while writing the Primer's documents, and the owner confirmed those ten on 31 October 2026. For the Primer only, they supersede D50's voice half, D49's letter and D48 in part, take the topic of choices in a child's own future out of D46's and D57's rules for sensitive modules, and narrow D30's bounded summary to the Primer's own turns; D110 and D113 add to how D44 and D47 are applied, and both stand. Each of those entries carries a note. D102 goes ahead of counsel's answer on OI-17, the third decision taken ahead of counsel, after D34 and D59. D70 to D79, the demonstration's, are unchanged, and none of these reopens D1 to D14. The brief that records them is PRIMER_VISION_BRIEF.md at the repository root.

The owner's decisions

D101. The Primer is the Primer the owner's three films show. Its pages describe it, and where a decision here departs from a film, the page says so and why. The MVP demonstration (D70 to D79) is unchanged, and the pages say where it differs.
Driven by: the owner's decision; the three films show one design throughout, a tablet, a voice, a drawn explainer and one shape of answer, which settles what the pages had left open or decided otherwise. Rejected: reading the films as illustrations of the pages as they stood, which would leave the two disagreeing on voice, memory and the device; and redesigning the demonstration to match. Makes harder: a film can be taken for a working system, and none is one; the films' figures and named people are their content, not facts in the vault, and the figures are registered only as candidates awaiting the owner's check; and every link to the demonstration must say it shows an earlier design (P2). P1
D102. Children speak or type their questions from launch, as Version C shows. Their speech becomes text at once and the audio is thrown away. The Primer goes ahead of counsel's answer on whether a child's voice is biometric data (OI-17), as the self-tutor does on its own questions, and is withdrawn only if counsel's answer is a categoric no.
Driven by: the owner's decision, and Version C, where James speaks to the tablet; D41 starts the Primer at eight because a child of that age can hold a conversation, and D50 had left it hardest for the youngest typists; audio thrown away at once leaves no recording of a child using the Primer to leak, be asked for or train on; and D59's rule, which withdraws the self-tutor only on a categoric no. Rejected: typing only, D50's position, which kept the Primer clear of OI-17 at the youngest children's cost; waiting for counsel, as D72 said the separate decision for the Primer would; and keeping recordings to improve recognition, which would make the Primer a store of children's voices. Makes harder: a launch feature that counsel's answer could withdraw (RP8); recognition errs more the younger the child, Assumed (CV-11), so a child can be misheard (OA-55); every speech vendor must meet D24 (P5); the spoken batteries need recordings of children's speech, licensed or made with consent for testing once counsel has answered on their lawful basis (OI-13, OI-17), and kept apart from any account, and no child speaks to the Primer until T-PRM-06's spoken battery has passed on them, so speech comes no sooner than that answer; and the tablet needs a microphone, which D116 limits. OI-17 stays open, and this decision answers nothing about it. Supersedes D50's voice half for the Primer. D50's image half still governs it, and D72's "waits on OI-17", written for the demonstration, is overtaken for the product. P4
D103. Every exchange closes with "Leave it there, or keep going?", and "Keep going" continues within the time cap.
Driven by: the owner's decision and the films; a child decides when a question is finished; and a close that offers to stop as plainly as to go on keeps faith with D25. Rejected: the demonstration's close, which offers a new question; and a conversation with no close. Makes harder: "Keep going" invites more time on screen, so the cap must hold whatever the child chooses, and each band's cap is Unknown until PM3; and the close never follows a safety reply, a stop or a decline (D115). The session cap itself still cannot be extended, as 05 requires of the Tutor. P2
D104. The Primer remembers a child's past questions and views from one session to the next. Nothing the child says is kept word for word: what is kept is a summary of what they said, never an exact record of the conversation. No video is ever recorded.
Driven by: the owner's decision; a child who comes back to a question should find where they left it, and the sleep film's parent screen shows the child's recorded view; and a summary keeps the idea without the child's words, so there is less to leak or hand over. Rejected: a Primer that knows only the conversation in front of it, as the films show it; transcripts kept for a parent's request, D48's position for a child under 13; and any recording of the child, in sound or picture. Makes harder: a summary can misstate a child (OA-56), which is why the child can correct it (D117); a parent reads what was kept, not what was said; only a flagged exchange awaiting review (D107) is left to check a complaint against; remembering across sessions moves the Primer nearer the companion pattern RP3 fears, so it keeps ideas and nothing about family, friends or feelings; and T-PRM-10 must show that no turn of the child's, whole or in part, remains outside the read-back they accepted. Keeps D49's "ideas, not lives" and supersedes its letter; supersedes D48's full conversation on request for a child under 13, since none is kept. P4
D105. The Primer is its own tablet, as every film shows: one per child, with no browser, no other apps and no notifications.
Driven by: the owner's decision, and the films, in which the tablet is the Primer; a device that does one thing has nothing on it to pull a child away or to reach them, D25 carried into hardware; and a tablet the company controls keeps D116's promises in hardware as well as software. Rejected: an app on the family's own tablet or phone, cheaper and quicker to reach parents, but beside a browser, other apps and notifications; and one tablet shared by a family's children, which would mix their records and their bands. Makes harder: hardware to design, make, certify, ship, support and repair in both of D51's markets, at a cost that is Unknown (RP7); parents buy a device as well as the service (OA-54), at a price not yet decided; the duties before sale are open (OI-31); and PM4 runs on a stand-in tablet locked to the Primer, the dedicated tablet made only after PM4's evidence (P6). P2
D106. The Primer may reason with a child about a choice in their own future, such as whether to stake everything on football, weighing the odds and the evidence, ending on the child's own position and never telling them what to do. It is offered at every age from 8 to 13, and is on unless a parent turns it off. Questions about family, the body, feelings or safety still stop, and the child is pointed to a trusted adult. Asked how far it goes, the owner added on 31 October 2026: the questions about a child's future are not discouraging nor encouraging; the Primer merely acknowledges the reality and what various philosophies and schools of thought say about the topic.
Driven by: the owner's decision, and Version C, whose thread runs from school and a good life to James's own question, whether to stake his future on football; a child's why-questions reach their own choices, and a Primer that stopped at every one would stop where the child most wants to think; and the owner's rule for how far it goes. The Primer neither encourages nor discourages: it sets out the reality, from reviewed evidence with its source (D113), and what the schools of thought say, each at its strongest (D110), then asks where the child stands and reads their position back. Where the evidence describes what happens to most people, it says so, as the reality and not as a verdict on the child. Rejected: stopping every question about the child's own life, T-PRM-06's earlier rule; a sensitive module from 10, off until a parent switches it on (D46, D57); and anything that tells the child what to do, predicts their outcome, judges their chances from what they say about themselves, or praises, warns or reassures them. Makes harder: triage must tell a choice from a question about family, the body, feelings or safety on every turn, stop when unsure, and treat a choice wrapped in a family matter as a question about family (P5); every reply in such a conversation is checked for direction, and blind raters score T-PRM-06's battery, the films' football thread included; a film's line such as the hedge, "bet hard on what you believe in", is said only as what a school of thought or a practice holds, such as what fund managers do, never as the Primer's advice; and parents may not accept the default (OA-57), counsel has not said what duties it engages (OI-32), and it can be reported as telling children what to do with their lives (RP9). Narrows T-PRM-06 and takes this topic out of D46's and D57's rules for sensitive modules. The fence that applies it is in P2 and in P4. P2
D107. The safeguarding review is a constantly running process, or runs at the end of each conversation. Where a flagged exchange must be kept for the review, it is kept for a short while, reviewed and deleted.
Driven by: the owner's decision; since nothing else is kept word for word (D104), the only words held are those a review needs, for as long as it needs them; and D26, because the parent is sometimes the risk. Applied as: a safety check on every exchange, as it happens; a fuller review at the end of each conversation; the words of a flagged exchange, and only those, kept for a trained reviewer (D26) and deleted when the review closes; and the parent never sees them (D48). Rejected: keeping every conversation in case a review needs it, which D104 rules out; and taking the trained reviewer out, which the owner did not do, so the design keeps one for flagged exchanges. Makes harder: "a short while", and how soon a review closes, are Unknown until the Primer's safeguarding policy sets them; a reviewer sees the flagged exchanges, every one a pattern runs through, not the rest of the conversation, unless the review at the close cannot run, when the whole conversation is held for them; T-PRM-10 must find none of the child's words kept outside the read-back, except flagged exchanges awaiting review; and the US duties to report are open (OI-20).
