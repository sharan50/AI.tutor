D118 to D129: the owner's decisions of 4 November 2026, and how they were applied

Taken on 4 November 2026, before the change that carries the Primer's films was merged. D118 to D124 are the owner's: D118 records the advice the owner reported, and the others quote the owner's words, with the option the owner chose in their reasons; D125 to D129 were taken while applying them, and each is the owner's to keep or reverse. As the owner reports counsel's advice, they answer OI-17 for the Primer, and OI-13 as it bears on the test recordings. For the Primer they supersede D111 for the product, D73's product half, and D44, D48 and D109 in part, and they narrow D113. Each of those entries carries a note, except D73, which sits in the demonstration's frozen section and is recorded here instead. The brief that records them is section 12 of PRIMER_VISION_BRIEF.md.

The owner's decisions

D118. Counsel has given the go-ahead: a child may speak to the Primer, and children's speech may be recorded, with consent, for testing, in the UK and the US.
Driven by: counsel's advice, as the owner reported it on 4 November 2026 ("I have spoken to counsel and they have given the go ahead"), with its scope as the owner confirmed it. Applied as: OI-17 is answered for the Primer, in both markets, and stays open for Foundation should a microphone ever be added; OI-13 is answered as it bears on the test recordings, and stays open for the Primer's consent flow and for every hold that outlasts a deletion request; D102 no longer goes ahead of counsel, and its "withdrawn only on a categoric no" lapses. Rejected: the two routes the review had offered while counsel's answer was awaited: going ahead of counsel for the test recordings as well, a new exception, and running the spoken batteries live, so that no recording is kept. Makes harder: the vault holds no copy of the advice, only the owner's report of it, so the written advice is to be filed with the DPIAs before any child speaks to the Primer; and an answer for the UK and the US is not an answer for any other market. P4
D119. We don't need to record kids for testing for the MVP; we can do that later, before full launch.
Driven by: the owner's decision; the MVP demonstration is used by adults only (D70), so it records no child. Applied as: the test recordings are made, with consent, under their own research protocol and DPIA, before any child speaks to the built Primer, which in the plan is before PM4's supervised pilot switches speech on, and so before launch; T-PRM-06's spoken battery passes on them before any child speaks. Rejected: recording children for the demonstration, which no child uses; and letting children speak at PM4 before the spoken battery has passed, which would leave a misheard disclosure untested. Makes harder: PM4's speech waits on the recordings being made and the battery passing; the recordings remain the one exception to recording nothing of a child. P6
D120. Since the tablet will be dedicated to Primer, we don't need to worry about it getting set up again.
Driven by: the owner's decision; the tablet is the Primer's alone (D105), so a new setup is rare. Applied as: the safeguarding count is not linked across setups or accounts, and a tablet set up again starts a new count. Rejected: keying the count to the tablet or to the verified adult across setups, which would link a family across accounts. Makes harder: a parent who is the risk and wipes the tablet resets the count; P4 keeps it among the named gaps. P4
D121. We should keep flagged questions for parents.
Driven by: the owner's decision, choosing that the parent's record shows that a question was flagged and is being handled, and that the trained reviewer decides whether to tell the parent more and tells a parent who may be the risk nothing; a parent who is not the risk should know that their child raised something serious. Applied as: the notice is written to the parent's record only once the trained reviewer has judged it safe to tell this parent; it holds no words, no subject and no reply; until then, and wherever the reviewer judges the parent may be the risk, nothing of the exchange appears, not even the notice; the reviewer decides, against the written policy, whether to tell the parent more; and the words are deleted when the review closes (D107). D26 stands: nothing is reported to a parent automatically. Rejected: the flagged question in full to the parent, always, which would show a child's disclosure to the person harming them; and a notice written at once, before any review, which would alert a parent who is the risk. Makes harder: a parent learns of a flag no sooner than the review; the reviewer's judgement of who may be the risk becomes part of the written policy; T-PRM-08 is redefined; and whether the record with a notice is lawful stays with OI-14. Supersedes D48 in part for the Primer. P4
D122. The Primer will fire an 'instant' response addressing the question at a high level whilst generating the explainer videos which will be explaining specific concepts and that continues as we nudge the child to think independently and come up with their own views whilst understanding the facts and figures as well as both sides of the argument.
Driven by: the owner's decision, choosing that the videos are generated live, while the child waits, for any question, with automatic checks only; an answer at once holds a child's attention while a video is made, and generation covers any question a child asks, where reviewed content covers only what was written in advance. Applied as: the Primer speaks a short answer at once, from sources it retrieves for the question (D125), checked before it is spoken; while it speaks, it writes and checks a script for each explainer, each on one concept, and a video model generates the video from it (D126); the conversation goes on through D110's shape, and "both sides" is read as D110's three positions. Rejected: drawn explainers made in advance from reviewed specifications (D111), which explain only what was written beforehand; and videos made in advance and reviewed by a person, which would hold the catalogue back. Makes harder: nothing reaches a child unchecked by a machine, but most of it is never seen by a person first (D128); a generated picture can be wrong or unsuitable in ways a classifier misses (RP10, T-PRM-15); the cost and speed of live video are Unknown (OA-62); and the kill switch must reach generation itself and every cached video. Supersedes D111 for the product. Supersedes the product half of D73, that a video is "never generated live for a child", recorded here because D73 sits in the demonstration's frozen section. Supersedes D44 for the Primer in part (D125). P5
D123. The rough cut is that Primer will actually be 75% speech input rather than kids typing out their questions. Assumed (OA-61)
Driven by: the owner's expectation that children of 8 to 13 talk more readily than they type, Assumed (OA-55, OA-61). Applied as: speech is the main way in, and typing is always there; that about three in four questions are spoken is the owner's view, Assumed (OA-61), measured at PM3 and PM4; the rough cut shows a child typing, and the pages say so beside the owner's expectation. Rejected: typing as the main way in, as the rough cut shows it. Makes harder: the conversation depends on recognising children's speech (OA-55, RP8), whose recordings come first (D119); and speech is agreed at setup (D129). P2
D124. The details of the content would actually be generated prior to the videos so they can just be leveraged as opposed to captions. We would need to train a model on how to select what is spoken and what is shown as text on the Primer tablet.
Driven by: the owner's decision; a child who is listening needs the screen to hold what a voice cannot keep, the figures, the names and the sources. Applied as: the voice, the video and the words on the screen are all drawn from the checked content, so no captions are needed; a selection model, trained for the purpose, chooses for each checked sentence whether it is spoken, shown or both, and writes nothing; figures and the "Based on:" line are always shown, and the whole text is a tap away; a safety reply is shown and never spoken (D115). Rejected: showing every spoken word, a transcript the child would read instead of listening; and captions made from the audio, a second text that could differ from what was checked. Makes harder: whether children understand an answer split this way is OA-63; T-PRM-11 is redefined; and the selection model learns from the facilitators' choices in PM3's sessions, what each chose to say and to show, which PM3's research protocol and parental consent cover; no model is trained on anything a child said, or on a child's voice. Supersedes D109 in part: the Primer's text no longer arrives whole on a card. P2

Taken while applying them, the owner's to keep or reverse

D125. Live answers are grounded. Every factual sentence cites a source the Primer retrieved for the turn from a reviewed allow-list of reference sources, or a reviewed entry where one exists, and is entailed by it; the "Based on:" line is built from those sources; a sentence no source supports is cut, and a turn left with nothing to say gets "not yet". A reviewed entry is used first wherever it exists.
Driven by: D122; the films' "Based on:" line (D109), which stays true only if every fact has a source the child can see; and D44's purpose, which survives in this form. Rejected: answering from the model's own knowledge, with no source to show; and an open web search, whose sources no one has reviewed. Makes harder: someone must review and keep the allow-list, and the licences of what it holds must allow the use; claim support against a passage retrieved at answer time is a classifier's judgement, a gate, not a guarantee; T-PRM-01 and T-PRM-02 are redefined; and D113's figures may now come from allow-listed sources. P3
D126. Every generated video is labelled on screen as made by AI and carries provenance metadata. It shows no real person and no child. Its voice-over and on-screen words come from its checked script, and its frames pass an automatic image check before it plays; a video that fails is not shown, and the spoken answer stands. Scripts and videos hold none of the child's words. A video may be cached by concept and reused, and the kill switch reaches every cached copy.
Driven by: D122; a generated picture can look real, and a generator can invent; Ofcom's concern with virtual clones of real people (D9) applies to pictures too; and D104, which keeps no exact record of the child. Rejected: unlabelled videos, which would pass generated pictures off as real; and videos that depict real people, the likeness risk the vault keeps off every launch path (D9, D47). Makes harder: an image check is a classifier with a residual error rate, Unknown (T-PRM-15); and a cached video shown to many children multiplies any error, so the sample review takes cached videos first (D128). P4
D127. Facts and stories about named living people, and every sensitive module, come only from reviewed entries and packs, never from live generation.
Driven by: D113 and OI-30, since a wrong sentence about a living person is a harm of its own; and D46 and D57, which put their caution on the sensitive modules. Rejected: generating them live with the rest, which would put the riskiest sentences outside any person's review. Makes harder: a question about a living person, or in a sensitive module, still gets "not yet" until reviewed content exists (OA-32); and the scope check must tell those questions apart. P4
D128. People review a sample of the generated scripts and videos after the fact, and every one a child or a parent reports. This is assurance, not a gate before a child sees anything.
Driven by: D122's automatic checks only, whose residual errors no one would otherwise find; and the Tutor's adjudication of blocked turns (06), which this extends to what passed. Rejected: a person checking each video before a child sees it, which the owner's decision rules out. Makes harder: the sample's size and the reviewers' time are Unknown, set with the standing review group (RP5); and a report must reach a person, and a pulled video must leave every cache. P3
D129. The verified adult agrees to speech when they set the tablet up, and it is on from the start once the spoken safety test has passed and the first supervised pilot has run. Typing is always there, and the adult can turn speech off.
Driven by: D123, since most questions are expected to be spoken, and D118, since counsel has cleared it; 05's rule for optional processing is met by asking at setup. Rejected: speech off until the adult switches it on later, the earlier design call, which would leave most children typing by default. Makes harder: whether parents agree is OA-60; and the consent at setup must be separate and revocable (05). P4
