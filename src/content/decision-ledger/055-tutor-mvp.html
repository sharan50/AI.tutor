D80 to D88: the Tutor's MVP

All dated 26 October 2026, when the owner decided to show the Tutor first with an MVP that runs the whole design for adults who play learners of 14 to 18, built by a team of Claude Code agents on a harness built first. D80 to D83 are the owner's; D84 to D88 were taken while writing the MVP build guide and are the owner's to keep or reverse. For itself only, the MVP departs from D24 for its text models (D86), from P1's validation (D82), from D19's board alignment (D85), from 06's bought speech and animation (D83), and from D29's quarterly rehearsal and D30's rolling summary (D88); the Tutor's design does not change. The brief that records them is TUTOR_MVP_BRIEF.md at the repository root.

The owner's decisions

D80. The Tutor is shown first by an MVP that matches the product's capabilities as closely as it can, built for speed, with running cost and latency deprioritised. It is built by a harness first and then a team of Claude Code agents. It is not M2's release, and it decides nothing about the route or which idea goes first.
Driven by: the owner's priorities, speed of building and fidelity to the product before cost and latency; and of the five ways to build it that were discussed, only a harness and an agent team are both fast and faithful (the guide). Rejected: one agent building plane by plane, the slowest faithful path; an agent-native tutor that chooses its own sources, which 06 rejects; buying the visible layer from likeness vendors that fail D24, D61 and D65; and an AI app builder, with too little control to keep the invariants. Makes harder: the most work comes before the first line of product code; a working MVP can be taken for M2's release, which is why every screen says nothing in it is validated; and whether the owner can direct the team without a hired engineer is OA-47. 11
D81. Only adults use the MVP, playing learners of 14 to 18. No child uses it, and it holds no child's data.
Driven by: the owner's decision; nothing the MVP teaches is validated, so P1 is not kept and no learner should be taught from it; and its safety route is the owner reading a queue. Rejected: inviting real learners of 14 to 18, which would put unvalidated content and an unstaffed safety route in front of children. Makes harder: nothing the MVP shows is evidence about learners; adults playing a teenager write differently from one, so the safety check meets different messages; and the MVP still holds adult testers' faces and voices, whose standing in data protection law it does not settle. The guide
D82. The MVP's content is the algebra of GCSE Mathematics, Level 2 Further Mathematics and A-level Mathematics, drafted in full by agents under the clean-room rule (D12), checked by the computer algebra system, and labelled on every screen as not validated by an examiner. A-level Further Mathematics and Additional Mathematics are left out.
Driven by: the owner's choice of the whole algebra strand for ages 14 to 18, named as GCSE Maths, GCSE Further Maths and A-level Maths and narrowed to those three when asked; algebra is where the style engine has something to do and the algebra system can adjudicate (03); and no examiner can be engaged before the MVP exists. Rejected: the two qualifications the owner left out; and a smaller bank of validated items, which needs the examiners M2 recruits. Makes harder: the content is the largest single job, and how many objectives it holds is Unknown until the decomposition; the algebra system catches only what it can check, and how many errors it misses is OA-46; and P1 is not kept. The guide
D83. The self-tutor's voice and face run on open models that the MVP hosts on rented GPUs, so no speech or animation vendor receives a likeness, and nothing is trained on one (D65).
Driven by: the owner's decision; open models that clone a voice from a short sample and animate a face to speech are published under permissive licences, Assumed (CV-24, CV-25); and conditioning at render time is how they work, so nothing is trained on a tester. Rejected: a hosted speech and animation vendor, which would need D24's zero retention before it received a face or a voice. Makes harder: the MVP runs its own models on GPUs rented from Modal, which also holds the encrypted likeness store; some open face pipelines depend on InsightFace's pretrained models, which are for non-commercial research only, Assumed (CV-25), so every model and dependency is audited first; whether a short live capture makes a recognisable likeness is OA-45; and the render time is Unknown until the first phase's spike. Taken while writing the guide

D84. The harness is built before any build prompt: a CLAUDE.md of the design's invariants, the contracts between the planes, the vault's tests written as failing tests, a frozen battery, hooks that run the tests, six builder agents and a reviewer. Nothing merges while the tests of finished phases fail.
Driven by: agents drift from a design they have only read about, and tests hold them to it; a test written before its code is cheap, and one written after tends to describe the code; and Claude Code reads a CLAUDE.md in every session, runs hooks set in the repository, and runs agents defined there, each in its own copy of the repository, Assumed (CV-30). Rejected: prompting the build straight from the design, the faster start. Makes harder: the first phase builds no product; a wrong test holds the build to a wrong design until a lead session changes it; and a hook or a permission can fail without saying so, which is why the first phase tests each one. The guide
D85. Both GCSE tiers. GCSE and A-level objectives are written from the DfE subject content. No DfE content for Level 2 Further Mathematics was found, and the qualification found comes from the board whose policy EV-21 records, so each of its objectives is written from the DfE GCSE or A-level content it extends, and the owner approves the list. Practice papers follow the MVP's own structure, not a board's.
Driven by: the DfE content is the statutory source 03 writes from; the qualification found for Further Mathematics is AQA's certificate, Assumed (CV-29), and AQA's policy bars the use of its material in training AI tools and in any work produced with AI (EV-21); the owner asked for the whole strand for ages 14 to 18, which spans both tiers; and an algebra-only paper cannot have a board's shape. Rejected: writing Further Mathematics from AQA's specification, which would put that board's material in front of the agents; and the Higher tier alone, which 03 plans for the product's launch. Makes harder: Further Mathematics rests on the agents' knowledge and the owner's approval, not on a published list, and the agents' sense of what such a course covers may echo the one course found, which is weak; and the papers show no board's paper shape. The guide
D86. The MVP's tools: Claude Opus 5.5 generates turns and classifies safety; Gemini 4 Argon checks entailment and scope, a different family as D27 requires; SymPy checks the mathematics; Chatterbox speaks and MuseTalk animates, with MediaPipe, a passive anti-spoofing model and Whisper checking liveness; and the app, its workers, the renderers and the likeness store run on Modal, with Postgres on Neon. The text models are used on their standard terms, a departure from D24 for the MVP alone, which no child uses. The choice binds the MVP only; the Tutor's model vendor stays deliberately undecided.
Driven by: for the turns, the Claude model the Primer's demonstration uses (D78), with a spend limit the owner sets, Assumed (CV-21); a current model from another family for the checks, Assumed (CV-28); GPUs billed by the second beside the app, on one account, Assumed (CV-27); and none of it on the Netlify team that hosts this site, whose credits, once spent, pause every project in the team, Assumed (CV-22). Rejected: zero-retention terms, which D24 requires of the product and which the MVP, with no child, does without; one provider for generation and checking, which D27 forbids; and the vault's Netlify team. Makes harder: two providers see testers' words without zero retention; models can be withdrawn at short notice; and four accounts must be kept within their limits. The guide
D87. Testers' data: accounts by invitation; transcripts and progress kept for the test period and deleted at its end, with everything else about testers except counts and timings; likenesses under D63's rules; and nothing about a tester's life beyond what they type.
Driven by: data that is not kept cannot leak; the parent report and the self-tutor's passcode need accounts; and the owner's adjudication under T-GND-01 needs the transcripts while the test runs. Rejected: no accounts, as the Primer's demonstration has (D79), which would lose the parent report and the passcode; and the twelve months 05 sets for the product. Makes harder: testers' words reach two providers on standard terms; and a tester's face and voice are held until they delete them or the test ends. The guide
D88. Left out: payment; real age assurance, for which a simulated check stands in; real creators, for which one fictional licensed preset shows the machinery; Route B's school features; the US course; D29's quarterly rehearsal, for which one rehearsal stands in; and D30's rolling summary, because a whole session fits in the window.
Driven by: none of them changes what a learner is taught or how it is checked, and each needs a contract, a provider or a person the MVP does not have; D29's rehearsal is quarterly because a product runs for years, and the MVP runs for one test period; and D30 exists to bound cost, which the owner put last. Rejected: a real age-assurance provider and a real creator, both slower than the MVP. Makes harder: the MVP cannot show a purchase, an age check, a creator's review or a school's safeguarding route; the fictional preset shows the machinery, not a creator's style; and the MVP's cost per turn grows with the session, so its cost is not the product's.
