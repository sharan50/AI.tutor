D1 to D14: settled in the brief, not to be re-opened

These were decided deliberately before this design was written; D1 has since been amended by the owner (below). If anything in this repository contradicts one of them, the repository is wrong and not the decision. They are recorded terse because the brief already carries their reasoning; the nineteen below are recorded at length because their consequences are what a future reader will need.

"Not to be re-opened" means not re-opened on preference. It does not mean unfalsifiable. D4, the wedge, has a pre-registered test (T-STY-02 at M3) and a pre-registered decision rule (M4 in 11) whose fourth outcome is that D4 is wrong. That is not a contradiction of the rule; it is the difference between a decision that may not be relitigated because someone has changed their mind, and one that is revised because the evidence it predicted arrived and pointed the other way.

Any settled decision may be re-opened by evidence obtained through a route written down in advance. None may be re-opened by argument alone.

Amendmentby the owner, 22 October 2026

The owner prefers launching both together and holds that the US has proven more receptive (OA-31); the UK goes first when resources are short because its academic system is the one the owner knows best. That is a re-opening on preference, which the rule above does not allow: no evidence was gathered, and no route was written down in advance. The owner, who wrote the brief, has overridden the rule for D1 alone; the other thirteen stand under it.

What it opens. Everything the UK design rests on has to be found again for the US, and none of it has been:

  • What the Tutor teaches there is not decided (decisions not taken). Until it is, 03, the board decisions D19 and D34 and the grade range apply to the UK only; whether US examination bodies allow what D12 relies on is OA-36.
  • US law is OI-21: data and online-services law, any duty to report and how it meets D26, and which entity contracts with a US parent, extending OI-7. The creator licence under US law is OI-22, with OA-9.
  • A US safeguarding route is needed: 05 signposts UK services only.
  • Whether US parents price the Tutor against a human tutor, as OA-10 assumes UK parents will, is OA-35. Testing it needs a US course and a US price page; the register holds no US tutoring rate.
  • If the two launch together, the US questions join M1 (11); if the UK goes first, they wait until a US launch is planned.
  • 10 prices the UK only. The economics instrument in econ/ opens the US nine months after the UK, which matches this decision's fallback, and later a market across the rest of the English-speaking world, which no decision provides for. A launch of the UK and the US together has not been modelled, and what "where resources allow" means is not decided either.
