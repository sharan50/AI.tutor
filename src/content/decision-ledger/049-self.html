D59 to D69: the self-tutor

All dated 23 October 2026, when the owner added the self-tutor to the Tutor: a learner may be taught by a tutor with their own face and voice. D59 is the owner's; D60 to D69 were taken while writing it into the documents and are the owner's to keep or reverse. The self-tutor proceeds ahead of counsel's answers on OI-23 to OI-27 under D59's rule, as D34 proceeds ahead of OI-3. It also needed D9 narrowed, which the owner did; that amendment is recorded with the settled decisions. The brief that records them is SELF_TUTOR_BRIEF.md at the repository root.

The owner's decision

D59. The Tutor launches with a self-tutor: a learner may choose to be taught by a tutor with their own face and voice, in any style the product offers (D66). It proceeds ahead of counsel's answers on OI-23 to OI-27, and is withdrawn in a market only if counsel advises in writing that no form of it the design allows may launch there; a conditional answer changes the design instead of stopping it.
Driven by: the owner's decision, and the owner's views that being taught by oneself may build confidence (OA-37) and that Ofcom's concern with virtual clones does not reach a private tutor whose output the company controls (OA-38). Rejected: designing it now and switching it on later by a written trigger, as D6 does for high-fidelity personas, which would have kept D9 whole and the launch free of children's face and voice data; and a still photograph at launch with voice later. Makes harder: children's face and voice data held from launch (05); a rendering path, a vendor and a cost per turn (06, 10); five counsel questions, four assumptions and four risks (R12 to R15); and D9 narrowed. The product still launches and runs without it, as it runs without creators (D11), so a categoric no removes a feature, not the launch. 01

Taken while writing the self-tutor

D60. The self-tutor is the learner's alone. No parent, school, creator or other user can see or hear it; nothing it renders can be shared, exported or downloaded; and the parent report shows the same progress for self-tutor sessions as for any other, never the likeness.
Driven by: trigger T2 in 05, which names "let students build their own tutor" as the feature most likely to be proposed on merit and the one that would put the service in the category Ofcom described; and the misuse a shareable likeness of a child invites. Rejected: letting the parent watch, which is arguably another user accessing a persona. Makes harder: a parent consents on a description, not a demonstration; and a new trigger, T9, joins the change-control list. 05
D61. A self-tutor is made only from a live capture in the product: a head-and-shoulders face scan and a voice sample read from prompted sentences, with a liveness check. No uploaded photograph, video or recording is accepted, and no full-body image is taken.
Driven by: a self-tutor of anyone but the learner would be a virtual clone of another real person, the incident in Ofcom's letter; and a full-body image of a child carries the most misuse and breach risk for no teaching purpose. Rejected: uploads, which are easier and are the route to a tutor in a classmate's or a teacher's likeness; and full-body capture, as the owner first described it. Makes harder: someone physically present can still be captured in the learner's place (OA-39), and how often the liveness check is fooled is Unknown until T-SLF-02 at M2. 06
D62. Before capture, the verified adult and the learner each consent, separately and explicitly, after being told in plain words what is kept, that no one else can see or hear it, and how to delete it. Either can withdraw at any time, which deletes the self-tutor. It is off by default.
Driven by: the learner is the person whose face and voice are used and the adult holds the account; and the Children's code's high-privacy default. Rejected: the adult's consent alone, and the learner's alone. Makes harder: whether these consents are the lawful basis, and who may give consent at 14 to 17, is OI-23, and the design does not answer it. 05
D63. The learner or the adult can delete the self-tutor at any moment. A deletion flag is read at every turn under the kill switch's sixty-second cache ceiling, so rendering stops at the next turn; the samples, anything derived from them and any rendered clip leave primary stores within seven days, and the vendor holds nothing under zero-retention terms (D24). A self-tutor is also deleted when the account closes, or after twelve months without a self-tutor session.
Driven by: the kill switch, which already makes a creator's withdrawal real within a turn (04); and data about a child should not outlive its use. Rejected: deletion on request through support, which is slower and depends on a person. Makes harder: transcripts kept for the learner hold text only, never audio or video. 06
D64. The self-tutor speaks only text that has passed the gate. Speech and animation are rendered after the verifier, from the text shown on screen, which is always shown; a blocked turn falls back as text does. Every clip carries a visible label that it is synthetic, and provenance metadata. The self-tutor never claims to be the learner, never comments on their face or voice, and offers no filter or alteration of the likeness.
Driven by: D21 and the gate, because a sentence in the learner's own voice is the last place an unchecked claim should reach; India's IT Rules 2026, which require synthetic content to be labelled, though whether they reach these clips is OI-26; and the line in 00 that the Tutor is not a companion. Rejected: rendering before the gate to save latency. Makes harder: a render step adds latency, and the budget for it is Unknown until M2; where animation cannot meet it, the self-tutor falls back to a still likeness that speaks. 06
D65. No model weights are trained or fine-tuned on a learner's face or voice. The likeness is conditioned at render time from the stored samples or embeddings derived from them, so deleting those deletes the likeness. A vendor that can clone only by fine-tuning is excluded.
Driven by: D8's reasoning, applied to learners: weights cannot be cleanly revoked, and a deletion that needs retraining is not a deletion. Rejected: per-learner fine-tuned voices and faces, which would be closer likenesses. Makes harder: fidelity is capped at what render-time conditioning achieves, and the choice of vendor narrows. 06
D66. Every archetype is available in the self-tutor, with the learner's own adjustments (D10). A named educator's preset is available in it only where that creator has opted in by a new term in the licence, and the label says whose style it is without implying that the creator appears.
Driven by: the owner's decision that style is included; and D5's licence covers a creator's name and likeness labelling a preset, not that preset taught in someone else's face and voice. Rejected: archetypes only, the first recommendation; and named presets without an opt-in. Makes harder: a new schedule in the heads of terms, and OI-27. 02
D67. At launch the self-tutor is offered only to families buying directly (Route A). Through schools (Route B) it is off until counsel has answered OI-23 for a school as controller.
Driven by: a school holding children's face and voice data adds a controller and a due-diligence question that Route B already finds hard (OA-14). Rejected: offering it to schools from launch. Makes harder: the two routes' products differ by one feature. 08
D68. In the experiment D14 sets up, the self-tutor is a second factor, not a third arm: learners are assigned to named or unnamed presets as D14 requires, and separately to being offered the self-tutor or not, so the wedge's measurement stays clean. T-SLF-01 measures the self-tutor's effect.
Driven by: a self-tutor taken up unevenly across D14's arms would confound the return-rate comparison D14 exists to make. Rejected: a third arm, which splits the sample and still confounds the comparison. Makes harder: the experiment needs a larger sample for the same power, by an amount that is Unknown until its design is fixed. 11
D69. No style dimension reaches the self-tutor's voice. The learner's own voice speaks the gated text at one neutral delivery under every preset; speaking rate, pitch and intonation are set by the voice rendering, never by the preset.
Driven by: D17 kept tone out of the weighted style set, and a preset that varied delivery would bring it back through the voice; and a grader who listened could tell presets apart by sound alone, so T-STY-02 would measure the wrong thing. Rejected: letting pace or energy carry into speech, which would make a named style feel closer in the learner's voice. Makes harder: a self-tutor sounds the same under every preset, so its style lives only in what it says. 02
