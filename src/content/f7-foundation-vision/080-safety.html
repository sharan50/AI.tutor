At school and at home, the face check discards any frame with a face, and no frame is kept (D97, D137), as for the first version (F4). Foundation watches technique, never safety; where a skill can hurt, an adult in the room is responsible for the learner (D138).

So there is no alert for danger, such as a hand near a blade, which would invite a school or a parent to rely on a camera for a child's safety (RF7). A home version that covers cooking, if the owner scopes one, needs an adult present at every session, which Foundation cannot check.

  • Who sees what. In schools, the pupil is shown nothing at any age, and the teacher acts (D93). At home, the app shows its feedback to adults, and to a learner of 13 or over who holds it; a child under 13 is shown nothing (D134).
  • A camera in a house. The face check must discard every member of the household, not only the child (RF8). Frames of a child cross to Foundation's cloud service and are discarded there (D97).
  • A learner of 13 or over is still a child under the Children's code, whose five age ranges pupils of 4 to 18 span, Assumed (CV-06). In the UK they are old enough to consent to an online service themselves, where consent is the basis, Assumed (CV-07); whether that lets them hold the app is part of Open OI-33.
  • A connected device. A search summary of the code's standard on connected toys and devices says it requires their provider to include effective tools to enable conformance to the code, Assumed (CV-43). Whether it reaches the home unit is part of OI-33.
  • Tests. Under F6's conditions, T-FND-01, T-FND-02 and T-FND-07 on the home unit (F3), and T-FND-06 on every unit in a school.
