At home: the home version

At home, a home unit connects to Foundation's cloud service, and the feedback comes through an app (D131). It is, in the owner's words, for "lessons and home practice". Which skills the home version covers is the owner's to scope; musical instruments, such as the piano and keyboard, are the owner's main examples (D133). This page designs only what every skill shares.

Both School classes (a sell-to-school based model where Foundation is hosted on-prem) AND a D2C version for lessons and home practice that would be connected to a cloud based version of Foundation and feedback is through an app.

DecisionD131, the owner's, 5 November 2026; supersedes D89 in part, for the home version

The home version brings a second customer, families, with a consumer sale and its support, Assumed (OA-66), and a camera in a family's home, whose lawful basis is Open OI-33. The first version stays a school's alone.

In the home version, the app shows its feedback to adults, and to a learner of 13 or over, who may hold the app themselves; a child under 13 is shown nothing.

DecisionD134, the owner's, 5 November 2026; supersedes D93 in part, for a learner of 13 or over at home

The home version keeps the desk unit's guarantees. The home unit is a camera on an arm or a stand, with no microphone, screen, speaker or light; an adult sets it up and aims it at the work, never at a face; its frames are read in Foundation's cloud service and discarded; the app belongs to a verified adult, who may add the child's own teacher; and what it reads is kept as text in the family's account, deleted when the adult asks or closes the account, and never used to infer a difficulty or to compare the child with others.

DecisionD137, taken while applying the owner's decisions, 5 November 2026; the owner's to keep or reverse
  • The home unit is a device of its own, not the family's phone or tablet, which has a microphone and a screen. Like the Primer's tablet (P6), it must be designed, made, certified and sold, and what it must meet before sale is part of OI-33. Unlike the desk unit's locked arm (D97), its mount can be moved after setup, and its frames reach the cloud before the face check discards any with a face (RF8). It cannot tell who is at it, so a sibling's work is read as the learner's: a limit, stated as P4 states the Primer's.
  • The app. A learner of 13 or over may hold it if their adult allows it (D137), and sees only their own feedback. For a child under 13 it is the adult's alone, and the home unit shows the child nothing, as the desk unit does (D90). Its words are templates and reviewed entries, never generated (D96), and it rewards no one: no stars, streaks, badges or confetti (D25).

Whether parents, and learners of 13 or over, accept such a camera at home is Assumed (OA-67).
