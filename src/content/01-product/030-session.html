The shape of a session

A session is between fifteen and thirty minutes and has one objective. The objective is an item-bank node, not a topic heading.

  1. Pick up. The tutor states what the student was last working on and what it believes they can and cannot yet do, in one sentence, and asks whether that is still right. The student can overrule it.
  2. One objective. Chosen by the student, or proposed by the scheduler from the gaps in their model. Never more than one.
  3. Teach, in the chosen style. This is where the style engine operates and it is the whole of the next section.
  4. Unaided check. Two or three held-out items, no hints available, no tutor commentary until the student commits an answer. This is the anti-surface-fluency mechanism and it is deliberately unpleasant.
  5. Honest close. The tutor says what the check showed, including when the check showed the student has not got it, and schedules the objective for return rather than declaring success.

The unaided check is scored on first commit, unaided, and the student cannot ask for a hint during it. The temptation is to allow hints and score leniently, because that produces better-looking session outcomes and a happier student. Verified The reported caveat on AI tutoring is precisely that students who default to the AI rather than working a problem develop surface fluency that does not hold under examination conditions. A product that measures in-session success is measuring the thing that a surface-fluency failure would flatter. Recorded in the decision ledger.
