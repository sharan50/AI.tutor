The self-tutor

A learner may choose to be taught by a tutor with their own face and voice. It teaches exactly as any other tutor here does, in any style the product offers, from the same gated turns: the likeness changes how a turn is presented, never what it says. It is a launch feature (D59), offered to families buying directly (D67), and off until the learner and the verified adult both turn it on.

The self-tutor proceeds ahead of counsel's answers on OI-23 to OI-27, as D34 proceeds ahead of OI-3. It is withdrawn in a market only if counsel advises in writing that no form of it this design allows may launch there; a conditional answer changes the design instead of stopping it. Nothing on this page answers any of those questions.

Decision D59the owner, 23 October 2026

The case for it is the owner's: being taught by oneself may build a learner's confidence (OA-37). Nothing has measured that. The nearest studies are adjacent: learners watching edited video of themselves succeeding, with effects mostly in young or autistic children Assumed (CV-13); adults who exercised more after watching a likeness of themselves do it Assumed (CV-14); and learner self-avatars in online lessons that changed motivation but not performance Assumed (CV-15). No benefit is claimed for the self-tutor until T-SLF-01 reports.

AssumptionOA-37, the owner's view

Turning it on

  1. Two consents. The verified adult and the learner each consent, separately, after a plain statement of what is kept (a face scan, a voice sample and what is derived from them), that no one else can see or hear the self-tutor, and how to delete it (D62). Whether these consents are the lawful basis, and who may give consent at 14 to 17, is OI-23; the design does not answer it.
  2. Live capture, nothing uploaded. A head-and-shoulders face scan, and a voice sample read from sentences prompted on screen, both taken live in the product with a liveness check (D61). No photograph, video or recording can be uploaded, and no full-body image is taken. This is what stops a self-tutor being made of a classmate or a teacher from their videos. It does not stop someone else sitting at the camera in the learner's place (OA-39).
  3. A sample, then a choice. The learner sees and hears the self-tutor say one fixed sentence, and can discard it there, which deletes it.

In a session

The session keeps its shape (above), and the self-tutor presents the tutor's turns. Each turn appears on screen as text first; speech and animation are rendered after the gate, from that text, so the self-tutor says nothing the learner cannot read (D64). A blocked turn falls back exactly as any blocked turn does. If animation cannot keep within the latency budget, which is Unknown until M2, the self-tutor shows a still likeness and speaks; if speech fails, the turn stays as text. Figures still render beside the likeness.

It never claims to be the learner, never comments on their face or voice, and offers no filter or alteration of the likeness (D64). Every spoken or animated turn carries a label that it is synthetic, and the line in what is always on screen names the likeness. It is a tutor, and keeps every line of what the product does not do.

Controls

  • The standard tutor, for one session. One tap, at any point, without deleting anything.
  • Delete, at any moment. The learner or the adult can delete it. Rendering stops at the next turn; the samples, anything derived from them and any clip leave primary stores within seven days, and the vendor holds nothing under zero-retention terms (D24). It is also deleted when the account closes, or after twelve months without a self-tutor session (D63).
  • Nobody else. No parent, school, creator or other user can see or hear it, and nothing it renders can be shared, exported or downloaded (D60).

Every archetype is available in it, with the learner's own adjustments; a named educator's preset only where that creator has opted in (D66, 02). Whether being offered it changes how learners do is read as a second factor in D14's experiment, not as a third arm (D68).
