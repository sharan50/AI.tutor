What the parent sees, and what they do not

The parent sees objectives attempted, objectives passed on the unaided check, time on task, and the schedule of what is coming back. The parent does not see session transcripts by default.

Decision D1614 September 2026

This is the design decision in the product most likely to be argued with, because the parent is the payer and the transcript is the thing they would most like to read. Three reasons it stands.

  • It would destroy the teaching. A student who knows every word is reported to a parent will not type "I do not understand any of this". The willingness to admit confusion is the raw material the tutor works with.
  • Best interests of the child. The ICO Children's code requires the child's best interests to be the primary consideration in design, and a surveillance-by-default reading channel is hard to defend against that standard. The mapping is in 05.
  • Transcripts are not what the parent actually wants. They want to know it is working. Objective-level progress against an unaided check answers that better than a wall of chat.

The parent retains, unconditionally: the right to see the full record on request through a subject access route, and the right to delete everything. "By default" is doing real work in this decision and the exceptions are not buried.

Safeguarding is the one place where the parent's rights are not unconditional, and it has to be said here rather than only in the safety document. If the tutor escalates a concern, a trained reviewer decides whether and whom to inform, against a written policy. The parent is not automatically told.

That is deliberate: automatic parental notification is unsafe in precisely the cases that matter most, because the parent is sometimes the risk. It is D26, the reasoning is in 05, and it is a term a parent should meet in the product's own description rather than discover during an incident.

Decision D26the exception to parental rights

Whether the default position above is defensible where the learner is under 13, given that parental responsibility and the UK GDPR Article 8 age threshold for information society services sit differently, and whether the answer changes between Route A and Route B where the school is the controller. Unblocks: the reporting design and the parent-facing terms. Cost of getting it wrong: a Children's code finding against the product's central reporting feature, and a rebuild of the parent surface.
