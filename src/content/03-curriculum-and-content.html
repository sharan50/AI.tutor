Curriculum and content

The item bank is the asset, and it is also what keeps the venture out of court, which is why the provenance rules are stricter than quality alone would require.

The clean-room rule

No exam board material enters the system. Not in the item bank, not in training, not in retrieval, not in internal benchmarking, not in a developer's local notes. This is stricter than the law may ultimately require, and it is adopted because AQA has published a policy forbidding use of its material in connection with AI tools and in any third-party work partly generated by AI, and because a clean-room position is provable whereas a fair-dealing argument is only arguable.

The item schema

Every item carries this record. An item with any required field missing cannot be served, and that rule is enforced at retrieval rather than at authoring, because authoring discipline decays and a serving check does not.

The source_basis field is the legal control. It has exactly three permitted values, none of which is a board's paper or mark scheme, so an item sourced from a board cannot be represented in the schema at all. That is deliberate: the defence should be structural rather than procedural.

Validation workflow

Five states, one direction, one exception.

  • draft: written against the published objective by an author, not yet seen by an examiner.
  • in_validation: with an examiner who has marked that specification. They check three things: that it is answerable at the stated level, that the expected response matches mark-scheme conventions, and that the language used is language that earns marks.
  • live: servable. Carries validator and date.
  • quarantined: pulled on a reported error or a specification change. Not servable, not deleted, and the reason is recorded. This is the exception to one-direction flow; quarantined items return to in_validation.
  • retired: superseded. Kept, because a session that used it must remain traceable.

The accuracy floor

Three parts, of which only the number is missing.

What is measured. The proportion of served responses on an objective whose substantive content matches the expected_response of a live item, scored by an examiner on a sample, not by the model on itself.

The behaviour below floor. On an objective that falls below floor, the tutor states that it is not confident on this topic, offers what it can support, and routes the student to their teacher. It does not degrade quietly, and it does not hedge its way through. Objectives below floor are visible on the parent dashboard.

The number. Not set. Setting it before an item bank exists would be theatre. What is settled is that it is measured on a human-scored sample rather than self-reported, that the below-floor behaviour is built before launch rather than after, and that the figure is reported internally every week.
