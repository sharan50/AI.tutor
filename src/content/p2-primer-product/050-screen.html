The screen and the tablet

The Primer is a tablet of its own, one per child, with nothing else on it. Its screen holds one question at a time, and it hears the child only while the talk key is held.

The Primer is its own tablet, as every film shows: one per child, with no browser, no other apps and no notifications.

DecisionD105, the owner's, 31 October 2026

The tablet is locked to the Primer, and the parent's screen is not on it (below). It is to be designed in parallel with the service and made only after PM4's evidence; PM4 is to run the Primer on a stand-in tablet locked to it (P6). Its lock, updates and security are P5's. What a connected tablet sold to families must meet before sale is Open OI-31 (P4), and what it costs to make is Unknown. This is a weak point: whether parents will buy a tablet for one child, rather than expect an app on one they own, is OA-54, and a tablet that costs more than families will pay is RP7.

The screen is as the films show it, and each feature below is a design choice: the 45 per cent is a design choice, not a measurement.

DecisionD109, from the films, confirmed by the owner, 31 October 2026
  • a matte off-white tablet that is "the Primer";
  • warm off-white, charcoal humanist type, and "Primer" small at the top left;
  • a wake screen, "What's your question?", with one input box;
  • a grey keyboard of grey keys with white letters across the lower 45 per cent of the screen, with a blue return key;
  • the Primer's text arriving whole, never typed out, on answer cards;
  • the child's own words, in their own spelling;
  • sources as a small grey line under a rule, "Based on: ...";
  • the read-back, "You started with... Where are you now?";
  • every exchange closing with "Leave it there, or keep going?" and two buttons;
  • no streaks, badges or reminders.

The tablet has no camera, and its microphone listens only while the child holds the talk key, with the screen showing plainly that it is listening: no wake word, and nothing listening in the background.

DecisionD116, taken while writing, confirmed by the owner, 31 October 2026

While the talk key is held, a plain sign on the screen says the Primer is listening; when it is released, the sign goes and so does the listening. The ICO's guidance on connected devices points the same way, Assumed (CV-37). Taking a child's speech at all goes ahead of counsel's answer on OI-17, under D102 (above).

Before the first question. Once, when the verified adult sets the tablet up with the child, a first-run screen tells the child, in words for their age, what the Primer is: a tutor for questions, not a place to tell things to and not a safeguarding service (05). It says what their parent can see and, where speech is switched on, that the Primer hears them only while they hold the talk key. After that, the wake screen holds only the question. The films do not show setup. Exactly what a child must be told is Open OI-14.
