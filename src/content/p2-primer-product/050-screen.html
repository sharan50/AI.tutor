The screen and the tablet

The Primer is a tablet of its own, one per child, with nothing else on it. Its screen holds one question at a time. The talk key comes first and the keyboard second, and the tablet hears the child only while the talk key is held. A selection model chooses what is spoken and what is shown, and every video says it was made by AI.

The Primer is its own tablet, as every film shows: one per child, with no browser, no other apps and no notifications.

DecisionD105, the owner's, 31 October 2026

The tablet is locked to the Primer, and the parent's screen is not on it (below). It is to be designed in parallel with the service and made only after PM4's evidence; PM4 is to run the Primer on a stand-in tablet locked to it (P6). Its lock, updates and security are P5's. What a connected tablet sold to families must meet before sale is Open OI-31 (P4), and what it costs to make is Unknown. This is a weak point: whether parents will buy a tablet for one child, rather than expect an app on one they own, is OA-54, and a tablet that costs more than families will pay is RP7.

The talk key first, the keyboard second. Speech is the main way in, so the talk key comes first: the child holds it, says the question and lets go. The keyboard comes second and is always there, for a child who would rather type or has been misheard (D123). The rough cut on the front page shows a boy typing; the owner expects most questions to be spoken (above).

The screen is as the films show it, and each feature below is a design choice: the 45 per cent is a design choice, not a measurement.

DecisionD109, from the films, confirmed by the owner, 31 October 2026
  • a matte off-white tablet that is "the Primer";
  • warm off-white, charcoal humanist type, and "Primer" small at the top left;
  • a wake screen, "What's your question?", with one input box;
  • a grey keyboard of grey keys with white letters across the lower 45 per cent of the screen, with a blue return key;
  • the Primer's text never typed out, on answer cards; since 4 November 2026 a card shows the sentences chosen to be shown rather than arriving whole, and the whole text is a tap away (D124);
  • the child's own words, in their own spelling;
  • sources as a small grey line under a rule, "Based on: ...";
  • the read-back, "You started with... Where are you now?";
  • every exchange closing with "Leave it there, or keep going?" and two buttons;
  • no streaks, badges or reminders.

The details of the content would actually be generated prior to the videos so they can just be leveraged as opposed to captions. We would need to train a model on how to select what is spoken and what is shown as text on the Primer tablet.

DecisionD124, the owner's, 4 November 2026

What is spoken and what is shown. The Primer writes its content first; the voice, the video and the words on the screen are all drawn from it, so no captions are needed. A selection model, trained for the purpose, chooses for each checked sentence whether it is spoken, shown or both; it writes nothing, and the whole text is a tap away. Figures and the "Based on:" line are always shown. A safety reply is shown and never spoken (D115, D124). The films show the text whole; this departs from them because a child who is listening needs the screen to hold what a voice cannot keep, the figures, the names and the sources, and a screen of every spoken word would be read instead of heard. T-PRM-11 tests that everything spoken or shown is checked content and that the selection model adds nothing (above). Whether children understand an answer split between what is spoken and what is shown as well as one shown whole is OA-63, measured at PM4.

Videos say they are made by AI. Every explainer video is labelled on screen as made by AI and carries provenance metadata; its voice-over and on-screen words come from its checked script, and it shows no real person and no child (D126; P4). T-PRM-15 checks every video for its label and for what its frames show (above).

Reporting a video. Beside every generated video the tablet shows a one-tap report, wordless, shown and never spoken, which records only the video's identifier and the time. A parent reports from the parent's screen against a conversation, and reviewers trace it through the turn log. A reported video leaves every cache until a reviewer has looked at it (D128; P4).

The tablet has no camera, and its microphone listens only while the child holds the talk key, with the screen showing plainly that it is listening: no wake word, and nothing listening in the background.

DecisionD116, taken while writing, confirmed by the owner, 31 October 2026

While the talk key is held, a plain sign on the screen says the Primer is listening; when it is released, the sign goes and so does the listening. The ICO's guidance on connected devices points the same way, Assumed (CV-37). Counsel has cleared a child speaking to the Primer, in the UK and the US, as the owner reported on 4 November 2026 (D118, above).

Before the first question. Once, when the verified adult sets the tablet up with the child, the adult is asked to agree to speech, separately and revocably (D129; P4), and a first-run screen tells the child, in words for their age, what the Primer is: a tutor for questions, not a place to tell things to and not a safeguarding service (05). It says what their parent can see and, where speech is on, that the Primer hears them only while they hold the talk key. After that, the wake screen holds only the question. The films do not show setup. Exactly what a child must be told is Open OI-14.
