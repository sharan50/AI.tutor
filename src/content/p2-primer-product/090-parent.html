What the parent sees

A parent sees summaries: the topics, the questions, and where their child ended up on each, as the read-back the child heard. They hold the switches. No full conversation is kept to show them.

The Primer remembers a child's past questions and views from one session to the next. Nothing the child says is kept word for word: what is kept is a summary of what they said, never an exact record of the conversation. No video is ever recorded.

DecisionD104, the owner's, 31 October 2026

The parent's screen, as the sleep film shows it, holds four things: the topics, the questions as the catalogue questions their entries answer, the child's position on each as the read-back they heard and could correct (D117), and the module switches. The film's narration keeps "Parents choose which topics are switched on". Because the child's tablet has nothing else on it (D105), the parent's screen runs on the parent's own phone or computer, signed in to the family's account. The age band and the switches are settings the verified adult makes, read at every session; they are not memory of the child (D117). The same summaries are what the Primer remembers between sessions: ideas, not lives (P4).

Superseded in part for the Primer on 31 October 2026 by D104 and D107: summaries only, no full conversation to show, and a flagged exchange kept only until its review closes.

A parent sees an activity record: the modules used, the questions asked and the views the child chose to record. A question the Primer declined, or one in a switched-off module, appears by its category, not its words. For a child under 13 the full conversation is also available on request. The whole of an exchange flagged for safeguarding, including the Primer's reply, is withheld from the record and from any transcript and routed to a trained reviewer, because the parent is sometimes the risk.

DecisionD48, 22 October 2026

What survives of D48: a question the Primer declined or stopped, or one in a switched-off module, appears by its category, never its words, and a flagged exchange never appears (D107).

The cost. A child no longer chooses which positions their parent sees: every read-back is kept and shown. So the child is told before they start, in words for their age, what their parent can see (above), and each read-back can be corrected before it is kept. A summary can also misstate a child; that it keeps a child's position faithfully enough for the child to accept it and a parent to understand it is OA-56, tested at PM4.

The parent also holds the switches, within the modules written for their child's band, "Choices ahead" among them, on unless they turn it off (D106), and a channel for ideas and requests, which go to review and are never shown to anyone else (D46). This differs from the Tutor's default, where a teenager's transcripts stay private (D16); the reasons, the cost and the counsel questions it raises, OI-8 and OI-14, are in P4.
