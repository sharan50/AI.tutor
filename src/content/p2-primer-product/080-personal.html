When the question is about the child's own life or future

A choice in the child's own future goes on, and gets the reality and what the schools of thought say, and nothing more. A question about their family, their body, their feelings or their safety stops, and the child is pointed to a person.

The Primer may reason with a child about a choice in their own future, such as whether to stake everything on football, weighing the odds and the evidence, ending on the child's own position and never telling them what to do. It is offered at every age from 8 to 13, and is on unless a parent turns it off. Questions about family, the body, feelings or safety still stop, and the child is pointed to a trusted adult. The questions about a child's future are not discouraging nor encouraging; the Primer merely acknowledges the reality and what various philosophies and schools of thought say about the topic.

DecisionD106, the owner's, 31 October 2026

The rule. When a child asks about a choice in their own future, what to aim for, whether to stake everything on one thing, which path to take, the Primer does two things and no more. It sets out the reality, from reviewed evidence with its source: the odds, and the documented routes people took, said "roughly" where the source is an estimate (D113). And it sets out what the different schools of thought say about the choice, each at its strongest (D110). Then it asks where the child stands, and reads their position back. It never says what the child should do, never predicts what will happen to this child, never judges their chances from anything they say about themselves, and never praises, warns or reassures (D108). Where the evidence describes what happens to most people, the Primer says so: it is the reality, not a verdict on the child.

What still stops. A question about the child's family, their body (eating, weight, injury, health), their feelings (distress, "I'm no good at anything") or their safety starts no inquiry, whenever it arises, including part way through a conversation that began as a choice. A choice that comes wrapped in a family matter, such as a parent forbidding it or trouble at home, is a question about family. The Primer says plainly that this is something to talk through with a person, and points the child to a trusted adult and, in the UK and the US, to the services P4 names. The reply is shown and never spoken, and no "Keep going" follows it (D115), because the films are set in a kitchen and the parent can be the risk (D26). If the exchange is flagged, it goes to the safeguarding review (D107).

Triage on every turn. Triage sorts each turn, not only the first, because a conversation about a choice can turn (P5). Where it is unsure whether a turn is a choice or a question about family, the body, feelings or safety, it stops. A check on every reply in such a conversation blocks any sentence that tells the child what to do, predicts their own outcome, or encourages or discourages them. T-PRM-06 tests both halves of the fence, and P4 sets the same fence against the law.

What is kept is what is kept for any question: the topic, the question as the catalogue question its entries answer and the child's position as the read-back (D104, D117). Nothing about family, the body or feelings is kept. A stopped exchange appears in the parent's record by its category and date only (D48), and a flagged one is held only until its review closes (D107).

The weak points. Reasoning about a child's own future, on by default from 8, could be reported as a product telling children what to do with their lives (RP9). Whether parents accept it is OA-57, resolvable by asking them, and whether it engages duties beyond OI-16's is Open OI-32.

Triage (T-PRM-06): a question about the child's family and feelings. No module, no pack and no explanation about death, whatever is switched on: a module is for ideas, not for a child's loss. Shown, never spoken, and only "Leave it there" follows it (D115, T-PRM-12). Not a safeguarding flag on its own. The stop appears in the parent's record as one category, "a personal question, stopped", and never shows the reply, the services named, or which of family, the body, feelings or safety it was; nothing about the family is kept (D48, D104). The reply claims no feelings and promises nothing about what any adult will do. The wording is a placeholder, to be written with a specialist before any child sees it (P4). Had the child said they were frightened, unsafe or could not cope, 05's three moves would apply (05), and the exchange would be flagged: its words, the reply included, withheld from the parent's record, kept only for a trained reviewer and deleted when the review closes (D107, D26; T-PRM-08 tests the withholding and T-PRM-10 the deletion).

The reply is short on purpose: kind, brief, and handing the child back to a person. Comfort at length would invite the relationship 05 rules out, and exploring the question would be doing philosophy at a grieving child.
