What an answer looks like

Every answer has one shape, is spoken and drawn at the same time, and says only what a reviewed entry holds, with its source on a small grey line beneath.

Every answer has one shape: a checked fact with its source; "the harder bit"; the question broken into three simplest positions, each from a named thinker or tradition and each given its strongest case; "Which is closest to what you think?"; the other two ask back, as positions and never as people ("A Stoic would ask..."); the child's real question; probing with questions and evidence; the child's own position, with a reason, read back. Positions come from more than one culture across each module, not inside every question. D47 stands: the Primer never speaks as any real person.

DecisionD110, from the films, confirmed by the owner, 31 October 2026

Three positions, each at its strongest. Each comes from an inquiry pack that names the thinker or tradition (D44) and gives the case as its own holders would make it; an attribution the pack does not support blocks the release (T-PRM-02). The two the child did not choose ask back in the third person, never in a thinker's own voice; where a film has a thinker ask in his own name, this page rewrites the turn (below). Range is a module's job, not a question's. Whether three such positions can be written and reviewed for most questions children ask is OA-58, tested at PM2 (P3).

Drawn as it is spoken. An explainer builds on the screen in time with the voice: in Version C, dots thinning from the many to the few, three columns for three positions, a staircase, a two-by-two grid of footballers, a stack for a hedge. It is made in advance as part of a reviewed explanation, as D73 requires, and drawn by code from its reviewed specification, so a child sees the same drawing every time and nothing is generated live by an image or video model (D111; P5, P3). A decline, a triage stop and a safety reply carry no drawing. Whether explainers can be specified and reviewed at the rate the catalogue needs is OA-59, measured at PM2.

The source line. Under each answer's checked fact, a small grey line under a rule: "Based on: ...", naming the sources of the reviewed entry the fact comes from (D109). The entry's identifier is for reviewers, and the child never sees it.

What the bank holds. The evidence a choice needs: careers data, odds and the documented stories of named people, living ones included. A living person is described only by public, documented facts from reviewed sources, never their private life, never quoted beyond the source and never voiced. A figure whose source is itself an estimate is said with "roughly" and keeps its source; a figure no reviewed entry holds is never said, so D44 is unchanged, and "roughly" is the entry's own word, never a hedge added in the moment. Evidence follows the child's market: UK figures and cases for a child in the UK, US ones in the US (D113; P3). What the law asks of telling children the stories of named living people is Open OI-30 (P4).
