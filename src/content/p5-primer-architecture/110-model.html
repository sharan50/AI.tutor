Models: taught on, not trained on

The Primer buys its models on the Tutor's terms, and buys three more: a recogniser for children's speech, a voice and a video model. It trains one of its own, the selection model, which chooses what is spoken and what is shown and writes nothing.

The generator and the verifier are bought as 06 buys them (06): zero retention and no training on our data as a procurement gate (D24); versions pinned, with a vendor update passing the battery before it reaches a child (D22); and the verifier on a different model from the generator (D27). The recogniser, the voice and the video model pass the same gate on their own material: zero retention of audio, text, scripts and video, and no training on any of them. All three are pinned: the recogniser because an update can change what triage hears, the voice because an update can change what a child hears, and the video model because an update can change what a child sees, so an update passes T-PRM-15's battery first. They are chosen at PM4: the recogniser on its accuracy with children's speech in every band, the voice on its time to the first spoken sentence, and the video model on its time to the first video, its cost and its record against the image check. No vendor is named, for the reason 06 gives, and selection runs at PM4 on the Primer's own battery.

The selection model is trained for its purpose and chosen at PM4 (D124). It is trained to choose, never to write: given a checked sentence, it returns spoken, shown or both, and no words, so nothing in its weights can reach a child as content, and pulling a module or a source needs no retraining (T-PRM-11). The selection model learns from the facilitators' choices in PM3's sessions, what each chose to say and to show, which PM3's research protocol and parental consent cover; no model is trained on anything a child said, or on a child's voice. How well it chooses is Unknown until PM4, and whether children follow an answer split its way is OA-63.

The Primer is taught on its modules, not trained on them. Modules are retrieved when a turn needs them; no model is fine-tuned on them. Rejected: fine-tuning on philosophical and critical-thinking material, which would make a module impossible to withdraw.

DecisionD45, 22 October 2026

Retrieval is how the Primer is taught. The reviewed entries and the sources on the reviewed allow-list are read when a turn needs them, by the composer, before generation (above); no model is fine-tuned on either. The cost is the one D8 accepts for the Tutor: depth and style are capped at what retrieval and prompting achieve. The benefit is that every fact the Primer states comes from a reviewed entry or a source on the reviewed allow-list, where a reviewer can read it and an operator can pull it. The vendor gate forbids training on anything a child said or on a child's voice, and the company does no such training either.
