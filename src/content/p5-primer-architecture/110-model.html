Models: taught on, not trained on

The Primer buys its models on the Tutor's terms, and buys two more: a recogniser for children's speech and a voice.

The generator and the verifier are bought as 06 buys them (06): zero retention and no training on our data as a procurement gate (D24); versions pinned, with a vendor update passing the battery before it reaches a child (D22); and the verifier on a different model from the generator (D27). The recogniser and the voice, needed because children speak from launch, ahead of OI-17 (D102), pass the same gate on their own material: zero retention of audio and of text, and no training on either. Both are pinned, the recogniser because an update can change what triage hears, the voice because an update can change what a child hears. They are chosen at PM4, the recogniser on its accuracy with children's speech in every band, the voice on its time to the first spoken sentence. No vendor is named, for the reason 06 gives, and selection runs at PM4 on the Primer's own battery.

The Primer is taught on its modules, not trained on them. Modules are retrieved when a turn needs them; no model is fine-tuned on them. Rejected: fine-tuning on philosophical and critical-thinking material, which would make a module impossible to withdraw.

DecisionD45, 22 October 2026

The cost is the one D8 accepts for the Tutor: depth and style are capped at what retrieval and prompting achieve. The benefit is that everything the Primer can say is in the catalogue, where a reviewer can read it and an operator can pull it. No model is trained on children's conversations or on their voices either: the vendor gate forbids it, and the company does not do it.
