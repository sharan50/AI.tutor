Explainer videos, generated live

The Primer speaks a short answer at once, and while it speaks a video model generates an explainer for each concept the answer needs, live, for any question outside what D127 holds to reviewed content. Each video is made from a script the checks have passed, and plays when its own checks pass. Nothing reaches a child that has not passed the automatic checks, but most of it is never seen by a person first (D122). This supersedes D111's drawn explainers for the product.

The Primer will fire an 'instant' response addressing the question at a high level whilst generating the explainer videos which will be explaining specific concepts and that continues as we nudge the child to think independently and come up with their own views whilst understanding the facts and figures as well as both sides of the argument.

Decision D122the owner's, 4 November 2026; supersedes D111 for the product

The owner chose that the videos are generated live, while the child waits, for any question, with automatic checks only. "Both sides of the argument" is read as D110's three positions, each at its strongest (above). The films show an explainer building in time with the voice from the first sentence. The product departs from them there: the spoken answer comes first, and each video plays once it is made and checked.

An explainer is a checked script first. While the answer is spoken, the generator writes a script for each explainer, each on one specific concept, with the band's wording card. Each script is written only from the concept, named in general terms, and the turn's retrieved sources (D125), never from the turn's text. The script passes the same checks as any turn, a to h (below), and a sentence no source supports is cut. Before a video is generated or cached, the script's checks add a deterministic block on any name, place or uncommon phrase from the child's turns, and the read-back's block on any detail of family, friends, body or feelings (T-PRM-10).

The video model draws no words. The video model is given a visual brief with no words and no figures. Every word and figure on a video is laid over it on the tablet from the checked script, as the selection model marks it (D124), compared deterministically as on any turn; the video model's own audio is never played, and the voice-over is the Primer's own voice reading the script (T-PRM-11). The image check blocks any frame in which the model has drawn text or numerals (T-PRM-15).

A video plays only when its own checks pass. Every generated video is labelled on screen as made by AI and carries provenance metadata. It shows no real person and no child. Its frames pass an automatic image check before it plays (T-PRM-15), and a video that fails is not shown, while the spoken answer stands (D126; P4). No turn waits for a video, so a failed or late video costs the video, never the answer, as a failed clip costs the self-tutor's learner the clip and never the turn (06). A stop, a safety reply, a decline or the close cancels every script and video still pending in the conversation, and the service names none of them to the tablet afterwards (T-PRM-12).

On a choice in the child's own future, diagrams only. In a conversation triage sorts as a choice in the child's own future, a generated video, cached or new, uses the diagram style only: charts and shapes, no people, scenes or faces, as Version C's drawings show. T-PRM-06 (b)'s raters score those videos too (below).

People look afterwards. People review a sample of generated scripts and videos after the fact, and every one a child or parent reports (D128; P3). That is assurance, not a gate before a child sees anything. Cached videos are reviewed first, since one shown to many children multiplies any error. A flag, raised by triage or by the review at the close, pulls every script and video generated in the flagged exchange from the cache and sends them to the sample review. A harmful video, or the source, prompt pattern or style behind it, is pulled in one write (below).

Some content is never generated live. Facts and stories about named living people, and every sensitive module, come only from reviewed entries and packs, never from live generation (D127). A turn that D127 holds to reviewed content shows no generated video, cached or new.

A video may be cached by concept and reused. The cache is keyed by module and sensitivity as well as concept, band and market, and the child's switches and D127 are applied before the cache is read. Each cached video records which checks its script passed, the contested question (d) and no direction (g) included. It is served into a contested or own-future conversation only if its script passed them there, or after d and g are run again on the script before it plays. It serves only children of the range it was checked for. The kill switch reaches every cached copy (below). Because scripts and videos hold none of the child's words, a cached video tells another child nothing of the child who first asked.

What this costs, and how fast it is, are not known. The cost and speed of video generation are Unknown until PM4 (below). That videos can be made fast enough for a conversation in real time, at a cost families will pay for, and that the automatic checks catch what is wrong or unsuitable for children of 8 to 13, is OA-62, measured at PM4 with T-PRM-14 and T-PRM-15. RP10 is the risk that a live answer or video tells or shows a child something false or unsuitable. The automatic checks and the sample review, the rules for every generated video, grounding in reviewed sources and the kill switch all reduce it, and the weakness is plain: no person sees it first.

D111, superseded

Superseded for the product on 4 November 2026 by D122 and D126: explainers are videos generated live from a checked script, and nothing is drawn by code from a reviewed specification. D122 also supersedes the product half of D73, that a video is never generated live for a child. D111 is kept below as the owner confirmed it on 31 October 2026. The demonstration keeps its own rules: its clips are generated live and unchecked (D74, D76; P2).

Each answer is drawn as it is spoken: an explainer that builds on the screen in time with the voice. It is made in advance as part of a reviewed explanation, as D73 requires, and drawn by code from its reviewed specification, so that a child sees the same drawing every time and nothing is generated live by an image or video model.
