Primer: architecture

The Primer runs on the Tutor's pipeline, extended at both ends and onto a tablet of its own. Speech is the main way in: a child holds the talk key and speaks, or types, and speech becomes text at once while the audio is thrown away. Triage sorts every turn, and a choice in the child's own future goes on under a fence. The composer follows the answer's one shape and retrieves each turn's sources from a reviewed allow-list, or a reviewed entry where one exists, and eight blocking checks run before anything is spoken or shown, among them one on manner, one on suitability for the child's age and one against telling a child what to do with their future. The Primer speaks a short answer at once; while it speaks, a video model generates an explainer for each concept from a checked script, and each video plays when its own checks pass. A selection model chooses what is spoken and what is shown, and a conversation closes on a read-back the child can correct, which is what is kept. Nothing reaches a child that has not passed the automatic checks, but most of it is never seen by a person first. No model is trained on anything a child said or holds a module's content, so a module, a source, a named person or generation itself can be pulled in one operation. Cost per session, the speed of live video, and whether the pace the films show can be kept are unknown until a built Primer is measured at PM4.
