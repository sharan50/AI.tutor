Summaries, not records

What the Primer keeps of a conversation is the read-back the child heard at its close, in plain words, with the topic and the catalogue question its entries answer (D104, D117). Nothing the child says is kept word for word, no audio or video is kept at all, and the words of a flagged exchange are held only until its review closes (D107).

The summary that is kept is the read-back the child hears at the close, in plain words, which the child can correct before it is kept. The age band and the module switches are settings the verified adult makes, read at every session; they are not memory of the child.

The read-back is checked against what the child said. The generator writes it as a summary of the conversation, and it passes the verifier like any turn, with claim support aimed at the child's own words rather than at an entry: every statement of what the child thinks must be entailed by what they said, and none may be moved towards a position. The read-back is the Primer's sentence and may repeat a short phrase of the child's where it states their position, never more. A deterministic comparison also blocks a read-back that repeats any run of the child's words longer than a short phrase, with or without its pronouns changed. How long a short phrase may be is a design choice, its length Unknown, set before PM4. The read-back keeps ideas, not lives (D104): a reason the child gave about their family, friends, body or feelings is kept as "a personal reason", never in its detail. The verifier's read-back check also blocks any detail of the child's family, friends, body or feelings ("a personal reason" passes). The child hears it and sees it, and can correct it by speaking or typing; a corrected read-back is rewritten and read back again, within the time cap, until the child has nothing to correct, and that version is kept. A conversation that ends without a read-back keeps its topic and question and no position. A blocked read-back has its own fallback: a fixed, reviewed line asks the child to say once more where they stand, and the read-back is written again. If that one is blocked too, the close comes without a read-back: the topic and the question are kept, with no position. Each blocked read-back goes to adjudication with the child's words masked and any detail of their family, friends, body or feelings removed, whoever's words carry it. The question itself is kept as the catalogue question its entries answer, which is reviewed text and holds none of the child's words; a stopped or declined question is kept by its category and date only.

At the close, the words go. The review runs (D107), the record is written, and the session store's text is deleted: the child's words, the Primer's turns and anything recognised from speech. The review at the close also runs when the time cap closes a conversation the child walked away from. The session store's words are deleted only after the review at the close has run; if that review cannot run, the conversation is held for the trained reviewer as a flagged exchange, and once the reviewer clears it, the record is written as at any close and its words are deleted. None of the session store, the flagged hold, the adjudication queue and the test recordings is ever written to backups, so no backup keeps a conversation's words after its close, a flagged exchange's blocked turns after its review, or anything cut from a recording. A flag raised by triage writes the flag and the exchange's words to the hold at once, not at the close. The hold is kept durable without backups, by replication, which is not a backup, and every copy is deleted when the review closes. No turn record ever held audio or video, so 06's rule that no Turn field holds them holds for the Primer too (06). T-PRM-10 audits storage, backups included, and the network for any turn of the child's, whole or in part, left after the close outside the read-back they accepted and the flagged hold, with each vendor's confirmation under D24; checks the kept summaries for any detail of the child's family, friends, body or feelings; and, in the release battery, compares each read-back with the child's turns for any run of their words longer than a short phrase. The release batteries run in testing, where transcripts exist because no child is talking to the Primer; the spoken batteries replay recordings made for testing (above). Live traffic keeps verdicts and identifiers, never words.

A flagged exchange outlasts a deletion request until its review closes. Neither a deletion request nor an account closure reaches it, or its escalation record, before then; when the review closes, the exchange is deleted. Otherwise the only record of a disclosure could go before a trained reviewer has seen it. Whether that is lawful goes to counsel with Open OI-13 and OI-14, and this page does not decide it.

The Primer remembers through the summaries alone. At a later session the composer may give the generator the child's earlier read-backs on the questions this one matched, marked as what the child concluded before; it never reads the categories of stopped or declined questions. A sentence about a child's earlier position must cite that summary and be entailed by it, and a summary is never evidence for anything else. Nothing else from an earlier conversation reaches the generator: the per-turn log, which the composer may read, though never for triage's outcomes, holds identifiers, verdicts and timings, and none of the child's words. The parent's screen reads only the summaries, the topics, the switches and the categories of stopped and declined questions (P2): never per-turn verdicts, and never anything of a flagged exchange (T-PRM-08). No full conversation exists to request, which is how D104 supersedes D48's on-request transcript for a child under 13.

A summary can misstate a child, and a child may accept a read-back that is not quite what they meant, to get on. The verifier's check catches a read-back that says more than the child did, not one that leaves out what mattered to them. Whether children accept read-backs and parents understand them is tested at PM4, and no release test measures faithfulness beyond the verifier's check.
