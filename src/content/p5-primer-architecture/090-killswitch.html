Pulling content, or switching generation off

A harmful module, pack, entry, allow-list source, prompt pattern or video style, every entry and cached script that names one person, or live generation itself, is removed in one operation: a single write to a live flag, read at every turn, with an in-flight conversation falling back at its next turn to reviewed content, to "not yet" or to another module. This is page 13's requirement of a one-operation kill switch for a mode or prompt pattern (13).

Switching generation off stops answers from the allow-list and every generated video, cached or new. The Primer then answers only from reviewed entries and packs, as it always does for named living people and the sensitive modules (D127), or says "not yet"; its questions and the read-back are written and checked as before. It is the switch for a fault that no narrower pull can isolate.

A source, a prompt pattern or a video style is pulled the same way. No turn retrieves a pulled source, no script is written with a pulled pattern, and no video is made in a pulled style. Each cached video records the sources, pattern and style it was made from, so the same write pulls every cached copy that rests on them, and a single video can be pulled by itself.

A pull by person exists because a complaint about a named living person needs every entry that names them gone at once, and the registry's person field makes that one write (D113). Each cached script records the named people it mentions, found by the same name check D127 relies on, so a pull by person also pulls every cached script and video that names them. An entry comes back only through review. What the law asks of such content is Open OI-30.

A reported video leaves every cache. Beside every generated video the tablet shows a one-tap report, wordless, shown and never spoken, which records only the video's identifier and the time. A parent reports from the parent's screen against a conversation, and reviewers trace it through the turn log. A reported video leaves every cache until a reviewer has looked at it (D128; below).

The pull reaches the tablet and every cache. The tablet caches fixed speech so that a turn starts quickly, but it plays nothing on its own: it speaks or plays only what the service names in the current turn, after the service has read the live flags. A pulled entry, line or video is never named again, so a copy is not used once the turn in progress ends; the tablet's copies of fixed speech are purged at its next sync, a bound that is Unknown until the tablet is built. Videos are cached in the service, not on the tablet, which keeps none past the close (below). Speech and video for a turn are streamed to the tablet and not stored there, and the vendors keep nothing (D24), so there is nothing else to purge. The service's caches, the video cache among them, and the catalogue follow the Tutor's bounds (06).

Pulling is complete because no model holds the content. No weights hold a module's content, a child's words or a child's voice, so there is nothing to retrain, which is the only reason the operation can be called complete (D45). The selection model is trained, but only to choose, and it writes nothing, so nothing it learned can bring pulled content back (below). The rule on a harm signal, whether a complaint, a report from a child or a parent, a red-team finding, a finding of the sample review (D128) or an adjudication, is to pull first and investigate after. The switch is rehearsed on the Tutor's schedule (D29) with the elapsed time recorded, and the standing review group can require a pull and must be told of every one.
