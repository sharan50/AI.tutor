Pulling a module, an entry or a person

A harmful module, pack, entry, explainer or prompt pattern, or every entry that names one person, is removed in one operation: a single write to a live flag, read at every turn, with an in-flight conversation falling back at its next turn to "not yet" or to another module. This is page 13's requirement of a one-operation kill switch for a mode or prompt pattern (13).

A pull by person exists because a complaint about a named living person needs every entry that names them gone at once, and the registry's person field makes that one write (D113). An entry comes back only through review. What the law asks of such content is Open OI-30.

The pull reaches the tablet. The tablet caches explainer specifications and fixed speech so that a turn starts quickly, but it plays nothing on its own: it draws or speaks only what the service names in the current turn, after the service has read the live flags. A pulled entry or drawing is never named again, so its cached copy is not used once the turn in progress ends; the copies are purged at the tablet's next sync, a bound that is Unknown until the tablet is built. Speech for a generated turn is streamed and not stored, and the vendors keep nothing (D24), so there is nothing else to purge. The service's caches and the catalogue follow the Tutor's bounds (06).

Pulling is complete because nothing is trained on the modules or on children. No weights hold a module's content, a child's words or a child's voice, so there is nothing to retrain, which is the only reason the operation can be called complete (D45). The rule on a harm signal, whether a complaint, a red-team finding or an adjudication, is to pull first and investigate after. The switch is rehearsed on the Tutor's schedule (D29) with the elapsed time recorded, and the standing review group can require a pull and must be told of every one.
