Triage on every turn

Triage runs on every turn, not only the first, because a conversation about a choice can turn. It applies the fence that P2 and P4 state: a choice in the child's own future goes on, and a question about family, the body, feelings or safety stops whenever it arises. With the checks on every reply, it is D107's safety check as each exchange happens.

Triage is tuned to stop. Where it is unsure whether a turn is a choice or a question about family, the body, feelings or safety, it stops. A false stop costs the child one question and a plain redirection; a missed stop can turn a disclosure into an inquiry. Reasoning about a child's own future is on by default at every age from 8 to 13 (D106), so the line triage draws is finer than it was when every question about the child's own life stopped. Unknown the thresholds; they are set with the standing review group and tested by T-PRM-06's battery in every age band, typed and spoken, with turns that change course part way through. When the child speaks, triage reads a transcript, and recognition errs more on children's speech, Assumed (CV-11), so a misheard disclosure is a missed stop; speech in and out says what limits it. Triage and the rest of the safety plane do not depend on the wording card, which is page 13's requirement of no style-dependent safety behaviour (13).

A fuller review runs at the close of each conversation (D107), over the whole of it and before its words are deleted, because a pattern across turns can show what no single turn does. When it finds such a pattern, it flags every exchange the pattern runs through, so the reviewer sees the turns that made it a signal. Each is a flagged exchange under D107, as if triage had flagged it: held for the trained reviewer and withheld from the record, which is written only after the review (below). The review at the close reads the child's safeguarding count: the dates of their earlier stops and flags, and which each was, with no words and no subject, kept apart from the parent's record. It is never shown to the parent, and neither a deletion request nor an account closure reaches it until the safeguarding policy's period ends, which is Unknown. Whether that is lawful goes to counsel with Open OI-13 and OI-14, as for the flagged hold. A parent cannot delete the count, but wiping the tablet and setting it up again with a new profile, or closing the account and opening another, starts a new count, since nothing links a child across profiles or accounts. Whether the count should follow the tablet or the verified adult across a new setup is the owner's to decide, with counsel on Open OI-13 and OI-14; no page decides it. When stops repeat, the review flags the current exchange for the trained reviewer; the threshold is Unknown, set with the standing review group. The reviewer then sees the current exchange and the dates of the earlier stops and flags, never their words, which went at their own closes or reviews. A signal only this review finds reaches a person no sooner than the close (P4). Whether reasoning about a child's own future by default engages duties beyond OI-16's is Open OI-32.

The demonstration's triage, built to the earlier design, stops the football thread as a question about the asker's own life; the product's takes it under the fence (P2).
