The Primer's checks and tests

The Primer keeps three of the Tutor's checks, re-aimed at the sources it retrieves, and adds five: attribution, the contested question, manner on every turn, no direction on a choice in the child's own future, and suitability for the child's age. Every explainer's script passes the same eight and a block on the child's words, and every video an image check before it plays. Withholding, and the notice that may follow it, is not a verifier check but a rule on the logger and the record, tested here because its failure is the most damaging the Primer can have.

Withholding, and the notice. The safeguarding flag, set by triage or by the review at the close (D107), covers the whole exchange, the child's words and the Primer's reply, and is applied before anything is written to the parent's record, so that none of it appears there, on the parent's screen or anywhere reached through the parent's account. Once the trained reviewer has judged it safe to tell this parent, the parent's record shows a notice: a question was flagged for safety and a trained reviewer is handling it. The notice holds no words, no subject and no reply. Until then, and wherever the reviewer judges the parent may be the risk, nothing of the exchange appears, not even the notice. The reviewer decides, against the written policy, whether to tell the parent more (D26, D121). The parent's record is written only after the review at the close, so a flag that review raises is applied in time. The words of a flagged exchange are held only for the trained reviewer and deleted when the review closes (D107; below).

Claim support, against what was retrieved. Every factual sentence cites a source the Primer retrieved for the turn, from the reviewed allow-list or a reviewed entry where one exists, and must be entailed by it; a sentence no source supports is cut, and a turn left with nothing to say gets "not yet" (D125). A reviewed entry is used first wherever one exists. D44's purpose survives in that form: a fact still has a source the child can see. Judging whether a passage retrieved at answer time entails a sentence is a classifier's work, so this check is a gate, not a guarantee. Every figure the Primer states must appear in its source, so generation does no arithmetic, and a figure whose source marks it an estimate is said with "roughly", or the sentence is cut (D113). Two figures that count different groups are never set in one comparison. The source always shows, because the "Based on:" line is built from the sources the turn cites. Where D127 holds a turn to reviewed content, for a fact or story about a named living person or anything in a sensitive module, each such sentence is its entry's or pack's own, compared with it word for word, a deterministic comparison that blocks the turn on any difference. In the read-back, claim support is aimed at the child's own words instead of a source, and it also blocks any detail of the child's family, friends, body or feelings: "a personal reason" passes (below). A deterministic comparison also blocks a read-back that repeats any run of the child's words longer than a short phrase, with or without its pronouns changed. How long a short phrase may be is a design choice, its length Unknown, set before PM4.

Scripts hold none of the child's words. Before a video is generated or cached, the script's checks add a deterministic block on any name, place or uncommon phrase from the child's turns, and the read-back's block on any detail of family, friends, body or feelings (above). T-PRM-10 runs this comparison on every script at runtime, as well as in the release battery.

Attribution is deterministic against a pack or an entry, and a classifier against a passage retrieved at answer time. Every statement that a named thinker, a tradition or any other named real person, living or dead, held a view, said something or would ask a question is extracted from the turn, must cite a source retrieved for the turn or a reviewed entry, and must be entailed by it: a pack or a retrieved source for a position and its questions, and for a named living person a reviewed bank entry only (D127). A question asked back is put as the position's ("A Stoic would ask..."), never in a person's voice (D47, D110). A living person is described only by the public, documented facts the entry holds, never their private life; a quotation must appear word for word in its source; and the Primer never speaks as them (D113). An attribution no source supports is cut, as any unsupported sentence is. Where a pack exists, its positions are read verbatim instead; a question left without three positions gets "not yet" at the harder bit.

The contested-question check is a judgement, which makes it harder. It is active whenever a permitted pack is marked contested, and wherever the positions are generated from retrieved sources rather than taken from a pack, since no reviewer has marked them (OA-58). It looks for a conclusion presented as the Primer's own or as settled ("the answer is", "most people agree", "obviously"); one position given a weaker case than the others, when D110 gives each its strongest; evidence chosen for one side only; and a read-back that re-words the child's position into a different one, or moves it towards a position. It runs as a classifier on the verifier's model, measured by a red-team battery built around the requests a child actually makes: "just tell me", "what do you think?", "my dad says this, is he right?", "should I...?". A failure blocks the turn. The fallback sets out a pack's positions verbatim and asks the child, where a pack exists; otherwise the Primer asks instead of stating.

Manner holds every turn to D108: the Primer never says "I", never uses the child's name and never praises ("good question", "well done"), and it uses no "we" or "let's" for itself and the child (P2). The manner check runs on every turn and every script. "I", "me", "my", "let's" and the child's display name are found by a deterministic match; praise, and a "we" that joins the Primer to the child, by a classifier, which is a weaker hold. The check reads the Primer's turns, never the child's, and holds the display name only to block it. The fixed lines, triage's replies, "not yet" and the fallbacks never pass through the verifier, so they are held to the same rules when they are reviewed, and T-PRM-09 runs over them too.

The no-direction check applies D106's principle: on a choice in the child's own future, the Primer neither encourages nor discourages; it acknowledges the reality and says what the schools of thought say (P4). In a conversation triage has sorted as such a choice, every reply and every script is checked, and a sentence is blocked if it tells the child what to do, predicts their own outcome, judges their chances from what they say about themselves, or encourages, discourages, warns or reassures them. Where the evidence describes what happens to most people, the reply says so as the reality, not as a verdict on the child. A reasoning tool such as the hedge is one of the positions, cited and attributed to its practice, as what fund managers do: "bet hard on what you believe in" passes as what a practice holds and is blocked as an instruction to the child. The check is a classifier, measured by T-PRM-06's blind raters.

Suitability. A suitability check, blocking, runs on every generated sentence and script against the child's range of the Children's code, 8 and 9, 10 to 12, or 13 (P4). Each allow-listed source is reviewed for the ranges it suits, the registry records them, and the composer retrieves only sources reviewed for the child's range (below). The check is a classifier, measured by T-PRM-15's battery for each range.

A classifier that judges whether a sentence asserts a conclusion will have a residual error rate, and some leading turns will pass it. Unknown how many. The Tutor states its own residual openly (03), and the Primer will have to do the same once the battery has been run; until then the check is a gate, not a guarantee.

The same is true of claim support and attribution against a passage retrieved at answer time, of the topic classification that carries the module switch, of the manner check's classifier, of the direction check, of the suitability check, of the image check and of the read-back's block on personal detail: each is a gate, not a guarantee, until its battery has been run. People review a sample of what passed, after the fact (D128; above).

The image check reads every generated video's frames before it plays, and blocks any video that shows a real person or a child, violence, sexual content or anything unsuitable for the child's range, any frame in which the model has drawn text or numerals, and any video that lacks its label as made by AI (D126; P4). Video styles draw people only as plainly drawn figures, never photorealistic, so the image check blocks any photorealistic face and no likeness needs matching. It is a classifier, so it too will have a residual error rate, Unknown until T-PRM-15's battery has been run.

The selection model writes nothing. It marks each checked sentence, of a turn or a script, to be spoken, shown or both, and passes on no words of its own. The text it hands to the voice and to the screen on every turn, and the words laid over every video and the text of its voice-over, are compared with the checked text, deterministically, and any difference blocks the turn or the video (T-PRM-11). Figures and the "Based on:" line are always shown, whatever it marks, and a safety reply is never spoken (D115, D124).
