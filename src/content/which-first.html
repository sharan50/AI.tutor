The answer so far

Foundation does not go first. That is the owner's order, and it is not a judgement that Foundation is the lesser idea. The reasons D42 gave described a design for a parent at home, which the owner has replaced with a classroom instrument for the teacher, and they are withdrawn with it. Between the Tutor and the Primer the choice is open. It will be made on what teachers, professors and parents say in the field research, against one rule written down in advance, and recorded in the decision ledger with what it rules out.

DecisionD94, the owner's, 29 October 2026; D42's order kept

Two things pull in opposite directions, and both are real. The Tutor is the most developed idea in this vault by a wide margin: a complete design for the UK, a recommended route to market, a first milestone made almost entirely of reading and asking, and an evidenced market of UK parents who already pay for tutoring (00). The Primer is the idea the owner believes is lower risk on the question that kills most products, whether it works at all, because conversation is what current AI does best and children of 8 to 13 can hold one on a screen Assumed (OA-23). It also answers Y Combinator's call for a Primer, which, as CV-01 records it, cites Aristotle teaching Alexander Assumed (CV-01).

The owner's leaning is recorded here so that it can be tested rather than hidden. It is not the decision. A leaning that survives the questions below is worth more in front of an investor than one that was never asked them.

Why Foundation does not go first

Because the owner says so, recorded as decision D94. Nothing more is claimed for the order, and nothing less is claimed for Foundation: it has the same six documents as the Primer, without yet an MVP guide or a mock-up, and they set out how it would be built when its turn comes.

What the earlier reasons were, and why they are gone. D42 gave three, about a screen that coached a parent through a session at home: a young child needs a person; schools already teach early reading for free; and its risk of not working was the highest of the three. The classroom design meets the first by construction, since the teacher is in the room and the child is shown nothing (D93). It turns the second around, since it is a tool for the school's own teaching. The third was a judgement about a product that no longer exists. They are withdrawn, not restated, and no new reason has been put in their place.

What Foundation's turn will meet. Not reasons for the order, but things the plan must cross: hardware on every desk, a camera in a Year 1 classroom and the two counsel questions it raises (OI-28, OI-29), and whether a camera above a desk can read a five-year-old's page at all (Assumed OA-48).

Y Combinator's request, as CV-01 records it, takes the opposite order: it asks for adaptive teaching of reading, writing and arithmetic to young children, starting as something parents buy for basic skills, and names the Primer of The Diamond Age as the long-term goal. The owner's order differs, and an application to YC should say so openly rather than quietly ignore the request: start where YC wants to end up. Foundation, the idea nearest that first step, is now a tool for the teacher sold to schools, not something parents buy, and in the owner's view a five-year-old is taught by a person, not a screen (OA-28).

AssumedCV-01, awaiting the owner's check

The novel YC's request takes its name from points the same way. In The Diamond Age, Nell's Primer is voiced live by a human actor who becomes something like a mother to her, and the book leaves open how much of what Nell becomes came from the machine and how much from the person behind it Assumed (CV-04).

What would change this. Only the owner. D94 rests on no assumption that evidence could overturn, so the field research does not test it. What the research tests is Foundation's own design: whether teachers want the alerts, whether parents and staff accept the camera, and whether schools would pay (OA-50 to OA-52), so that Foundation is ready when its turn comes. Its documents, F1 to F6, set out the hard questions, and F6 says what would justify building it.

The Tutor or the Primer: what decides it

One rule, written down before any conversation so that the answer cannot be fitted to the evidence afterwards. It follows the shape of the D6 trigger in 11. Its thresholds are set before PM1 begins (D58), as D37 set the thresholds for the Tutor's route. P6 describes the evidence PM1 gathers for it; this is the only rule.

The Primer passes if all three hold at the end of PM1:

  1. Parents of 8 to 13 year olds, asked what they already spend on their children's learning outside school, show a purchase the Primer could replace or join, and a landing page converts at least as well as the Tutor's (OA-22).
  2. Teachers of the age group and the academics consulted judge that children of 8 to 13 can sustain a typed conversation long enough for an inquiry to work (OA-23), and parents say they would allow it with the modules switched as they chose (OA-26).
  3. Counsel's first view on OI-13, OI-15 and OI-16 reveals no blocker for the Primer that the Tutor does not also face, and leaves the consent flow for children under 13 affordable.

The Tutor passes if its first milestone, M1, leaves conditions C1 and C2 of 09 standing.

Then: if only one passes, it goes first. If both pass, the owner chooses and records why, with the case for the idea not chosen written beside it, as 09 does for the route not recommended. If neither passes, neither goes first: the result is recorded and the plan is revisited before anything is built.

Proposed rulerecorded as deliberately not taken; thresholds before PM1 (D58)

The rule is deliberately weighted towards the Tutor where the evidence is ambiguous, because the Tutor has already paid for most of its design and an ambiguous result does not justify paying again. That is not a judgement that the Tutor is the better idea.

What the three share, and what they do not

All three rest on one rule carried over from the Tutor: nothing reaches a child that cannot be traced to a source someone has checked, and what cannot be traced is blocked, or answered with an honest "not yet". All three refuse engagement tricks, with no streaks, badges or reminders; none acts as a friend; an adult is always accountable; and any piece of content can be withdrawn in one step. They share a platform, not a customer: the ages, the buyers, the data and the law all differ.

One product or three

Deliberately not decided, and recorded as such in the ledger. The vault treats the three as separate products that may share a platform (D40). Whether they should become one product, a tutor that grows with a child from four to eighteen, is a question this vault cannot yet answer.

The case for one product. A family that trusts the Primer at nine has a reason to keep to the Tutor at fifteen, and a family that met Foundation through their child's school at five already knows the name; YC's request, starting with basic skills and naming the Primer as the long-term goal, points the same way Assumed (CV-01). One account, one platform and one brand cost less to build and to explain. The value of a customer kept for a decade instead of a single exam year is Unknown, and could dominate everything else in the economics if it is real.

The case for three. The buyers differ, and so do their reasons: a parent buying exam preparation, a parent buying a tutor for their child's questions and a school buying a camera for every desk are three sales. The law differs, and so does the attention it draws: a controversy over what the Primer said to a nine-year-old (RP1) would reach the Tutor's conversations with schools if they shared a name. And focus matters most when nothing is built.

Two directions, not one. 13 describes a single product that grows upwards in ambition, from exam practice to a gated Primer mode for older students. YC's request, as CV-01 records it, starts with basic skills for young children and names the Primer as its long-term goal Assumed, which would make a single product grow upwards in age instead. They are different products, and either could be right.

What would settle it. Questions in the field research: whether parents who would buy the Primer would buy the Tutor when their child reached its age, whether parents who met Foundation through a school would trust the others more or less for it, and whether a shared name would raise or lower their trust in each. Neither needs anything built.
