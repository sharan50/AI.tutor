What would make the Primer go first

Foundation does not go first (D94). Between the Tutor and the Primer there is one decision rule, on which idea goes first, and this page keeps no second one. Its thresholds are written down before PM1 begins, as D37 wrote down the thresholds for the Tutor's route, so that the result cannot be fitted to the evidence afterwards Decision D58.

Until then, the thresholds that turn PM1's evidence into a yes or a no are not known.

Thresholds unknownwritten down before PM1 starts, as D37 did for the Tutor

PM1 supplies the Primer's half of the evidence the rule needs: whether parents of 8 to 13 year olds would pay with no exam to prepare for, for a purchase the Primer could replace or join or for the time they lack (OA-22, OA-53), and buy a tablet for one child to do it (OA-54); priors from teachers and professors on whether children in the band can sustain a spoken or typed inquiry, one question in the three to six minutes D112 sets as a target (OA-23, OA-55); whether parents accept the module design and reasoning about their child's own future on unless they turn it off (OA-26, OA-57); and counsel's first view on OI-13, OI-15, OI-16 and OI-17, the last of which the Primer proceeds ahead of under D102, and on OI-30 to OI-32.

Four things do not count as evidence: enthusiasm for the idea, interest from an accelerator, how long a child stays engaged in a demonstration, and the films, which are illustrative, made with AI, and show nothing that has been built. Y Combinator's requests for startups include one called "The Primer" Assumed (CV-01); the ages and the order reported for it differ from this one (D41, D94), and an application is not demand. Page 13 states the principle that applies: no stage advances because the team likes the concept or because engagement is high (13).
