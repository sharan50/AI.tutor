Economics

Almost every figure the Primer's business needs is unknown, and none is filled in here; the tablet adds a second set, for making and supporting hardware, as unknown as the first, and live video adds a cost to every session. The table names what would measure each one, and when. That is the discipline the Tutor's economics follows (10), applied to an idea at an earlier stage.

For the Tutor, 10 derives the variable that dominates the economics. For the Primer it cannot yet be derived. There are four candidates: the price a parent will pay (OA-22, OA-54); the cost of generating each answer and its videos live, which recurs with every question a child asks (OA-62); the cost of review, of the allow-list, the reviewed core, the sensitive modules' packs, the entries on named living people and the sample, which grows with the catalogue and does not get cheaper as models improve (RP5); and the tablet's cost to make, certify, send and support, which recurs with every child (RP7). If generation dominates, the Primer's costs grow with every question; if review does, its economics look more like a publisher's than a software company's; if the tablet does, more like a device maker's.
