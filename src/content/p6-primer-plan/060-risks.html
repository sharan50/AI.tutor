Risks

The owner has not ranked the Primer's risks, as the owner ranked the Tutor's (12). They are ordered here by severity as judged on this page: harm to a child first, then what could end the product, then what could make it unviable. RP7 to RP9 come from the films; RP3 and RP4 are restated for summaries in place of records, and for the safeguarding review that runs on every conversation.

#RiskLeading indicatorMitigation in the design
RP4A safeguarding disclosure inside an inquiry is missed or misheard, or reaches a parent who is the risk (D48); or, in a quieter form, the parent's record reveals a question or a view the child could not safely hold at home.Near-misses in T-PRM-06's battery, spoken and typed; recognition errors on that battery, worst for the youngest Assumed (CV-11); any flagged exchange found in a parent's view (T-PRM-08), or kept after its review closes (T-PRM-10); a safety reply spoken (T-PRM-12); a reviewer backlog.A safety check on every exchange as it happens, and a fuller review at the end of each conversation (D107); triage on every turn, tuned to stop; the flag applied to the whole exchange before anything is written to the parent's record, screen or account, with the reply left on the child's tablet only until the conversation closes (P4); a flagged exchange's words, and only those, kept for a trained reviewer (D26), deleted when the review closes and never shown to the parent (D107); no audio in any record (D102, ahead of OI-17); a safety reply shown, never spoken, with no "Keep going" after it (D115); a declined question shown to the parent by its category, and a stopped one only as "a personal question, stopped", never with the reply, the services named, or which of family, the body, feelings or safety it was (D48); summaries the child heard and could correct before they were kept (D104, D117); signposting first (P4). The quieter form is reduced, not removed.
RP1A conversation about a big question is reported as the product indoctrinating a child; or what the bank says of a named living person, or a figure it gives, is wrong, out of date or objected to (Open OI-30).A parent's complaint or a press enquiry about a sensitive module; an objection from a named person or their representatives; a figure whose source has changed.Sensitive modules off by default (D46); no conclusion asserted on a contested question (T-PRM-03); every position attributed and given its strongest case (D110); review group sign-off; one-operation pull (P5). For people and figures, D113: public, documented facts from reviewed sources only, no private life, no quotation beyond the source and no voice; "roughly" for an estimate, with its source; and no figure that no reviewed entry holds (D44). The residual is real: an exchange can be filmed or quoted out of context, and the design cannot prevent that.
RP9Reasoning about a child's own future is reported as a product telling children what to do with their lives (OA-57, Open OI-32).A parent's complaint or a press enquiry about the topic; any reply in T-PRM-06's own-future battery that tells a child what to do, predicts their outcome, or encourages or discourages them; the share of parents who turn the topic off, observed and never targeted.The fence (P2, P4): the reality from reviewed evidence, and what the schools of thought say, each at its strongest, then the child's own position, read back; never what to do, never a prediction for this child, never praise, warning or reassurance (D106, D108); a check on every reply that blocks direction (T-PRM-06); questions about family, the body, feelings and safety still stop; a parent can turn the topic off (D106). The residual is real: on by default from 8 is the owner's choice, and a line such as the film's hedge, "bet hard on what you believe in", quoted without the positions around it, reads as advice.
RP3The Primer is classed as a companion chatbot in the US.A state law or federal guidance that names educational chatbots; counsel's answer to OI-15.05's anti-companionship constraints and the Primer's own (P4); it never says "I", never uses the child's name and never praises (D108, T-PRM-09); memory of past questions and views as summaries, nothing word for word (D104, T-PRM-10); no personas (D47); a synthetic voice that is no real person's (D9, D47); no streaks, badges, reminders or notifications (D25, D105). A Primer that answers aloud is nearer the companion pattern than a typed one, and none of this settles OI-15.
RP8A child's voice is misheard, or counsel's answer on OI-17 withdraws voice after launch (OA-55).Recognition errors on children's speech at PM4, worst for the youngest Assumed (CV-11); conversations that stall at PM3 or PM4; counsel's answer on OI-17.The Primer proceeds ahead of OI-17 under D102, and only a categoric no withdraws voice; typing is always there (D102), so a withdrawal would change the Primer, not end it; speech is tried with children at PM3 and recognised by the built Primer at PM4, or in a supervised pilot held to PM4's conditions if the spoken battery passes later, before any child at home has it. The weakness is plain: the audio is thrown away at once, so a mishearing cannot be checked afterwards.
RP2Children use it and parents do not pay for the tablet and the service (OA-22, OA-54).PM1's conversations and landing page, then PM5.None in the design. This is a market question: PM1 exists to answer it cheaply, and the tablet is made only after PM4 (making the tablet), so the dearest spending waits for more evidence.
RP7The tablet costs more to make, ship, support or certify than families will pay (OA-54, Open OI-31).Makers' quotations during PM4; what counsel's answer to OI-31 requires; faults and breakage among PM4's stand-ins; then PM5's purchases.Nothing is made before PM4's evidence, and the stand-in carries the pilot (making the tablet). Nothing in the design lowers the cost itself: if it is more than families pay, the design has no fallback short of reversing D105, which only the owner can do.
RP6No measurable effect on reasoning (OA-24, OA-25).T-PRM-07 at PM4.No outcome claim before evidence (P3); holding at PM4 is a designed outcome, not a failure of nerve.
RP5Reviewers, or the making of explainers, become the bottleneck, and "not yet" dominates (OA-32, OA-59).Review time per module, and the time to specify and review an explainer, at PM2; the share of "not yet" answers at PM3.A writing queue ordered by what children ask; narrow modules; each explainer drawn by code from one reviewed specification, so that it is made once for every child who asks (D111); the catalogue treated as the asset it is.
