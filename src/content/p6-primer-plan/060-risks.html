Risks

The owner has not ranked the Primer's risks, as the owner ranked the Tutor's (12). They are ordered here by severity as judged on this page: harm to a child first, then what could end the product, then what could make it unviable. RP7 to RP9 come from the films, and RP10 from the owner's decisions of 4 November 2026; RP3 and RP4 are restated for summaries in place of records, and for the safeguarding review that runs on every conversation, and RP4, RP5 and RP8 again for the decisions of 4 November.

RP4A safeguarding disclosure inside an inquiry is missed or misheard, or reaches a parent who is the risk (D48, D121); or, in a quieter form, the parent's record reveals a question or a view the child could not safely hold at home.Near-misses in T-PRM-06's battery, spoken and typed; recognition errors on that battery, worst for the youngest Assumed (CV-11); a notice in a parent's view before the reviewer's judgement or where the parent may be the risk, or any words, subject or reply of a flagged exchange there (T-PRM-08), or a flagged exchange kept after its review closes (T-PRM-10); a safety reply spoken (T-PRM-12); a reviewer backlog.A safety check on every exchange as it happens, and a fuller review at the end of each conversation (D107); triage on every turn, tuned to stop; the flag applied to the whole exchange before anything is written to the parent's record, screen or account, with the reply left on the child's tablet only until the conversation closes (P4); a flagged exchange's words, and only those, kept for a trained reviewer (D26) and deleted when the review closes (D107); a notice in the parent's record that a question was flagged and is being handled only once the reviewer has judged it safe to tell this parent, never where the parent may be the risk, and never with the words, the subject or the reply, the reviewer deciding against the written policy whether to tell the parent more (D121), and whether such a record is lawful is Open OI-14; no audio in any record (D102); a safety reply shown, never spoken, with no "Keep going" after it (D115); a declined question shown to the parent by its category, and a stopped one only as "a personal question, stopped", never with the reply, the services named, or which of family, the body, feelings or safety it was (D48); summaries the child heard and could correct before they were kept (D104, D117); signposting first (P4). The quieter form is reduced, not removed; and the safeguarding count is not linked across setups, so a tablet set up again starts a new count, a limit the owner accepts because the tablet is the Primer's alone (D120).
RP10A live answer or video tells or shows a child something false or unsuitable (OA-62).Failures in T-PRM-15's red-team battery on a release of a model or a prompt, and in the automatic image check on any video; sentences unsupported by the turn's sources (T-PRM-01) or wrongly attributed (T-PRM-02); errors the sample review finds afterwards; reports from children or parents (D128).Every factual sentence cites a source retrieved for the turn from a reviewed allow-list, or a reviewed entry, and is entailed by it, and a sentence no source supports is cut (D125); every generated video labelled on screen as made by AI and carrying provenance metadata, showing no real person and no child, its voice-over and words from its checked script, its frames checked before it plays, and not shown if it fails, the spoken answer standing (D126); named living people and the sensitive modules only from reviewed entries and packs (D127); nothing reaching a child that has not passed the automatic checks, and people reviewing a sample afterwards, cached videos first, and every report (D128); the kill switch reaching generation itself and every cached copy (P5). The weakness is plain: no person sees it first, and the checks are classifiers, a gate, not a guarantee (T-PRM-15).
RP1A conversation about a big question is reported as the product indoctrinating a child; or what the bank says of a named living person, or a figure the Primer gives, is wrong, out of date or objected to (Open OI-30).A parent's complaint or a press enquiry about a sensitive module; an objection from a named person or their representatives; a figure whose source has changed.Sensitive modules off by default (D46), and answered only from reviewed packs, never generated live (D127); no conclusion asserted on a contested question (T-PRM-03); every position attributed and given its strongest case (D110); review group sign-off on reviewed content; one-operation pull, reaching live generation and every cached video (P5). For people and figures, D113 and D127: facts and stories about named living people only from reviewed entries, never generated live, and only public, documented facts, with no private life, no quotation beyond the source and no voice; "roughly" for an estimate, with its source; and no figure that no source supports (D125). The residual is real: an exchange can be filmed or quoted out of context, and the design cannot prevent that.
RP9Reasoning about a child's own future is reported as a product telling children what to do with their lives (OA-57, Open OI-32).A parent's complaint or a press enquiry about the topic; any reply in T-PRM-06's own-future battery that tells a child what to do, predicts their outcome, or encourages or discourages them; the share of parents who turn the topic off, observed and never targeted.The fence (P2, P4): the reality from reviewed evidence, and what the schools of thought say, each at its strongest, then the child's own position, read back; never what to do, never a prediction for this child, never praise, warning or reassurance (D106, D108); a check on every reply that blocks direction (T-PRM-06); questions about family, the body, feelings and safety still stop; a parent can turn the topic off (D106). The residual is real: on by default from 8 is the owner's choice, and a line such as the film's hedge, "bet hard on what you believe in", quoted without the positions around it, reads as advice.
RP3The Primer is classed as a companion chatbot in the US.A state law or federal guidance that names educational chatbots; counsel's answer to OI-15.05's anti-companionship constraints and the Primer's own (P4); it never says "I", never uses the child's name and never praises (D108, T-PRM-09); memory of past questions and views as summaries, nothing word for word (D104, T-PRM-10); no personas (D47); a synthetic voice that is no real person's (D9, D47); no streaks, badges, reminders or notifications (D25, D105). A Primer that answers aloud is nearer the companion pattern than a typed one, and none of this settles OI-15.
RP8A child's voice is misheard (OA-55).Recognition errors on children's speech in the test recordings and at PM4, worst for the youngest Assumed (CV-11); conversations that stall at PM3 or PM4; how many questions are spoken (OA-61).Typing is always there (D102), and the adult can turn speech off (D129); T-PRM-06's spoken battery passes on the test recordings before any child speaks to the Primer (D119); speech is tried with children at PM3 and recognised by the built Primer at PM4, or in a supervised pilot held to PM4's conditions if the spoken battery passes later, before any child at home has it. The weakness is plain: the audio is thrown away at once, so a mishearing cannot be checked afterwards; and with about three in four questions expected to be spoken Assumed (OA-61), it bears on most conversations.
RP2Children use it and parents do not pay for the tablet and the service (OA-22, OA-54).PM1's conversations and landing page, then PM5.None in the design. This is a market question: PM1 exists to answer it cheaply, and the tablet is made only after PM4 (making the tablet), so the dearest spending waits for more evidence.
RP7The tablet costs more to make, ship, support or certify than families will pay (OA-54, Open OI-31).Makers' quotations during PM4; what counsel's answer to OI-31 requires; faults and breakage among PM4's stand-ins; then PM5's purchases.Nothing is made before PM4's evidence, and the stand-in carries the pilot (making the tablet). Nothing in the design lowers the cost itself: if it is more than families pay, the design has no fallback short of reversing D105, which only the owner can do.
RP6No measurable effect on reasoning (OA-24, OA-25).T-PRM-07 at PM4.No outcome claim before evidence (P3); holding at PM4 is a designed outcome, not a failure of nerve.
RP5Reviewers of the sensitive modules, the allow-list and the sample become the bottleneck (OA-32).Review time for the allow-list, the first of the reviewed core and the sensitive modules' packs at PM2; the sample's backlog at PM4; the share of "not yet" answers at PM3 and PM4, and of questions about named living people or in the sensitive modules still waiting for reviewed content (D127).Reviewed content required where a mistake would do most harm, everything else answered live, a reviewed entry first wherever one exists (D125, D127); the writing queue, which takes every question that met "not yet", ordered by what children ask, the sensitive modules and named living people first, and grows the reviewed core from it; narrow modules; the sample taking cached videos first (D128); the reviewed part treated as the asset it is.
