Children's code, mapped point by point

The standard names below are reproduced from the ICO Children's code as understood at the time of writing and have not been re-verified against the published text in this pass. The mapping is the work product; the enumeration must be checked against the published code before this document is relied on by anyone. Checking it is an hour of reading and it is a task at M1 in 11 Roadmap.

AssumedRead this before relying on the table
StandardHow this design meets it
1. Best interests of the childThe primary consideration in the three decisions where it conflicts with commercial interest: no parental transcript access by default (D16), no engagement optimisation (D25), and an unaided check that reports honestly rather than flatteringly (D15). The self-tutor's case rests on a benefit nobody has measured (OA-37), so it is weighed in the DPIA before launch, not assumed.
2. Data protection impact assessmentsRequired and not yet written. Named as a gap below, with a trigger. The self-tutor makes one unavoidable before it launches.
3. Age appropriate applicationSingle audience, single age band. Every learner account is a child account by default whatever was entered.
4. TransparencyThe marketing-site line above, plus a learner-facing explanation written for a fifteen-year-old, not a parent-facing notice with a child-facing summary bolted on. The self-tutor's consent screen is written the same way, for the learner (D62).
5. Detrimental use of dataThe not-inferred list, no engagement optimisation, no pricing or marketing use. The provisional grade range is the one output capable of discouraging a child, and it carries five specific safeguards set out above. The self-tutor never comments on a learner's face or voice and offers no filter or alteration of either (D64).
6. Policies and community standardsThe content safety policy below, and we uphold our own published terms rather than reserving discretion.
7. Default settingsHigh privacy by default. Notifications off. Parental transcript access off. Nothing optional is pre-enabled. The self-tutor is off until the adult and the learner both turn it on (D62).
8. Data minimisationThe held list is short and each row has a necessity justification. No date of birth is collected at all. The self-tutor adds a head-and-shoulders scan and a short prompted voice sample, taken only with both consents and kept only until deleted (D61, D63); no full-body image is taken.
9. Data sharingNone, other than the age assurance provider (which receives the adult's data, not the child's) and the model vendor under D24 terms. For the self-tutor, the speech and animation vendor, under the same terms, with no training on a learner's face or voice (D65).
10. GeolocationNot collected. Not inferred. Not used.
11. Parental controlsParental controls exist and, where they would be visible to the child, the child is told. The default transcript position in D16 is itself a parental-control decision taken in the child's favour. A self-tutor needs the adult's consent and the adult can delete it, but cannot see or hear it (D60), and the learner is told so before consenting.
12. ProfilingOn, because it is the service, with a lawful basis proposed above and scoped to academic performance in the enrolled subject. The scope limit is the justification. A self-tutor's face scan and voice sample are used to render the likeness and for nothing else.
13. Nudge techniquesD25. No streaks, no badges, no loss aversion, no return notifications, no engagement objective.
14. Connected toys and devicesNot applicable. No connected device. The product records a learner's voice once, when they make a self-tutor (D61), and never listens otherwise.
15. Online toolsDeletion, access and objection routes are in the product, in plain language, reachable by the child as well as the adult. Deleting a self-tutor is one action, open to the learner and to the adult (D63).
