Children's code, mapped point by point

The standard names below are reproduced from the ICO Children's code as understood at the time of writing and have not been re-verified against the published text in this pass. The mapping is the work product; the enumeration must be checked against the published code before this document is relied on by anyone. Checking it is an hour of reading and it is a task at M1 in 11 Roadmap.

AssumedRead this before relying on the table
StandardHow this design meets it
1. Best interests of the childThe primary consideration in the three decisions where it conflicts with commercial interest: no parental transcript access by default (D16), no engagement optimisation (D25), and an unaided check that reports honestly rather than flatteringly (D15).
2. Data protection impact assessmentsRequired and not yet written. Named as a gap below, with a trigger.
3. Age appropriate applicationSingle audience, single age band. Every learner account is a child account by default whatever was entered.
4. TransparencyThe marketing-site line above, plus a learner-facing explanation written for a fifteen-year-old, not a parent-facing notice with a child-facing summary bolted on.
5. Detrimental use of dataThe not-inferred list, no engagement optimisation, no pricing or marketing use. The provisional grade range is the one output capable of discouraging a child, and it carries five specific safeguards set out above.
6. Policies and community standardsThe content safety policy below, and we uphold our own published terms rather than reserving discretion.
7. Default settingsHigh privacy by default. Notifications off. Parental transcript access off. Nothing optional is pre-enabled.
8. Data minimisationThe held list is short and each row has a necessity justification. No date of birth is collected at all.
9. Data sharingNone, other than the age assurance provider (which receives the adult's data, not the child's) and the model vendor under D24 terms.
10. GeolocationNot collected. Not inferred. Not used.
11. Parental controlsParental controls exist and, where they would be visible to the child, the child is told. The default transcript position in D16 is itself a parental-control decision taken in the child's favour.
12. ProfilingOn, because it is the service, with a lawful basis proposed above and scoped to academic performance in the enrolled subject. The scope limit is the justification.
13. Nudge techniquesD25. No streaks, no badges, no loss aversion, no return notifications, no engagement objective.
14. Connected toys and devicesNot applicable. No connected device, and no voice at launch (D9).
15. Online toolsDeletion, access and objection routes are in the product, in plain language, reachable by the child as well as the adult.
