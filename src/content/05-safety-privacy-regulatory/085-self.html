The self-tutor: a child's face and voice

The self-tutor is the one place the product holds a child's face and voice. It is off by default, made only with two consents, shown and played by the product to no one but the learner, and switched off at the next turn when either consent is withdrawn. Whether that is enough is a set of questions for counsel that this document does not answer.

D59 puts the self-tutor on the launch path ahead of those answers. It is withdrawn in a market only if counsel advises in writing that no form of it the design allows may launch there, and a conditional answer changes the design instead. It is the second recorded exception of its kind, after D34 on OI-3, and each part of this document that applies it says so.

What is held, and for what

A head-and-shoulders face scan and a voice sample read from sentences chosen afresh for each capture, both captured live in the product (D61); whatever conditioning the rendering vendor derives from them (D65); and the two consent records. The clips the self-tutor renders are streamed to the learner and never stored, and transcripts keep the text of a turn, never its audio or video. The rows are in the data table. No one has routine access to the likeness store, and any access is logged. They are used to present the tutor in the learner's likeness and for nothing else: never to identify the learner, check anyone's age, profile them or infer anything about them (the not-inferred list). No full-body image is taken.

Deletion follows the line above. When the learner or the adult deletes, a flag read at every turn stops the rendering at the next one (D63); primary stores are purged within seven days and backups age out within thirty-five. A self-tutor unused for twelve months, or on an account that closes, is deleted without anyone asking.

Two consents, and the question behind them

Before anything is captured, the verified adult and the learner each consent, separately and explicitly, after being told in plain words what is kept, that the product will show or play it to no one else, the adult included, and how to delete it (D62). The learner's words are written for a fifteen-year-old, as the transparency standard asks, not adapted from the adult's. Either can withdraw at any time, and withdrawal is deletion. The self-tutor then plays only behind a passcode the learner sets, because a learner's sessions sit under the adult's account and nothing else would keep the account holder out (D60). A parent's subject access request for their child's data is the one route by which an adult could ask for the face scan and voice sample; how it meets D60, including where the parent is the risk (D26), is part of OI-23.

OI-23: Are a learner's face scan and voice sample, and anything derived from them, biometric or special category data under UK GDPR when used to present a tutor in their likeness rather than to identify them? What lawful basis applies? Can a learner of 14 to 17 give the consent, or must the verified adult, or both? What does the Children's code require of the feature? How does a subject access request by a parent for their child's data meet D60? Does a school-controlled version change any of it? Unblocks: D59, D62 and D67. Cost of getting it wrong: a child's face and voice processed on a basis that does not hold, the risk this document opens with.

Ofcom

The vault's reading, set out above and not confirmed by counsel (OI-4), is that a private one-to-one tutor with no sharing between users sits outside the Act's user-to-user duties. The self-tutor stays inside that reading only because no one but the learner can see or hear it (D60), which T9 in the trigger list makes a change-control gate.

That Ofcom's warning about virtual clones of real people was made in a child protection and safety context, and that a self-tutor only its learner can access, which says only what the company's gate has passed, is therefore not a concern. This is the owner's view, and the basis of launching with the learner's own voice under D9 as amended.

The United States

D1 opens the US alongside the UK where resources allow. Which state laws on biometric identifiers and on minors' data reach a learner's face and voice used this way, and what consent, notice, retention and deletion duties follow, is OI-25, with OI-21. D59's rule applies market by market: a categoric no in one market does not withdraw the self-tutor from the other.

Safeguarding and wellbeing

Seeing and hearing oneself teach is new, and for a teenager it may not be benign: self-image, distress, or an attachment the anti-companionship constraints exist to prevent (R14). The design answers with rules rather than monitoring (D64):

  • The self-tutor never claims to be the learner, and never speaks as though it were them.
  • It never comments on the learner's face or voice, and no filter or alteration of either is offered.
  • It is off by default, and the standard tutor is one tap away in every session.
  • A safeguarding reply, and any turn the safety classifier flags, is shown as text from the standard tutor, never in the learner's own face and voice: a child should not hear a pointer to help in their own voice.
  • The anti-companionship constraints apply unchanged: no feelings, no contact it initiates, no social memory.
  • Anything a learner says that suggests distress, about the self-tutor or anything else, takes D26's route like any other disclosure.

Nothing here claims the self-tutor helps. That is OA-37, the owner's view, and T-SLF-01 is its test; until it reports, no benefit is claimed for the feature anywhere.
