Named gaps

Three, each with what it blocks and when it must be closed. None is a matter of opinion; all three are work that has not been done.

GapStatusMust be closed by
Data protection impact assessment. Mandatory here: large-scale processing of children's data combined with profiling.Not written. Not started.Before the first live child user. It is a precondition of launch, not a launch task.
The safeguarding policy itself. D26 routes disclosures to "a written policy" that decides whether and whom to inform. Retention of escalation records depends on it too.Referenced throughout this document. Not written. Not started.Before the first live child user, alongside the DPIA. A disclosure route with no written decision policy behind it is an escalation into a vacuum, and it is worse than the signposting it sits on top of.
International transfers. Assumption If the Indian operating company processes UK children's data, that is a restricted transfer requiring an IDTA or the UK Addendum to SCCs plus a transfer risk assessment. Standard position, not confirmed for this fact pattern.Identified, not resolved.Before any UK child data is processed in India. Interacts with OI-7, which entity contracts with the parent.
UK representative. Assumption That UK GDPR Article 3(2) and the Article 27 UK representative requirement apply to an India-established controller serving UK children. Standard position, unconfirmed for this fact pattern.Identified, not resolved.Before launch. Cheap to fix, embarrassing to be caught without.
