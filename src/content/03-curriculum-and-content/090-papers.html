Practice papers, and the word "mark scheme"

A practice paper is original items assembled to the shape of the board's paper. Assembly is deterministic, it draws only from the validated bank, and it introduces no new content. That is what makes a paper checkable rather than merely plausible.

What "board-aligned" means here, precisely. The paper matches the structure a candidate will meet: question count, mark distribution across the paper, the mix of command words, topic coverage against the specification, and the time allowance. It does not reproduce, adapt or paraphrase any board question, and the structural description is written in our own words from publicly observable paper shape. The clean-room rule is unchanged, and D12 is not weakened by this feature; it constrains it.

Every paper ships with a mark scheme, and it is ours, for our items, validated by our examiners. Nothing in it comes from a board.

"Mark scheme" is a term of art that sounds like it belongs to an awarding body, and a parent skim-reading a marketing page would reasonably assume it did. So the product says "CINE mark scheme" in every learner-facing and parent-facing surface. This is a naming rule, not a preference, and it belongs with the board-naming convention in 04.

Decision D3614 September 2026

What this costs. Very little in content, because a paper is an assembly of items that already exist and already carry validation. It costs in metadata: every item now needs a mark tariff, a command word and a difficulty label, and those are set at authoring and checked at validation. It also costs a structural specification per board and per paper, which is the piece of work that has to be repeated if a second board is added, and is the real reason D19 matters.
