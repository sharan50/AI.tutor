The uncomfortable arithmetic of that floor

A 99.5% per-turn floor sounds strong. Per session it is not, and the honest number is the one a parent experiences.

Per-session risk is 1 - (1 - p)^n, where p is the per-turn floor and n is the number of tutor turns in a session. Unknown n is not known; it is measured at M2 and is marked unknown in 06 and 10 as well. So the honest presentation is the relationship, not a single number.

At the launch floor, somewhere between one session in twenty and one in seven contains an unsupported assertion, depending on a session length nobody has measured. That is the range to hold in mind, and it is wide because the input is unknown rather than because the arithmetic is uncertain.

Derived from ACC-2and it is not comfortable

Two things follow, and both are commitments rather than observations.

The per-session figure is the reported figure. Internally and externally, groundedness is reported as "proportion of sessions containing at least one unsupported assertion", not as a per-turn percentage. Per-turn numbers flatter the product by a factor of roughly twenty and are the number a competitor would quote.

No marketing claim in the form "it does not make things up" is made until ACC-2 is measured at or above 99.9% per turn on two consecutive weekly runs. Until then the product claim is the narrower and true one: every substantive statement cites a validated item, and the tutor refuses rather than guesses when it cannot. That claim is supportable at the launch floor; the stronger one is not.

Decision D2114 September 2026

This is the single clearest case in the repository of a number that would have been easier to leave at the per-turn figure. It is stated this way so that someone reading in six months cannot accidentally market a 99.5% floor as though it meant what it sounds like it means.
