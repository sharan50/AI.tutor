Rights dossier

The owner ranks a suit by a creator or an exam board as the failure most likely to end the company. This chapter exists to be handed to a UK media lawyer and to a prospective creator on the same day, and for both to be able to act on it.

The exam boards have already published the policy

AQA's copyright and intellectual property policy states that AQA does not permit the use of any AQA material, in any manner or for any purpose, in connection with the training of artificial intelligence powered tools or technologies. It further states that AQA will not allow the use of any AQA material in or on any edition of a third-party work where any part of that work is generated or produced using AI.

Verifiedaqa.org.uk copyright and IP policy, read Sep 2026

The second limb is the broader one. Read plainly it bars an AI tutoring product from containing AQA material at all, not merely from training on it. This is not a risk to be managed. It is a published position that predates us.

Pearson permits approved examination centres to reproduce past papers and mark schemes for practice, revision or teaching within the centre, on condition that students are not charged for them. A paid consumer product falls outside that permission by construction.

VerifiedPearson copyright policy, qualifications.pearson.com

What the boards can and cannot stop

The line

Copyright protects expression, not facts and not curricula. Nobody owns the requirement that a student know Newton's second law, and GCSE and A-level subject content is set and published by the Department for Education. Teaching to a published specification, using wholly original items and explanations, is not an act a board has standing to stop. Reproducing its papers, its mark schemes, or the text of its specification document is.

That makes the exposure narrower than it first appears, and it makes the clean-room policy in document 03 the central defensive asset rather than a compliance chore. Every item in the bank carries its provenance. Nothing sourced from a board ever enters the system, including in testing, including internally, including for benchmarking.

Naming a board in order to describe what a course prepares a student for is capable of being honest descriptive use under section 11(2) of the Trade Marks Act 1994, provided endorsement is not implied. This is stated as counsel's likely starting point, not as advice, and the exact permitted wording has to come from counsel before any marketing copy is written.

Why the offshore strategy was considered and dropped

The proposition was to sell from India so that any action would have to be fought in Indian courts, on the Uber model of operating through cease-and-desist orders while building market share. It runs the opposite way to how it reads.

The United Kingdom is a notified reciprocating territory under section 44A of the Code of Civil Procedure 1908, and the English High Courts are notified superior courts. A UK superior court money decree is executable in India as if an Indian District Court had passed it, without a fresh suit, subject only to the section 13 conclusiveness tests. The Delhi High Court has enforced an ex parte English Commercial Court judgment for USD 47m on exactly this basis.

VerifiedNotification F.34-I/52-L, 1 Mar 1953; Delhi HC, 2023

Ofcom does not need an Indian court at all. Between 1 April 2025 and 30 June 2026 it imposed penalties totalling £6.1m on nine companies that were either not UK-based or did not state their jurisdiction, and it has pursued business disruption measures including a possible UK access block against a non-compliant overseas provider.

VerifiedOfcom FOI response, 18 Aug 2026

On likeness specifically, electing India is choosing the least favourable forum available. Indian courts are currently the most aggressive in the world on AI persona misuse, and in March 2026 the Delhi High Court restrained this precise fact pattern, an AI chatbot replicating a real person, in Sonakshi Sinha v. Character Technologies.

Verified2026 SCC OnLine Del 1177, 20 Mar 2026

The structural point about Uber: it broke a regulatory licensing rule, where no single party owned a property right in what it was taking, the counterparty was diffuse, and the end state was a legislature rewriting the rule under public pressure. Here the counterparties are named private owners of specific property rights. No legislature is going to amend the Copyright, Designs and Patents Act so that a startup may use AQA's question papers. Private rights-holders do not settle into legalisation; they settle into a licence fee, or they obtain an injunction and costs.

The Indian operating company is retained for ordinary tax and capital reasons. The litigation-evasion rationale is dropped.

The one genuinely aggressive move available

Sign the UK GCSE creators first. They are mid-tier, they are reachable, their ad revenue is modest, and the agreement is one page. Speed is the moat that works in this situation. Defiance is the one that ends with a payment processor closing the account.
