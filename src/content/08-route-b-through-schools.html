Route B: through schools

Slower, better defended, and the only route that ever produces proof that the thing works.

The buyer

A trust or a school, buying per pupil per year against a budget line that is currently shrinking. The decision involves a subject lead, a data protection lead and a business manager, and takes an academic-year cycle.

58% of schools reduced their tutoring offer year on year, and school-organised tutoring reached 20% of pupils, down from 22% in 2023. This route sells to a buyer who is withdrawing from the category, which is the central argument against it.

VerifiedSutton Trust, Private Tutoring 2026

What it buys you

  • Processor status. The school holds the controller relationship and the lawful basis for pupil data, which materially reduces the exposure that the ICO is currently enforcing against.
  • Annual renewal against a budget line rather than a parent cancelling in July.
  • Access to actual outcomes: real results attached to real usage, which is the only way the efficacy claim ever stops being an assumption.

What it costs you

  • Nine to eighteen months to first revenue.
  • A field sales motion, safeguarding documentation, a DPIA the school will read, and a reference site before anyone second buys.
  • The persona wedge, in practice. A school procuring a teaching tool is not buying pupil-selected YouTubers, and the feature that distinguishes the product is the feature the buyer is least comfortable with.

That last point is the real cost and it is not a detail. Under Route B the venture is competing on efficacy evidence against Century Tech and Third Space Learning, who have years of it, rather than on a differentiator nobody else has.

Compliance posture

Processor acting on the school's documented instructions, with a data processing agreement, safeguarding policy, and the school's own age and consent position inherited. Lighter on the ICO's current enforcement theme, heavier on contractual and procurement scrutiny.
