Named living people

A choice can need the documented stories of named people, living ones included, and Version C tells the stories of Pike, Rooney and Vardy. Facts and stories about named living people come only from reviewed entries and packs, never from live generation (D127), and the explanation bank may hold such stories under one rule: public, documented facts from reviewed sources, never a private life, never quoted beyond the source and never voiced (D113). What duties apply when content for children of 8 to 13 tells the stories of named living people is Open OI-30: privacy and defamation law, any rights in a person's name or image, and the Children's code, in the UK and the US.

The explanation bank holds the evidence a choice needs: careers data, odds and the documented stories of named people, living ones included. A living person is described only by public, documented facts from reviewed sources, never their private life, never quoted beyond the source and never voiced. A figure whose source is itself an estimate is said with "roughly" and keeps its source; a figure no reviewed entry holds is never said, so D44 is unchanged. Evidence follows the child's market: UK figures and cases for a child in the UK, US ones in the US.

DecisionD127, taken while writing, 4 November 2026

D127 keeps the stories of living people out of live generation, because a wrong sentence about a living person is a harm of its own (OI-30); it keeps the sensitive modules out too, since they are where D46 and D57 put their caution (modules). It narrows D113 beside D125: the rest of a choice's evidence, the careers data and the odds, may now come from a source on the reviewed allow-list, said "roughly" where the source is an estimate, while nothing about a named living person may. Keeping them out of live answers depends on recognising a named living person in a question or in a generated sentence, which is a classifier's judgement, and a classifier can miss (named gaps).

  • Only what the choice needs: the documented route a career took, told as what happened, never as a model to follow or a forecast for the child, and nothing about the person's health, family or life away from it.
  • Never voiced or performed. The Primer tells a story in its own voice and words; it never speaks as the person, and never quotes them beyond the reviewed source (D47).
  • No likeness. The films' grid of footballers is illustrative. No generated video shows a real person (D126; what a generated video may show), so a living person's story is never told in a picture of them; whether even the name may be shown is part of OI-30.
  • Pulled in one operation. A story whose source changes, or whose subject complains, comes out of every conversation at once through the kill switch (P5), and goes back only through review. Each cached script records the named people it mentions, found by the same name check D127 relies on, so a pull by person also pulls every cached script and video that names them.
  • By market. UK cases for a child in the UK and US ones in the US (D113), so each market needs its own reviewed stories, and a child whose market has none hears "not yet", since no live answer may tell one (D127; OA-27).

The films' figures and named players are the films' content, not facts in this vault, and this page tells no fact about the players and repeats none of the figures. How the bank holds figures and stories is P3's (P3), and so is who writes and reviews the stories of living people (P3).
