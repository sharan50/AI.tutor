What a parent sees

A parent sees what the sleep film depicts: the topics, each question as the catalogue question its entries answer, and the child's view as the read-back they accepted or corrected, with the switches beside them. There is no full conversation to show (D104); a declined or stopped question appears by its category, and a flagged exchange never appears (D48, D107).

Superseded in part for the Primer on 31 October 2026 by D104 and D107: summaries only, no full conversation to show, and a flagged exchange kept only until its review closes.

A parent sees an activity record: modules used, questions asked and the views the child recorded. A question the Primer declined, or one in a switched-off module, appears by its category, not its words. For a child under 13 the full conversation is also available on request. The whole of an exchange flagged for safeguarding, including the Primer's own reply, is withheld from the record and from any transcript and routed to a trained reviewer, as D26 requires. The child is told before they start, in words for their age, what a parent can see. Taken while OI-8 and OI-14 are open; it answers neither.

The parent's screen is on the verified adult's own phone or computer, never on the child's tablet (the tablet). The child is told what a parent can see, in words for their age, on the tablet's first screens, which they go through with the adult before the first question; the films do not show setup. This still differs from the Tutor's default, where a teenager's transcripts stay private (D16): a parent of a younger child sees more, and nobody sees a transcript, because none is kept.

The record can itself expose a child. A question about identity, belief or the body, in a household hostile to it, is not a safeguarding flag, yet D26's reasoning, that the parent is sometimes the risk, applies. A summary says less than a transcript and a category less than a summary, but neither removes the risk, since a category can say too much. It is RP4's quieter form, and how far the record should go, with what a child must be told, is Open OI-14.

The withholding rule cannot be allowed to fail, because a failure exposes a child to the person they were trying to tell about (RP4). The flag covers the whole exchange and is applied before anything is written to the parent's record, so that none of it appears there, on the parent's screen or anywhere reached through the parent's account; T-PRM-08 tests it (P5). On the child's own tablet a stopped or flagged exchange stays on the screen until the conversation closes, the child's own words leaving it at once (named gaps).

The record is not a safeguard. The Primer's own case is that most evenings nobody has the time (D114), so the design does not count on a parent reading the record: the design and the tests carry the protection, and the record is a window for a parent who looks.
