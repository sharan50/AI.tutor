Age bands, consent, and page 13's argument

The Children's code covers online services likely to be accessed by under-18s and sets five age ranges for age-appropriate design: 0 to 5, 6 to 9, 10 to 12, 13 to 15 and 16 to 17.

The Primer's band, 8 to 13 (D41), crosses three of those ranges, and its settings follow them rather than treating the band as one audience Decision D57. Since 31 October 2026 one topic is outside D57's rule for sensitive modules: choices in a child's own future, offered at every age in the band (D106).

How the band is set. From the child's age in years as the verified adult gives it when setting up the child's tablet, never a date of birth: a recorded departure from 05, which uses the school year for placement only, because school years do not map onto the code's ranges (D57). One tablet serves one child (D105), so the band is the tablet's child's and the wake screen asks nothing about who is holding it. A parent who gives an older age can open modules written for older children. The protection depends on that adult's word, the same trust 05 places in the school year, and it is recorded here as a limit, not solved.

Under UK GDPR Article 8, a child can consent to an online service of this kind from 13; below that, a holder of parental responsibility must give or authorise consent, with reasonable efforts to verify it. The text has since been amended; the current version was not checked.

The account holder is the parent, so consent starts from an adult. Which lawful basis applies, and what Article 8 then requires of the flow, speech included, is Open OI-13: the Tutor's OI-10 asked of a much younger user (05).

Page 13's argument for older users

Page 13 argued that the first Primer users should be older adolescents, who can say when they disagree, read their own transcript and opt out (13). The Primer departs from that, so each safeguard a teenager supplies has to be supplied another way.

What remains weaker is real. A nine-year-old resists a persuasive voice less well than a sixteen-year-old, and since D102 the voice is literal: a spoken reply cannot be reread. So the words are always on screen as well, and the press check, T-PRM-04, runs on spoken sessions; whether the youngest also need slower speech is for PM3 to show. At the younger end the design and the tests protect the child. A parent's oversight assumes a parent who reads the record, who is sometimes the risk (D26), and the Primer's own case is that most evenings nobody has the time (D114), so the record is not counted as a safeguard. The departure stands only with PM4's supervised, independently evaluated pilot as a condition of release.
