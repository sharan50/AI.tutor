Escalation and the safeguarding review, in the UK and the US

Triage comes first. Every turn is classified before any reply (P5, T-PRM-06), and a spoken turn is turned into text and triaged whole before any reply is spoken or any video is made; T-PRM-06's battery includes spoken input in every range. A question about the child's family, body, feelings or safety points them to a person they trust (above); a message about immediate safety, abuse, self-harm, exploitation or severe distress takes 05's three moves. Every such reply is shown, never spoken, and no "Keep going" follows it (D115); nothing is generated for the turn, neither a spoken answer nor a video. A stop, a safety reply, a decline or the close cancels every script and video still pending in the conversation, and the service names none of them to the tablet afterwards. T-PRM-12's battery includes a stop that arrives while a video is still being generated (P5). A flag, raised by triage or by the review at the close, pulls every script and video generated in the flagged exchange from the cache and sends them to the sample review. Triaging the whole turn first costs time, so T-PRM-14, redefined, measures the time to the spoken answer with triage and the checks inside it; the time to the first video is observed, and is Unknown (what a generated video may show).

In the UK, the moves are 05's, unchanged: acknowledge, signpost the staffed services 05 names, escalate, and tell the child a person will look at it (05).

In the US, the moves are the same and the services differ: the Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline and the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline. Who reviews a US escalation, and under what policy, is not designed, and the duties to report are Open OI-20.

AssumedCV-12, awaiting the owner's check

The safeguarding review is a constantly running process, or runs at the end of each conversation. Where a flagged exchange must be kept for the review, it is kept for a short while, reviewed and deleted.

DecisionD107, the owner's, 31 October 2026

D107 is applied in four parts. The owner did not remove the trained reviewer, so the design keeps one for flagged exchanges.

  1. As it happens: a safety check on every exchange, the triage above and the checks on every reply (P5).
  2. At the close: a fuller automated review of the whole conversation, before its words are deleted, which can flag what the live check missed. A conversation whose review at the close cannot run is held as a flagged exchange, so a trained reviewer may read a conversation in which nothing was flagged; once the reviewer clears it, the record is written as at any close and its words are deleted.
  3. The words of a flagged exchange, and only those, are kept for a trained reviewer (D26), who decides against a written policy whether it is safe to tell the parent, whether to tell them more, and whether and whom to inform, and are deleted when the review closes. How long a review may take is Unknown until the safeguarding policy sets it, and whether a US state's duty to report would require them to be kept longer is part of OI-20. The escalation record holds none of the child's words: only the category the reviewer assigned, the dates, the reviewer's decisions, on the notice to the parent among them, and whom they informed.
  4. The parent never sees them. Once the reviewer has judged it safe to tell this parent, the parent's record shows only a notice: a question was flagged for safety and a trained reviewer is handling it, with no words, no subject and no reply. Where the reviewer judges the parent may be the risk, not even the notice appears (D121, T-PRM-08; What a parent sees).

05's honest limit applies with more force. Next-business-day review is not adequate for an acute disclosure, which is why signposting comes first; and a signal that only the review at the close finds reaches a person no sooner than that. A question about death can turn into something about home in one message. So the Primer never presents itself as a place to tell things to: the parent's terms and the tablet's first screens say it is a tutor for questions, not a safeguarding service. It never asks what lies behind a question in the child's life: the step towards the child's real question (D110) asks about the idea, the child answers in their own words, and the answer passes triage like any other turn.

Since the tablet will be dedicated to Primer, we don't need to worry about it getting set up again.

DecisionD120, the owner's, 4 November 2026

The safeguarding count. Stops spread over several conversations are seen together only through the child's safeguarding count: the dates of their earlier stops and flags, and which each was, with no words and no subject (What the Primer holds). A parent never sees it, since it is kept apart from the parent's record, and neither a deletion request nor an account closure reaches it until the safeguarding policy's period ends. Whether that is lawful goes to counsel with Open OI-13 and OI-14, as for the flagged hold. When stops repeat, the review at the close flags the current exchange for the trained reviewer, who sees the dates of the earlier stops and flags. Two limits remain. The earlier stops' words are gone, deleted at their own closes or reviews (P5). And the count is not linked across setups: a tablet set up again starts a new count, as a new account does, a limit the owner accepts because the tablet is the Primer's alone (D120). A parent cannot delete the count, but a parent who is the risk and wipes the tablet resets it; linking the count across setups would mean tracking a family across accounts, and the limit stays among the named gaps.
