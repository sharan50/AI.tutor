# AI.tutor: the Foundation classroom brief

Commit this file at the repository root as `FOUNDATION_CLASSROOM_BRIEF.md`. It records
what the owner fixed on 29 October 2026 about Foundation, the idea for children of 4 to
7, and turns it into instructions a fresh session can execute. Everything binding is in
this file; nothing depends on conversation history.

`BUILD_BRIEF.md` remains the source instruction for the vault's rules, and
`FACETS_BRIEF.md` still governs the Primer and the shared pages. This brief governs
Foundation's six documents, F1 to F6, its film on the front page, and the register
entries it adds: decisions D89 to D100, counsel questions OI-28 and OI-29, assumptions
OA-48 to OA-52, and test T-FND-06.

## 1. What changed, in one paragraph

Foundation is no longer a screen that coaches a parent or teaching assistant through a
ten-minute session at home. It is a classroom instrument for the teacher, sold to
schools. A camera on a thin arm over every desk watches the page and the child's hand,
never the face; software reads what is written, whether it is right, where the working
went wrong and how the pencil is held; and the teacher's PC shows a quiet class view
that turns one desk's tile amber, with a plain description and a suggested next step,
when a child needs help. The child is shown nothing and told nothing. The old design is
replaced entirely, on the owner's instruction, and five decisions written for it are
superseded for Foundation.

## 2. The owner's decisions, 29 October 2026

Recorded in a new ledger section, `src/content/decision-ledger/057-foundation.html`,
`<h2 id="foundation">D89 to D100: Foundation in the classroom</h2>`, after the
Tutor's MVP and before the decisions not taken.

- **D89.** Foundation is a purpose-built tool that helps teachers understand how the
  children in their class are doing. It is sold to schools first, as a tool for the
  teacher, not to parents and not for home use. It runs in the background of a normal
  lesson, and the teacher taps nothing during it. It covers maths, verbal and
  non-verbal reasoning, and motor skills. It replaces the earlier design entirely, and
  supersedes D43 and D55 for Foundation.
- **D90.** The desk unit: on every desk, a thin white flexible arm rises from a hole at
  the edge furthest from the chair, curves over, and ends in a small camera angled down
  at the desk surface. It watches the page and the hand only, never the child's face.
  It has no screen, no sound and no light; nothing on the desk shows the child anything.
  The desk units and the teacher's PC are hardware in the room; Foundation itself is
  software on the school's servers or in the cloud. Supersedes D50's "no camera" for
  Foundation; D50 still governs the Primer.
- **D91.** What Foundation reads from the camera: what is written, character by
  character, as it is written; whether an answer is right, and where in the working a
  mistake happened; and how the pencil is held: which fingers are on it, the grip
  (tripod or fist), and the pressure. Supersedes D56's "the record holds what the adult
  tapped" for Foundation.
- **D92.** The teacher's PC: a desktop app on an ordinary Windows PC on the teacher's
  desk, showing a quiet class view with one tile per desk. When a child needs help, the
  tile turns bold and amber with the detail, and a small corner notice saying only
  "Foundation" appears, with a ping. The wording is plain and factual ("Molly: 4 x 6
  answered 16. No working shown."), and offers an initial triage: what the problem looks
  like and a suggested next step. Foundation keeps a term summary for each child, which
  the teacher refers to on that screen at parents' evening.
- **D93.** What it never does: talk to the child, show the child anything, or reward the
  child (no stars, streaks, badges or confetti, as D25 already requires); point a camera
  or a microphone at a face; or replace the teacher, who is always the one who acts.
- **D94.** Foundation does not go first. That is the owner's order, and it is not a
  judgement that Foundation is the lesser idea. D42's three reasons described the home
  product and are withdrawn with it.
- **D95.** The idea is named Foundation, as the film names it. The vault called it
  Foundations until 29 October 2026. Page addresses keep their old spelling, so that no
  link breaks.

## 3. Taken while writing, the owner's to keep or reverse

- **D96.** Recognition, not generation. The models that read the page and the hand
  recognise characters, marks and joints; none generates text. Every word the teacher
  reads is a template filled from what was read, or an entry from a reviewed catalogue
  of errors and next steps, chosen by rules; every answer is checked by computation,
  never by a model. This keeps D54's reason and supersedes its letter for Foundation.
- **D97.** Nothing seen is kept. Frames are processed as they arrive and discarded;
  no image, video or audio is stored or sent anywhere beyond the processing service. The
  arm is set at installation and then locked, and a frame in which a face appears is
  discarded by the face check before anything else reads it. The desk unit has no
  microphone.
- **D98.** What is held and never inferred. Foundation holds, for each child, what it
  read in each lesson and the term summary built from it, as the school's record, kept
  for the school year and deleted when the child leaves the class. It never infers or
  names a difficulty or condition, and never ranks a child against classmates in
  anything a parent sees. Keeps D56's second half.
- **D99.** A child whose parent objects is not watched: that desk's camera stays off,
  and its tile says so. The teacher can turn any desk's camera off from the class view
  at any time, and does for a child who asks. The school tells the class what the arm
  does, in words children of this age understand. Taken pending OI-28; it answers
  nothing about it.
- **D100.** Before a lesson the teacher tells Foundation what the class is working on:
  a task from Foundation's library, sums from the board, or free writing. During the
  lesson the teacher does nothing with it.

## 4. Register entries

**Counsel questions,** in `14-open-items/010-counsel.html`, marked new:

- **OI-28.** In England, what lawful basis lets a school, or Foundation for it, run a
  camera over every pupil's desk that reads their hand and their work but never their
  face; whether a hand's pose, a grip, handwriting or pressure, read this way, is
  biometric or special category data, and whether the check that detects a face only
  to discard the frame is itself such processing; whether the rules on processing pupils'
  biometric information in schools reach it; what the Children's code requires of
  transparency to children of 4 to 7 when the unit shows no light; and whether a DPIA
  is required before any classroom pilot.
- **OI-29.** For a US launch: which federal and state laws on student records,
  biometric identifiers and cameras in classrooms reach the desk unit, and what
  consent, notice and retention they require.

OI-13, OI-15, OI-19 and OI-20 still apply to Foundation and are reread against the new
design; OI-17 now concerns the Primer, and Foundation only if a microphone is ever
added. The lede and the open-items count follow. `CLAUDE.md`'s "OI-1 to OI-27" becomes
"OI-1 to OI-29".

**Assumptions,** in a new group, "Foundation in the classroom", replacing the group's
old rows where they tested the home design:

- **OA-48.** That one overhead camera can read the handwriting and working of children
  of 4 to 7, character by character, accurately enough for alerts a teacher trusts.
- **OA-49.** That grip, and pressure in particular, can be read reliably from one
  camera above the desk.
- **OA-50.** That teachers want alerts during a lesson and act on them, and that the
  number of alerts across a whole class stays manageable.
- **OA-51.** That schools will pay for hardware on every desk as well as the software,
  and that someone in the school holds that budget.
- **OA-52.** That parents and staff accept a camera on every desk when it sees the page
  and the hand and never the face.

OA-29, OA-30, OA-33 and OA-34 tested the home design and are withdrawn, listed by
number under the group with the reason. OA-28 stays as the owner's view; it now
supports D93's rule that the child is shown nothing. The pattern line's total and its
count of cheap assumptions are recomputed.

**Tests.** T-FND-01 to T-FND-05 are redefined for the new design, and T-FND-06 added:
never a face (01); nothing seen is kept (02); answers computed (03); reading accuracy
against a human reader (04); the teacher's alerts judged useful in an observed pilot
(05); nothing on the desk shows the child anything (06).

**Map.** The five Foundation nodes in `tools/depmap/graph.json` become the desk unit,
the page and hand reader, the error catalogue, the class record and the teacher's class
view; `gov.novoice` keeps its id and covers both ideas.

## 6. The film

The owner's narrated cut, 76 seconds at 720p, goes to `docs/media/foundation.mp4`, with
a poster frame of the desk unit. The card's caption says it is illustrative, made with
AI, and that nothing shown has been built. The film itself carries no AI label, which
the README's film rules ask for; the owner is told.

**The film's canon,** fixed for every future clip; not product facts:

- Miss Smith, Year 1 teacher: mid twenties, white British, hair tied back, light blue
  V-neck, beige cardigan with sleeves pushed up, mid-grey pencil skirt, kitten heels,
  wedding band, small round pendant.
- Molly, six: blonde pigtails, blue eyes, navy jumper over a white polo, grey pleated
  skirt.
- The class: ten to twelve children at a British state primary, mostly white British
  with one or two Black British, South Asian and East Asian children, all in navy
  jumpers and white polos.
- The whiteboard reads 2 x 2, 4 x 6, 5 x 3, 8 x 3 and 12 x 2; the wrong answer is
  4 x 6 = 16; the sentence is "i love my dog spike"; the column sum is 16 + 8 + 19 with
  a carried 1, where the units make 23, so the carry should be 2: the mistake in the
  working that Foundation points to. Parents at parents' evening are a white couple in their thirties, backs
  to the camera.
- The reader's view: a hand skeleton of 21 points with labelled fingers, boxes around
  the hand and the writing, a status line (ANALYSING, CHECKING ANSWER, HAND TRACKING,
  CHECKING WORKING, CHECKING WRITING) and a short verdict panel.
