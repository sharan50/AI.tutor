Foundations: safety, privacy and regulatory

Foundations holds less than either of the other ideas about the youngest children in the vault. The child never converses with it, has no account, and is never recorded, photographed or addressed; nothing in the product converses with anyone, and there is nowhere to type. The user is a verified adult, and the only data about the child is a reading record built from what that adult taps. That design removes most of the hard questions. It does not answer the ones that remain, and five of them are for counsel.

The position in one paragraph

Foundations is a service for adults about children. A verified adult holds the account and uses the product; the child is taught by that adult and has no relationship with the product at all: no account, no conversation, no microphone, no camera, no rewards and no notifications, and nothing to type into for the child or the adult. These pages work on the basis that the Children's code applies, because the service is about and in front of children of four to seven, among the youngest the code contemplates; that is the working position, not advice Assumed CV-06. The Online Safety Act position in 05 carries over with less to defend, because nothing any user creates reaches any other user, and the child creates nothing. What is held about the child is a reading record: which sounds and number ideas have been introduced and what the adult reported. The questions left open are which lawful basis that record rests on (OI-13), who controls it in a school (OI-19), what a US launch requires (OI-15), what duties to report suspected abuse would fall on the company there (OI-20), and, for a feature not being built, what listening to a child would engage (OI-17).

Age bands and consent

The Children's code covers online services likely to be accessed by under-18s and sets five age ranges for age-appropriate design, of which the youngest are 0 to 5 and 6 to 9.

AssumedCV-06, from secondary summaries; the code's text not yet read for this age

Foundations' band, four to seven (D41), spans both of those ranges. It applies one set of settings across the whole band, chosen for the younger range, because the child never uses the product directly and nothing is gained by graduating protections that a child never meets.

Who is verified, and who consents

  • The account holder is a verified adult, by the same method and the same third-party providers as the Tutor's Route A (05). We receive a result, never a document, image or biometric.
  • The child never self-declares and is never asked their age. There is no date-of-birth field. The adult states the child's stage, which is needed to place them in the sequence, not to gate anything.
  • Every child record carries the highest-privacy settings, whatever stage was entered.
  • In a school, staff authenticate through the school's identity provider and the school enrols pupils under its own duties, as 05 describes for Route B. See below.

Under UK GDPR Article 8, the age at which a child can consent to an information society service is 13. Below it, where consent is the basis, consent must be given or authorised by a holder of parental responsibility, with reasonable efforts to verify it. The text has since been amended and the current consolidated version was not checked.

AssumedCV-07, awaiting the owner's check

Every Foundations child is under 13. Which lawful basis applies to the reading record, and what the Article 8 threshold then requires of the sign-up flow, is the question put to counsel. This page specifies a flow in which the verified adult creates the child's record inside their own account; it does not claim that flow is sufficient.

No microphone at launch

Foundations collects no child's voice at launch. The adult is the ears: they hear the child read and tap what happened. Listening to a child read is a possible later feature, separately consented, with no audio kept.

Decision D5022 October 2026

Three reasons, any one of which would be enough.

  1. Accuracy. Automatic speech recognition makes more errors on children's speech than on adults', and more the younger the child; fine-tuning narrows the gap but has not closed it. Assumed CV-11. A machine that wrongly tells a five-year-old they misread a word risks discouraging a beginner, and the youngest are where it would be wrong most often.
  2. What a voice is in law. Whether a recording of a child reading, or anything derived from it, is biometric or special category data in the UK is unanswered (Open OI-17). In the US, the amended COPPA Rule brings voiceprints into the definition of personal information Assumed CV-08.
  3. It is not needed. D43 already puts an adult in the room, who can hear the child and see what they are pointing at.

The same reasoning rules out a camera. Foundations never sees the child's writing or their face. That is stated here as part of the same posture; it is not a separate decision because nothing in the design ever wanted a camera.

What it costs. The record is only as good as the adult's ears and honesty. A tired parent who taps "read" for a word the child guessed teaches the product something false, and the product cannot tell. That is accepted. The alternative is a product that listens to five-year-olds and is wrong about them most often.

The commitment is tested, not just stated: T-FND-05 is a blocking release check that no audio is captured, transmitted or stored by any part of the product (F3).

What is not held, and what is never inferred

  • No audio, images or video of any child (D50; T-FND-05).
  • No date of birth and no surname.
  • No free-text notes about the child (D56). A notes field is where an adult would record what they noticed about a child's health or home, and that is information Foundations has no business holding (F2).
  • No inference of a difficulty or condition (D56). Foundations never infers or suggests that a child has a reading, speech, hearing or developmental difficulty, and never ranks or compares a child with others. It follows 05's list of what is deliberately not inferred (05), extended to this age.

The record is deleted on request on the same terms as the Tutor's data, including the backup period that 05 states openly rather than hides (05).

Escalation

The adult is present in every session, and the product hears and sees nothing. So nearly everything that would trigger an escalation in the Tutor happens in front of an adult instead of in front of the product. That is the design working, and it has one hard edge.

  • A child who says something worrying during a session says it to the adult. The product does not hear it and does not pretend to. There is no chat and no free-text field anywhere in the product, for the child or for the adult: the explanations the adult can open between sessions are written in advance (F5). Nothing invites a disclosure, for the reason 05 gives: a product that invites disclosure and cannot answer it is worse than one that does not invite it (05).
  • Where the present adult is the risk, Foundations cannot detect it, and it must not claim to. This is the hard edge. It is stated in the terms the adult agrees to, so that nobody mistakes a reading product for a safeguarding service.
  • Help for the adult. The help pages point adults to staffed services: in the UK, those named in 05; in the US, the Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline and the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline Assumed CV-12.
  • Concerns about development. The help pages carry static advice, the same for every adult and never triggered by anything tapped: if the adult has any worries about the child's hearing, speech or development, the school, a GP or a health visitor is the place to raise them. The product never draws that conclusion from the record and never names a condition (D56).
  • Duties to report, in the US. Which duties to report suspected abuse would fall on the company there, on support staff who learn something from an adult, for example, or on anyone observing a pilot, is Open OI-20.
  • In a school, the school's designated safeguarding lead and the school's own policy apply, as for the Tutor's Route B.

Through schools

A route through schools has real advantages for Foundations: a teaching assistant is an adult already present and already working with children, the school knows its pupils, and the school's own phonics teaching settles the order question. It also brings the duties 05 describes for the Tutor's Route B, unexamined here as they are there.

  • Who controls the record in a school, the school or us, and on what terms, is Open OI-19, to be asked with OI-13. Nothing here assumes the answer.
  • The statutory school frame, Keeping Children Safe in Education, filtering and monitoring expectations and any supplier standard, has not been read against this design. OA-20 applies to Foundations as it does to the Tutor.
  • Processing in India. The objection 05 anticipates for the Tutor's school route applies here too (OA-14).
  • Age assurance largely dissolves, because staff authenticate through the school and the school enrols its pupils (05).

The US position

D51 puts the US alongside the UK where resources allow. Every Foundations child is under 13, so the US question for Foundations is a children's privacy question from the first user.

The amended COPPA Rule took effect in 2025, with most operators required to comply by April 2026. Verifiable parental consent applies to children under 13; the amendments add consent methods, require separate consent for disclosures not integral to the service, bring voiceprints into personal information, and require a written data retention policy.

AssumedCV-08, awaiting the owner's check
  • Because Foundations collects no audio at launch (D50), the voiceprint question does not arise until listening is considered, and then OI-17 and OI-15 must both be answered first.
  • The reading record is still a child's information collected through an online service, with the parent as account holder. What consent method, notices and retention that requires is a question for US counsel.
  • US states have begun regulating companion chatbots, with obligations aimed at minors Assumed CV-10. Nothing in Foundations converses, with the child or with the adult, so on our reading it is not a chatbot at all. That is reasoning, not advice, and OI-15 asks counsel whether a tutor that declines companionship by design falls outside those laws.
  • US schools bring their own student-privacy duties, which this vault has not examined.
  • Duties to report suspected abuse differ from state to state. Which would fall on the company is a question put to counsel, OI-20, not answered here.

What COPPA as amended requires of Foundations, which state laws add to it, and whether any companion-chatbot law reaches a product the child never talks to: unanswered, and not guessed at here.

For counselOI-15 and OI-20

Named gaps

  1. Five counsel questions are open and each could change this page: OI-13 (lawful basis and consent), OI-15 (the US), OI-17 (listening), OI-19 (who controls the record in a school) and OI-20 (duties to report in the US).
  2. The Children's code has been read for this age only through secondary summaries (CV-06). Reading the code's own text for the 0 to 5 and 6 to 9 ranges is an hour of work and belongs in FM1.
  3. No safeguarding policy for Foundations exists. The product cannot protect a child from the adult beside them, and the terms must say so plainly.
  4. No ethics protocol exists for any session with a child. The field research is with adults only and collects no child's data. Before FM2 or FM3 puts a single child in front of the product, a protocol must be written with: informed parental consent; the child's own assent, asked in words a young child understands; a way for the child to stop at any moment, without being asked why; vetting, to the standard required for work with children in the country where the pilot runs, of anyone from the company or its evaluators present with a child; a DPIA before FM3; a stated retention period for pilot records; and independent review. Until it exists, no session with a child happens (F6).
  5. Adults who do not read English easily are excluded by a guide that must be read during a session. Reading the guidance aloud to the adult is possible, since it is the adult's audio and not the child's, but it has not been designed, and whether it is enough is Unknown.
  6. US content, escalation services and school duties are unreviewed. A US launch of Foundations waits on them, not just on OI-15.
