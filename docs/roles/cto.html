Chief technology officer

You own the architecture and the style engine: the turn pipeline, the verifier that is the product, deterministic retrieval, the kill switch and the read path that makes it a bound rather than a promise, the data model, the vendor position and cost per session; and the parameter object, the style card renderer, the conformance tests and the seam where exemplar conditioning would attach. Nothing here has been built. What you are held to is the set of tests specified in these pages and not yet run, and the invariants those tests protect: a claim the verifier cannot ground does not reach a learner, every persona feature degrades to an archetype, and a vendor update passes the frozen battery before a learner sees it. You also own the architecture of the Primer and of Foundations, which reuse the Tutor's pipeline and add the Primer's attribution and contested-question checks and Foundations' decodability check. You also own the build guide for the Primer's demonstration and its specification for Claude Code, which bind the demo and nothing else. Before you change a mechanism, run the impact command; most of what you own is stated on more than one page.

Decisions

  • D10 Student-facing style control: a creator selects a preset, the student may move individual dimensions.
  • D17 No tone dimension in the weighted style set.
  • D18 The exemplar retriever supplies style context only; the verifier rejects any curricular claim supported solely by an exemplar.
  • D22 Model versions are pinned; a vendor update must pass the blocking regression battery before reaching a learner.
  • D23 T-ZERO-01: the full regression battery runs with the persona registry empty, as a blocking check on every release.
  • D27 The verifier runs on a different pinned model from the generator; the mathematics check runs on no model at all.
  • D28 Figures are rendered deterministically from a structured specification, never generated by an image model.
  • D29 The kill switch is rehearsed quarterly against a live archetype, with the elapsed time recorded.
  • D30 The conversation window is a bounded rolling summary, giving approximately constant cost per turn.
  • D45 The Primer is taught on its modules, not trained on them. Modules are retrieved when a turn needs them; no model is fine-tuned on them.
  • D54 Foundations has no generative model in front of the adult at all. Everything the adult reads, in a session or between sessions, was written, reviewed and checked in advance and is chosen by inspectable rules from the reading record; there is no free-text box anywhere. A model may help writers draft offline.
  • D61 A self-tutor is made only from a live capture in the product: a head-and-shoulders face scan and a voice sample read from prompted sentences, with a liveness check. No uploaded photograph, video or recording is accepted, and no full-body image is taken.
  • D63 The learner or the adult can delete the self-tutor at any moment. The deleting write clears the cached deletion flag, so rendering stops at the next turn, and the kill switch's sixty-second cache ceiling bounds it if the clearing fails; the samples and anything derived from them leave primary stores within seven days, rendered clips are never stored, and the vendor holds nothing under zero-retention terms (D24). A self-tutor is also deleted when the account closes, or after twelve months without a self-tutor session.
  • D64 The self-tutor speaks only text that has passed the gate. Speech and animation are rendered after the verifier, from the text shown on screen, which is always shown; a blocked turn falls back as text does. Every clip carries a visible label that it is synthetic, and provenance metadata. The self-tutor never claims to be the learner, never comments on their face or voice, and offers no filter or alteration of the likeness. A safeguarding reply, and any turn the safety classifier flags, is shown as text from the standard tutor, never in the learner's likeness.
  • D65 No model weights are trained or fine-tuned on a learner's face or voice. The likeness is conditioned at render time from the stored samples or embeddings derived from them, so deleting those deletes the likeness. A vendor that can clone only by fine-tuning is excluded.
  • D66 Every archetype is available in the self-tutor, with the learner's own adjustments (D10). A named educator's preset is available in it only where that creator has opted in by a new term in the licence, and the label says whose style it is without implying that the creator appears.
  • D69 No style dimension reaches the self-tutor's voice. The learner's own voice speaks the gated text at one neutral delivery under every preset; speaking rate, pitch and intonation are set by the voice rendering, never by the preset.
  • D71 The demo answers any question a tester asks, with no list of topics: flexibility over accuracy. Every answer, block and video says on screen that it is not checked. D44 is unchanged for the Primer itself.
  • D72 Testers can speak their question. The recording is transcribed, shown as text they can correct, and not kept by the demo. Only adults use it, so D50 is untouched; whether the Primer itself should ever take a child's voice is a separate decision that waits on OI-17.
  • D74 In the demo, each building block's video is generated live, from a prompt drafted in the same request that plans the blocks, and all three videos start as soon as the safety check passes, alongside the short answer.
  • D75 There is no "I already know this" control: every building block is offered in full.
  • D76 The demo's videos follow fixed rules: no words, numbers or people in the picture; the clip's own sound muted under the narration; one visual style; one clip per block, eight seconds at 720p in 16:9, looped; a cache by concept, so that a repeated question costs nothing; a daily limit of sixty new videos, enforced by the app; one retry, then a still picture with the narration; and the provider behind one interface.
  • D78 The demo's tools: Claude Opus 5.5 on Anthropic's API writes every word, the safety check included; Google's Gemini API makes the videos with Gemini Omni Flash, transcribes speech with Gemini 3.5 Transcribe and reads aloud with Gemini 3.8 Flash-Lite TTS; and Netlify hosts the app, its functions and its storage. The choice binds the demo only; the Primer's model vendor stays deliberately undecided.

Key blocks

Prose drifts. A paragraph describing how someone teaches captures register and loses structure, cannot be diffed between versions, cannot be tested for conformance, and has an uncomfortable relationship with the thing it describes. Twelve numbers can be audited, composed, adjusted one at a time, reproduced exactly, and are not a copy of anything.

Twelve dimensions, each an integer from 0 to 100. Decision The seven dimensions named in the brief are all present; five were added because the seven did not span the differences visible in real teaching, and one of the additions is flagged as low-weight for a reason given below.

Warmth, encouragement rate and register are deliberately excluded from the twelve. They are the easiest dimensions to implement, the easiest to perceive, and they fail admission test 2: they change how a session sounds without changing what the student is asked to do. A style engine built on them would demonstrate beautifully and teach identically, and the D14 return-rate instrumentation would then be measuring nothing. Register is carried in the style card as free text for naturalness, is not scored, and is not adjustable by the student.

What this makes harder: presets will feel less distinct on a first read than they would if tone varied, which raises the risk that T-STY-02 fails for a reason that is real but not the reason we care about. That risk is accepted and noted in where this design is weak.

Decision D1714 September 2026: no tone dimension in the weighted set

The model never sees the object. It sees a style card, a block of text generated from the object by a deterministic, versioned renderer.

The agreement threshold. Setting it needs pilot data from three raters scoring the same material, which does not exist. Writing a number here would be inventing one. The threshold is set at M3 in 11 Roadmap from the pilot, and the pilot is the first place the rubric meets a real educator. Cost of getting it wrong: set too loose, presets are noise wearing a name; set too tight, no creator is fittable and the wedge cannot ship.

Unknownrequired input

D10. Picking a creator selects a preset; the student may then move individual dimensions. The handle gets them in, and then they can turn it.

The licence gives the creator a continuing right to require a specific behaviour to be removed within 14 days. That right needs a mechanism, or it is a promise with nothing behind it. There are exactly three, in escalating order, and none requires a release:

The likeness carries no style. A self-tutor teaches from the same style object as any other tutor, and the learner's face and voice change only how its turns are presented. Style is still twelve numbers counted in a transcript, and the transcript is the same whether or not a self-tutor presents it (01).

A style that cannot be verified is a claim, not a feature. Four tests. None has been run.

One field, one renderer slot, one retriever. The seam is specified now so that exercising D6 is a configuration change plus a licence, not a rewrite under time pressure.

Four places, in descending order of how likely they are to matter.

The load-bearing property: the model never retrieves. The composer selects the permitted sources deterministically from the objective before generation begins. The model is given those sources and cannot reach outside them, so the set to check against is known in advance rather than reconstructed from whatever the model decided to look at.

CheckMechanismFailure action
a. Citation presenceDeterministic parse. Every sentence classified as a substantive curricular assertion must carry a citation to a node in the permitted set.Block. This is a format failure and is cheap to detect.
b. Claim supportEntailment check of the assertion against the cited node. A model call, on a pinned cheaper model, with the node and the assertion only.Block, and queue for human adjudication. Every automated failure is adjudicated, which is what makes T-GND-01 measurable rather than self-reported.
c. ScopeClassifier against the declared objective set.Block. The learner sees the refusal in 01.
d. MathematicsComputer algebra system on every symbolically checkable step. Deterministic, local, no model call.Block. A CAS disagreement is never overridden by the generator.

The verifier runs on a different pinned model from the generator, and check (d) runs on no model at all. A verifier that shares the generator's weights shares its errors, and the correlated-failure mode is exactly the one that produces a confident wrong answer that passes its own check. What it makes harder: two vendor relationships to qualify instead of one, two regression surfaces under D22, and a second cost line per turn.

Decision D2714 September 2026

Diagrams are produced by deterministic rendering from a structured figure specification stored in the item bank, not by an image model. A number line, an axis pair, a labelled triangle and a function plot are all describable as data, and rendering them is a template.

Decision D2814 September 2026

Practice papers and the provisional grade range are computed, not generated. Neither involves a model call, and that single property is what lets them inherit the item bank's validation instead of needing a verification story of their own.

The consequence worth stating: neither feature adds a cost line in 10, because neither consumes vendor tokens. They are close to free at the margin, which is unusual for the two features most visible to a paying parent.

The service levels are in 04. This is what makes them achievable, and the fifteen-minute figure is a consequence of exactly one design choice repeated in several places: nothing derived from a persona is cached for longer than sixty seconds, and nothing derived from a persona is ever baked into an artefact.

The kill switch is rehearsed quarterly against a live archetype and the elapsed time is recorded. An untested switch is a claim, and this one is a contractual commitment to a creator. The rehearsal is cheap because D11 guarantees the product keeps running with the preset disabled.

Decision D2914 September 2026

The self-tutor adds steps after the gate and changes nothing before it. The likeness is applied only to text that has already passed, so a renderer can fail to say a sentence but can never change one. Everything in this section follows from keeping the likeness downstream of the verifier (D64).

Bought, pinned, abstracted, and qualified before use. No specific vendor is named in this document, for a reason that is not evasion.

  1. Gate on contract terms first. D24: zero retention of prompts and outputs, no training on our data. A vendor failing this is disqualified before quality is considered.
  2. Run the frozen battery T-REG-01 on each candidate and measure ACC-2, ACC-3, ACC-4, the gate-fire rate, and T-STY-01 conformance.
  3. Measure cost per session on the same battery, which is the only honest way to get the number.
  4. Pin the winning version. D22: a vendor update is a change that must pass the battery before reaching a learner.

The conversation window is a rolling summary of bounded size, not the full session history. Every term in ctx_tokens is therefore bounded, which makes cost per turn approximately constant rather than growing with session length.

This matters more than it looks. With an unbounded window, cost per session grows roughly with the square of the number of turns, and the sessions that cost the most are precisely the long ones with a struggling student, which is the student we most want to keep. A pricing model built on average session cost would then be wrong in the worst direction. What it makes harder: the tutor's recall of early-session detail is lossy, and the summariser becomes a component that can itself be wrong; it sits inside the content plane and its output is not citable evidence.

Decision D3014 September 2026: bounded context, therefore constant cost per turn

What can be said now without inventing anything: the only cost lever we control is bounded context multiplied by turns, every other term is a price someone else sets, and the architecture is shaped to keep both factors bounded. The arithmetic that follows from this structure, and its consequences for pricing, is in 10, which identifies the variable that actually dominates and finds that it is not this one.

The row to watch is the first one. Deterministic retrieval is what makes the correctness promise enforceable, and it is also the thing that makes the product feel narrow next to a free chatbot that will cheerfully answer anything. That trade is the venture in miniature, it will show up as a retention problem before it shows up anywhere else, and 12 treats it accordingly.
