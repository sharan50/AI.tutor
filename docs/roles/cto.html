Chief technology officer

You own the architecture and the style engine: the turn pipeline, the verifier that is the product, deterministic retrieval, the kill switch and the read path that makes it a bound rather than a promise, the data model, vendor position and cost per session; and the parameter object, style card renderer, conformance tests and the seam where exemplar conditioning would attach. Nothing here has been built. You are held to the tests these pages specify, none yet run, and the invariants they protect: a claim the verifier cannot ground does not reach a learner, every persona feature degrades to an archetype, and a vendor update passes the frozen battery before a learner sees it. You also own the Primer's architecture, which reuses the Tutor's pipeline and adds its own checks, speech in and out, drawn explainers timed to the voice, summaries in place of records and its locked tablet, and Foundation's, with its page and hand reader. You also own the build guides and specifications of the Primer's demonstration and the Tutor's MVP, and the demo's mock-up, which bind those builds alone. Run the impact command before changing a mechanism; most of what you own is on several pages.

Decisions

  • D10 Student-facing style control: a creator selects a preset, the student may move individual dimensions.
  • D17 No tone dimension in the weighted style set.
  • D18 The exemplar retriever supplies style context only; the verifier rejects any curricular claim supported solely by an exemplar.
  • D22 Model versions are pinned; a vendor update must pass the blocking regression battery before reaching a learner.
  • D23 T-ZERO-01: the full regression battery runs with the persona registry empty, as a blocking check on every release.
  • D27 The verifier runs on a different pinned model from the generator; the mathematics check runs on no model at all.
  • D28 Figures are rendered deterministically from a structured specification, never generated by an image model.
  • D29 The kill switch is rehearsed quarterly against a live archetype, with the elapsed time recorded.
  • D30 The conversation window is a bounded rolling summary, giving approximately constant cost per turn.
  • D45 The Primer is taught on its modules, not trained on them. Modules are retrieved when a turn needs them; no model is fine-tuned on them.
  • D54 Foundation has no generative model in front of the adult at all. Everything the adult reads, in a session or between sessions, was written, reviewed and checked in advance and is chosen by inspectable rules from the reading record; there is no free-text box anywhere. A model may help writers draft offline.
  • D61 A self-tutor is made only from a live capture in the product: a head-and-shoulders face scan and a voice sample read from prompted sentences, with a liveness check. No uploaded photograph, video or recording is accepted, and no full-body image is taken.
  • D63 The learner or the adult can delete the self-tutor at any moment. The deleting write clears the cached deletion flag, so rendering stops at the next turn, and the kill switch's sixty-second cache ceiling bounds it if the clearing fails; the samples and anything derived from them leave primary stores within seven days, rendered clips are never stored, and the vendor holds nothing under zero-retention terms (D24). A self-tutor is also deleted when the account closes, or after twelve months without a self-tutor session.
  • D64 The self-tutor speaks only text that has passed the gate. Speech and animation are rendered after the verifier, from the text shown on screen, which is always shown; a blocked turn falls back as text does. Every clip carries a visible label that it is synthetic, and provenance metadata. The self-tutor never claims to be the learner, never comments on their face or voice, and offers no filter or alteration of the likeness. A safeguarding reply, and any turn the safety classifier flags, is shown as text from the standard tutor, never in the learner's likeness.
  • D65 No model weights are trained or fine-tuned on a learner's face or voice. The likeness is conditioned at render time from the stored samples or embeddings derived from them, so deleting those deletes the likeness. A vendor that can clone only by fine-tuning is excluded.
  • D66 Every archetype is available in the self-tutor, with the learner's own adjustments (D10). A named educator's preset is available in it only where that creator has opted in by a new term in the licence, and the label says whose style it is without implying that the creator appears.
  • D69 No style dimension reaches the self-tutor's voice. The learner's own voice speaks the gated text at one neutral delivery under every preset; speaking rate, pitch and intonation are set by the voice rendering, never by the preset.
  • D71 The demo answers any question a tester asks, with no list of topics: flexibility over accuracy. Every answer, block and video says on screen that it is not checked. D44 is unchanged for the Primer itself.
  • D72 Testers can speak their question. The recording is transcribed, shown as text they can correct, and not kept by the demo. Only adults use it, so D50 is untouched; whether the Primer itself should ever take a child's voice is a separate decision that waits on OI-17.
  • D74 In the demo, each building block's video is generated live, from a prompt drafted in the same request that plans the blocks, and all three videos start as soon as the plan is ready and the safety check has passed, while the short answer is still arriving.
  • D75 There is no "I already know this" control: every building block is offered in full.
  • D76 The demo's videos follow fixed rules: no words, numbers or people in the picture; the clip's own sound muted under the narration; one visual style; one clip per block, eight seconds at 720p in 16:9, looped; a cache by concept, so that a repeated question costs nothing; a daily limit of sixty video generations, retries included, enforced by the app; one retry for a failed clip and none for a slow one, then a still picture with the narration; and the provider behind one interface.
  • D78 The demo's tools: Claude Opus 5.5 on Anthropic's API writes every word, the safety check included, with Anthropic's refusal fallback turned on; Google's Gemini API makes the videos with Gemini Omni Flash, transcribes speech with Gemini 3.5 Transcribe and reads aloud with Gemini 3.8 Flash-Lite TTS; and Netlify hosts the app, its functions and its storage. The providers are used on their standard terms, without the zero retention that D24 makes a gate for the Primer's vendors: a departure for the demo alone, which no child uses. The choice binds the demo only; the Primer's model vendor stays deliberately undecided.
  • D83 The self-tutor's voice and face run on open models that the MVP hosts on rented GPUs, so no speech or animation vendor receives a likeness, and nothing is trained on one (D65).
  • D84 The harness is built before any build prompt: a CLAUDE.md of the design's invariants, the contracts between the planes, the vault's tests written as failing tests, a frozen battery, hooks that run the tests, six builder agents and a reviewer. Nothing merges while the tests of finished phases fail.
  • D86 The MVP's tools: Claude Opus 5.5 generates turns and classifies safety; Gemini 4 Argon checks entailment and scope, a different family as D27 requires; SymPy checks the mathematics; Chatterbox speaks and MuseTalk animates, with MediaPipe, a passive anti-spoofing model and Whisper checking liveness; and the app, its workers, the renderers and the likeness store run on Modal, with Postgres on Neon. The text models are used on their standard terms, a departure from D24 for the MVP alone, which no child uses. The choice binds the MVP only; the Tutor's model vendor stays deliberately undecided.
  • D88 Left out: payment; real age assurance, for which a simulated check stands in; real creators, for which one fictional licensed preset shows the machinery; Route B's school features; the US course; D29's quarterly rehearsal, for which one rehearsal stands in; and D30's rolling summary, because a whole session fits in the window.
  • D91 From the camera, Foundation reads what is written, character by character, as it is written; whether an answer is right, and where in the working a mistake happened; and how the pencil is held: which fingers are on it, the grip, tripod or fist, and the pressure.
  • D96 Recognition, not generation. The models that read the page and the hand recognise characters, marks and joints; none generates text. Every word the teacher reads is a template filled from what was read, or an entry from a reviewed catalogue of errors and next steps, chosen by rules; every answer is checked by computation, never by a model.
  • D111 Each answer is drawn as it is spoken: an explainer that builds on the screen in time with the voice. It is made in advance as part of a reviewed explanation, as D73 requires, and drawn by code from its reviewed specification, so that a child sees the same drawing every time and nothing is generated live by an image or video model.
  • D116 The tablet has no camera, and its microphone listens only while the child holds the talk key, with the screen showing plainly that it is listening: no wake word, and nothing listening in the background.

Open items

  • Open OI-3 What exact wording may be used to state which specification a course follows, without implying endorsement? We are proceeding ahead of this answer under decision D34 and the convention in 04, because a product that will not name the examination it prepares for cannot be sold. This is the only question in this register we have not waited for. Since 23 October 2026, OI-23 to OI-27 are not waited for either: the self-tutor proceeds ahead of them under D59. Since 31 October 2026, nor is OI-17 for the Primer, which proceeds ahead of it under D102.
  • Open OI-6 What is the minimum viable creator licence for name and likeness only, and what does the exemplar-conditioning upgrade under D6 add to it?
  • Open OI-7 Which entity contracts with the parent, and what does that do to consumer jurisdiction, UK representative obligations and VAT?
  • Open OI-9 New. Under what licence may the DfE-published subject content be used as the source for commercial item authoring, and does that use carry conditions?
  • Open OI-11 New. If a provisional grade range is shown to a teacher in a form that could inform a Foundation or Higher tier entry decision, does Article 22 become engaged, and what is then required?
  • Open OI-13 New. For the Primer and Foundation, whose users are almost all under 13 and whose account holder is a parent: which lawful basis applies to the child's data, and what does the UK GDPR Article 8 threshold require of the consent flow? Since 29 October 2026 Foundation is sold to schools and has no parent account; for it the question is the lawful basis for the school's record, and OI-28 asks it for the camera.
  • Open OI-14 New. Is the Primer's parent activity record (D48), with turns flagged for safeguarding withheld from the parent and routed to a trained reviewer, lawful and consistent with the Children's code, and what must the child be told about it?
  • Open OI-15 New. For a US launch of the Primer and Foundation: what does COPPA as amended in 2025 require (consent method, retention, notices), which state laws add to it, including laws on companion chatbots, and does a tutor that declines companionship by design fall outside the latter? Since 31 October 2026 the Primer to be assessed talks with a child in real time, in a voice, on a tablet of its own (D102, D105, D112), never says "I", never uses the child's name and never praises (D108), and remembers past questions and views only as summaries (D104).
  • Open OI-17 New. If either new idea ever listens to a child, the Primer to take spoken questions or, should a microphone ever be added, Foundation, is the recording or anything derived from it biometric or special category data under UK GDPR, or a voiceprint under amended COPPA; and does processing on the device and discarding the audio change the answer? We are proceeding ahead of this answer for the Primer under D102.
  • Open OI-23 New. Are a learner's face scan and voice sample, and anything derived from them, biometric or special category data under UK GDPR when they are used to present a tutor in the learner's likeness rather than to identify them? What lawful basis applies; can a learner of 14 to 17 give the consent, or must the verified adult, or both; what does the Children's code require of the feature; how does a parent's subject access request for a child's data meet D60, which keeps the likeness from everyone but the learner, including where the parent is the risk (D26); and does a school-controlled version change any of this?
  • Open OI-25 New. For a US launch (D1), which state laws on biometric identifiers and on minors' data apply to a learner's face and voice used this way, and what consent, notice, retention and deletion duties follow? With OI-21.
  • Open OI-26 New. What labelling and provenance duties apply to synthetic audio and video of a real person shown only to that person: under India's IT Rules 2026, the operating company being in India, and in the UK and the US?
  • Open OI-28 New. In England, what lawful basis lets a school, or Foundation for it, run a camera over every pupil's desk that reads their hand and their work but never their face? Is a hand's pose, a grip, handwriting or pressure, read this way and kept only as text, biometric or special category data, and is the check that detects a face only to discard the frame itself such processing; do the rules on processing pupils' biometric information in schools reach it; is an objection enough, or is consent needed; what does the Children's code require of transparency to children of 4 to 7 when the unit shows no light; and is a DPIA required before any classroom pilot?
  • Open OI-30 New. What duties apply when content for children of 8 to 13 tells the documented stories of named living people, such as footballers, and shows their names: privacy and defamation law, any rights in a person's name or image, and the Children's code, in the UK and the US?
  • Open OI-31 New. What does a connected tablet sold to families as a child's learning device have to meet before sale, in the UK and the US: product safety law for children's products, the security rules for connectable consumer products, radio equipment rules, and what a children's product needs in testing and certification?
  • Open OI-32 New. Does reasoning with a child about choices in their own future, offered by default from age 8, engage duties beyond OI-16's: the Children's code's best interests and detrimental use standards, consumer protection law on advice to children, or any US state law on AI and minors?
  • Assumed OA-39 New. That live capture with a liveness check stops a self-tutor being made of anyone but the learner often enough. A person physically present at the camera can still be captured in the learner's place.
  • Assumed OA-32 New. That the explanation bank can answer most of the questions a child in a band actually asks, at launch. Since 31 October 2026 an answer needs its drawn explainer as well as its words (D111), so an entry counts only with both (OA-59).
  • Assumed OA-54 New. That parents will buy a dedicated tablet for one child, at a price that covers it, rather than expect an app on a tablet they own.
  • Assumed OA-55 New. That children of 8 to 13 speak to the Primer readily, and that recognising their speech is accurate enough that the conversation does not stall. Speech recognition makes more errors on children's speech, and more the younger the child, Assumed (CV-11).
  • Assumed OA-56 New. That a summary keeps a child's position faithfully enough that the child accepts the read-back and a parent understands it.
  • Assumed OA-57 New. That parents accept a Primer that reasons with their child about choices in the child's own future, on unless they turn it off.
  • Assumed OA-59 New. That a drawn explainer can be specified and reviewed at the rate the catalogue needs.
  • Assumed OA-60 New. That parents will let the Primer hear their child, consenting to speech when they set up the tablet, as OA-40 asks of the self-tutor's face and voice.
  • Assumed OA-48 New. That one camera above the desk can read the handwriting and working of children of 4 to 7, character by character, accurately enough for alerts a teacher trusts.
  • Assumed OA-49 New. That grip, and pressure in particular, can be read reliably from one camera above the desk.

What binds you

  • D5 (ceo) Personas pass a student usefulness test, not a fan's ear test. Name and likeness only; no creator material ingested. via gov.licence, proc.creator
  • D9 (ceo) Cloning a creator's voice, or anyone's but the learner's own, is off the launch path; a creator's voice is a separate opt-in limb of the licence. Amended by the owner on 23 October 2026. via gov.licence, gov.manner, gov.speech
  • D12 (ceo) Clean-room content. Original items written to the published specification, examiner-validated. No exam board material enters the system. via db.items
  • D15 (ceo) The unaided check is scored on first commit, with no hints available. via ui.check
  • D16 (ceo) The parent does not see session transcripts by default. via db.turns, svc.reporting, ui.parent
  • D19 (coo) Launch alignment is Pearson Edexcel. Explicitly provisional. via ext.board
  • D21 (coo) Groundedness is reported per session, not per turn; and no "it does not make things up" claim until the per-turn floor reaches 99.9%. via gov.floors, gov.outcome, gov.p2, ui.refusal
  • D24 (counsel) Zero retention of prompts and outputs, and no training on our data, as a model vendor procurement gate. via gov.zeroretention
  • D25 (counsel) No engagement objective anywhere in the system. No streaks, badges, loss aversion or return notifications. via gov.notcompanion, ui.session
  • D26 (counsel) A safeguarding disclosure is not automatically reported to the parent. via proc.primerreview, svc.escalation, ui.activity, ui.parent
  • D34 (counsel) Name the board in public copy, under a fixed convention, ahead of counsel's answer on OI-3. via ext.board, gov.outcome
  • D35 (counsel) Ship a provisional grade range, and gate nothing on it. via ui.grade
  • D36 (coo) Every mark scheme in the product is named "CINE mark scheme". via gov.outcome, ui.paper
  • D44 (coo) Every factual statement the Primer makes cites a reviewed explanation, and every philosophical position it presents cites a reviewed inquiry pack that names the thinker or tradition. A question neither can answer gets an honest "not yet" and becomes a request for new content. via db.explanations, db.packs, db.primersources, gov.figures, proc.primermeasure, ui.primer
  • D46 (counsel) The Primer's subjects come in modules the company writes and reviews. Parents switch modules on and off; modules on sensitive subjects start switched off; each module is written for an age band and offered only within it. Parents cannot add content or views; they may send ideas and requests, which go to the company's review and are never shown to anyone else. via ui.modules
  • D47 (ceo) The Primer names methods, never people. via db.packs, gov.manner, ui.answer, ui.primer
  • D48 (counsel) A parent sees an activity record of their child's Primer use: modules used, questions asked and the views the child recorded, except that a question the Primer declined, or one in a switched-off module, appears by its category, not its words. For a child under 13 the full conversation is also available to the parent on request. An exchange flagged for safeguarding, the Primer's own reply included, is withheld from the record and from any transcript and routed to a trained reviewer, as D26 requires. Taken while OI-8 and OI-14 are open; it answers neither. via ui.activity, ui.different
  • D49 (counsel) The Primer remembers ideas, not lives: the questions a child asked, the views they recorded and the modules they used, and nothing about their family, friends or feelings beyond the session. via db.ideas
  • D50 (counsel) Neither the Primer nor Foundation collects a child's voice or image at launch. via gov.novoice
  • D57 (counsel) Within its band, the Primer's settings follow the Children's code's age ranges: 8 and 9, 10 to 12, and 13. Modules on sensitive subjects are written for 10 and over, so a child of 8 or 9 is offered none. The range is set from the child's age in years as the verified adult gives it, never a date of birth. via ui.modules
  • D59 (ceo) The Tutor launches with a self-tutor: a learner may choose to be taught by a tutor with their own face and voice, in any style the product offers (D66). It proceeds ahead of counsel's answers on OI-23 to OI-27, and is withdrawn in a market only if counsel advises in writing that no form of it the design allows may launch there; a conditional answer changes the design instead of stopping it. via ui.selftutor
  • D60 (counsel) The self-tutor is the learner's alone. It plays only in the learner's own session, behind a passcode the learner sets when it is made; no surface the product gives a parent, school, creator or anyone else shows or plays it; nothing it renders can be shared, exported or downloaded; and the parent report shows the same progress for self-tutor sessions as for any other, never the likeness. via ui.selftutor
  • D62 (counsel) Before capture, the verified adult and the learner each consent, separately and explicitly, after being told in plain words what is kept, that the product shows it to no one else, and how to delete it. Either can withdraw at any time, which deletes the self-tutor. It is off by default. via ui.selfsetup
  • D67 (ceo) At launch the self-tutor is offered only to families buying directly (Route A). Through schools (Route B) it is off until counsel has answered OI-23 for a school as controller. via ui.selftutor
  • D68 (ceo) In the experiment D14 sets up, the self-tutor is a second factor, not a third arm: cohorts are given named presets or archetypes only, as D33 randomises availability, and separately the self-tutor on offer or not. D14's comparison stays causal, read with the interaction between the factors pre-registered, because D66 offers only opted-in creators' presets inside the self-tutor. T-SLF-01 measures the self-tutor's effect. via ui.selftutor
  • D90 (ceo) On every desk, a thin white flexible arm rises from a hole at the edge furthest from the chair, curves over, and ends in a small camera angled down at the desk surface. It watches the page and the hand only, never the child's face. It has no screen, no sound and no light; nothing on the desk shows the child anything. The desk units and the teacher's PC are hardware in the room; Foundation itself is software on the school's servers or in the cloud. via ui.desk
  • D93 (ceo) Foundation never talks to the child, never shows the child anything and never rewards the child: no stars, streaks, badges or confetti. It never points a camera or a microphone at a face. It never replaces the teacher, who is always the one who acts. via gov.novoice
  • D97 (counsel) Nothing seen is kept. Frames are processed as they arrive and discarded; no image, video or audio is stored or sent anywhere beyond the processing service. The arm is set at installation and then locked, and a frame in which a face appears is discarded by the face check before anything else reads it. The desk unit has no microphone. via gov.novoice
  • D98 (counsel) Foundation holds, for each child, what it read in each lesson and the term summary built from it, as the school's record, kept for the school year and deleted when the child leaves the class. It never infers or names a difficulty or condition, and never ranks a child against classmates in anything a parent sees. via db.classrecord
  • D99 (counsel) A child whose parent objects is not watched: that desk's camera stays off, and its tile says so. The teacher can turn any desk's camera off from the class view at any time, and does for a child who asks. The school tells the class what the arm does, in words children of this age understand. via ui.desk
  • D101 (ceo) The Primer is the Primer the owner's three films show. Its pages describe it, and where a decision here departs from a film, the page says so and why. The MVP demonstration (D70 to D79) is unchanged, and the pages say where it differs. via ui.primer, ui.worked
  • D102 (counsel) Children speak or type their questions from launch, as Version C shows. Their speech becomes text at once and the audio is thrown away. The Primer goes ahead of counsel's answer on whether a child's voice is biometric data (OI-17), as the self-tutor does on its own questions, and is withdrawn only if counsel's answer is a categoric no. via db.primersources, gov.novoice, gov.speech, proc.primermilestones, ui.different, ui.primer
  • D103 (ceo) Every exchange closes with "Leave it there, or keep going?", and "Keep going" continues within the time cap. via ui.primer
  • D104 (counsel) The Primer remembers a child's past questions and views from one session to the next. Key blocks

Prose drifts. A paragraph describing how someone teaches captures register and loses structure, cannot be diffed between versions, cannot be tested for conformance, and has an uncomfortable relationship with the thing it describes. Twelve numbers can be audited, composed, adjusted one at a time, reproduced exactly, and are not a copy of anything.

Twelve dimensions, each an integer from 0 to 100. Decision The seven dimensions named in the brief are all present; five were added because the seven did not span the differences visible in real teaching, and one of the additions is flagged as low-weight for a reason given below.

Warmth, encouragement rate and register are deliberately excluded from the twelve. They are the easiest dimensions to implement, the easiest to perceive, and they fail admission test 2: they change how a session sounds without changing what the student is asked to do. A style engine built on them would demonstrate beautifully and teach identically, and the D14 return-rate instrumentation would then be measuring nothing. Register is carried in the style card as free text for naturalness, is not scored, and is not adjustable by the student.

What this makes harder: presets will feel less distinct on a first read than they would if tone varied, which raises the risk that T-STY-02 fails for a reason that is real but not the reason we care about. That risk is accepted and noted in where this design is weak.

Decision D1714 September 2026: no tone dimension in the weighted set

The model never sees the object. It sees a style card, a block of text generated from the object by a deterministic, versioned renderer.

The agreement threshold. Setting it needs pilot data from three raters scoring the same material, which does not exist. Writing a number here would be inventing one. The threshold is set at M3 in 11 Roadmap from the pilot, and the pilot is the first place the rubric meets a real educator. Cost of getting it wrong: set too loose, presets are noise wearing a name; set too tight, no creator is fittable and the wedge cannot ship.

The load-bearing property: the model never retrieves. The composer selects the permitted sources deterministically from the objective before generation begins. The model is given those sources and cannot reach outside them, so the set to check against is known in advance rather than reconstructed from whatever the model decided to look at.

The verifier runs on a different pinned model from the generator, and check (d) runs on no model at all. A verifier that shares the generator's weights shares its errors, and the correlated-failure mode is exactly the one that produces a confident wrong answer that passes its own check. What it makes harder: two vendor relationships to qualify instead of one, two regression surfaces under D22, and a second cost line per turn.

Decision D2714 September 2026

Diagrams are produced by deterministic rendering from a structured figure specification stored in the item bank, not by an image model. A number line, an axis pair, a labelled triangle and a function plot are all describable as data, and rendering them is a template.

Decision D2814 September 2026

Practice papers and the provisional grade range are computed, not generated. Neither involves a model call, and that single property is what lets them inherit the item bank's validation instead of needing a verification story of their own.

The consequence worth stating: neither feature adds a cost line in 10, because neither consumes vendor tokens. They are close to free at the margin, which is unusual for the two features most visible to a paying parent.

The service levels are in 04. This is what makes them achievable, and the fifteen-minute figure is a consequence of exactly one design choice repeated in several places: nothing derived from a persona is cached for longer than sixty seconds, and nothing derived from a persona is ever baked into an artefact.

The kill switch is rehearsed quarterly against a live archetype and the elapsed time is recorded. An untested switch is a claim, and this one is a contractual commitment to a creator. The rehearsal is cheap because D11 guarantees the product keeps running with the preset disabled.

Decision D2914 September 2026

The self-tutor adds steps after the gate and changes nothing before it. The likeness is applied only to text that has already passed, so a renderer can fail to say a sentence but can never change one. Everything in this section follows from keeping the likeness downstream of the verifier (D64).

Bought, pinned, abstracted, and qualified before use. No specific vendor is named in this document, for a reason that is not evasion.

  1. Gate on contract terms first. D24: zero retention of prompts and outputs, no training on our data. A vendor failing this is disqualified before quality is considered.
  2. Run the frozen battery T-REG-01 on each candidate and measure ACC-2, ACC-3, ACC-4, the gate-fire rate, and T-STY-01 conformance.
  3. Measure cost per session on the same battery, which is the only honest way to get the number.
  4. Pin the winning version. D22: a vendor update is a change that must pass the battery before reaching a learner.

The conversation window is a rolling summary of bounded size, not the full session history. Every term in ctx_tokens is therefore bounded, which makes cost per turn approximately constant rather than growing with session length.

This matters more than it looks. With an unbounded window, cost per session grows roughly with the square of the number of turns, and the sessions that cost the most are precisely the long ones with a struggling student, which is the student we most want to keep. A pricing model built on average session cost would then be wrong in the worst direction. What it makes harder: the tutor's recall of early-session detail is lossy, and the summariser becomes a component that can itself be wrong; it sits inside the content plane and its output is not citable evidence.

Decision D3014 September 2026: bounded context, therefore constant cost per turn

What can be said now without inventing anything: the only cost lever we control is bounded context multiplied by turns, every other term is a price someone else sets, and the architecture is shaped to keep both factors bounded. The arithmetic that follows from this structure, and its consequences for pricing, is in 10, which identifies the variable that actually dominates and finds that it is not this one.

The row to watch is the first one. Deterministic retrieval is what makes the correctness promise enforceable, and it is also the thing that makes the product feel narrow next to a free chatbot that will cheerfully answer anything. That trade is the venture in miniature, it will show up as a retention problem before it shows up anywhere else, and 12 treats it accordingly.
