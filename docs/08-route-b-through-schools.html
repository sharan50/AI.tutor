The budget line is shrinking, first

The honest opening for this route is that the money is moving the wrong way, and it has been for three years.

20% of pupils received one-to-one or small-group tutoring organised by their school, down from 22% in 2023. Separate 2025 polling found 58% of schools had reduced their tutoring offer year on year.

VerifiedSutton Trust, Private Tutoring 2026, February 2026

That 58% figure is the central fact of this route and it should not be softened. It says that the majority of prospective customers actively cut spending on the thing being sold, in the year before the sale. A pitch that treats schools as an underserved market with latent demand is contradicted by the only verified evidence available.

Two readings are available and they lead to different strategies.

The pessimistic reading, which is the default

Tutoring provision in schools was inflated by catch-up funding and is now reverting. The budget line is closing. Selling into a closing line means competing for a shrinking pot against incumbents with existing relationships, and Verified against Oak National Academy's Aila, which is state-backed and free.

The optimistic reading, which needs evidence we do not have

Schools cut tutoring because human tutoring at prevailing rates is unaffordable at scale, not because the need disappeared. A product at a fraction of the per-pupil cost addresses the same need within the reduced budget. Assumption This is plausible and entirely unevidenced; the cut may equally reflect the funding ending and the need going unmet.

Which reading holds is answerable by asking about thirty schools, and it is a named research task at M1 in 11 Roadmap. Until it is answered, Route B rests on an assumption doing a great deal of work.

Pricing and unit economics

No price is set, for the same reason as Route A: nothing has been tested. The structural differences from Route A are what can be stated.

  • Per-pupil per-year, or a cohort licence, rather than a monthly consumer subscription. Billing annually against a purchase order removes the monthly cancellation decision that Route A has to survive every month.
  • CAC amortises across a cohort. One sale covering a Year 11 maths cohort spreads acquisition cost across many learners; Route A pays CAC per household. This is Route B's strongest economic argument.
  • Seasonality is annual and contractual, not behavioural. There is still a renewal cliff, but it is a known date on a contract rather than a monthly churn decision, and it is negotiable in a way a parent's cancellation is not.
  • Revenue per learner is almost certainly lower. A school buying for a cohort will not pay per pupil what a motivated parent pays per child. Whether the volume compensates is the open question and it depends on numbers nobody has.
  • Sales cost is real and is not CAC in the consumer sense. Field sales into schools is a salaried cost with a long cycle, and it appears before any revenue does. In a cash-constrained venture this is the difference between the two routes that matters most, and it is the one 09 weighs.

Prevailing per-pupil pricing for comparable school software, typical departmental budget for a single subject, and sales cycle length from first contact to purchase order. None is in the verified findings. All three are obtainable by talking to schools, and all three are needed before Route B can be costed at all.

Data and consent model

This is where Route B is genuinely, structurally better, and the advantage is not marginal.

QuestionRoute B answerCompare Route A
Who is the controller?The school. We are a processor acting on its instructions.We are the controller and carry everything.
Age assuranceLargely dissolves. The school knows who its pupils are and enrols them through its own identity provider. There is no verification cost per learner and no provider to procure.Open item OI-5, an unknown per-account cost, and the specific failure the ICO fined Reddit £14.47m for.

SafeguardingRoutes to the school's designated safeguarding lead, under a policy that already exists and is better than anything we would invent.Routes to our own reviewer, next business day, which 05 states plainly is not adequate for an acute disclosure.

What Route B takes on in exchange: a data processing agreement per school or per trust, supplier due-diligence questionnaires that are long and repeated, security review by people whose job is to find reasons to say no, and sub-processor disclosure obligations that make the vendor choices in 06 visible to every customer.

That processing UK pupil data in India will be raised as an objection in school due diligence, independently of whether the transfer mechanism is legally sound. The transfer question is a named gap in 05; this is the separate commercial point that a correct answer may still lose the sale. Unchecked, and it interacts with D13 and OI-7.

Distribution motion

  • Free pilot, cohort-sized, evidence-generating. A single department, one strand, one term, with the outcome measurement designed in from the start rather than bolted on when a case study is needed.
  • Sell on the evidence the pilot produces, not on the demonstration. Schools have seen demonstrations.
  • Trust-level expansion after two or three school-level proofs, because a trust sale is the only version of this route with economics worth having.
  • Aligned to the academic-year clock, which means the pilot must start in the autumn term to inform a summer purchasing decision. Assumption, per above, and it sets the whole calendar if true.

What we are selling against

Oak National Academy's Aila is a teacher lesson-planning assistant, reported to save teachers three to four hours per week, state-backed and free. Century Tech sells adaptive pathways. Third Space Learning's Skye is voice-based conversational maths tutoring.

VerifiedSeptember 2026

Aila is the harder problem of the three, and not because it competes on features. It is free, it is state-backed, and it addresses teacher workload, which is what a school leadership team is most motivated to fix. It occupies the attention available for "AI in this school" without consuming any budget. A paid pupil-facing product has to clear a bar that a free teacher-facing product has already lowered the urgency for.

That last row deserves emphasis because it is where the two routes genuinely conflict. Class-level features are natural in a school product and several of them are on the Online Safety Act trigger list. Route B's most obvious product roadmap runs directly into the scoping decision that keeps the service out of user-to-user duties, and that collision should be priced into any Route B plan rather than discovered during a feature review.

The reason Route B may matter more than it looks

The defensibility argument in 00 does not ultimately rest on the item bank, which is copyable by anyone with capital. It rests on outcome evidence, and outcome evidence is very hard to generate in Route A and natural in Route B.

A school pilot has the things a consumer cohort does not: a defined cohort, a comparison group, a common assessment taken by everyone on the same day, and a partner with an institutional interest in measuring honestly. Verified The comparators that carry weight in education, ASSISTments at 0.18 to 0.29 SD with an ESSA Tier 1 rating, and Carnegie Learning MATHia at 0.21 to 0.38 SD across more than 18,000 students in 147 schools, are all school-based evidence. That is the currency.

So there is a case, which 09 has to weigh honestly, that Route B is not an alternative to Route A but the evidence engine for it: run school pilots to generate the outcome evidence, and sell that evidence to parents. The cost of that position is that it requires funding two motions at once, which a seed-stage venture generally cannot do.

What breaks first

  1. The sales cycle is longer than the runway. Noticed as a pipeline that advances but does not close, typically one full academic year in. The most common way an education venture dies, and the annual procurement clock makes it worse.
  2. The 58% is the pessimistic reading. Noticed in the first thirty conversations, which is why those conversations come before anything is built.
  3. Data residency in India blocks due diligence, regardless of the legal merits. Noticed at the first security review, which is late in the cycle and after the sales cost has been incurred.
  4. Schools ask for class features that sit on the Online Safety Act trigger list, and saying no costs deals while saying yes changes the regulatory posture of the whole company.
  5. Per-pupil revenue is too low to cover a field sales motion, and the only viable version is trust-level, which we cannot reach cold. Noticed when the first school-level contract value is compared against the cost of winning it.
