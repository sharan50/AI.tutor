Three ideas for teaching children with AI, each accountable for what it says.

The Tutor is an exam tutor built in Bengaluru, for GCSE and A-level in the UK and for the US alongside it where resources allow. The student picks a named educator whose style they already recognise, and every substantive statement cites an examiner-validated item and is blocked if it cannot. That is a gate, not a guarantee: 03 states the residual rate that gets past it, and it is not negligible. The Primer is for the why-questions a child asks when nobody has the time to answer them properly: it takes them seriously and asks what the child thinks, and why. Foundation tells a teacher of four to seven year olds which child in the class needs help, and with what. Everything in this vault either supports one of them, prices it, or says honestly that we do not yet know.

UnprovenNo pilot, no users, no revenue as at Sep 2026

The Tutor's decision map

The Tutor is organised around one choice: can a named teaching style make a general-purpose model worth paying for?

How to read this

Every claim in these documents carries a marker, in the right margin or inline. Green was checked against a named source on a named date. Amber is a reasoned assumption that has not been confirmed. Red is a question for a lawyer, unresolved, with something depending on it. Violet is a number the plan needs and does not have, never filled with a plausible substitute. Plain is a decision taken here. If a page of argument carries no markers, it has not been written properly yet. Document 14 and the decision ledger are the registers those markers point to, so they carry few of their own.

ConventionSet by the acceptance bar

For the Tutor, documents 07 and 08 describe two routes to market that have not yet been chosen between. Document 09 is the one that makes the choice possible, and it recommends Route A on three conditions, two of which can be tested this quarter for almost nothing. Read 00, then 09, then whichever of 04 and 05 matches whoever you are.

Where to look first

None of these is a document you read straight through. They are what the documents point at, and where a sceptical reader should start.

Status

Everything open, unproven or waiting, on one page: the three conditions that decide the route, every counsel question, every assumption, every test and its state. Generated from the registers at build time, so it cannot drift from the documents.

Evidence register

Every external claim in the vault, with its source and the date it was checked. A claim that is not in there may not carry a green marker anywhere, which is the rule that stops the word "verified" drifting.

Decision ledger

Every decision, with what was rejected, what it cost, and what it makes harder later. Including the three that reverse earlier positions in this vault, recorded as reversals rather than quietly replaced.

Status of the whole thing

This is a design and planning record, not a product. There is no code, no pilot and no customer. No test described anywhere in this vault has been run, because there is nothing to run it against. The purpose is that a lawyer, an investor, a curriculum specialist or a co-founder can each open one chapter and act on it the same day.

Of those assumptions, twenty-two are resolvable by reading a published document or asking someone, rather than by building anything. They include the most load-bearing assumption about the Tutor, whether a parent prices it against a human tutor or against free software, and the Primer's, whether a parent will pay for a tablet and a service for one child, with no exam to prepare for. That is why milestone M1 and the Primer's first milestone contain almost no engineering, and why the field research guide exists.
