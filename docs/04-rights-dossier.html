Rights dossier

Two readers, one document. A UK media lawyer needs the copyright line, the licence architecture and the enforcement analysis. A creator needs to know what they are giving, what they are not, and how fast they can take it back. Both are here, and the creator's answer is that they can take it back in fifteen minutes, for any reason, with no notice period.

The exam boards

The hardest constraint in the venture, and the one that dictates the content strategy in 03. Read the AQA wording carefully: on its face the second limb is broader than a training prohibition.

AQA

AQA's published policy states that AQA does not permit the use of any AQA material, in any manner or for any purpose, in connection with the training of AI-powered tools or technologies; and that it will not allow the use of any AQA material in or on any edition of a third-party work where any part of that work is generated or produced using AI.

VerifiedAQA published copyright and intellectual property policy, checked September 2026

Read plainly, the second limb bars an AI tutoring product from containing AQA material at all, not merely from training on it. That is a wider prohibition than the AI-training clauses most publishers have adopted, and it is the reason the clean-room policy is absolute rather than a training-time restriction.

Does AQA's clause reach runtime retrieval, that is retrieval-grounded use at inference, as well as training? The wording is broad enough to cover both and this has not been tested. Unblocks: whether any board-derived material could ever sit in a retrieval index, and therefore whether the clean room is a strategy or merely the current one. Cost of getting it wrong: a product built on a retrieval index that has to be destroyed, and a claim we would struggle to defend.

For counselopen item OI-1

Pearson Edexcel

Pearson/Edexcel permits approved examination centres to reproduce past papers and mark schemes for practice, revision or teaching within the centre, on condition that students are not charged. A paid consumer product is outside that permission by construction.

Verifiedchecked September 2026

Note what this does and does not tell us. It is a permission granted to centres, not a general licence, and the no-charge condition excludes Route A by its own terms. It says nothing directly about AI. It is nonetheless a narrower and more legible constraint than AQA's, which is the basis of the provisional alignment decision D19 in 03.

OCR, WJEC and Eduqas

Whether OCR, WJEC and Eduqas take the same position as AQA has not been checked. It is assumed for planning that at least one of them has an AI clause of similar breadth, because AQA's is unlikely to be unique. That assumption has no evidential basis. Checking three published policies is a few hours of work and it is listed as a research task at M1 in 11 Roadmap, not as a counsel question, because it requires reading rather than advice.

The copyright line

Copyright protects expression, not facts and not curricula. Nobody owns the requirement that a student know Newton's second law, and GCSE and A-level subject content is set and published by the DfE. Verified Teaching to a published specification with wholly original items and explanations is not an act a board has standing to stop. Reproducing its papers, its mark schemes or its specification text is.

That line is the whole content strategy, so it is written as an operational rule rather than as a principle. The rule lives in 03 and is enforced by the provenance record on every item, the two-person rule, the per-item attestation, and the validator engagement terms that forbid a current examiner bringing board material into the work.

Naming a board: the convention, and a decision taken ahead of counsel

This is the one place in this vault where we act before counsel has answered. It is flagged as such rather than blended in, because the posture is different from everywhere else and a reader is entitled to see that.

Why waiting is not the neutral option. You cannot sell examination preparation without saying which examination. A product that will not name the board it prepares a student for is not a cautious version of this product; it is an unsellable one, because the parent's entire question is "is this the right one for my child's exam". Silence has a cost here, and it is not a smaller cost than the risk.

The basis we are proceeding on. Naming an exam board to describe what a product prepares a student for may be honest use under section 11(2) of the Trade Marks Act 1994, provided endorsement is not implied. That is counsel's likely starting point, not settled advice, and OI-3 remains open. Which limb of section 11(2) applies is itself part of the question, and nothing here should be read as settling it; the section has been amended and the choice between the descriptive limb and the limb covering use necessary to indicate intended purpose is exactly the sort of thing a guess would get wrong.

What the risk actually is, and it is not litigation

Per the war-game, a board's fastest move is not a claim form; it is a trade mark complaint to an app store, which acts on a complaint rather than on the merits and can remove a product in days. So the mitigation is operational, not argumentative:

  • Every board-naming string is centralised in one place in the codebase and in the marketing site, so all of them change in a single edit. This is an engineering requirement, not a content guideline.
  • Rollback trigger: on receipt of any objection from any board, board naming reverts to DfE-subject-content wording within 24 hours everywhere we control, before any substantive reply is sent. Argue afterwards, from a compliant position.
  • Notice has to arrive somewhere. The same published address that receives creator kill-switch notices receives board objections, it is monitored on the same basis, and the 24 hours runs from receipt. A rollback trigger with no address and no monitoring duty is not a trigger; the kill switch is careful about this and so is this.
  • The counter-notice pack in the war-game covers this case as well as the copyright case.

What the 24 hours does not reach, stated because "a single edit" is not true of all of it. App store listing copy sits in a third-party review queue on somebody else's timetable, which is the same point the war-game makes about removals. Served ad creative, cached and archived pages, affiliate and press copy, and a creator's own video about the product are all outside our control entirely.

And the rollback changes the label, not the thing: board_alignment in item metadata and the per-board paper structural specification in 03 are unaffected by any wording change. An objection that reaches the product rather than the copy is a content question, not a naming one, and it is not contained by this.

Limits of the containmentthe honest version of "revert in 24 hours"

The exposure is analysed here only as trade mark, and that is a gap. Consumer protection law and the advertising standards regime both bite on consumer education copy that names an examination board, and a complaint to an advertising regulator is cheaper and faster for a complainant than anything in the war-game.

Note on the fallback: reverting to "DfE subject content" wording assumes that wording is itself available, which depends on OI-9, also unanswered. The rollback is to a less exposed position, not to a proven one. Rejected: waiting for OI-3. It would have cost the ability to market the product at all, for an unknown number of months, to remove a risk that is contained rather than eliminated by waiting. What it makes harder: if counsel comes back against the convention, marketing copy, app store listings and the product's own course naming all change at once, and any paid acquisition running at that moment is wasted. OI-3 stays open and stays first in the counsel instruction.

What a UK persona right actually consists of

There is no statutory personality right in the UK. Protection of a creator's persona runs through: passing off for false endorsement (following Irvine v Talksport), trade marks, copyright in the underlying videos, performers' rights, and data protection insofar as a voice or image is personal data. This framing has not been confirmed by counsel for this fact pattern.

Assumptionnot verified, counsel's likely framing

Three consequences for the design, and they are why D5, D8 and D9 are shaped as they are.

  • False endorsement is the live risk, not copying. Under D5 we ingest nothing, so copyright in the underlying videos and performers' rights are largely not engaged. What remains is the risk that a consumer believes the creator endorses, writes or checks the tutoring. That risk is managed by the on-screen label in 01, by the attribution change when a student adjusts an axis, and by an express non-endorsement term in the licence.
  • A licence does not remove the risk, it relocates it. A signed creator cannot easily complain, but a consumer misled about endorsement is a different matter, and so is a creator whose licence has ended. Hence the kill switch SLA and the post-termination deletion terms.
  • Voice is categorically worse than name and image. Hence D9: voice cloning is off the launch path entirely and becomes a separate opt-in limb. Verified Virtual clones of real people were among the trigger incidents in Ofcom's open letter of 8 November 2024, and Verified Indian courts have restrained AI voice cloning directly (Arijit Singh, Bombay High Court, 2024).

That most creators the venture wants are US-resident, which brings US state right-of-publicity law into scope. If true, the licence needs a US-law limb and the enforcement analysis acquires a forum we have not analysed. Unchecked.

The kill switch, with a time to effect

Fifteen minutes to disabled, seven days to deleted, thirty-five days to gone from backups. Measured from receipt of a valid notice at a published address, not from when someone reads it.

Why this is a real kill switch and not a promise

Because of D8. No persona LoRAs, no fine-tuned weights, no adapters carrying creator style, ever. Retrieval and prompting only. Weights cannot be cleanly revoked, and a kill switch that requires retraining is not a kill switch; it is a project plan. The fifteen-minute figure is only credible because there is nothing to retrain, and any future proposal to fine-tune on persona data should be read as a proposal to delete this table.

Because of D11. The product runs with zero personas. Disabling every preset simultaneously degrades the product; it does not stop it. A kill switch that would take the service down is a switch nobody will pull.

What changes under D6 and D9

  • D6, exemplar conditioning: add invalidation of cached prompt fragments by T+15 minutes, and deletion of the licensed corpus and every derived index by T+7 days. The 15-minute disable figure survives; the deletion story becomes materially more complex and is the largest operational cost of D6.
  • D9, voice: a voice model is deleted on the same seven-day line, and because D8 forbids weights carrying creator style, there is no trained artefact to chase. If that ever ceases to be true, this entire section stops being accurate.

The exposure that comes from building in India

This runs the opposite way to how it is usually read. Building in India does not put the venture further from a creator's reach; on personality rights it puts it closer, because Indian personality-rights jurisprudence is currently the most aggressive in the world on AI likeness.

Anil Kapoor (Delhi High Court, 2023); Jackie Shroff (2024); Arijit Singh (Bombay High Court, 2024, AI voice cloning); Hrithik Roshan (CS(COMM) 1107/2025, Delhi High Court, 72-hour takedowns and domain suspensions); and Sonakshi Sinha v Character Technologies Inc & Ors, Delhi High Court, 20 March 2026, 2026 SCC OnLine Del 1177, restraining misuse of identity through AI chatbots, deepfakes and voice cloning.

VerifiedSeptember 2026

The Sonakshi Sinha decision is the one to read first: it concerns AI chatbots specifically, and it is recent. An operating company in Bengaluru running a persona-bearing AI tutor is squarely in the fact pattern that court has been willing to restrain, and the Delhi High Court has shown itself willing to order takedowns on 72-hour timelines and to suspend domains.

This does not mean the venture should not be built from India; the reasons for the Indian operating company are ordinary tax and capital reasons, per D13. It means the risk register should record that our own base of operations is a forum in which a well-advised aggrieved creator would be pleased to find us, and that the D5 design, licensing name and likeness only with an instant kill switch, is doing more work than it looks like it is.

The IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Amendment Rules 2026, G.S.R. 120(E), notified 10 February 2026 and in force 20 February 2026, define synthetically generated information, require prominent labelling and permanent provenance metadata that cannot be stripped, and compress takedown for flagged unlawful content to three hours and for non-consensual sexual imagery to two.

VerifiedVerified: labelling obligations that follow from building in India

The on-screen label in 01 and the provenance metadata requirement in 06 are therefore not only a passing-off mitigation; they are a compliance obligation arising from where the company is built. Why the offshore strategy was considered and dropped

Decision D13. The Indian operating company is retained for ordinary tax and capital reasons. The "fight it in India" rationale is dropped, because the theory runs the opposite way to how it was first read.

Three verified findings kill it, and each one is independently sufficient.

Section 44A, Code of Civil Procedure 1908. The United Kingdom is a notified reciprocating territory and the English High Courts are notified superior courts (Notification F.34-I/52-L, 1 March 1953). A UK superior court money decree is executable in India as if passed by an Indian District Court, without a fresh suit, subject only to the Section 13 conclusiveness tests. The Delhi High Court has enforced an ex parte English Commercial Court judgment for USD 47m on this basis.

VerifiedVerified: a UK judgment reaches you in India without a fresh suit

The Act reaches providers with links to the UK regardless of where they are established. Between 1 April 2025 and 30 June 2026 Ofcom imposed penalties totalling £6.1m on nine companies that were either not UK-based or did not state their jurisdiction. It has pursued business disruption measures, including a possible UK access block, against a non-compliant overseas provider fined £950,000.

VerifiedVerified: the Online Safety Act does not care where you are established

And third, the point made above: Indian personality-rights jurisprudence is the most aggressive in the world on AI likeness, and Sonakshi Sinha reached AI chatbots specifically in March 2026. An Indian base is not a refuge from a persona claim; it is a convenient forum for one.

So the offshore theory fails three ways at once. A UK money judgment follows you home under Section 44A. A UK regulator can disrupt the service without ever obtaining one, by reaching the ISPs, app stores and payment providers that the business actually depends on. And the jurisdiction chosen as a shield is the jurisdiction most willing to restrain exactly this product.

What remains true, and is the reason D13 keeps the entity: Bengaluru is a sound place to build from for cost, talent and capital reasons. Those reasons are honest and they are the only ones that should be given to an investor.

Zero-persona operation, proved rather than asserted

D11 is a hard requirement, and this is where the proof of it belongs, because it is a rights property before it is a product property. The claim is: no single unsigned contract can block launch, and no signed contract ending can stop the service.

What makes it true is structural rather than aspirational.

  1. Every preset is an archetype by construction. A licensed preset is an archetype's parameter vector with a name attached and, at most, twelve fitted values. Remove the name and the vector and an archetype remains. The two worked vectors in 02 are archetypes, not licensed presets, and they are the ones used throughout this repository.
  2. The catalogue ships archetypes on day one regardless. D14 requires named presets and unnamed archetypes to ship simultaneously, so the archetype path is not a fallback that has never been exercised; it is half the launch product and half the experiment.
  3. The fallback target is defined per preset. Every licensed preset names the archetype it degrades to. The kill switch has somewhere to send a live session at T+16 minutes because that target is a required field, not a runtime decision.
  4. No content depends on a persona. The item bank, the verifier and the accuracy floor sit entirely in the content plane. Personas live in the style plane. Every accuracy promise in 01 holds with zero personas signed, because none of them references a persona.
  5. The test exists. Decision D23 T-ZERO-01: the full regression battery T-REG-01 is run with the persona registry empty, on every release, as a blocking check. If the product cannot pass its own content tests with no personas configured, D11 has quietly stopped being true and the release does not ship. Status: specified, not run.

Point 5 is the difference between proving and asserting. Without a blocking test that runs with an empty registry, D11 degrades into a claim that was true on the day it was written, and the first persona-dependent code path added under delivery pressure would falsify it silently.
