Foundations: plan

Almost every number Foundations' plan needs is unknown, and the plan is built around finding them out cheaply before anything is built. Its first milestone is conversations and reading. Its last is a decision, written as a rule in advance, about whether to build Foundations at all; ending it there would be a legitimate outcome of this plan, not a failure of it.

Who might pay, and why that is unknown

Two possible payers, and the vault has no evidence about either. That is the honest starting point, and it is the second of the owner's reasons for not putting Foundations first.

Parents. The register's figures on what families spend on tutoring describe secondary pupils (00) and say nothing about children of four to seven. What families spend on early reading help is Unknown. The owner's view is that parents of young children feel less urgency to pay than parents of exam candidates, because school teaches early reading for free (OA-29). That is testable by asking, and it is flagged as cheap.

One hypothesis is worth testing alongside it (OA-34): that urgency, where it exists, concentrates where a child is falling behind the school's sequence, for example around the Year 1 phonics check (CV-09). If so, Foundations is a product for some families at some moments, not for every family all the time, and both its price and its message would follow from that.

HypothesisOA-34, not evidenced; relies on CV-09, awaiting the owner's check

Schools. A school could use Foundations for short sessions run by teaching assistants with children who need more practice than the class gives them. Who in a primary school would hold that budget, and whether it exists, is Unknown. The register's only school data concerns the shrinking of tutoring offers, at secondary level, and its cause is itself unevidenced (08, OA-17).

What the family already pays. Every session costs an adult's time. Any price is charged on top of that, which is one more reason Foundations may suit fewer families than its subject suggests (OA-30).

Markets. Foundations launches in the UK and the US together where resources allow, and in the UK first where they do not. India is not pursued for any idea sold direct to families while section 9(3) of its data protection law stands, the finding in the evidence register.

Decision D5122 October 2026

For Foundations, "the US together" carries more weight than for the Primer. A reading sequence is not portable the way a conversation about a why-question is: US schools' expectations for early reading are not in the register, and the pronunciation the decodability rule depends on differs between accents. A US launch therefore needs its own content review as well as US counsel (OI-15), and in a resource-constrained plan that makes the UK-first fallback the likely case for Foundations.

Two structural points survive the absence of numbers. First, the dominant costs are content, which is fixed and front-loaded, and the adult's time, which falls on the family; cost per session is the least important figure on the list, so engineering spent reducing it is low-leverage (F5). Second, the product ends when it succeeds. That caps what a family is worth to Foundations alone. Whether a family that finishes Foundations moves on to the Primer is the question of whether the three ideas are one product or three, which is deliberately not decided (D40, which idea goes first), and no plan here counts on it.

Before any milestone puts a child in a room with someone from the company. FM2 brings an observer into sessions with young children, and FM3 an observer and an independent assessor. Neither starts until: everyone from the company or its evaluators who is present with a child has been vetted to the standard required for work with children in the country where the pilot runs; the ethics protocol in F4 exists, with informed parental consent and the child's own assent, asked in words a young child understands; the child can stop at any moment, for any reason, without being asked why; a DPIA is complete before FM3; and the protocol states how long pilot records are kept and when they are deleted, a period that is Unknown until the protocol is written.

What would justify building it, and what would end it

The decision rule at FM4. Foundations is built beyond the prototype only if all four of these hold:

  1. Teachers and parents confirm that an adult-present product is wanted and workable for this age (FM1).
  2. Untrained adults can follow the guide and come back to it without being nudged (FM2, T-FND-04).
  3. Parents or schools show they would pay, with something stronger than a stated preference (FM1, then FM3).
  4. Whichever of the Tutor and the Primer goes first does not need the resources (D42).
Proposed rulefor the owner to adopt or amend before FM1 reports

What would end it, any one of: adults do not sit (RF1); nobody pays on either route (RF2); counsel's answers to OI-13 or OI-15 make even the reading record costly to hold; or the research read at FM1 shows that guided practice at home by untrained adults adds little to what the same time unguided would do.

What would reopen D42 instead. If the conversations and the reading at FM1 show the owner's view to be wrong, and children of four to seven do learn early reading well from a screen without an adult (OA-28), then the case for the order in YC's request Assumed CV-01 grows, and D42 should be revisited with a written reason. That outcome would not justify this design; it would call for a different one, aimed at the child, which these pages have deliberately not drawn.

If Foundations ends, the vault records why, in the ledger, as it records every decision. The work is not wasted: the reading record, the decodability rule and the adult's guide are small, and a school that wants them may still be the right customer for the Tutor or the Primer later. Ending Foundations on evidence is the plan working, not the plan failing.
