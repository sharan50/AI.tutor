Foundation: plan

Schools are the only customer, and nobody knows whether a school will pay for a camera on every desk. The plan finds that out first, by asking; then whether a camera above a desk can read a five-year-old's page at all, on a bench, before any unit goes near a classroom; then whether teachers use the alerts, in one observed pilot. It ends in a decision written as a rule in advance. Foundation does not go first (D94): its first milestone can run alongside the other ideas, and the rest wait for its turn.

Who pays, and why that is unknown

The school pays, if anyone does. Foundation is not sold to parents (D89), so the whole commercial case rests on a primary school, or the trust or authority behind it, buying hardware for every desk as well as the software. The vault has no evidence that any will.

Who in a school holds the budget is Unknown: the head, a multi-academy trust, a local authority, or a fund meant for pupils who need more help. Whether a primary school buys equipment of this kind at all, and how, is the first question of FM1 (Assumed OA-51). The register's only school data concerns tutoring offers at secondary level, whose shrinking has an unevidenced cause (08, OA-17). And a school's procurement may follow the academic year, so that a missed decision costs a year, as OA-12 assumes for the Tutor's route through schools.

What the school is buying. Not a teacher's time, which Foundation does not replace (D93), and not a promise that children will learn more, which these pages do not make (F2). It is buying a view of every page in the room during the lesson, and a term summary for parents' evening. Whether that is worth a budget is what the conversations at FM1 must find out.

What the school already pays. Fitting the units, a network that carries their frames, and, if Foundation runs on the school's servers, a machine to run it on (F5). All of it falls on the school before the first lesson.

Decision D5122 October 2026

For Foundation, "the US together" carries more weight than for the Primer. A camera over a pupil's desk meets US student records law, state biometric law and state rules on cameras in classrooms, none of which this vault has read (OI-29). The error catalogue's words would need a US review too, because US teachers name the same working differently and their grades are not England's years. In a resource-constrained plan that makes the UK-first fallback the likely case for Foundation.

Two structural points survive the absence of numbers. First, the dominant costs grow with every desk: hardware and processing, on top of a content cost that is fixed and front-loaded. Engineering that reduces processing per desk is therefore high-leverage, the reverse of the earlier design (F5). Second, the customer is the school, which keeps the units when a class moves up and a new class arrives. Success does not end the relationship, as it did when a child who could read had outgrown a home product. Whether a child who leaves Foundation's classes meets the Primer later is the question of whether the three ideas are one product or three, which is deliberately not decided (D40, which idea goes first), and no plan here counts on it.

Before any milestone collects a child's writing or hand, or runs a unit in a classroom. FM2 collects children's writing and hands, and FM3 puts units and an observer in a classroom. Neither starts until: everyone from the company or its evaluators who is present with a child has been vetted to the standard required for work with children in the country where it runs; the ethics protocol in F4 exists, with informed parental consent and the child's own assent, asked in words a young child understands; the child can stop at any moment, for any reason, without being asked why; a DPIA is complete; and the protocol states how long collected samples and pilot records are kept and when they are deleted, a period that is Unknown until the protocol is written.

What would justify building it, and what would end it

The decision rule at FM4. Foundation is built beyond the pilot only if all five of these hold:

  1. Counsel's answers to OI-28, and to OI-29 for the US, allow the desk unit at a cost a school can carry, and parents and staff accept it (FM1, FM3).
  2. The reader meets T-FND-04's threshold, written down before FM2.
  3. Teachers judge the alerts right and useful at T-FND-05's threshold, written down before FM3.
  4. Schools show they would pay for the units and the software, with something stronger than a stated preference (FM1, then FM3).
  5. Its turn has come: the idea that goes first does not need the resources (D94).
Proposed rulefor the owner to adopt or amend before FM1 reports

What would end it, any one of: counsel's answer to OI-28 makes the camera unlawful, or lawful only on terms few parents or schools accept (RF1); the reader cannot read the page (RF2); no school pays (RF3); or teachers in the pilot do not use the alerts (RF5).

What would not change the order. D94 gives no reason that evidence could overturn: Foundation does not go first because the owner says so, and the owner is the one who would change it. Good evidence at FM1 makes Foundation readier when its turn comes; it does not bring the turn forward.

If Foundation ends, the vault records why, in the ledger, as it records every decision. Ending it on evidence is the plan working, not the plan failing.
