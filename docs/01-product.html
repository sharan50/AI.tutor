The learner

A Year 11 student sitting GCSE Mathematics at Higher tier in the coming summer, who is not failing and is not excelling, who has a specific topic they have decided they are bad at, and who has between fifteen and thirty minutes of genuine attention available on a phone at some point between four in the afternoon and eleven at night.

That is a narrow description and it is meant to be. Every design choice in this repository is made for that person. Verified Year 11 is the peak year for private tutoring at 25% of pupils, with Year 10 next at 10% (Sutton Trust, Private Tutoring 2026, February 2026), so the narrowness costs less than it looks.

Three things follow from the description that are easy to miss.

  • They arrive with a topic, not a subject. The unit of demand is "I do not understand why the sign flips", not "help me with maths". The product is organised around objectives, not courses.
  • They are on a phone, vertically, possibly in bed. Diagrams must work at 360 CSS pixels wide. Sessions must survive a fifteen-minute interruption without losing state.
  • They did not buy it. The payer is the parent and the user is the child, and those two want measurably different things. That tension runs through the parent reporting section and through 05.

P2 is the promise that carries the business. Its below-floor behaviour is not a statistical tolerance; it is a hard runtime gate, and what the learner sees when the gate fires is shown below.

The shape of a session

A session is between fifteen and thirty minutes and has one objective. The objective is an item-bank node, not a topic heading.

  1. Pick up. The tutor states what the student was last working on and what it believes they can and cannot yet do, in one sentence, and asks whether that is still right. The student can overrule it.
  2. One objective. Chosen by the student, or proposed by the scheduler from the gaps in their model. Never more than one.
  3. Teach, in the chosen style. This is where the style engine operates and it is the whole of the next section.
  4. Unaided check. Two or three held-out items, no hints available, no tutor commentary until the student commits an answer. This is the anti-surface-fluency mechanism and it is deliberately unpleasant.
  5. Honest close. The tutor says what the check showed, including when the check showed the student has not got it, and schedules the objective for return rather than declaring success.

The unaided check is scored on first commit, unaided, and the student cannot ask for a hint during it. The temptation is to allow hints and score leniently, because that produces better-looking session outcomes and a happier student. Verified The reported caveat on AI tutoring is precisely that students who default to the AI rather than working a problem develop surface fluency that does not hold under examination conditions. A product that measures in-session success is measuring the thing that a surface-fluency failure would flatter. Recorded in the decision ledger.

Decision D1514 September 2026

Two things that sit outside the session

A session teaches one objective. Two features sit above that, and both are assembled from the same validated item bank rather than generated fresh, which is what makes them checkable.

Board-aligned practice papers

A practice paper is original items, assembled to the shape of the board's paper: the question count, the mark distribution, the command-word mix, the topic coverage and the time allowance. It is not a past paper, it does not contain any board material, and the clean-room rule in 03 is unchanged by it.

Every paper ships with a mark scheme. Ours, for our items, validated by our examiners. The phrase "mark scheme" is a term of art that sounds like it belongs to a board, so the product says "CINE mark scheme" everywhere the learner or a parent can see it. Assembly is deterministic and a paper is valid as a whole, not just item by item, which is a separate claim with a separate test (T-PAP-01 in 03).

The provisional grade range

A band, not a number, computed only from unaided-check performance on validated items, shown with the evidence it rests on: how many items, over what period, covering what proportion of the specification.

At launch it is uncalibrated, because calibration needs a cohort to have sat a real examination, which is about a year after launch. Until then the product describes it as a current performance band and does not use the word prediction. It is never a ceiling, never comparative, suppressed below a minimum evidence threshold, and the learner can hide it from their own view while being told that the parent's report still shows it. The full reasoning, including why Article 22 is not engaged and the one change that would engage it, is in 05.

Nothing in the product is gated on the range. No content path, no price, no tier, no restriction. Adaptive sequencing continues to run on objective-level mastery, which is a narrower signal. This is the constraint that keeps the range an output rather than a decision, and it is the one most likely to be eroded by a reasonable-sounding feature request.

Decision D3514 September 2026

The same topic taught twice

Objective: understand why the inequality sign reverses when both sides are multiplied or divided by a negative number. GCSE Mathematics, Higher tier. Both transcripts below are grounded in the same single item-bank node, IB-MAT-INEQ-004, and would be produced from the same objective by the same model. The only difference is the style parameter object.

The point of showing both is that the difference is not vocabulary. The tutor does not become chattier or more formal. It enters the topic at a different place, withholds the general rule for a different length of time, and does something structurally different when the student is wrong.

Preset A: entry from a concrete instance, late formalism, one running example, predict-then-falsify on error

Preset A · style vector A in 02 · objective INEQ-004
Tutor

Quick one before we start. Is 3 less than 5?

Student

yes obviously

Tutor

Good. Now multiply both sides by −1. Write down what you get, including the sign in the middle. Do not think about rules, just write what you think it says.

Student

-3 < -5

Tutor

Right, that is what most people write. Hold onto it, we are going to check it rather than me telling you.

Put −3 and −5 on a number line. Which one is further left?

Dimension: error_response = predict-then-falsify. The student is wrong and has not been told. Under preset B this turn is a correction. Here the wrong answer is kept on the table so the student can knock it down, which is the entire mechanism of this style.
Student

-5 is further left

Tutor

And further left means smaller. So which is smaller, −5 or −3?

Student

-5. oh. so it's -3 > -5

Tutor

Yes. You started with 3 < 5 and ended with −3 > −5. The sign turned round.

Stay with the same two numbers. Try it with −2 instead of −1: what do 3 and 5 become, and which is bigger now? IB-MAT-INEQ-004

Dimension: example_policy = one running example. The 3 and 5 pair is carried through the whole session rather than replaced with fresh numbers. Preset B replaces them every turn.
Student

-6 and -10, and -6 is bigger

Tutor

So it happened again. Now say what you think the rule is, in your own words, before I say it.

Student

if you times by a negative the inequality swaps round

Tutor

That is the rule, and you got there from two examples rather than from me. Formally: multiplying or dividing both sides of an inequality by a negative number reverses the direction of the inequality. It is worth knowing why, because it stops you forgetting it: multiplying by a negative reflects every number to the other side of zero, and reflecting a line reverses the order of everything on it. IB-MAT-INEQ-004

Dimension: formalism_latency = high. The general statement appears at turn 11, after two worked instances and after the student has articulated it themselves. In preset B it is the first sentence of the session.

Preset B: entry from the formal statement, early formalism, many varied examples, direct correction on error

Preset B · style vector B in 02 · same objective, same source node
Tutor

Rule first, then practice.

Multiplying or dividing both sides of an inequality by a negative number reverses the direction of the inequality. The reason is that multiplying by a negative reflects the number line about zero, and a reflection reverses order. IB-MAT-INEQ-004

Three to try, getting harder. First: solve −2x > 8.

Dimensions: entry_point = formal, formalism_latency = zero. The general rule and its justification are turn one. No instance precedes them.
Student

x > -4

Tutor

No. You divided by −2 and kept the sign the same. Dividing by a negative reverses it, so −2x > 8 gives x < −4.

Next: solve 5 − 3x ≤ 17.

Dimension: error_response = direct correction. Same student error as in preset A. Here it is named, corrected and moved past inside one turn. The student is not asked to diagnose it. example_policy = varied: a new expression each time rather than one carried through.
Student

-3x <= 12 so x <= -4

Tutor

First step right, second step wrong, same reason as before. Dividing −3x ≤ 12 by −3 reverses it: x ≥ −4.

Last one, and this is the one that catches people in the exam: solve −x/4 + 1 > 3. IB-MAT-INEQ-004

What changed, and what did not

The two transcripts differ on four dimensions of the style object. Everything in the right-hand column is held constant by design.
Changed by the style objectHeld constant regardless of preset
Where the session enters the topic: a true numerical instance, or the general statement.The objective, and the item-bank node it is grounded in.
How long the general rule is withheld: eleven turns, or zero.The mathematical content, which is identical and comes from the same validated node.
Whether one example is carried through or each turn brings a new one.The correctness gate. Both transcripts are verified against the same node before display.
What happens when the student is wrong: the error is kept and falsified by the student, or named and corrected by the tutor.The unaided check at the end, its items, and how it is scored.

This is the test of D4 and D7 in miniature. If a reader looks at those two transcripts and sees a difference of tone rather than a difference of teaching, the style engine has failed and the wedge fails with it. The formal version of that judgement is T-STY-02, the blind discrimination test in 02, which replaces this informal impression with a measurement.

That the difference shown above is a difference students can perceive and will care about. These transcripts were written to illustrate a specification, not produced by a system, and the author is not a fifteen-year-old. Nothing here establishes that preset A and preset B feel different to the learner, only that they are structurally different on paper.

What a blocked turn looks like

P2 is enforced by a gate, not by a tolerance. When the verification layer cannot support a generated statement from the cited node, the turn is never shown. The learner sees this instead.

Below-floor behaviour · verifier rejected the generated turn
Student

does this work the same way for quadratic inequalities

Tutor

I am not going to answer that one, because I could not check it against anything I trust.

Quadratic inequalities are on the Higher specification but I do not have a checked explanation of them in front of me yet, and I would rather say that than guess. I have flagged it. Here is what I can give you now, which is the worked solution an examiner wrote for the closest thing I do have: IB-MAT-INEQ-011

Three properties of that turn are deliberate and each costs something.

  • It says why. A silent deflection teaches the student that the tutor is evasive. Naming the reason is what makes the correctness promise legible rather than invisible.
  • It offers the validated item verbatim. Falling back to examiner-written material is always available, because the item bank exists independently of the generation path.
  • It costs a session. This will fire on real questions that a free chatbot would have answered fluently and probably correctly. That is the actual price of P2, it will be visible in retention, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest. The rate at which it fires is a measured quantity, specified as the gate-fire rate in 06.

What is always on screen

Persistently, in every session, not behind a menu:

AI tutor. Style preset licensed from [Name]. [Name] does not write or check these answers.

For an unnamed archetype the same line reads "AI tutor. Style preset: Groundwork." The wording is load-bearing for the passing-off analysis in 04, because the risk being managed is false endorsement, and it is load-bearing for synthetic-content labelling in 05. It is not marketing copy and it should not be edited by anyone who has not read both.

What the parent sees, and what they do not

The parent sees objectives attempted, objectives passed on the unaided check, time on task, and the schedule of what is coming back. The parent does not see session transcripts by default.

Decision D1614 September 2026

This is the design decision in the product most likely to be argued with, because the parent is the payer and the transcript is the thing they would most like to read. Three reasons it stands.

  • It would destroy the teaching. A student who knows every word is reported to a parent will not type "I do not understand any of this". The willingness to admit confusion is the raw material the tutor works with.
  • Best interests of the child. The ICO Children's code requires the child's best interests to be the primary consideration in design, and a surveillance-by-default reading channel is hard to defend against that standard. The mapping is in 05.
  • Transcripts are not what the parent actually wants. They want to know it is working. Objective-level progress against an unaided check answers that better than a wall of chat.

The parent retains, unconditionally: the right to see the full record on request through a subject access route, and the right to delete everything. "By default" is doing real work in this decision and the exceptions are not buried.

Safeguarding is the one place where the parent's rights are not unconditional, and it has to be said here rather than only in the safety document. If the tutor escalates a concern, a trained reviewer decides whether and whom to inform, against a written policy. The parent is not automatically told.

That is deliberate: automatic parental notification is unsafe in precisely the cases that matter most, because the parent is sometimes the risk. It is D26, the reasoning is in 05, and it is a term a parent should meet in the product's own description rather than discover during an incident.

Decision D26the exception to parental rights

Whether the default position above is defensible where the learner is under 13, given that parental responsibility and the UK GDPR Article 8 age threshold for information society services sit differently, and whether the answer changes between Route A and Route B where the school is the controller. Unblocks: the reporting design and the parent-facing terms. Cost of getting it wrong: a Children's code finding against the product's central reporting feature, and a rebuild of the parent surface.
