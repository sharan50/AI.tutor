01 AI.tutor design vault, revised September 2026 Contents

Product

A tutor a fifteen-year-old opens at half past nine the night before a mock, and a thing a parent can see the point of without being taught how to use it.

The learner

Primary: a Year 11 pupil in an urban area, sitting GCSEs in June, already watching revision videos on YouTube, already told by a teacher that they need to practise more. Not failing. Stuck at a grade boundary and unsure which of the things they do not understand is the one costing them marks.

Secondary: the parent, who is paying, who is not confident enough in the subject to help, and who currently faces a choice between £25 to £45 an hour for a human tutor and doing nothing.

The session

A session is scoped to one specification objective, not to a subject and not to a mood. The student says what they are stuck on, or picks up what their last session left unfinished. The tutor teaches it in the style currently selected, asks them to attempt something, reads the attempt, and either moves on or goes back.

The style selection is visible and changeable mid-session. If the student is not following, the first thing offered is not a simpler explanation, it is a different one.

Exam practice studio

When a student is ready to practise, they first choose their exam board—AQA, Pearson Edexcel or another explicitly supported board—then their subject, tier and specification version. The tutor assembles an original timed paper only for that exact combination, from live validated items mapped to it, and declares its objective coverage, marks and difficulty mix before the student begins. The point is not to imitate the typography or wording of a board paper; it is to create enough fresh, board-aligned practice that a small historical paper set is not exhausted.

Each paper ships with an item-level mark scheme generated from the validated expected responses in the bank. A student can self-mark with explanations, or submit an attempt for feedback against those mark points. The product never calls this an official mark scheme, and any paper containing an unvalidated or quarantined item cannot be generated.

The results view may show an estimated grade range, rather than a grade or a board boundary. It is explicitly provisional, tied to the selected board, qualification, tier, paper blueprint and specification version, and appears only once the difficulty calibration and board-specific comparison sample are sufficient. It must say what evidence produced the range, when that evidence was last refreshed, and that awarding organisations set actual grade boundaries after the examination series.

Past papers remain useful as a student-facing reference point, but they are not copied into the product or used to generate derivative questions. Until a rights-holder licence says otherwise, the product may link a learner to an official source and offer original practice around the same published objectives; it cannot host, reproduce or transform the paper or its mark scheme.

The same objective, taught two ways

This is the claim the whole venture rests on, so it is shown rather than asserted. Below is one objective, current in a series circuit, taught under two style objects. The student is the same, the misconception is the same, the target is the same. Nothing about the tone differs meaningfully. What differs is the structure.

The topic is illustrative. It appears on every board's specification, and using it here does not pre-empt the launch scope decision in document 03.

Style A: Ground Up

Entry from a concrete situation, formalism withheld until derived, errors met by prediction then falsification, one running example, visual-first, slow abstraction.

Student: i don't get why current is the same everywhere in a series circuit

Tutor: Before we touch that, tell me what you think a bulb does to the current going through it. Not what you have been told, what you actually picture.

Student: it uses some of it up so theres less after

Tutor: Good. That is a real, specific picture, and it is worth testing rather than me just telling you it is wrong. Here is a circuit with a cell and two identical bulbs in a line, and an ammeter I can move. If your picture is right, what should the ammeter read after the first bulb compared with before it?

Student: less. maybe half

Tutor: So predict both readings for me. Say the reading before the first bulb is 0.3 A. What is the reading between the bulbs, and what is the reading after the second one?

Student: 0.15 then 0

Tutor: That is a clean prediction, and it makes a testable claim: the second bulb has nothing left going through it. Look at the circuit. Are the two bulbs equally bright?

Student: yeah they look the same

Tutor: So the second bulb, which on your picture has zero current, is as bright as the first. Something in the picture has to give. What would you change?

Student: maybe it doesn't get used up then. but something must get used up

Tutor: That instinct is right, and it is the whole idea. Something is used up; it is just not the current. Charge goes round the loop and comes back, so the same amount passes every point each second. What the bulbs take is energy from that charge. Now put that as a sentence about the ammeter readings.

Student: all three readings are 0.3

Tutor: Yes. And now the symbols are worth having, because you already know what they mean: in series, I is the same at every point. Try this one before I say anything else. Three bulbs in series, ammeter after the third reads 0.2 A. What does an ammeter between the first and second read?

Style B: Straight Line

Entry from the formal statement, formalism immediate, errors met by direct correction, many varied examples, verbal, fast abstraction.

Student: i don't get why current is the same everywhere in a series circuit

Tutor: In a series circuit there is one path, so the current is identical at every point: I is the same everywhere. Current is charge flow per second, and charge is conserved, so it cannot accumulate or disappear. What the components take is energy, which shows up as potential difference dropping across each one. Current shared, voltage split.

Student: so the bulb doesn't use up current?

Tutor: No, and that is the single most common error on this topic. A bulb drops potential difference; it does not consume current. If you write that current is used up, you lose the mark even when the rest of the answer is right. Four quick ones. Two bulbs in series, ammeter before the first reads 0.4 A. Reading after the second?

Student: 0.4

Tutor: Correct. Three resistors in series, 6 V supply, readings 2 V and 3 V across the first two. Across the third?

Student: 1 V

Tutor: Correct, because the potential differences sum to the supply. Now a parallel branch, because examiners pair these deliberately to see whether you have learned the rule or the situation.

What to look at in those two. Style A spends six turns before notation appears and reaches one practice question; Style B reaches four in the same space. Style A treats the wrong answer as the material to teach with; Style B names it as a mark-losing error and moves on. Neither is better. Style A suits a student whose model is wrong, Style B suits a student whose model is sound and whose recall is slow, and a student in April is usually the second even if they were the first in November.

The product claimThis difference is what the style object buys

These transcripts are written by hand to specify the target behaviour. They are not model output, and no system has been built that produces them. They function as the acceptance test for the style engine: a session generated under Ground Up that reads like Style B is a conformance failure, and the harness in document 02 exists to catch exactly that.

OpenSpecification, not evidence. No implementation exists

What is promised

  • Everything taught is traceable to an item in the bank, which is written to the published specification and validated by someone who has examined it.
  • The student can choose how they are taught, starting from a named educator they already know, and adjust from there.
  • The parent can see what was covered and how it went, in plain language, and delete all of it whenever they want.
  • The student can generate fresh, specification-aligned practice papers with transparent coverage, mark points and—when evidence supports it—a clearly provisional grade range.

What is not promised, and should be said out loud

  • Not a grade guarantee. Nobody can honestly offer one, and the ones who imply it are the reason this sector is distrusted.
  • Not a marking service for coursework, and not a route to having work done. The tutor declines and says why.
  • Not a companion. It does not pretend to be a person or a friend, and it does not simulate a relationship. See document 05.
  • Not exam board endorsed. It prepares a student for a specification; it is not approved by anyone.
  • Not an official past paper, official mark scheme or official grade boundary. Those labels belong to the awarding organisation, not the product.
