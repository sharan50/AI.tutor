The three conditions that decide the route

09 recommends Route A on three conditions. Two can be tested this quarter for almost nothing, and either can flip the decision on its own.

#ConditionHow it is testedState
C1Parents anchor the price against tutoring (£25 to £45 an hour, verified) rather than against software or against free.Landing-page price testing before any product exists. M1 in 11 Roadmap. Decision rule: run a tutoring-anchored price point and a software-anchored one and compare revenue per visitor.untested

Questions for counsel

Deliberately unanswered. One, OI-3, we are proceeding ahead of under D34, and it says so where it is applied. Full text in 14.

#QuestionUnblocks
OI-1Does AQA's AI clause reach runtime retrieval, or only training?Whether any board-derived material could ever sit in a retrieval index, and therefore whether the clean room is a strategy or…
OI-2Do the other boards' positions differ, and does any board licence for AI use at any price?Board alignment (D19), and whether a licensed route exists at all.
OI-3What exact wording may be used to state which specification a course follows, without implying endorsement? We are proceeding ahead of this answer under decision D34 and the convention in 04, because a product that will not name the examination it…Every piece of marketing copy, and the product's own course naming. Already in use.
OI-4Is a one-to-one tutor with no inter-user sharing outside the Online Safety Act user-to-user duties, and precisely which feature changes that?The whole regulatory posture, and the trigger list in 05.
OI-5What age assurance method would satisfy the ICO for a service whose entire user base is children, at a cost compatible with a consumer price point?The Route A launch, and the unit economics, since verification cost subtracts directly from the acquisition budget.
OI-6What is the minimum viable creator licence for name and likeness only, and what does the exemplar-conditioning upgrade under D6 add to it?The first creator signature, and the D6 contingency.
OI-7Which entity contracts with the parent, and what does that do to consumer jurisdiction, UK representative obligations and VAT?Incorporation, the payment stack, the terms of business, and the international transfer position.
OI-8New. Is the default of no parental transcript access (D16) defensible where the learner is under 13, and does the answer differ between Route A and Route B?The parent reporting surface and the parent-facing terms.
OI-9New. Under what licence may the DfE-published subject content be used as the source for commercial item authoring, and does that use carry conditions?The entire item bank. The clean room depends on there being a lawful source to write from.
OI-12New. What exposure do consumer protection law and the advertising standards regime create for education marketing that names an examination board, and how does that interact with the trade mark analysis in OI-3?The wording convention in D34, which is already in use.
OI-11New. If a provisional grade range is shown to a teacher in a form that could inform a Foundation or Higher tier entry decision, does Article 22 become engaged, and what is then required?Any teacher-facing view of the range, which is a predictable Route B feature request.
OI-10New. Is Article 6(1)(b) the correct basis for processing a child's data under a contract held by their parent, or is legitimate interests sounder? How does the Article 8 threshold interact?The privacy notice, the DPIA, and the consent architecture.

Assumptions the plan rests on

None verified. Eight of them resolve by reading a published document or making a phone call, rather than by building anything, which is why milestone M1 contains almost no engineering.

#AssumptionShapes
OA-1That AQA's AI clause may bite at inference as well as at training. The wording is broad enough; untested.The absoluteness of the clean room.
OA-2That OCR, WJEC and Eduqas take a similar position to AQA. Not checked.Board alignment, and multi-board expansion.
OA-3That naming an exam board descriptively may be honest use under section 11(2) of the Trade Marks Act 1994, provided endorsement is not implied. Counsel's likely starting point, not settled advice.All marketing copy.
OA-4That UK persona protection runs through passing off for false endorsement (Irvine v Talksport), trade marks, copyright in the underlying videos, performers' rights and data protection, there being no statutory UK personality…The entire licence architecture and the on-screen label.
OA-5That Save My Exams, Physics & Maths Tutor, Seneca, Cognito and Twinkl sell board-aligned commercial revision material without licensing past papers. This is inference from their existence, not from their terms.Confidence that the clean-room line is commercially survivable.
OA-9That most creators the venture wants are US-resident, bringing US state right-of-publicity law into scope.The licence's governing law and the enforcement analysis.
OA-6That a private one-to-one tutor with no sharing between users sits outside the Online Safety Act user-to-user duties. Reasoned from Ofcom's published examples; needs counsel.The scoping decision in 05.
OA-7That UK GDPR Article 3(2) and the Article 27 UK representative requirement apply to an India-established controller serving UK children. Standard position, unconfirmed for this fact pattern.The corporate and compliance structure.
OA-8That the proportion-of-screen and proportion-of-audio requirements for synthetic content labelling under the Indian rules appeared in the October 2025 draft; the final notified text has not been checked.The specific display parameters of the on-screen label.
OA-13New. That UK tax law requires six-year retention of billing records.The retention table in 05.
OA-16New. That the fifteen Children's code standards are enumerated as set out in 05. Reproduced from understanding, not re-verified against the published text.The compliance mapping.
OA-20New. That this design meets what a school will actually assess it against: Keeping Children Safe in Education, the school's filtering and monitoring expectations, and any DfE supplier standard.Every Route B conversation. It is the most likely reason a pilot discussion ends early.
OA-14New. That processing UK pupil data in India will be raised as an objection in school due diligence, independently of whether the transfer mechanism is legally sound.Route B feasibility and the corporate structure.
OA-11New. That a recognisable educator's teaching is stable enough across topics to be a point in the style space rather than a cloud.The entire preset representation.
OA-21New. That a Year 10 household pays across roughly twice the span of a Year 11 one, including through a summer with no examination.The acquisition ceiling by year group, and whether the two cohorts have genuinely different economics.
OA-19New. That performance on our validated items maps onto examination grades closely enough for a provisional range to be useful before it is calibrated. Nothing supports this at launch;A feature shown to every learner and every parent from day one.
OA-18New. That the structural differences between presets shown in 01 are perceptible to a fifteen-year-old and matter to them.D4, the wedge, and therefore the venture's differentiation.
OA-10New, and the most load-bearing assumption in the repository. That parents anchor an AI tutoring subscription against the hourly cost of a human tutor rather than against software prices, or against free.Every price point, and the entire Route A case.
OA-12New. The school budget-holder analysis in 08, and that procurement is aligned to the academic year so a missed summer decision is a twelve-month delay. Not one school has been asked.The entire Route B sales motion and its cash planning.
OA-17New. That schools cut their tutoring offer because human tutoring is unaffordable at scale, rather than because the budget line is closing. Verified that 58% cut it; entirely unevidenced as to why.Whether Route B has a market at all.
OA-15New. That variable cost per month is small relative to price, which is what makes engineering cost reduction low-leverage in 10.Where engineering effort goes.
