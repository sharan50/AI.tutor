The rule this document follows

A financial model is the easiest place in a venture document to put a number that nobody checks and everybody later repeats. Every quantity below is one of exactly three things: a figure from the verified findings, a design choice made in this repository, or an Unknown that is named and left empty. There is no fourth category and there are no illustrative figures, because illustrative figures get quoted.

Two structural properties follow from the architecture and hold whatever the vendor prices turn out to be. First, cost per turn is approximately constant rather than growing with session length, because D30 bounds every term in the context. Second, the struggling learner is expensive linearly, not quadratically, which matters because the struggling learner is the one the product exists for and is the one an unbounded-context design would have made uneconomic.

The variable that dominates, derived

Contribution per acquired household over one examination cycle:

C = (p - c) x m  -  CAC  -  V

  p   = price per month
  c   = variable cost per month  (sessions x cost_per_session, plus payment processing)
  m   = retained months
  CAC = acquisition cost per acquired household
  V   = one-off age verification cost per account   (Route A only)

Price and duration enter only through the product (p - c) x m. A one per cent change in m therefore moves contribution by exactly as much as a one per cent change in (p - c). Neither dominates the other mathematically, and any document claiming that price, or retention, is "the key driver" has skipped this step.

What breaks the symmetry is not the arithmetic. It is that three of the five terms are bounded by facts outside our control and two are not bounded at all.

TermBounded byHow wide is the uncertainty
pExternally anchored. Verified £25 to £45 an hour at GCSE above; free general tools below.Narrow. A monthly price is confined to a band we can already describe.
cArchitecture. D30 bounds context; D28 makes figures free.Narrow, and reducible by our own work.
mThe examination calendar. Verified Year 11 is the peak year at 25%. The need ends on a known date. A September Year 11 acquisition has at most the academic year; a Year 10 acquisition has roughly twice that.Bounded above by a hard ceiling that no amount of product quality can lift.
CACNothing.Unbounded. No anchor anywhere in this repository.
VNothing. Open item OI-5.Unbounded, and charged once against a denominator that is already capped.

The dominant quantity is the payback ratio

R = (CAC + V) / ((p - c) x m)          viable iff R < 1

and it dominates for a structural reason rather than a numerical one: its denominator is capped by external facts while its numerator has no anchor at all. Price is anchored by the verified tutoring rate, variable cost is bounded by the architecture, and retained months are capped by the examination calendar. The whole business therefore reduces to a single question, which is whether acquisition plus verification can be brought in under a ceiling that somebody else has already set.

The finding14 September 2026

This is why condition C2 is a kill condition rather than a cost line. V is a one-off subtracted from a capped denominator, so every pound of verification cost removes a pound from the acquisition budget directly. A large verification quote does not make Route A more expensive; it makes it arithmetically impossible, and no amount of product work recovers it.

The acquisition ceiling, and the Year 10 result

Rearranging the viability condition gives the number the business should actually be run against:

max affordable (CAC + V) = (p - c) x m

It cannot be evaluated yet, because p, c and m are not known. But its structure yields a usable result immediately, from verified facts alone.

The ceiling is linear in m. Verified Year 11 is the peak tutoring year at 25% and Year 10 follows at 10%, and the examination calendar gives a Year 10 household roughly twice the retained months of a Year 11 household acquired at the same point in the year.

So the maximum affordable acquisition cost for a Year 10 household is approximately double that for a Year 11 household, at the same price and variable cost. The two cohorts have different economics and must never be averaged into a single blended CAC target, which is exactly what a conventional consumer subscription dashboard would do by default.

This holds without knowing a single unknown, because it depends only on the ratio of retained months, and that ratio comes from the calendar.

What it does assume, and this is not from the calendar. That a Year 10 household actually pays across roughly twice the span, including through a summer with no examination in it. A household that stops in July and returns in September has a retained-month figure much closer to a Year 11 household's, and the factor collapses toward one.

So the shape of the result is safe and its magnitude is not. The operational instruction that follows, report the two cohorts separately and never blend them, is sound either way, because it is precisely the measurement that would reveal the factor to be smaller than the calendar suggests.

Assumedcontinuous payment across the summer, OA-21
DecisionDerived result: you can afford roughly twice as much to acquire a Year 10 household

The operational consequence is in 07: acquire Year 10 deliberately in the autumn, accept a worse conversion rate for the better denominator, and report the two cohorts separately from the first customer rather than adopting the split later when the blended number becomes misleading.

Sensitivity

Since R is a ratio of a sum to a product, its sensitivities are simple and worth stating plainly, because they say where effort should go.

MoveEffect on RPractical reading
Halve CAC + VHalves R.The highest-leverage action available, and the only one not capped by an external fact.
Double m (Year 10 rather than Year 11)Halves R.Equally powerful, and achievable by choosing who to acquire rather than by improving anything.
Raise p by 20%Reduces R by less than 20%, because c does not fall.Constrained by the verified price anchor above and by free tools below. Real but narrow.
Halve cSmall effect unless c is large relative to p.Engineering cost reduction is probably not where the leverage is. Worth stating, because it is the work an engineering-led team will naturally do first.

That c is small relative to p, which is what makes engineering cost reduction low-leverage. This follows from the architecture bounding context per turn, but it has not been measured and depends on vendor prices that are unknown. If c turns out to be a large fraction of p, that row reverses and cost engineering becomes the priority. This is the first thing to check when M2 produces a measured cost per session.

Examiner validation as a line item

It is a fixed cost, not a variable one, and putting it in the right place changes what it means. Content cost does not scale with learners; it scales with items, once. So it does not appear in c and does not affect R. It sets the number of households the business must reach before it covers its content base.

launch content cost = items x minutes_per_item x examiner_rate / 60   + writer cost
                    = ~600 x  UNKNOWN        x  UNKNOWN

households needed to cover it = launch content cost / C

Both unknowns are specified in 03, along with the timed pilot that resolves them. minutes_per_item is the more important of the two, and not because of the launch bill. It is the constant that determines whether the content moat extends to a second strand, a second subject and a second board. If it is small, the moat compounds; if it is large, the content strategy is a one-strand business wearing the language of a platform, and the defensibility argument in 00 does not survive contact with a spreadsheet.

Price points

The substitution arithmetic against the verified £25 to £45 GCSE tutoring rate is tabulated in 07 and is not repeated here, to avoid two copies of the same table drifting apart.

The addition this document makes is the reminder that price is the narrowest of the five terms, bounded above by a verified comparator and below by free tools. Effort spent optimising price is effort spent on the term with the least room in it. The same effort spent on CAC + V, or on shifting acquisition toward Year 10, moves R far more.

That parents anchor against tutoring rates rather than against software prices or against free. If the anchor is software, p collapses, the denominator collapses with it, and the affordable acquisition ceiling falls to something no consumer channel can deliver. This is condition C1 in 09 and it is testable with a landing page before anything is built.

Assumptioncarried over from 07 and repeated because the whole model rests on it

Ordered by cost to obtain, cheapest first, which is also roughly the order of how likely each is to end the venture.

Items 1 and 2 cost almost nothing and together decide whether Route A is viable at all. Items 5 and 6 cost a year and a product, and they are the ones a conventional plan would guess at in order to produce a forecast. This document does not produce that forecast. The model is built, the inputs are named, and the two cheap measurements that gate everything else are scheduled first.
