The ordering rule, and why there are no dates

Milestones are ordered by what the information costs to obtain, cheapest first, and not by what is most interesting to build. A milestone is complete when the evidence is in hand, not when the work is done.

There are no dates in this roadmap. Dates on an unstarted plan with an unhired team are decoration, and they would be the first invented numbers in a repository that has otherwise refused to invent any. Sequence is real and is stated; duration is not known.

Decision D3214 September 2026

The rule has one uncomfortable consequence that is worth stating up front: the first milestone contains almost no building. It is reading, phone calls and a landing page. Two of its items can end the venture, and both of them cost less than a week of engineering time. Doing them after the product exists would be the most expensive available ordering, and it is the ordering most teams choose.

M2: the content and accuracy floor

Evidence obtained: whether the correctness promise is deliverable, and what it costs.

  • Timed validation pilot: two examiners, the same 20 items. Produces minutes_per_item by item kind and a rate check. These are the two numbers that decide whether the content moat scales (10).
  • Decompose the DfE Algebra content into objectives. Confirms or corrects the planning figure of 60.
  • Build the first tranche of the item bank with full provenance, and run T-VAL-01 as a census.
  • Build the frozen battery and run T-GND-01, T-MTH-01, T-SCP-01 against candidate models. Produces ACC-2, ACC-3, ACC-4, the gate-fire rate, and the first real cost_per_session.
  • Select and pin a model under the procedure in 06, gating on D24 contract terms before quality.
  • Procure similarity screening for the clean room.

What would falsify the plan: ACC-2 sitting far below the 99.5% floor with no tractable path to it; a gate-fire rate so high that the product refuses constantly; or minutes_per_item so large that a second strand is unaffordable, which would mean the defensibility argument in 00 does not hold.

Exit: the floors in 03 met on a held-out set with a pinned model, and a measured cost per session.

M3: style falsification

Evidence obtained: whether the style engine does anything a stranger can detect. Deliberately scheduled before any creator is signed, so a failure costs an engineering cycle rather than a contract and a relationship.

  • Rating protocol pilot: three raters score the same public material for one real educator, against the rubric in 02. Produces the inter-rater agreement data that sets the threshold, which is currently an explicit unknown.
  • Within-creator variance check: score the same educator across four topics of differing difficulty. Tests OA-11, that an educator is a point rather than a cloud. A failure here changes the schema.
  • Run T-STY-01 (signature conformance), T-STY-02 (blind discrimination) and T-STY-03 (style changes the teaching, never the truth).

What would falsify the plan: T-STY-02 at chance. Before concluding the wedge is dead, run the pre-specified diagnostic in 02: check whether graders discriminate better on longer samples. If they do not, D4 is in serious trouble and 00 already names that as a live outcome rather than a tail risk.

Exit: blind discrimination significantly above chance, and T-STY-03 passing, which matters more than T-STY-02 because a style engine that changes the truth is worse than no style engine.

M4: the wedge test

Evidence obtained: whether a name is worth more than an archetype to a real learner, and whether acquisition works without one.

This is D14 and it is the experiment the venture turns on. It requires named presets and unnamed archetypes shipped simultaneously from day one, which means at least one signed creator, which means M1's counsel answers and the term sheet.

The D14 instrumentation, in detail

A learner chooses their preset, so choice cannot be randomised without removing the thing being tested. Availability is randomised instead, at cohort level: one arm sees archetypes only, the other sees named presets and archetypes together. The comparison between arms is the causal estimate.

Within the both-arm, comparing learners who chose a named preset against those who chose an archetype is selection-biased and is exploratory only. It will be the more tempting number because it is bigger and easier to compute, and it does not answer the question.

Why return rate and not satisfaction. D14's purpose is to distinguish "the wedge is weak" from "the fidelity is short", because those have opposite fixes. Satisfaction confounds them: a learner who likes the idea of a named preset will rate it well whether or not it changed their behaviour. Returning is a behaviour, and it is the one that has to be true for the business to work.

The baseline return rate, without which the minimum detectable effect cannot be computed and the sample size cannot be set. The analysis is pre-registered before the first cohort runs, and the sample size is fixed once the baseline is observed in the first weeks. Writing a sample size here would require inventing the baseline.

M5: revealed willingness to pay

Evidence obtained: the payback ratio, measured rather than modelled.

M1 tested the price anchor with a landing page, which is a stated preference. This milestone tests what people actually do with a card in their hand, which is different and is the number that matters.

  • Paid conversion from the landing page tested at M1.
  • m, retained months, split by year group, never blended, per the Year 10 result in 10.
  • c from live traffic rather than from the battery.
  • R = (CAC + V) / ((p - c) x m), the dominant variable, computed for the first time from measured inputs.
  • Correlation between gate-fire rate and second-session return, which prices what the correctness promise costs in retention.

What would falsify the plan: R at or above 1 with no lever available. Price is anchored, variable cost is bounded, retained months are capped by the examination calendar, so an R above 1 that cannot be fixed by shifting acquisition to Year 10 or by reducing CAC is a finding about the business rather than a bad quarter.

The D6 trigger, as a decision rule

D6 requires the route to high-fidelity personas to be designed now, and the interface is specified in 02. This is the rule that decides whether it is ever exercised. Both conditions must hold. Either alone points somewhere else.

Named presets go to exemplar conditioning only when they show a clear retention advantage over unnamed archetypes and qualitative feedback attributes the shortfall to fidelity rather than to relevance.

Row three is the one to guard against. Learners will say the persona does not sound enough like the person, because that is the easiest criticism to articulate, and exemplar conditioning is the expensive answer to exactly that complaint. Spending a content licence negotiation, a corpus store and a materially slower and more complex deletion obligation behind the kill switch on a complaint that is not moving behaviour would be the most costly mistake available at M4, and it is why the trigger requires the behavioural condition and the qualitative one rather than either.

M6: route confirmation or reversal

Evidence obtained: enough to confirm Route A or to flip to Route B on evidence rather than on fatigue.

The three conditions in 09 are re-evaluated against measured inputs rather than expectations. C2 will have been answered at M1 and may already have flipped the decision. C1 is answered twice, at M1 by stated preference and at M5 by revealed preference, and the two can disagree.

The reversal is cheap and is meant to be used. 09 puts the switching cost at months rather than a rewrite, because roughly 80% of the design does not care who pays (D3). A plan that treats a route reversal as a failure will delay it past the point where it helps; this one treats it as the expected use of the parallel planning that D2 and D3 paid for.

No hiring plan, no funding plan, and no dates. All three depend on numbers that do not exist yet: the content cost from M2, the payback ratio from M5, and the answers from M1. Writing them now would require inventing the very inputs this repository has refused to invent everywhere else, and a roadmap with fabricated dates is the artefact most likely to be quoted back later as though it had meant something.
