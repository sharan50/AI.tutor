Foundation vision, in four sentences

Foundation vision is Foundation's expanded scope: once the first version, for Reception to Year 2, has passed its pilot, the same instrument would judge other skills practised at a fixed station, such as cooking at a counter and playing the piano or keyboard, for pupils of 4 to 18 in schools and, in parallel, in a home version for families (D130, D133). In schools, Foundation runs on the school's own servers and the teacher sees the class view, as now (D135); at home, a home unit connects to Foundation's cloud service, and the feedback comes through an app (D131). The camera is the only sensor: there is no microphone and no data from the instrument, so Foundation vision judges only what can be seen (D132). In schools, the pupil is shown nothing at any age, and the teacher acts (D93); at home, the app shows its feedback to adults, and to a learner of 13 or over who holds it, and a child under 13 is shown nothing (D134).

It could be that we expand after a pilot and proof of concept, expand to all the use cases where there is a certain amount of fixed positioning. So like evaluating cooking skills, evaluating piano or other musical instruments. And we can talk about the expanded scope as like Foundation vision or something along those lines.

DecisionD130, the owner's, 5 November 2026

The first version does not change. It goes to market as the classroom instrument for Reception to Year 2 that F1 to F6 describe. Foundation vision is not a fourth idea, and skills practised on the move are outside it, because no fixed camera can follow them.

What comes first, and the gate before each phase

The first version goes to market first. Every later step passes its own gate.

The version which we start to GTM with will be Reception to Year 2, then we will phase capability to expand to 4-18 + a parallel home D2C version which I will have to scope out in terms of what additional use cases we can apply. For the latter, the main examples that come to mind are musical instruments like piano and keyboard.

DecisionD133, the owner's, 5 November 2026; supersedes D41's band for Foundation's later phases
  1. First, the first version, for Reception to Year 2, through FM1 to FM4 (F6). D41's band of 4 to 7 holds for it.
  2. Then, in schools, capability for pupils of 4 to 18, one skill or age range at a time.
  3. In parallel with those phases, a home version sold to families. Which skills the home version covers is the owner's to scope; musical instruments, such as the piano and keyboard, are the owner's main examples (D133).

Nothing of Foundation vision is built before FM4 decides to build Foundation further. Each new skill, each new age range and the home version then passes its own bench test and its own observed pilot, as FM2 and FM3 require of the first version, before it is offered. Its cheap questions are asked at FM1, alongside Foundation's own.

DecisionD136, taken while applying the owner's decisions, 5 November 2026; the owner's to keep or reverse

D136 reads the owner's "pilot and proof of concept" as FM2's bench test, the proof of concept, and FM3's observed pilot. Each new skill and age range has its own labelled set and thresholds under T-FND-04, written down before its bench test, and a skill that fails it is not offered. How long the ladder takes is Unknown. The cheap questions are conversations with teachers of food and music, Assumed (OA-65), and with parents, Assumed (OA-66, OA-67), from the field research guide.

Nothing here changes which idea goes first (D94). The gate also keeps Foundation vision from drawing a small team off the first version (RF9).

What would end Foundation vision, or a part of it, any one of: the first version ends at FM4 (F6); a skill's readings miss their threshold (OA-64); schools or families will not pay, or teachers will not use the alerts (OA-65, OA-66); or counsel's answer to OI-33 rules out the home version.

What counts as a fixed station

The owner's phrase is "a certain amount of fixed positioning". This proposed rule turns it into five conditions.

The fixed-station rule. Foundation vision takes on a skill only if all five of these hold:

  1. The learner works at one station that does not move, such as a desk, a keyboard or a counter.
  2. One fixed camera sees the hands and the work, and never needs the face.
  3. The skill is judged by what can be seen, since nothing is heard (D132).
  4. Correct technique can be written down as a catalogue, and checked by someone qualified who did not write it.
  5. The camera never stands in for supervision (D138).
Proposed rulefor the owner to adopt or amend
The rule applied, with no figure and no claim of accuracy. Every reading here is untested (OA-64).
SkillApplying the rule
The piano and keyboardHand position, fingering and posture. Whether a camera above a keyboard can see which keys are pressed, and when, is untested (OA-64).
Cooking at a counterKnife holds, weighing and measuring; not heat, taste or whether food is cooked. A knife hold is read as technique, never as a safety check, and an adult in the room is responsible for the learner (D138).
Other instrumentsPosture and hand position only, and not for an instrument played at the mouth or under the chin, which brings the face into view (D97). Their sound, tone and tuning are heard, not seen.
Handwriting and drawing at older agesHandwriting as the first version reads it (F3); drawing would need its own catalogue.
What fails itSport and dance, which move; singing and speech, which are heard.

In schools: older pupils, other rooms, the school's own servers

The school version is the first version in more rooms and at more ages: the same desk units, or units at a keyboard or a counter; the teacher's class view (F2); and the pupil shown nothing at any age, while the teacher acts (D93).

Everything Foundation does in schools runs on the school's own servers, so no picture of a child leaves the building.

DecisionD135, the owner's, 5 November 2026; supersedes D90's "or in the cloud" for schools

Foundation runs on the school's own servers (D135), so no picture of a child leaves the building; the choice F5 had left open is settled (F5). The school houses and powers the machine, and the company supports hardware it cannot reach. What the machine costs, and how many desks it can serve, are Unknown until FM2 measures processing per desk.

  • The rooms: food rooms, and keyboard or music rooms, where one teacher has many stations. Whether those teachers want alerts during a practical lesson and act on them is Assumed (OA-65).
  • The curriculum: what it asks of these skills is not in the register, so this page says nothing about it. Finding out is reading, for FM1.
  • The record is the school's (D98), never used to infer a difficulty or to rank a pupil. A pupil whose parent objects is not watched (D99). A shared station is off if any pupil at it is not to be watched (D99). Readings belong to a station, not a face (F4), so those of pupils who share one cannot be told apart.

At home: the home version

At home, a home unit connects to Foundation's cloud service, and the feedback comes through an app (D131). It is, in the owner's words, for "lessons and home practice". Which skills the home version covers is the owner's to scope; musical instruments, such as the piano and keyboard, are the owner's main examples (D133). This page designs only what every skill shares.

Both School classes (a sell-to-school based model where Foundation is hosted on-prem) AND a D2C version for lessons and home practice that would be connected to a cloud based version of Foundation and feedback is through an app.

DecisionD131, the owner's, 5 November 2026; supersedes D89 in part, for the home version

The home version brings a second customer, families, with a consumer sale and its support, Assumed (OA-66), and a camera in a family's home, whose lawful basis is Open OI-33. The first version stays a school's alone.

In the home version, the app shows its feedback to adults, and to a learner of 13 or over, who may hold the app themselves; a child under 13 is shown nothing.

DecisionD134, the owner's, 5 November 2026; supersedes D93 in part, for a learner of 13 or over at home

The home version keeps the desk unit's guarantees. The home unit is a camera on an arm or a stand, with no microphone, screen, speaker or light; an adult sets it up and aims it at the work, never at a face; its frames are read in Foundation's cloud service and discarded; the app belongs to a verified adult, who may add the child's own teacher; and what it reads is kept as text in the family's account, deleted when the adult asks or closes the account, and never used to infer a difficulty or to compare the child with others.

DecisionD137, taken while applying the owner's decisions, 5 November 2026; the owner's to keep or reverse
  • The home unit is a device of its own, not the family's phone or tablet, which has a microphone and a screen. Like the Primer's tablet (P6), it must be designed, made, certified and sold, and what it must meet before sale is part of OI-33. Unlike the desk unit's locked arm (D97), its mount can be moved after setup, and its frames reach the cloud before the face check discards any with a face (RF8). It cannot tell who is at it, so a sibling's work is read as the learner's: a limit, stated as P4 states the Primer's.
  • The app. A learner of 13 or over may hold it if their adult allows it (D137), and sees only their own feedback. For a child under 13 it is the adult's alone, and the home unit shows the child nothing, as the desk unit does (D90). Its words are templates and reviewed entries, never generated (D96), and it rewards no one: no stars, streaks, badges or confetti (D25).

Whether parents, and learners of 13 or over, accept such a camera at home is Assumed (OA-67).

What one camera can and cannot judge

The camera is the only sensor: there is no microphone and no data from the instrument, so Foundation vision judges only what can be seen (D132).

Foundation vision judges a skill by camera only: there is no microphone, and no data from the instrument.

DecisionD132, the owner's, 5 November 2026

OI-17 stays open for Foundation, should a microphone ever be added.

What a camera could be asked to read, each untested, Assumed (OA-64): hand pose, fingering, wrist and arm posture, knife holds, and the work itself. Whether a camera above a keyboard can see which keys are pressed, and when, is untested (OA-64). What it cannot judge: sound, tone, tuning, taste and heat. What matters most in playing, its sound, is outside Foundation's view (RF6), so Foundation vision suits instruments where the hands and posture are much of what is taught.

Where it runs. In schools, on the school's own servers (D135); at home, in Foundation's cloud service, where frames are read and discarded (D97). Whether the home unit could read its frames itself is for its bench test (D137). Recognition, not generation: the models recognise hands and work, and none generates a word (D96).

What others are described as showing, each reached only through search summaries and deciding nothing here (evidence register). Research is described as pointing both ways on reading keys from video alone, Assumed (CV-44, CV-45). One study is described as mapping the keyboard and hand movements in each frame of a piano video to the keys pressed, trained and tested on videos from one pianist's channel, Assumed (CV-44); another as taking note timings from MIDI recorded alongside the video, because key presses are subtle to see, Assumed (CV-45). ROLI's Airwave, used with the ROLI Learn app, is described as a camera add-on that tracks the hands over a ROLI keyboard and gives feedback on posture and finger position, Assumed (CV-42). Four practice apps are described as hearing what is played, through the device's microphone or a MIDI connection to a digital keyboard, Assumed (CV-41).

What it would need to know

Each skill needs its own catalogue, like the first version's error catalogue (F3): correct technique, the common errors, what each looks like in plain words, and a suggested next step.

It is written by someone qualified in the skill and reviewed by another; the writer and the reviewer are never the same person. No exam board's or commercial scheme's material enters it, as F3's clean room requires. No frame from a school or a home trains a model, because none is kept (D97). What each catalogue costs is Unknown.

Safety and privacy

At school and at home, the face check discards any frame with a face, and no frame is kept (D97, D137), as for the first version (F4). Foundation watches technique, never safety; where a skill can hurt, an adult in the room is responsible for the learner (D138).

Foundation never supervises. It watches technique, never safety: where a skill can hurt, such as a knife or a hob, an adult in the room is responsible for the learner, at school or at home, and nothing Foundation shows or says suggests otherwise.

DecisionD138, taken while applying the owner's decisions, 5 November 2026; the owner's to keep or reverse

So there is no alert for danger, such as a hand near a blade, which would invite a school or a parent to rely on a camera for a child's safety (RF7). A home version that covers cooking, if the owner scopes one, needs an adult present at every session, which Foundation cannot check.

  • Who sees what. In schools, the pupil is shown nothing at any age, and the teacher acts (D93). At home, the app shows its feedback to adults, and to a learner of 13 or over who holds it; a child under 13 is shown nothing (D134).
  • A camera in a house. The face check must discard every member of the household, not only the child (RF8). Frames of a child cross to Foundation's cloud service and are discarded there (D97).
  • A learner of 13 or over is still a child under the Children's code, whose five age ranges pupils of 4 to 18 span, Assumed (CV-06). In the UK they are old enough to consent to an online service themselves, where consent is the basis, Assumed (CV-07); whether that lets them hold the app is part of Open OI-33.
  • A connected device. A search summary of the code's standard on connected toys and devices says it requires their provider to include effective tools to enable conformance to the code, Assumed (CV-43). Whether it reaches the home unit is part of OI-33.
  • Tests. Under F6's conditions, T-FND-01, T-FND-02 and T-FND-07 on the home unit (F3), and T-FND-06 on every unit in a school.

For the home version, in the UK and the US: the lawful basis, consent and notice for a camera in a family's home, with its frames read in the cloud; whether a learner of 13 or over may hold the app; and what the home unit must meet before sale. For the school version: OI-28, and OI-29 in the US, reread for pupils of up to 18 and rooms such as a food room. Unanswered, and not guessed at here.

What is not decided

  • The home version's skills: the owner's to scope (D133).
  • Which skill or age range comes first in schools.
  • Whose record a shared station's readings go into.
  • Whether the home unit reads its frames itself, so that none leaves the home (D137).
  • Where Foundation's cloud service runs, and how the home unit and its frames are kept secure, including how an adult unpairs it (OI-33).
  • Who installs the home unit, and how a family is shown that it is aimed right.
  • When the home unit runs, how anyone in the house can tell it is on, since it shows no light, and who turns it off for a learner who asks (D99, OI-33).
  • How the app knows a learner is 13 or over (D134).
  • The child's teacher: what they see, and how the adult removes them.
  • The home record: how long it is kept, what it holds, and whether a learner of 13 or over can see and delete their own.
  • Prices, for the school version, the home unit and the service.
  • One product or three: whether the home version and the first version share an account or a brand with the Primer and the Tutor, which stays open (which idea goes first, D40).
