For counsel: twenty-nine questions, none answered

No answer, no likely answer, and no range is offered for any of these. All are also unacted on except OI-3, where D34 proceeds under a fixed convention because waiting would mean a product that cannot describe itself, and OI-23 to OI-27, where the self-tutor proceeds under D59's rule: it is withdrawn only if counsel's answer is a categoric no. Supplying one would be worse than leaving the question open, because a plausible guess in a repository like this one gets quoted later as though it were advice. Seven come from the brief; five (OI-8 to OI-12) were raised while writing the Tutor's design; eight (OI-13 to OI-20) were raised by the Primer and Foundation; two (OI-21 and OI-22) by the Tutor's US launch, after the owner amended D1; five (OI-23 to OI-27) by the self-tutor; and two (OI-28 and OI-29) by Foundation's classroom design. We are proceeding ahead of OI-3 under D34 and of OI-23 to OI-27 under D59, and each row says so.

Open assumptions: reasoned, not verified

Every one of these shapes the plan and none has been confirmed. Nine are carried from the brief; twelve were introduced by the Tutor's design, thirteen by the Primer and Foundation, four of which were withdrawn with Foundation's design for a parent at home, two by the Tutor's US launch, four by the self-tutor, four by the Primer's demonstration, three by the Tutor's MVP and five by Foundation's classroom design, and all of those are marked new. They are grouped by what they would break.

The pattern in this list

Worth noticing, because it shapes what M1, PM1 and FM1 should be: eighteen of the forty-eight assumptions are resolvable by reading or by asking, not by building or by advice. OA-2 is a few hours with three published policies. OA-5 is reading five sets of terms. OA-8 is one notified text. OA-13 is a tax reference. OA-16 is an hour with the Children's code. OA-42 and OA-43 are a limits page and a set of terms, for the Primer's demonstration. OA-17 is thirty phone calls. OA-10 is a landing page, and OA-35 a US one once the US course is chosen. OA-20 is Keeping Children Safe in Education and whatever supplier standard a school applies. OA-22, OA-26 and OA-40 are conversations with parents; OA-28 is a conversation with Reception and Year 1 teachers; OA-51 and OA-52 are conversations with heads, staff and parents of a class that would have Foundation's desk units; OA-31 is reading published market data. The field research guide carries the questions for all of them but OA-42 and OA-43, which the MVP build guide puts among the tasks before its first day.

Those nineteen include the most load-bearing assumption about the Tutor and the most load-bearing one about the Primer (OA-22), the one that decides whether Route B has a market, and the one most likely to end a school conversation in its first ten minutes. None of them requires a lawyer, an engineer or a product. They are in M1 and PM1 for that reason, the demonstration's two aside, and a plan that starts by building instead is choosing to pay the highest available price for the same information.
