Primer: plan

The Primer's plan is built around what is not known: who pays, how much, whether a family will buy a tablet for one child, what an answer with live video costs and how fast it comes, and whether it works. Its milestones buy that information cheapest first: conversations, then adults red-teaming the generation pipeline, then a human-run pilot, before the rest of the Primer is built. Children are recorded for testing only after that, before any child speaks to the built Primer, and the Primer's own tablet is made only after the built Primer has been tested on a stand-in. Its economics are almost entirely unknown, and this page names what would measure each figure rather than guessing it.

Who pays, and why that is unknown

The payer is a parent and the user is a child, and nobody yet knows why that parent would pay, or whether they would buy a tablet to do it. The Tutor's case is that its parent is choosing between the product and an hour of a human tutor before an examination that matters to the family Assumed (OA-10, the Tutor's most load-bearing assumption; 00). The Primer's parent has no examination, no deadline, and no purchase the Primer obviously replaces.

That parents of 8 to 13 year olds will pay for the Primer with no exam to prepare for. What purchase, if any, it replaces is unknown: enrichment such as clubs, classes and books; a tutor for a child who is ahead rather than behind; or nothing at all. This is the Primer's most load-bearing assumption.

AssumedOA-22, resolvable by asking parents and a landing page

The films' answer is time. Version A opens: "Every child asks why. Most evenings, nobody has the time to answer properly." The owner's case for the Primer begins there (D114): the parent buys the answer they have no time to give, which replaces no purchase. It is the owner's view and the films' premise, not evidence, and asking parents tests it as cheaply as OA-22.

That parents of 8 to 13 year olds find, most evenings, that they lack the time to answer their children's why-questions properly, and would pay for something that does. If it holds, it is one answer to why a parent pays (OA-22).

AssumedOA-53, the owner's view behind D114; resolvable by asking parents

The parent also buys a tablet. The Primer is its own tablet, one per child, with nothing else on it (D105), so a family that owns a tablet already is asked to buy another rather than install an app. Whether parents will buy a dedicated tablet for one child, at a price that covers it, is Assumed (OA-54), resolvable by asking parents and with a landing page. Whatever shape the price takes, a tablet is paid for, by the family or by the company, before the first question is asked.

Three further facts about the buyer shape the plan. The Primer cannot use engagement to keep paying families: it has no engagement objective, no streaks and no return notifications (D25), and its tablet shows no notifications at all (D105), so whatever retention it has must come from a child wanting to bring the next question. The Primer may reason with a child about choices in the child's own future, at every age from 8 to 13 and on unless a parent turns it off (D106), and whether parents accept that is Assumed (OA-57). And the owner's view is that the US market has proven more receptive than the UK to consumer education products for children (OA-31), which is the basis for launching in both at once and which is checkable by reading published market data.

Routes: parents and schools, the UK and the US

Parents first, schools later. The first route is direct to parents, on the pattern of the Tutor's Route A (07): a verified adult holds the account and switches the modules, and the tablet reaches the family direct. A route through schools, on the pattern of Route B (08), has one real advantage, which is that escalation goes to the school's designated safeguarding lead (05). It also brings the school's own frame of assessment (OA-20 applies) and, for a product that discusses politics and belief, the expectations on schools to present political issues in a balanced way (Open OI-16); who controls a child's record when a school uses it is Open OI-19. It is not a launch route.

The Primer and Foundation launch in the UK and the US together where resources allow, and in the UK first where they do not. D1 continues to govern the Tutor. India is not pursued for any idea sold direct to families while section 9(3) of the DPDP Act stands.

DecisionD51, 22 October 2026

What the Primer teaches travels well; its tablet travels less well. The Primer is not tied to a curriculum or an examination board, but a device meets rules of its own in each country before it is sold, which an app does not. A US launch adds four things: the amended COPPA Rule and the state companion-chatbot laws (Open OI-15); a US escalation route (P4); an allow-list of sources and reviewed content for families in two countries (OA-27), which includes each country's own figures and cases, UK ones for a child in the UK and US ones in the US (D113); and the question of which entity contracts with a US parent, which extends the Tutor's Open OI-7. A child's voice is not among them: counsel has cleared a child speaking to the Primer, and the recording of children's speech with consent for testing, in the US as in the UK, as the owner reported on 4 November 2026 (D118). That answer is for those two markets only. India is held back because the register's finding on section 9(3) prohibits behavioural monitoring of children whatever a parent consents to (04).

Distribution is of hardware. How the tablet is sold, sent, returned and repaired in either country is Unknown. App stores are no longer the child's route to the Primer, since its tablet has no store; they may still be one for PM4's stand-ins, and for the parent's screen if it is a phone app; payment providers are still the chokepoint they are for the Tutor (04).

What the tablet must meet before sale is for counsel (Open OI-31). Applied, D119 settles when a child's speech is first recorded and first heard. No child is recorded for the MVP demonstration, which adults use (D70). The test recordings are made, with consent, under their own research protocol and DPIA, before any child speaks to the built Primer, which in this plan is before PM4's supervised pilot switches speech on, and so before launch. They remain the one exception to recording nothing of a child, they are never written to backups, and anything a child says of their own is cut out (below). No child speaks to the Primer until T-PRM-06's spoken battery has passed on the test recordings, and speech is first switched on for children in a supervised pilot, PM4's or one held to its conditions. Counsel has cleared the recordings, with consent, in the UK and the US, as the owner reported on 4 November 2026 (D118).

PM3 is the first milestone that involves children, and it cannot start until the safeguarding policy, the research protocol and parental consent exist, and until these preconditions are met: facilitators vetted to the standard required for work with children in each country; a parent present, or the session observed by a second adult; the child's own assent, and a way to stop at any moment; a DPIA for the pilot; no camera on the child and nothing of the child recorded as audio or video, as the Primer itself keeps neither (D102, D104, D116); no script or video the Primer makes holding any of the child's words (D126); the Primer's rules kept by the facilitator, a safety reply shown and never spoken among them (D115); and pilot records deleted at the pilot's end unless the parent consents otherwise, kept meanwhile for a period Unknown until the policy sets it (P4). They are the conditions for a child in any Primer session, PM4's included, and in any session that makes test recordings. There a child reads a script, written and reviewed with the safeguarding specialist, that voices nothing of their own life; anything a child says of their own takes 05's path, and is cut from the recording before it becomes test material, held in the hold, only for the trained reviewer, until the review closes where it takes 05's path, and otherwise deleted at once, with no backup of the recording kept; and the recording is the one exception to recording nothing of a child. Everything before PM3 is with adults only, PM2's red-team included, which is why the field research guide collects no child's data.

A demonstration for adult testers, built in days, can support PM1's conversations (D70). It is described in the MVP build guide. It is not the built Primer of PM4: it answers from no reviewed source, no child uses it, and nothing it shows is evidence about children. (D103); and Version C's football thread stopped as a question about the asker's own life. A tester at PM1 who sees both is told which is the design: the films, which are illustrative and made with AI, show the design and no working system, and the demonstration lets a tester try an earlier one. Making the tablet

The tablet is designed early and made late. PM4 runs the built Primer on a stand-in tablet locked to the Primer; the dedicated tablet is designed alongside PM4 and made only once PM4's evidence is in. Making means tooling, a first batch and certification for sale in each country, the spending least recoverable if PM4 stops the Primer, so it waits, as D32's order asks. The design runs in parallel so that a good result at PM4 does not then wait for one; its cost is Unknown, and it is lost if PM4 stops the Primer.

The Primer is its own tablet, as every film shows: one per child, with no browser, no other apps and no notifications.

DecisionD105, the owner's, 31 October 2026

The stand-in, for PM4. A tablet already on sale, one per pilot child, locked so that it runs only the Primer, with no browser, no other apps and no notifications; its lock also switches off the system's own voice assistant and wake word. It shows the screen the films show, which is software (D109), and it plays the explainer videos generated for each answer as they arrive (D122); its microphone is live only while the child holds the talk key, with the screen showing plainly that it is listening (D116); T-PRM-13 checks every path that can open the microphone, on each stand-in before any child uses it and on every software release, the stand-in's own system updates included. On PM4's stand-ins, the system's own updates are held by the device management until T-PRM-13's software check has passed on them. Its body is not the films' matte off-white tablet. Which tablet suits is Unknown, and it is chosen before PM4: one without a camera, so that T-PRM-13 runs on it in full, and one whose screen, speakers and connection play generated video well enough for the conversation. If none suits, the owner chooses between one whose camera is switched off and covered, which T-PRM-13 would not pass, and pilot units of the dedicated design, which would have to be ready before PM4 (P4); that choice is the owner's and has not been made. What using a stand-in in a supervised pilot requires is part of the pilot's protocol and DPIA; OI-31 asks what a tablet sold to families must meet.

The dedicated tablet, designed alongside PM4. It is designed to D105, D109 and D116: the matte off-white tablet of the films, nothing on it but the Primer, no camera, and a talk key the microphone obeys. It is also a tablet that plays generated video: its screen, speakers and connection must carry the explainer videos made for each answer as they arrive (D122), and what that asks of the hardware is Unknown until PM4 shows what the videos need. Until counsel answers OI-31, it is designed and costed as if each regime the candidates describe applies: the security rules in the UK, radio authorisation in both countries and, in the US, children's product testing (routes, P4). T-PRM-13 inspects every hardware revision, and checks every path that can open the microphone on every software release. Who designs and makes it, and where, is deliberately not decided (ledger).

Made only after PM4's evidence. Tooling, a first batch and certification follow PM4, and PM5's purchases are of the dedicated tablet and the service. The batch is still a bet placed before PM5 shows whether parents pay at a real price, which is why it waits for PM4 at least. Whether PM5 takes orders before the batch is made, so that what parents pay is known before the batch is paid for, is not decided here; the batch's size is Unknown.

If the tablet cannot be made and sold at a price families will pay (RP7), the design has no fallback. An app on a family's own tablet is the alternative OA-54 tests against, and only the owner could choose it, by reversing D105.
