Primer: plan

The Primer's plan is built around what is not known: who pays, how much, and whether it works. Its milestones buy that information cheapest first, beginning with conversations and a human-run pilot before anything is built. Its economics are almost entirely unknown, and this page names what would measure each figure rather than guessing it.

Who pays, and why that is unknown

The payer is a parent and the user is a child, and nobody yet knows why that parent would pay. The Tutor's case is that its parent is choosing between the product and an hour of a human tutor before an examination that matters to the family Assumed (OA-10, the Tutor's most load-bearing assumption; 00). The Primer's parent has no examination, no deadline, and no purchase the Primer obviously replaces.

That parents of 8 to 13 year olds will pay for the Primer with no exam to prepare for. What purchase, if any, it replaces is unknown: enrichment such as clubs, classes and books; a tutor for a child who is ahead rather than behind; or nothing at all. This is the Primer's most load-bearing assumption.

AssumedOA-22, resolvable by asking parents and a landing page

Two further facts about the buyer shape the plan. The Primer cannot use engagement to keep paying families: it has no engagement objective, no streaks and no return notifications (D25), so whatever retention it has must come from a child wanting to bring the next question. And the owner's view is that the US market has proven more receptive than the UK to consumer education products for children (OA-31), which is the basis for launching in both at once and which is checkable by reading published market data.

Routes: parents and schools, the UK and the US

Parents first, schools later. The first route is direct to parents, on the pattern of the Tutor's Route A (07): a verified adult holds the account and switches the modules. A route through schools, on the pattern of Route B (08), has one real advantage, which is that escalation goes to the school's designated safeguarding lead (05). It also brings the school's own frame of assessment (OA-20 applies) and, for a product that discusses politics and belief, the expectations on schools to present political issues in a balanced way (Open OI-16); who controls a child's record when a school uses it is Open OI-19. It is not a launch route.

The Primer and Foundations launch in the UK and the US together where resources allow, and in the UK first where they do not. D1 continues to govern the Tutor. India is not pursued for any idea sold direct to families while section 9(3) of the DPDP Act stands.

DecisionD51, 22 October 2026

The Primer is the idea that travels best, because it is not tied to a curriculum or an examination board. A US launch still adds four things: the amended COPPA Rule and the state companion-chatbot laws (Open OI-15); a US escalation route (P4); content reviewed for families in two countries (OA-27); and the question of which entity contracts with a US parent, which extends the Tutor's Open OI-7. India is held back because the register's finding on section 9(3) prohibits behavioural monitoring of children whatever a parent consents to (04). Distribution channels for either country are Unknown, and app stores and payment providers are the chokepoint here as they are for the Tutor (04).

Milestones defined by evidence

The ordering rule is the Tutor's: buy information in ascending order of what it costs, and write no dates (D32, 11). Each milestone is complete when the evidence is in hand, and each names the result that would stop the Primer there.

It tests the child's half of OA-23, whether a child can sustain the inquiry, and OA-32, before anything is built, and sets the session caps and the matching threshold. PM5Revealed willingness to pay. Real purchases at a real price.The business case.OA-22 fails in what parents do, whatever they said at PM1.
PM6Release as a gated product, or hold.The Primer as a product.Page 13's test: if it cannot help a child think more independently while leaving them free to disagree, hold the boundary rather than widen and hope.

PM3 is the first milestone that involves children, and it cannot start until the safeguarding policy, the research protocol and parental consent exist, and until these preconditions are met: facilitators vetted to the standard required for work with children in each country; a parent present, or the session observed by a second adult; the child's own assent, and a way to stop at any moment; a DPIA for the pilot; and pilot records deleted at the pilot's end unless the parent consents otherwise, kept meanwhile for a period Unknown until the policy sets it (P4). Everything before PM3 is with adults only, which is why the field research guide collects no child's data.

What would make the Primer go first

Foundations does not go first (D42). Between the Tutor and the Primer there is one decision rule, on which idea goes first, and this page keeps no second one. Its thresholds are written down before PM1 begins, as D37 wrote down the thresholds for the Tutor's route, so that the result cannot be fitted to the evidence afterwards Decision D58.

PM1 supplies the Primer's half of the evidence the rule needs: whether parents of 8 to 13 year olds would pay with no exam to prepare for (OA-22); priors from teachers and professors on whether children in the band can sustain a typed inquiry (OA-23); whether parents accept the module design (OA-26); and counsel's first view on OI-13, OI-15 and OI-16.

Three things do not count as evidence: enthusiasm for the idea, interest from an accelerator, and how long a child stays engaged in a demonstration. Y Combinator's requests for startups include one called "The Primer" Assumed (CV-01); the ages and the order reported for it differ from this one (D42), and an application is not demand. Page 13 states the principle that applies: no stage advances because the team likes the concept or because engagement is high (13).
