The recommendation, and what would overturn it

Route A, direct to parents. The verified demand data points that way and the verified school data points away from Route B. But the recommendation holds only if three conditions are met, all three are testable before anything is built, and failing any one of them should flip the decision rather than start an argument about it.

Private tutoring demand is rising and concentrated: 29% of secondary pupils have had it, up from 18% twenty years ago; Year 11 peaks at 25%; London 45%; urban 33%. Over the same period the school channel is contracting: school-organised tutoring fell to 20% from 22% in 2023, and 58% of schools reduced their tutoring offer year on year.

Household demand is growing and institutional demand is shrinking. That is the strongest single argument in this repository for going direct, and it is a fact rather than a preference.

What each route forecloses

Choosing Route A forecloses

  • Little, structurally. The shared core (D3) is unchanged and a school motion can be added later.
  • Time, which is the real cost: outcome evidence accumulates slowly or not at all in a consumer cohort, and outcome evidence is what 00 identifies as the only durable moat.
  • Some school credibility. A product known as a consumer app enters school procurement differently, though not fatally.

Choosing Route B forecloses

  • Part of the product roadmap. Class-level features are natural in schools and several sit on the Online Safety Act trigger list in 05. Route B's obvious roadmap collides with the scoping decision that keeps the whole company out of user-to-user duties.
  • Consumer brand-building during the years the sales cycle consumes.
  • Cash flexibility. A field sales motion is a fixed cost that cannot be turned down quickly when funding tightens.

What it costs to change your mind

Months, not a rewrite, in either direction. This is the return on decision D3, and it is the reason the parallel planning was worth doing rather than an indulgence.

SwitchWhat has to be builtWhat carries over unchanged
A to BField sales motion, DPA and procurement apparatus, school identity integration, teacher-facing reporting, security review readiness. The age assurance investment is written off.Item bank, verifier, style engine, architecture, safety design, accuracy harness. Roughly 80% (D3).
B to AAge assurance procurement and integration, consumer billing, consumer support at volume, our own safeguarding reviewer, consumer marketing capability from zero.The same 80%, plus the outcome evidence generated in schools, which is an asset in the consumer pitch rather than a sunk cost.

Note the asymmetry, because it is the strongest argument in this document and it is easy to miss: B to A carries the evidence over; A to B does not carry anything comparable back. Starting in schools and moving to parents arrives at the consumer market with the efficacy evidence already in hand. Starting with parents and moving to schools arrives with a consumer brand and no evidence, facing a buyer who wants evidence.

That asymmetry is a real argument for Route B, it cuts against the recommendation above, and it is the single point on which a reasonable person most often flips. It is not decisive here only because the evidence advantage is realised on a timescale of years and the runway question is settled in months.

The strongest case for Route B

Stated as well as it can be made, because a recommendation that has not met the best version of the alternative is not worth much.

  1. Route B solves, at zero cost, the problem most likely to kill Route A. Age assurance is an unpriced compliance obligation on a consumer service whose entire user base is children, and it is the exact failure the ICO fined Reddit £14.47m for in February 2026 Verified. In Route B the school already knows who its pupils are. That is not an incremental advantage; it removes a whole category of risk and an unknown cost.
  2. Route B generates the only durable moat. 00 concedes that a validated item bank is copyable by anyone with capital, and that what is actually protective is outcome evidence. Every efficacy comparator that carries weight in education is school-based Verified. Route A may never produce a number anyone respects.
  3. Route B fixes the safeguarding gap honestly. 05 admits that Route A's human review is next business day and that this is not adequate for an acute disclosure. In Route B it routes to a designated safeguarding lead under a policy that already exists. A serious person reading only that admission could reasonably decline to build Route A.
  4. Route B's denominator is better. CAC amortises across a cohort, contracts are annual, and renewal is a negotiation rather than a monthly cancellation decision taken by a tired parent in May.
  5. The consumer market is where the free tools are. Verified Study Mode, Guided Learning and Learning Mode sit in front of exactly the parent Route A must convert, and Google is giving twelve-month subscriptions away at national scale. Schools buy on procurement criteria, evidence and support, which are criteria a free consumer tool does not compete on at all.

Defending the choice to an investor who prefers Route B

The honest defence is not that Route B is wrong. It is that Route B is a better business which this venture cannot currently reach, and that Route A is the only route on which the questions get answered while there is still money to act on the answers.

  • On timing. Route B's answer arrives in about a year and costs a field sales motion to obtain. Route A's first two conditions, C1 and C2, are answerable this quarter for almost nothing. A venture at this stage should buy information in the cheapest available order, and that ordering is not a preference about end-state markets.
  • On the evidence moat. Conceded. It is the best argument against the recommendation, and the reversal asymmetry above makes it sharper. The answer is sequencing rather than disagreement: Route A first to establish that anyone pays, school pilots second to generate the evidence, once there is revenue to fund a second motion. What is being rejected is running both from the start on seed funding, not the value of the evidence.
  • On the demand data. This is where Route B's case is weakest and it should be pressed. The only verified figures available say household tutoring demand is rising and school tutoring provision is falling, with 58% of schools cutting their offer in a single year Verified. A plan to sell into the shrinking side of that picture needs an argument for why the cut means "seeking cheaper supply" rather than "closing the line", and 08 states plainly that we do not have that argument, only an unevidenced hope.
  • On compliance. Conceded again, and it is why C2 is a hard condition rather than a soft one. If the age verification quote comes back large, the investor who prefers Route B has won the argument on the facts, and the recommendation flips without anyone having to lose face.

Route A is recommended on the strength of one genuinely good piece of evidence, the Sutton Trust demand and school-provision figures, against a route whose central objection, the 58% cut, has two readings and we do not know which holds. Both of those are resolvable by asking questions rather than by building. The recommendation should be treated as the current best reading of thin evidence, with three pre-committed tests attached, rather than as a settled strategy.
