Tutor: MVP build guide

The first build of the Tutor is an MVP that runs the whole design for adults who play learners of 14 to 18: one-objective sessions, the verified turn pipeline, the style engine, the self-tutor in the tester's own face and voice, and the parent report. It teaches the algebra of GCSE Maths, Level 2 Further Maths and A-level Maths, drafted by agents and checked by a computer algebra system, and validated by no examiner. The owner builds it by directing a team of Claude Code agents on a harness built first. Speed of building and fidelity to the product come first; running cost and latency come last.

What you are building

The product as 01 describes it, for adult testers. A tester opens an invitation, plays a learner, picks an objective and is taught in a chosen style, then sits the unaided check and hears an honest close. Every turn goes through the pipeline of 06: the composer picks the sources before the generator is called, the generator cites them, the verifier runs its four checks, and a failed turn falls back to the item verbatim. Around the session sit figures rendered from data, practice papers, the provisional grade range, the self-tutor, the parent report, safety routing to a reviewer, and a console where an examiner would validate items.

Three things are not real, and every screen says so. No examiner has validated any item, so P1 is not kept and each item says it is not validated. No child uses it. No creator has signed, so one fictional licensed preset shows the machinery.

The Tutor is shown first by an MVP that matches the product's capabilities as closely as it can, built for speed, with running cost and latency deprioritised, by a harness first and then a team of Claude Code agents. It is not M2's release and decides nothing about the route or which idea goes first.

Five ways to build it, and the one chosen

Only a harness and an agent team are both fast and faithful. The other four lose speed, lose the property that carries the business (P2), or put a likeness with vendors the design would refuse.

PathForAgainst
One agent, plane by planeFaithful and simpleThe slowest faithful path; the owner reviews every step
A harness, then an agent teamFast and faithful; the tests hold it to the designThe most work before the first line of product code
An agent-native tutorFluent sessions with little codeThe model chooses its sources, which 06 rejects, so P2 becomes a hope
Buy the visible layerThe quickest polished self-tutorLikeness vendors that train on uploads or keep media fail D24, D61 and D65
An AI app builderThe quickest first screensToo little control to keep the invariants; usually rebuilt

The harness, before any build prompt

The harness turns the vault into rules the agents cannot skip. It is built in the first phase, with Claude Code, before anything is asked of the product.

  • A CLAUDE.md of invariants, read by every agent: the model never retrieves; nothing reaches the screen before the gate; style never reaches the content plane; the likeness comes only after the gate; no date of birth is stored; nothing can be uploaded; no board material is used.
  • Contracts: the turn plan, the verifier's verdict, the preset object, the style card and the likeness interfaces, as typed schemas shared by every plane.
  • The vault's tests as failing tests: T-GND-01, T-MTH-01, T-SCP-01, T-VAL-01, T-PAP-01, T-GATE-01, T-STY-01 to T-STY-03 and T-SLF-02 to T-SLF-06, each written as code before what it tests exists and marked with the phase by which it must pass. Where a test asks for a live item, the MVP accepts a draft one the algebra system has checked, because none is validated.
  • A frozen battery of objectives, as T-REG-01 specifies, run on every change to a model, prompt, renderer or retrieval rule, and again with no licensed preset, as T-ZERO-01 requires.
  • Hooks that check each file as it is edited, refuse five times over to let a session or an agent finish while the tests of finished phases fail, and refuse any fetch from an exam board's site; a git hook that refuses a merge which breaks those tests; and permissions that ask the owner before a contract, a test or a threshold changes, Assumed (CV-30).
  • Six builders and a reviewer, defined as files in the repository, each builder working in its own copy of it, so that every session starts with the same team, Assumed (CV-30).

The harness comes before any build prompt: the invariants, the contracts, the vault's tests as failing tests, the frozen battery, the hooks, and the agents. Nothing merges while the tests of finished phases fail.

The content

The algebra of three qualifications, drafted in full, checked by machine, validated by no one. The content author decomposes each into objectives and writes ten items for each, the ratio 03 plans with, across its four kinds, each with a worked solution, its misconceptions, a figure specification where one helps, and the provenance record of 03, its status kept at draft. SymPy checks every checkable step, and an item it rejects is rewritten or dropped. How many objectives there are is Unknown until the decomposition.

  • GCSE Mathematics, both tiers, from the algebra of the DfE subject content, Assumed (CV-29).
  • A-level Mathematics, from the algebra and functions of the DfE subject content, Assumed (CV-29).
  • Level 2 Further Mathematics, which the owner called GCSE Further Maths. No DfE content for it was found, and the qualification found is AQA's Level 2 certificate, Assumed (CV-29), from the board whose policy bars the use of its material in training AI tools and in any work produced with AI (EV-21). Each objective is written from the DfE GCSE or A-level content it extends, never from a board's material, and the owner approves the list before items are drafted. This is weak: the agents' sense of what such a course covers may echo the one course found.

Practice papers follow the MVP's own structure, because an algebra-only paper cannot have a board's shape. The MVP serves draft items, which the product never would, and every one carries the label "Not yet validated by an examiner".

The content is the algebra of GCSE Mathematics, Level 2 Further Mathematics and A-level Mathematics, drafted in full by agents under the clean-room rule, checked by the computer algebra system and labelled as not validated. A-level Further Mathematics and Additional Mathematics are out.

DecisionD82, the owner's, 26 October 2026

Both GCSE tiers; GCSE and A-level objectives from the DfE subject content; Further Mathematics objectives from the DfE content each extends, approved by the owner; papers of the MVP's own structure.

The self-tutor on open models

Every part of the self-tutor in 01 is built, and no speech or animation vendor receives a likeness. The voice and face models run on GPUs rented from Modal, which also holds the encrypted likeness store, and conditioning happens at render time, so nothing is trained on a tester.

  • Turning it on: both consents, the tester's confirmation that the face and voice are their own and that they are 18 or over, then capture in the browser: a short head-and-shoulders clip and prompted sentences read aloud, chosen afresh each time. There is no upload route.
  • Liveness: head turns and blinks, in an order chosen afresh, checked by MediaPipe's face landmarker in the browser and again on the server; a passive anti-spoofing model on the server; and Whisper confirming the prompted sentences were spoken, Assumed (CV-26).
  • Rendering after the gate: Chatterbox speaks each passed sentence in the tester's voice, and MuseTalk animates the captured clip to it, Assumed (CV-24, CV-25). If the face is slow, a still likeness speaks; the text never waits.
  • The store: an encrypted volume that only the capture, the two renderers and the deletion job can reach, so nothing that reads a tester can return a face or a voice.
  • Labels and deletion: a visible synthetic label and provenance metadata on every clip, which is streamed and never stored; deletion stops the next turn and clears the store within seven days (D63).

Some open face pipelines depend on InsightFace's pretrained models, which are for non-commercial research only, Assumed (CV-25), so the first phase audits the licence of every model and dependency before any is used (OA-45).

The self-tutor's voice and face run on open models the MVP hosts on rented GPUs, so no speech or animation vendor receives a likeness, and nothing is trained on one (D65).

Testers, safety and data

Adults only, by invitation, and the safety design of 05 runs as written. A message about the tester's own life, feelings or safety gets 05's three moves: a brief acknowledgement, the signposts 05 names, and an escalation to a reviewer's queue, which the owner works to a written policy. Any other message outside the work gets a fixed reply that returns to it. The tutor claims no feelings, never initiates contact and caps the session.

Who sees what. Anthropic and Google see what testers type and the turns being written and checked. Modal runs the app and the open models and holds the encrypted likeness store; Neon holds accounts, transcripts and progress. No speech or animation vendor sees a face or a voice.

Only adults use the MVP, playing learners of 14 to 18. No child uses it, and it holds no child's data.

DecisionD81, the owner's, 26 October 2026

Testers' data: accounts by invitation; transcripts and progress kept for the test period and deleted at its end, with everything else about testers except counts and timings; likenesses under D63's rules; nothing about a tester's life beyond what they type.

What the MVP cannot show

  • Whether learners engage or learn. Adults are playing them, and D14's experiment needs real cohorts.
  • Whether the content is right to P1's standard. No examiner has validated it; how many errors the algebra system misses is OA-46.
  • The product's cost. The MVP is not built to save money, and keeps whole sessions in its window; the cost per session in 10 comes from M2's battery.
  • The safety of a child's session. No child, no age assurance and no safeguarding role beyond the owner.

Left out: payment; real age assurance, for which a simulated check stands in; real creators, for which one fictional licensed preset shows the machinery; Route B's school features; the US course; D29's quarterly rehearsal, for which one rehearsal stands in; and D30's rolling summary, because a whole session fits in the window.

DecisionD88, taken while writing, 26 October 2026

Show it as an MVP, say that nothing in it has been validated, and never let anyone under 18 use it.
