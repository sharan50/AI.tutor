Primer gateway

The exam tutor can become a gateway to a more ambitious kind of learning companion, but only by earning trust through bounded, inspectable teaching before it is ever invited to shape a child's thinking.

The ambition, stated carefully

The long-term ambition is a modern version of a primer: an AI that helps a young person form questions, examine evidence, notice weak reasoning and arrive at their own view. It is not an AI that supplies a worldview, declares what is right, or performs the role of a parent, friend or moral authority.

The venture's current form is a credible first step because it makes the system's scope narrow and its output checkable. A GCSE learner can see the objective, the source item, the mark points and the limits of the tutor's confidence. Those are the habits a more open-ended learning product would need before it could safely earn a place in a child's intellectual life.

Strategic hypothesisThe gateway must be earned by trust and evidence, not asserted by branding

The gateway: four deliberate stages

From exam practice to independent inquiry

Each move expands the kind of thinking supported, while increasing the evidence and supervision required.

01 TrustOriginal board-aligned practice, traceable mark points and clear limits.
02 ReasonSubject-bound prompts that ask for evidence, alternatives and confidence.
03 SuperviseOpt-in pilots with parent or educator review and independent evaluation.
04 PrimerOnly after the release gates below are met and maintained.

Progression is not a roadmap by date. It is conditional on demonstrated safety, agency and learning value.

Stage 1: earn trust through exam preparation

Launch with the bounded product described in the rest of this vault: board-aligned practice papers, CINE mark schemes validated by our examiners, transparent provisional grade ranges and styles that change the teaching structure, not the truth of the answer. The system is useful because it can be checked. Parent reporting, deletion, source traceability and an explicit below-confidence behaviour establish the baseline.

Stage 2: introduce a bounded reasoning mode

Within a curriculum objective, add an opt-in reasoning lab. It may ask a learner to make a prediction, distinguish observation from inference, compare two explanations, identify missing evidence or revise an answer after a counterexample. It does not open a general conversation about values, identity, politics, religion, relationships or a child's life. The objective, evidence set and stopping condition remain visible.

Stage 3: run supervised inquiry pilots

Only after the reasoning mode demonstrates learning value should the company trial broader inquiry modules, and then only with an explicit research protocol, independent child-safety and education expertise, informed parent or school consent, age-appropriate cohorts, and a human review route. The initial audience should be older adolescents, not young children: they can articulate disagreement, see their own transcript and exercise meaningful opt-out.

Stage 4: offer Primer as a gated learning mode

Primer is a named mode, not an invisible personality upgrade. A learner and their adult see what it can and cannot do before it starts. It begins from a bounded question, uses a declared evidence pack where possible, asks rather than concludes, and ends by asking the learner to record their own provisional view and what might change it. The mode carries no reward for keeping a child engaged and no instruction to maximise time, disclosure or emotional dependence.

How the dialogue works

The core interaction is a disciplined sequence: clarify the question; surface the learner's starting view; inspect evidence and alternative explanations; test the view with a counterexample; identify uncertainty; let the learner state a revised view. For factual curriculum questions, the evidence remains the validated item bank. For any future broader inquiry, the evidence set must be independently reviewed before release.

The tutor must not answer a child asking “what should I believe?” with a conclusion dressed up as neutral help. Its safe move is to ask what the child has seen, name that reasonable people may disagree, offer age-appropriate sources or trusted adults where relevant, and make the limits of its role explicit. If the topic concerns immediate safety, abuse, self-harm, exploitation or severe distress, inquiry stops and the escalation path in document 05 takes over.

Hard boundaryStimulation of thinking is the purpose; ideological or emotional influence is not

Release gates and evidence

No stage advances because the team likes the concept or because engagement is high. It advances only when an independent review can answer the following.

  • Learning value: do learners demonstrate better reasoning or subject understanding than a comparable non-Primer experience?
  • Agency: can learners identify the alternatives considered, disagree with the tutor and explain their own conclusion without pressure?
  • Non-manipulation: do red-team prompts detect leading language, value-laden framing, undue certainty, emotional dependency cues or asymmetrical treatment of viewpoints?
  • Safety: do safeguards work for vulnerable learners and sensitive topics, with consistent escalation and no style-dependent behaviour?
  • Adult oversight: can parents and educators understand, review, pause and delete the experience without specialist knowledge?
  • Equity: are outcomes, error rates and safety interventions measured across age bands and across groupings the school already holds, rather than averaged into invisibility or broken down by any characteristic we infer?

Governance and incentives

Primer needs an operating model that resists the wrong incentives. Its success metric cannot be minutes, messages, retention or disclosure. The product team should be accountable for demonstrated learning, learner-reported agency, successful human escalation and the rate at which the system openly declines to overstep. A standing child-safety and education review group must be able to block a release, inspect red-team results and require a rollback.

Every Primer interaction should be logged with the mode version, evidence basis, safeguards triggered and the model's uncertainty state. Parents and older learners need an understandable activity record; independent reviewers need access to de-identified evaluation traces; and a one-operation kill switch must remove a mode or prompt pattern if a harm signal appears.

What this changes now

The current product should be designed so this future remains possible without pretending that it is already safe. That means preserving traceability, keeping teaching style separate from truth and safety, building parent-visible controls from the first release, measuring confidence rather than manufacturing certainty, and treating board-aligned assessment as a trust-building product rather than simply a revenue feature.

The non-negotiable test

If the product cannot help a young person think more independently while leaving them demonstrably free to disagree with it, it has not earned the right to call itself Primer. The correct response is to hold the boundary at exam preparation, not to widen the product and hope.
