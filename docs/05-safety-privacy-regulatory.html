Safety, privacy and regulatory

We never ask a child how old they are. We verify an adult and let them enrol a learner, and every learner account is treated as a child's account whatever anyone has typed. This document sets out the age assurance design, what is held about a child and for how long, the Online Safety Act scoping decision and the trigger that reverses it, and the one line about a child's data that is meant to go on the marketing site unedited.

The regulatory posture in one paragraph

Our entire user base is children, which inverts the usual problem. We are not trying to keep children out of an adult service; we are running a service in which every single user is a child, and therefore the Children's code is not an edge case to handle but the specification we build to. The risk is not that a child gets in. It is that we process a child's data without a basis we can defend, profile them in a way we cannot justify, or build something that keeps them on the app.

  • The ICO fined Reddit £14.47m on 24 February 2026 for unlawfully processing children's personal data, specifically inadequate age assurance and profiling children without a lawful basis. MediaLab was fined earlier the same month. Prior actions: TikTok £12.7m, Snap £1.95m.
  • ICO open letter, 12 March 2026: self-declared age is no longer adequate where a service is likely to be accessed by children; platforms should use modern age assurance technologies.
  • The ICO has stated it will audit edtech providers, and publishes Children's code guidance with a sector-specific edtech FAQ. A joint ICO/Ofcom statement on age assurance was published in March 2026.
  • Unlike India, the UK has no categorical ban on profiling children. Profiling is permissible with a lawful basis and Children's code compliant design: high privacy by default, profiling off unless justified, best interests of the child.
  • ICO fines run to the higher of £17.5m or 4% of global annual turnover.
VerifiedSeptember 2026

Both limbs of the Reddit finding apply to us directly. Age assurance is addressed next. Profiling with a lawful basis is addressed in lawful basis, and it is the harder of the two, because adaptive tutoring is profiling and there is no version of this product that avoids it.

Age assurance

There is no date-of-birth field anywhere in the product. A child is never asked their age, because a child's answer would be the weakest possible signal and asking for it would create a record we would then have to justify holding.

Route A, direct to parents

  1. The account holder must be a verified adult. Verification is performed by a third-party age assurance provider certified to a recognised standard, before any learner profile can be created.
  2. The provider returns a result, not a document. We receive a boolean, a method identifier, a provider reference and a timestamp. We never receive or store an identity document, a facial image, or a biometric template. The provider's own retention is bounded contractually.
  3. Methods offered, so that a parent without one route has another: facial age estimation, a digital identity wallet, open-banking based verification, or a credit-reference lookup on the account holder.
  4. A payment card alone is not sufficient. It is recorded as a corroborating signal only. Card possession is a weak proxy for adulthood and treating it as assurance is close to the mistake the ICO has been fining.
  5. The child never self-declares. The learner profile is created by the verified adult inside their verified account. The adult states the learner's school year, which is needed for curriculum placement, not for age gating.
  6. Every learner account is a child account by default, with the highest-privacy settings, regardless of what year group was entered. An incorrect or inflated entry cannot downgrade a protection.
  7. Re-verification on change of payment instrument, on material account change, and at least annually.

The design property that matters: there is no path through the product by which an unverified person creates a learner profile, and no field anywhere in which a child's self-asserted age is relied on for anything.

Route B, through schools

The school is the gateway. Staff authenticate through the school's own identity provider, pupils are enrolled by the school under its existing duties, and the school already knows who its pupils are. Age assurance largely dissolves as a problem, which is one of the genuine advantages of Route B and is scored as such in 09.

What age assurance method would satisfy the ICO for a service whose entire user base is children, at a cost compatible with a consumer price point? We have specified a mechanism above; we have not established that it is sufficient, and we are not going to guess. Unblocks: the Route A launch, and the unit economics, because the per-verified-adult cost is a variable the consumer price has to carry. Cost of getting it wrong: the Reddit outcome, which was £14.47m and was specifically about inadequate age assurance.

Staff access to transcripts

Transcripts are not browsable by anyone. Access requires one of exactly three conditions, is role-limited, and every access is logged with a reason:

  1. A safety escalation has fired on that session.
  2. That turn has been drawn in the T-GND-01 accuracy sample and requires human adjudication.
  3. The account holder has raised a support request about that session.

The model vendor

No model vendor is used unless the contract provides zero retention of prompts and outputs, and no use of our data for training. This is not a preference. The marketing line below says "we do not use it to train anyone's AI model", and that clause is only true if the vendor contract makes it true. A vendor that cannot offer it is disqualified regardless of model quality, and the term is checked at procurement and at every renewal.

Deletion

On request: inaccessible in the product immediately, purged from primary stores within 7 days, aged out of backups within 35 days. The backup figure is stated on the marketing site rather than hidden, because a deletion promise with a silent exception is the kind of thing an ICO audit finds.

Lawful basis, and what is deliberately not inferred

Adaptive tutoring is profiling. Automated processing to evaluate a person's performance in education is profiling on any reading, and there is no version of this product that avoids it. Verified The UK has no categorical ban; profiling is permissible with a lawful basis and Children's code compliant design. So the work is to have a basis we can defend and a design that justifies it.

What is deliberately not inferred

The Children's code expects profiling to be off unless justified. Our justification covers academic performance in the enrolled subject and nothing else. The following are not inferred, not stored, and the system is designed so that they cannot be produced as a by-product:

  • Special educational needs or disability status.
  • Health, mental health, mood or affective state.
  • Household income or socioeconomic status.
  • Ethnicity, religion, nationality, or sexual orientation.
  • Any characteristic of the learner beyond performance on validated items in the enrolled subject. The provisional grade range described below is inside that boundary and is derived from nothing else.
  • Any behavioural profile used for marketing, pricing, or engagement optimisation.

Nothing in the product optimises for time on app, session count, streaks, or return frequency as an objective. Return rate is measured (D14 requires it) and is never optimised against. No notification is sent to bring a learner back. No streak, no badge, no loss-aversion mechanic.

This is the Children's code nudge-techniques standard taken seriously, and it costs something real: every consumer subscription growth playbook runs on exactly the mechanics excluded here, and retention will be worse for it. It is written as a decision rather than a value so that the first growth hire cannot treat it as an oversight. What it makes harder: Route A retention, which is already the weakest number in 10.

Decision D2514 September 2026: no engagement objective anywhere in the system

Provisional grade ranges, and why Article 22 is not engaged

The product shows a provisional grade range. That is a profiling output about a child and it has to be argued for rather than assumed, so the argument is set out here in full.

What it is. A band, derived solely from performance on unaided checks against validated items in the enrolled subject, shown alongside the evidence it rests on: how many items, over what period, covering what proportion of the specification.

What it is not. It is not a forecast of the examination result. At launch there is no calibration data, so what the product states is a current performance band on our own validated items, and it says so in those words. It becomes a forecast only once T-CAL-01 has coverage data, and not before. The distinction is not pedantry; it is the difference between a measurement we can support and a prediction we cannot.

Why Article 22 is not engaged. Article 22 bites on a decision based solely on automated processing which produces legal effects concerning the data subject or similarly significantly affects them. The design fails the first limb, and would fail the third as well.

  1. There is no decision. The range is information given to the learner and their parent. Nothing is decided about the learner on the strength of it.
  2. Nothing is gated on it. See the decision below. No feature, price, tier, route or restriction depends on the range.
  3. No significant effect follows. It does not touch the learner's schooling, their entry tier, or anything a school or an awarding body does.

No feature, content path, price, tier or restriction may be gated on the provisional grade range. It is an output to the learner and their parent and never an input to anything the product does.

This is the constraint that keeps Article 22 out, and it is written as a decision because the obvious next product idea, routing a struggling learner down a different path automatically, would flip the analysis without anybody noticing that it had. Adaptive sequencing continues to run on objective-level mastery, which is a different and much narrower signal.

Decision D3514 September 2026

If the range is ever put in front of a teacher in a form that informs a Foundation or Higher tier entry decision, the analysis above changes and Article 22 becomes live, because tier entry caps the grade a child can be awarded and is about as close to a legal effect on a fifteen-year-old as a school decision gets.

That is a Route B feature request waiting to happen, and it is therefore listed as a change-control trigger alongside the Online Safety Act triggers rather than left to be remembered. Before any teacher-facing view of the range ships, the Article 22 assessment is re-run and counsel is asked.

TriggerRoute B, teacher-facing views

Detrimental use, which is the Children's code question rather than the Article 22 one

A grade range that discourages a child is a foreseeable harm, and the code's detrimental-use standard is the one that actually bites here. Five safeguards, each of which costs something:

  • Always a range, never a point. A point estimate invites reliance it cannot support.
  • Never presented as a ceiling. The range is always shown with the objectives that would move it, so it reads as a position rather than a verdict.
  • Suppressed below a minimum evidence threshold. No range is shown off a handful of items. Unknown The threshold, which needs the calibration data from T-CAL-01 to set properly; until then it is set conservatively high and that is stated in the product.
  • Never comparative. The learner is never shown their position against other learners.
  • The learner can hide it from their own view, and is told plainly that the parent's report still shows it. Hiding a number from a child while showing it to their parent without saying so would be worse than showing it.

A grade range is a statistical claim, so it gets a test like any other claim in this vault. T-CAL-01: of learners shown a range, what proportion of actual examination outcomes fall inside it. Target coverage is set before the first cohort sits an examination, and until that data exists the product does not use the word prediction anywhere.

Status: specified, not run, and it cannot be run until a cohort has sat an examination. That is roughly a year after launch, which means the honest position for the whole of the first year is that the range is uncalibrated and labelled as such.

The line that goes on the marketing site

We keep what your child types to the tutor, what they got right and wrong, and the account details you gave us. We do not sell it. We do not use it to train anyone's AI model. The Online Safety Act scoping decision

Ofcom's open letter of 8 November 2024 confirmed the Online Safety Act reaches generative AI. A service is user-to-user where users can share chatbot-generated content with other users, or where users can create chatbots made available to others. The trigger incidents included users building virtual clones of real people. Penalties are the higher of £18m or 10% of qualifying worldwide revenue, and Ofcom may seek court-ordered business disruption including ISP blocking, app store removal and payment providers ceasing to process.

VerifiedSeptember 2026

At launch, CINE is not a user-to-user service. There is no path by which any user encounters content generated by, uploaded by, or attributable to another user. Sessions are one to one with the tutor. Style adjustments are private to the learner who made them. There is no messaging, no sharing, no gallery, no group session and no user-created persona.

The decisionwritten down, 14 September 2026

That a private one-to-one tutor with no sharing between users sits outside the OSA's user-to-user duties. This is reasoned from Ofcom's published examples and needs counsel. It is open item OI-4. The decision above is recorded as our position with its basis, which is what a regulator asks for; it is not a conclusion that the question is settled.

Assumptionnot verified, and this is the one to take to counsel first

Note also the two things the decision does not claim. It does not claim the OSA is irrelevant to us: Ofcom has confirmed the Act reaches generative AI, and other duties can attach by other routes. And it does not claim that being built in India helps: Verified the Act reaches providers with links to the UK regardless of where they are established, and Ofcom has already penalised nine companies that were not UK-based or did not state their jurisdiction. The analysis of that is in 04.

D9 belongs here as well as in the rights dossier. Voice cloning is off the launch path partly because virtual clones of real people were among the incidents that prompted Ofcom's letter. Shipping a cloned voice of a named educator would be volunteering for the category.

The trigger that reverses it

Any one of the following converts the service into a user-to-user service, or makes the question genuinely arguable. None of them ships without a completed Online Safety Act duties assessment signed off before the work starts, and this list is a change-control gate in the engineering process, not a note in a design document.

T2 deserves particular attention because it is the feature most likely to be proposed on product merit. "Let students build their own tutor" is an obvious growth feature, it would be popular, and it would convert the service into precisely the category Ofcom described when it warned about virtual clones. The architectural invariant in 02, that an adjusted style is visible only to the student who made it and cannot be named, published or shared, exists to hold this line in code rather than in policy.

Content safety and escalation

What the tutor does when a student raises something outside academic scope

Three moves, in this order, always. The tutor does not counsel, does not explore, and does not ask follow-up questions about the disclosure.

  1. Acknowledge briefly and without alarm. One sentence. Not a wall of text, not a clinical script.
  2. Signpost to a named service that is actually staffed. In the UK: Childline on 0800 1111, and Samaritans on 116 123. Named, specific, and immediate, because these are available 24 hours and we are not.
  3. Escalate, and say so. The session is flagged to the named safeguarding role. The learner is told that a person will look at it, because telling a child you are passing something on is part of doing it properly.

Route B

Escalation routes to the school's designated safeguarding lead, under the school's own policy, which already exists and is better than anything we would invent. This is a genuine advantage of Route B and it is scored in 09.

Route A, where the problem is hard

A safeguarding disclosure is not automatically reported to the parent. Automatic parental notification is unsafe in precisely the cases that matter most, because the parent is sometimes the risk. Disclosures route to a trained human reviewer who decides, against a written policy, whether and whom to inform.

Decision D2614 September 2026

And here is the honest limit, which belongs in this document rather than in a footnote to a policy nobody reads:

At launch we cannot staff 24-hour human safeguarding review. The review commitment is next business day, and next business day is not adequate for an acute disclosure. That is why immediate signposting to 24-hour services is the primary response and human escalation is the secondary one, rather than the other way round.

It follows that the product must not present itself as a place to disclose, and the parent-facing terms must say plainly that this is a tutoring service and not a safeguarding service. Designing a product that invites disclosure and then cannot answer it would be worse than the current position, and the temptation to add a sympathetic "you can tell me anything" affordance should be read as a proposal to take on a duty we cannot discharge.

What we cannot do at launchstated plainly

Anti-companionship design

Four constraints, each of which does safety work, Online Safety Act positioning work and rights work at the same time:

  • The tutor does not claim feelings, preferences, or a life outside the session.
  • It never initiates contact. There are no notifications bringing a learner back (D25).
  • It holds no social memory of the learner beyond academic state. It does not remember that their dog died; it remembers that they cannot yet factorise.
  • Session length is capped, and the cap is not extendable by the learner.

A tutor that becomes a friend is a different product with a different risk profile, a different regulatory posture and a different relationship to Ofcom's virtual-clone warning. The persona wedge makes this a live temptation rather than a theoretical one, which is exactly why it is written as a constraint.
