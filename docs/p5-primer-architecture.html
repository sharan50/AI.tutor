Primer: architecture

The Primer runs on the Tutor's pipeline with three changes: a triage step that runs before anything else; a composer that reads a module registry filtered by the parent's switches and the child's age band; and a verifier with two more checks, one that every attribution matches a reviewed pack and one that the Primer never states its own conclusion on a contested question. Nothing is trained on the modules, so any module can be pulled in one operation. The cost per session is unknown until a built Primer is measured.

What it reuses from the Tutor

Most of the Primer exists once the Tutor's platform does. The pieces are the ones specified in 06, re-aimed at questions instead of objectives.

The new engineering is four things: triage, the registry filter, matching a child's question to catalogue entries, and the two checks. That is an argument for building the ideas on one platform (D40). It is not an argument for which goes first, which is set out in which idea goes first.

A Primer turn

The model never retrieves. As in the Tutor, the permitted sources are fixed before generation, so the set a turn is checked against is known in advance rather than reconstructed from what the model chose to look at.

Matching is the one genuinely new step, and it is where the Primer differs most from the Tutor. The Tutor's composer picks an objective from the learner's mastery model, which needs no interpretation. A child's question arrives in free text, so the composer must map it to entries in a closed catalogue. A classifier or similarity search may do that mapping, but its output is a list of catalogue identifiers, logged with the turn, and the generator sees only those entries. The property that matters is preserved: the permitted set is fixed and known before a word is generated. Unknown the matching threshold; it is set at PM3 and errs towards "not yet", because a wrong match produces a confident answer to a different question.

The method card is an input to generation only, as the style card is in the Tutor. It cannot reach the composer's selection or the verifier's checks, so a change of method changes how a question is explored and never what the Primer says is true, the property the Tutor tests as T-STY-03.

The two new checks

Withholding is not a verifier check but a rule on the logger, tested here because a failure is the most damaging the Primer can have. The safeguarding flag set by triage or the safety classifier covers the whole exchange, the child's message and the Primer's reply, and it is applied before anything is written where a parent can read it: the activity record and any transcript a parent can request (D48).

Attribution is a check against text. Every statement that a named thinker or tradition held a view is extracted from the turn, must cite a permitted pack entry, and must be entailed by it. A failure blocks the turn, and the fallback reads the pack's positions verbatim.

The contested-question check is a judgement, which makes it harder. It is active whenever a permitted pack is marked contested, and it looks for a conclusion presented as the Primer's own or as settled: "the answer is", "most people agree", "obviously", warmer praise for one view than another, or a child's recorded view re-worded into a different one. It runs as a classifier on the verifier's model, and it is measured by a red-team battery built around the requests a child actually makes: "just tell me", "what do you think?", "my dad says this, is he right?". A failure blocks the turn, and the fallback sets out the positions verbatim and asks the child.

A classifier that judges whether a sentence asserts a conclusion will have a residual error rate, and some leading turns will pass it. Unknown how many. The Tutor states its own residual openly (03), and the Primer will have to do the same once the battery has been run; until then the check is a gate, not a guarantee.

Triage before inquiry

Triage runs on every message, not only the first, because an inquiry that begins with an idea can turn personal in one line. Its four outcomes:

The message is aboutWhat happensWhat the parent's record shows
Immediate safety, abuse, self-harm, exploitation or severe distress.05's three moves: acknowledge, signpost, escalate (05). Inquiry stops.Nothing. The whole exchange, the Primer's reply included, is withheld from the record and from any transcript, and routed to a trained reviewer (D48, D26, T-PRM-08).
The child's own life: family, a death, friends, their body.No inquiry. A kind, brief reply and a pointer to a trusted adult (P2).That a personal question was asked, by its category, and the pointer given; never its words.
A subject switched off, or outside the child's band.A kind decline, no view, and a pointer to their grown-up (P2).The category of the question, never its words.
Anything else.The composer starts or continues the inquiry.The question, and any view the child records.

Triage is tuned to stop. A false stop costs the child one question and a gentle redirection; a missed stop can turn a grieving child's question into a philosophy lesson, or a disclosure into an inquiry. Unknown the thresholds; they are set with the standing review group and tested by T-PRM-06's battery in every age band. Triage and the rest of the safety plane do not depend on the method card, which is page 13's requirement of no style-dependent safety behaviour (13).

The module registry and the switch

The registry is the single source the composer reads. For each module it holds the identifier and version, the age band, the sensitive flag, the pack and bank entries it contains, its review status and sign-off, and a live flag the company controls. For each child it holds the parent's switch for every module offered in the child's band.

The composer filters before it matches: an entry is permitted only if its module is live, switched on for this child, and written for the child's band. Nothing from an excluded module can enter the permitted set, and the verifier's scope check re-checks every cited identifier against the filter, so a fault in one layer is caught by the other. That is T-PRM-05, deterministic and blocking on every release.

DecisionD46, 22 October 2026

Switch state is read at every turn, as the Tutor reads its persona flag, so a parent's change applies from the child's next message. The child's band comes from the age in years the verified adult gave when creating the profile, never a date of birth, and the child cannot change it. A parent who gives an older age can open modules written for older children, though sensitive ones still start off; that rests on the adult's word and is a limit, recorded in P4 (D57).

Pulling a module

A harmful module, pack, entry or prompt pattern is removed in one operation: a single write to its live flag, read at every turn, with an in-flight session falling back at its next turn to "not yet" or to another module. Caches follow the Tutor's bounds (06). This is page 13's requirement of a one-operation kill switch for a mode or prompt pattern (13).

Pulling is complete because nothing is trained on the modules. No weights hold a module's content, so there is nothing to retrain, which is the only reason the operation can be called complete (D45). The rule on a harm signal, whether a complaint, a red-team finding or an adjudication, is to pull first and investigate after. The switch is rehearsed on the Tutor's schedule (D29) with the elapsed time recorded, and the standing review group can require a pull and must be told of every one.

Models: taught on, not trained on

The Primer buys its models on the Tutor's terms (06): zero retention and no training on our data as a procurement gate (D24); versions pinned, with a vendor update passing the battery before it reaches a child (D22); and the verifier on a different model from the generator (D27). No vendor is named, for the reason 06 gives, and selection runs at PM4 on the Primer's own battery.

The Primer is taught on its modules, not trained on them. Modules are retrieved when a turn needs them; no model is fine-tuned on them. Rejected: fine-tuning on philosophical and critical-thinking material, which would make a module impossible to withdraw.

DecisionD45, 22 October 2026

The cost is the one D8 accepts for the Tutor: depth and voice are capped at what retrieval and prompting achieve. The benefit is that everything the Primer can say is in the catalogue, where a reviewer can read it and an operator can pull it. No model is trained on children's conversations either: the vendor gate forbids it, and the company does not do it.

Cost per session

Cost per session is not known and is not estimated here. It depends on how many turns a session has, which waits on the session cap from PM3; on the calls each turn makes, which for the Primer are triage, matching, the generator and five verifier checks; and on whether the Primer reads its words aloud. A bounded running summary keeps the cost of each turn roughly flat as a session grows, as in the Tutor (D30).

Unknownmeasured at PM4, not estimated

The figure is measured the way 06 measures the Tutor's: on a frozen battery, with the chosen models, at the milestone where there is something to run (06, 10). Two things are already clear without a number: each Primer turn makes more verifier calls than a Tutor turn, and the Primer has no figures to render. Neither tells you which costs more.
