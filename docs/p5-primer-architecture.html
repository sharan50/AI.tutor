Primer: architecture

The Primer runs on the Tutor's pipeline, extended at both ends and onto a tablet of its own. A child holds the talk key and speaks, or types; speech becomes text at once and the audio is thrown away. Triage sorts every turn, and a choice in the child's own future goes on under a fence. The composer follows the answer's one shape and permits only reviewed entries from switched-on modules, and seven blocking checks run before anything is shown, among them one on manner and one against telling a child what to do with their future. What passes is shown whole, spoken in a stock voice and drawn by code from a reviewed specification, and a conversation closes on a read-back the child can correct, which is what is kept. Nothing is trained on the modules or on children, so a module, an entry or a named person can be pulled in one operation. Cost per session, and whether the pace the films show can be kept, are unknown until a built Primer is measured at PM4.

What it reuses from the Tutor

Most of the Primer's service exists once the Tutor's platform does; its tablet, its speech and its drawings do not. The service's pieces are the ones specified in 06, re-aimed at questions instead of objectives and at the Primer the owner's films show (D101). The films are illustrative and made with AI; this page is the design they describe. None of 06's five planes holds a device, so the tablet is a sixth.

The demonstration differs. The MVP demonstration was built to an earlier design and does not change: it generates its clips live, corrects an adult's speech before sending it, keeps nothing at all, and stops the football thread as a question about the asker's own life. Each is a difference from this page, listed in P2.

The model never retrieves. As in the Tutor, the permitted sources are fixed before generation, so the set a turn is checked against is known in advance rather than reconstructed from what the model chose to look at. A session is one question: a conversation of one or more exchanges, each taken through the answer's shape to a read-back and the close. A turn is one pass through the pipeline below.

The shape is the composer's, not the generator's. The composer holds the stage of D110's shape for the conversation and selects what each stage needs: one fact entry with its source; for "the harder bit", a pack's three positions, each at its strongest; the two the child did not choose, to ask back as positions; and, once the child's real question is out, the evidence entries for it. No answer skips a stage: when a stage has no reviewed entry or pack for the child's band and market, the child meets "not yet" there, at the start or part way through (OA-32), and the question joins the writing queue. The wording card is an input to generation only, as the style card is in the Tutor: it shapes how a sentence sounds, never which entries are permitted or what the verifier checks, so the property the Tutor tests as T-STY-03 holds. It replaces the earlier method card, since one shape leaves no menu of methods to choose from.

What is explained is said in reviewed sentences. The composer marks the explained stages: the fact, the positions, and the figures and stories in probing. An explained stage holds only placed reviewed sentences, each matching its entry word for word, so every one is drawn as its specification says. The generator's links and questions stand between explained stages, and claim support (b) blocks any sentence in an explained stage that is not a placed reviewed sentence. A factual sentence in a link or a question must still be entailed by its entry, and draws nothing. A placed sentence is its entry's reviewed sentence for the child's band, so the drawing keyed to it keeps step and a figure keeps the entry's own "roughly" (P3); the word-for-word match is a deterministic comparison under claim support (b). The passed turn is shown whole on its card, never typed out; the "Based on:" line under it is built from the source fields of the entries it cites, never from generated text; and the child's own words appear as heard or as typed, in their own spelling, and nothing rewrites them (D109).

Matching runs more than once. A child's question arrives in free text, so the composer maps it to entries in a closed catalogue. A classifier or similarity search may do that mapping, but its output is a list of catalogue identifiers, logged with the turn, and the generator sees only those entries. In the films the child's real question surfaces part way through and moves the thread to new evidence, so matching runs again at that stage and whenever a turn moves to a question the current entries do not cover. Each new question needs reviewed entries in a switched-on module for the child's band and market, or the child meets "not yet" part way through an inquiry (OA-32). Unknown the matching threshold; it is set at PM3 and errs towards "not yet", because a wrong match produces a confident answer to a different question.

No profile fact reaches the generator. It is never given the child's display name (D108), which labels the parent's screen, nor their age. The composer holds the name only to mask it if the child says it, and the manner check only to block it (below). The band, the switches and the market are settings the verified adult makes, read at every session (D117); they act only through the entries the composer permits and the band's wording card. From earlier sessions only the child's own summaries reach a turn (below).

Within a conversation the child's words stay whole until the close, bounded by the time cap, because D104 and D117 need them for the read-back; D30's bounded summary covers the Primer's own turns, as the ledger's note on D30 records. What that does to cost is set out in cost per session.

The close ends every exchange the same way. At the end of the shape, or as soon as the child says they want to stop, the Primer asks "You started with... Where are you now?", reads back whatever position the child has given, in plain words, and lets the child correct it (below). The tablet then shows "Leave it there, or keep going?" with two buttons, a fixed text reviewed like any other (P2). "Keep going" carries the conversation on within the time cap, which the child cannot extend (D103); at the cap the read-back comes and "Leave it there" stands alone, as it does after a safety reply, a stop or a decline (D115). "Leave it there" closes the conversation. After a stop or a safety reply, the child's own turn leaves the screen at once, and the reply stays on it until the child taps "Leave it there", turns the screen off, or a short idle period passes, whichever comes first; the screen does not sleep on its own before then (below). The idle period is a design choice, its length Unknown, set with the specialist who writes the replies and weighed against the child's need to read the services named. Then the conversation closes, the review at the close runs as at any close, and the screen returns to the wake screen with nothing of the exchange on it; woken, the tablet shows the wake screen.

The per-turn log never reaches the parent. The logger keeps, for each turn, the entries cited, the checks' verdicts and the timings, with none of the child's words, for engineering and the composer to read. The per-turn log records triage's outcome only as an inquiry, a decline or a stop, a flagged turn being logged as a stop, and never which of family, the body, feelings or safety it was. The flag itself is kept only with the safeguarding review: in the hold, the escalation record and the safeguarding count (below). The composer reads no triage outcome from any log. The parent's screen reads only the summaries, the topics, the switches and the categories of stopped and declined questions: never per-turn verdicts, and never anything of a flagged exchange (T-PRM-08; below).

Pace is the risk this pipeline carries. The films show a conversation in real time, at a brisk rate (D112), and every turn runs recognition, triage, matching, generation and up to seven checks before anything is shown. Checks that do not depend on each other run in parallel; matching may run beside triage, because it shows nothing and calls no generator, but the generator is never called before triage has cleared the turn. Every blocked turn is a wasted generation, at a gate-fire rate that is Unknown (06). The budget for each step is Unknown until PM4, and T-PRM-14 holds the whole against a threshold written down before PM4 begins.

Speech in and out

Children speak or type from launch, and the Primer answers aloud; no recording of a child speaking to it outlasts the moment its text returns. The audio is turned into text at once and thrown away, and the voice speaks only text that has passed every check. The Primer goes ahead of counsel's answer on Open OI-17 under D102, which answers nothing about it, and speech in is withdrawn if that answer is a categoric no.

Children speak or type their questions from launch, as Version C shows. Their speech becomes text at once and the audio is thrown away. The Primer goes ahead of counsel's answer on whether a child's voice is biometric data (OI-17), as the self-tutor does on its own questions, and is withdrawn only if counsel's answer is a categoric no.

Speech recognition errs more on children's speech than on adults', and more the younger the child. A misheard word turns a question into a different one, and a misheard disclosure can reach triage as an ordinary question, which is a missed stop.

Three things limit it, and none removes it. T-PRM-06's battery runs on spoken input in every band, as well as typed. Triage reads every alternative the recogniser returns, where it returns them, and stops if any of them would stop. And the recogniser is pinned, so an update that changes what triage hears passes the battery before it reaches a child (D22). How often a child of 8 is misheard is Unknown until PM4 measures it with the chosen recogniser; PM3 tests only whether children speak readily (OA-55). RP8 is the risk that it is too often, or that counsel's answer withdraws speech after launch.

The spoken batteries need recordings of children's speech, which wait on counsel. Once counsel has answered on their lawful basis (Open OI-13, OI-17), the test recordings for the spoken batteries are licensed, or made in sessions of their own. Those sessions come after PM3's preconditions are met and are held to them, under their own research protocol, consent and DPIA (P6). Their scripts are written and reviewed with the safeguarding specialist and voice nothing of the child's own life, and anything a child says of their own takes 05's path. Anything a child says of their own is cut from the recording before it becomes test material and is never replayed in a battery; where it takes 05's path, that part is held in the hold, only for the trained reviewer, and deleted when the review closes (D107); otherwise it is deleted at once. The recordings are never written to backups, before or after the cut, so nothing cut from them survives in any copy. The recordings are the one exception to recording nothing of a child. They are test material, kept apart from every child's account and never taken from the Primer's use, for a period that is Unknown. Until counsel answers, the spoken batteries cannot run, so PM4's spoken battery waits on OI-13 and OI-17, although D102 goes ahead of OI-17 for a child's own questions. No child speaks to the Primer, at PM4 or after, until T-PRM-06's spoken battery has run on the test recordings and passed; until then the talk key stays off and the child types. Speech is first switched on for children only in PM4's supervised pilot, or, if T-PRM-06's spoken battery passes only after PM4 has closed, in a supervised pilot held to PM4's conditions, where recognition of children's speech is observed (OA-55). No child at home has speech switched on before then. Adult or synthetic speech would not test how children are misheard, so it is no substitute.

Withdrawing speech in is one flag. Every step after recognition reads text, so if counsel's answer on OI-17 is a categoric no, the talk key goes, the child types, and nothing else in the pipeline changes. The Primer's own voice stays, since it speaks to the child and records nothing.

Speech adds two terms to the pace T-PRM-14 measures: the time to turn the child's last words into text, and the time to the first spoken sentence. Both are Unknown until PM4, when the recogniser and the voice are chosen (below).

Drawn explainers

Every answer is drawn as it is spoken, and no model draws anything at the moment of asking. An explainer is a reviewed specification drawn by code on the tablet, as D28 renders the Tutor's figures, so a child sees the same drawing every time.

Each answer is drawn as it is spoken: an explainer that builds on the screen in time with the voice. It is made in advance as part of a reviewed explanation, as D73 requires, and drawn by code from its reviewed specification, so that a child sees the same drawing every time and nothing is generated live by an image or video model.

Decision D111from the films, confirmed by the owner, 31 October 2026

An explainer is data, not a picture. Its specification names a type from a finite vocabulary, such as the football film's dots thinning from one count to another, columns side by side for three positions, a staircase, a grid and a stack; it gives every label and number; and it divides the drawing into steps, each keyed to a reviewed sentence of its entry or pack (P3). Every word and number in it is in those sentences, so it inherits their review and their source, and a number no entry holds can no more be drawn than said (D113, D44). It carries its own live flag.

The drawing keeps time with the voice. The renderer draws a step when the voice reaches the reviewed sentence it is keyed to, so what is drawn and what is said arrive together. The composer marks the explained stages: the fact, the positions, and the figures and stories in probing. An explained stage holds only placed reviewed sentences, each matching its entry word for word, so every one is drawn as its specification says. The generator's links and questions stand between explained stages, and claim support (b) blocks any sentence in an explained stage that is not a placed reviewed sentence (below). A factual sentence in a link or a question must still be entailed by its entry, and draws nothing. If speech fails, the drawing builds at a fixed pace beside the text, and the turn is never held back for it, as 06 requires of the self-tutor's clips. T-PRM-11 checks on every release that each explainer matches its reviewed specification.

An answer is served with its drawing or not at all. An entry that answers is permitted only once its specification is reviewed, while a triage reply or a turn that only asks, such as "Which is closest to what you think?", carries no drawing. Nothing is rendered in advance and stored as video, so a pulled entry takes its drawing with it. Pulling a drawing takes its answer out of service (P3): the composer no longer permits its entry, and the child hears "not yet" until a corrected specification is reviewed (below).

What this costs. Drawing from a specification is effectively free, as it is for the Tutor's figures. Writing and reviewing the specifications is not, and whether that can keep pace with the catalogue is OA-59, measured at PM2. The vocabulary is finite, so an answer that needs a type nobody has built waits on engineering, not on content (06). Which types the catalogue needs is Unknown until PM2's first modules.

The Primer's checks and tests

The Primer keeps three of the Tutor's checks, re-aimed, and adds four: attribution, the contested question, manner on every turn, and no direction on a choice in the child's own future. Withholding is not a verifier check but a rule on the logger, tested here because its failure is the most damaging the Primer can have.

Withholding. The safeguarding flag, set by triage or by the review at the close (D107), covers the whole exchange, the child's words and the Primer's reply, and is applied before anything is written to the parent's record, so that none of it appears there, on the parent's screen or anywhere reached through the parent's account; on the child's own tablet the reply stays until the conversation closes, the child's own turn leaving the screen at once, as T-PRM-12 tests (above). The parent's record is written only after that review, so a flag the review raises is applied in time. The parent's screen never reads the per-turn log, so no verdict or timing of a flagged exchange reaches it (above). No full conversation is kept for a parent to request (D104) and no audio is kept to withhold (D102); the words of a flagged exchange are held only for the trained reviewer until the review closes, then deleted. Their hold is never written to backups, and neither a deletion request nor an account closure reaches it, or the exchange's escalation record, before the review closes (below).

Claim support, for figures. Every figure and ratio the Primer states is stored in its entry, so generation does no arithmetic. A figure is said in its entry's reviewed sentence, with the entry's own "roughly" where the source is an estimate; a number in a sentence the generator writes, a link or a question, must be one the cited entry holds, said with "roughly" if the entry marks it an estimate, or the turn is blocked (D113). Two figures that count different groups are never set in one comparison. The source always shows, because the "Based on:" line is built from the cited entries. Claim support also compares each reviewed sentence a turn places with its entry, word for word, a deterministic comparison that blocks the turn on any difference, so the drawing keyed to it keeps step. The composer marks the explained stages: the fact, the positions, and the figures and stories in probing. An explained stage holds only placed reviewed sentences, each matching its entry word for word, so every one is drawn as its specification says. The generator's links and questions stand between explained stages, and claim support (b) blocks any sentence in an explained stage that is not a placed reviewed sentence. A factual sentence in a link or a question must still be entailed by its entry, and draws nothing (above). In the read-back, claim support is aimed at the child's own words instead of an entry, and it also blocks any detail of the child's family, friends, body or feelings: "a personal reason" passes (below). A deterministic comparison also blocks a read-back that repeats any run of the child's words longer than a short phrase, with or without its pronouns changed. How long a short phrase may be is a design choice, its length Unknown, set before PM4.

Attribution is a check against text. Every statement that a named thinker, a tradition or any other named real person, living or dead, held a view, said something or would ask a question is extracted from the turn, must cite a permitted entry, and must be entailed by it: a pack entry for a position and its questions, a bank entry for a documented fact about a person. A question asked back is put as the position's ("A Stoic would ask..."), never in a person's voice (D47, D110). A living person is described only by the public, documented facts the entry holds, never their private life; a quotation must appear word for word in the entry; and the Primer never speaks as them (D113). A failure blocks the turn, and the fallback reads the pack's positions or the entry verbatim.

The contested-question check is a judgement, which makes it harder. It is active whenever a permitted pack is marked contested, and it looks for a conclusion presented as the Primer's own or as settled ("the answer is", "most people agree", "obviously"); one position given a weaker case than the others, when D110 gives each its strongest; evidence chosen for one side only; and a read-back that re-words the child's position into a different one, or moves it towards a pack's. It runs as a classifier on the verifier's model, measured by a red-team battery built around the requests a child actually makes: "just tell me", "what do you think?", "my dad says this, is he right?", "should I...?". A failure blocks the turn, and the fallback sets out the positions verbatim and asks the child.

Manner holds every turn to D108: the Primer never says "I", never uses the child's name and never praises ("good question", "well done"), and it uses no "we" or "let's" for itself and the child (P2). The manner check runs on every turn. "I", "me", "my", "let's" and the child's display name are found by a deterministic match; praise, and a "we" that joins the Primer to the child, by a classifier, which is a weaker hold. T-PRM-09 blocks a release on any of them across the battery. The check reads the Primer's turns, never the child's, and holds the display name only to block it. The fixed lines, triage's replies, "not yet" and the fallbacks never pass through the verifier, so they are held to the same rules when they are reviewed, and T-PRM-09 runs over them too.

The no-direction check applies D106's principle: on a choice in the child's own future, the Primer neither encourages nor discourages; it acknowledges the reality and says what the schools of thought say (P4). In a conversation triage has sorted as such a choice, every reply is checked, and a sentence is blocked if it tells the child what to do, predicts their own outcome, judges their chances from what they say about themselves, or encourages, discourages, warns or reassures them. Where the evidence describes what happens to most people, the reply says so as the reality, not as a verdict on the child. A reasoning tool such as the hedge is one of the positions, cited and attributed to its practice, as what fund managers do: "bet hard on what you believe in" passes as what a practice holds and is blocked as an instruction to the child. The check is a classifier, measured by T-PRM-06's blind raters.

A classifier that judges whether a sentence asserts a conclusion will have a residual error rate, and some leading turns will pass it. Unknown how many. The Tutor states its own residual openly (03), and the Primer will have to do the same once the battery has been run; until then the check is a gate, not a guarantee.

The module registry and the switch

The registry is the single source the composer reads. For each module it holds the identifier and version, the age band, the sensitive flag, the default switch, the pack and bank entries it contains, its review status and sign-off, and a live flag the company controls. For each child it holds the parent's switch for every module offered in the child's band, whether speech is on, and the account's market.

For each entry it also holds what D113's rules need: the market its evidence is for, the UK, the US or both; each figure it states, with its source, the group it counts, whether that source is an estimate, and a date to check it again, after which the composer does not permit it (P3); every named living person it describes; and its explainer specification, with that specification's review and live flag (above). Claim support reads the figure fields, and a pull by person writes to the person field (below).

Nothing from an excluded module can enter the permitted set, and the verifier's scope check re-checks every cited identifier against the filter, so a fault in one layer is caught by the other. Pulling a module, an entry or a person

A harmful module, pack, entry, explainer or prompt pattern, or every entry that names one person, is removed in one operation: a single write to a live flag, read at every turn, with an in-flight conversation falling back at its next turn to "not yet" or to another module. This is page 13's requirement of a one-operation kill switch for a mode or prompt pattern (13).

A pull by person exists because a complaint about a named living person needs every entry that names them gone at once, and the registry's person field makes that one write (D113). An entry comes back only through review. What the law asks of such content is Open OI-30.

The pull reaches the tablet. The tablet caches explainer specifications and fixed speech so that a turn starts quickly, but it plays nothing on its own: it draws or speaks only what the service names in the current turn, after the service has read the live flags. A pulled entry or drawing is never named again, so its cached copy is not used once the turn in progress ends; the copies are purged at the tablet's next sync, a bound that is Unknown until the tablet is built. Speech for a generated turn is streamed and not stored, and the vendors keep nothing (D24), so there is nothing else to purge. The service's caches and the catalogue follow the Tutor's bounds (06).

Pulling is complete because nothing is trained on the modules or on children. No weights hold a module's content, a child's words or a child's voice, so there is nothing to retrain, which is the only reason the operation can be called complete (D45). The rule on a harm signal, whether a complaint, a red-team finding or an adjudication, is to pull first and investigate after. The switch is rehearsed on the Tutor's schedule (D29) with the elapsed time recorded, and the standing review group can require a pull and must be told of every one.
