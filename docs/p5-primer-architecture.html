Primer: architecture

The Primer runs on the Tutor's pipeline, extended at both ends and onto a tablet of its own. Speech is the main way in: a child holds the talk key and speaks, or types, and speech becomes text at once while the audio is thrown away. Triage sorts every turn, and a choice in the child's own future goes on under a fence. The composer follows the answer's one shape and retrieves each turn's sources from a reviewed allow-list, or a reviewed entry where one exists, and eight blocking checks run before anything is spoken or shown, among them one on manner, one on suitability for the child's age and one against telling a child what to do with their future. The Primer speaks a short answer at once; while it speaks, a video model generates an explainer for each concept from a checked script, and each video plays when its own checks pass. A selection model chooses what is spoken and what is shown, and a conversation closes on a read-back the child can correct, which is what is kept. Nothing reaches a child that has not passed the automatic checks, but most of it is never seen by a person first. No model is trained on anything a child said or holds a module's content, so a module, a source, a named person or generation itself can be pulled in one operation. Cost per session, the speed of live video, and whether the pace the films show can be kept are unknown until a built Primer is measured at PM4.

A session is one question: a conversation of one or more exchanges, each taken through the answer's shape to a read-back and the close. The shape is the composer's, not the generator's. The composer holds the stage of D110's shape for the conversation and retrieves what each stage needs: sources for a short answer at a high level; for "the harder bit", three positions, each at its strongest, from a pack where one exists and otherwise from retrieved sources, each attributed (OA-58); the two the child did not choose, to ask back as positions; and, once the child's real question is out, the evidence for it. The owner's "both sides of the argument" is read as these three positions (D122). No answer skips a stage: when no source supports a stage for the child's band and market, the child meets "not yet" there, at the start or part way through (OA-32), and the question, in general terms, joins the writing queue (P3). The wording card is an input to generation only, as the style card is in the Tutor: it shapes how a sentence sounds, never which sources are permitted or what the verifier checks, so the property the Tutor tests as T-STY-03 holds. It replaces the earlier method card, since one shape leaves no menu of methods to choose from.

The answer comes at once, and the videos while it is spoken. The first stage is a short answer, at a high level, from the sources retrieved for the question, checked automatically before it is spoken. While it is spoken, the generator writes the script for each explainer, each on one specific concept; the script is checked, and a video model generates the video from it. Each video plays when its checks pass (D122; below). The conversation goes on through the harder bit, three positions, the child's real question and the evidence, to the child's own view, read back, and each later stage may carry explainers the same way. Each script is written only from the concept, named in general terms, and the turn's retrieved sources, never from the turn's text. Before a video is generated or cached, the script's checks add a deterministic block on any name, place or uncommon phrase from the child's turns, and the read-back's block on any detail of family, friends, body or feelings, so no script and no video holds the child's words (D126). The video model is given a visual brief with no words and no figures, and every word and figure on a video is laid over it on the tablet from the checked script. A turn that D127 holds to reviewed content shows no generated video, cached or new, and a cached video plays in a contested or own-future conversation only if its script passed the contested-question and no-direction checks (d and g) there, or passes them again (below).

What is spoken and what is shown are chosen, never written. The Primer writes its content first; the voice, the videos and the words on the screen are all drawn from it, so no captions are needed. A selection model, trained for the purpose, chooses for each checked sentence whether it is spoken, shown or both; it writes nothing, and the whole text is a tap away. Figures and the "Based on:" line are always shown, and a safety reply is shown and never spoken (D115, D124). The "Based on:" line is built from the sources the turn and its scripts cite, never from generated text, and the child's own words appear as heard or as typed, in their own spelling, and nothing rewrites them (D109). Whether children follow an answer split this way as well as one shown whole is OA-63, measured at PM4.

Retrieval runs more than once. A child's question arrives in free text. The composer matches it to the reviewed entries first, since a reviewed entry is used first wherever one exists, and otherwise retrieves passages from the reviewed allow-list, from sources reviewed for the child's range only (P3; below). A classifier or similarity search may do either, but its output is a list of identifiers, of entries and of passages, logged with the turn, and the generator sees only what they name. In the films the child's real question surfaces part way through and moves the thread to new evidence, so retrieval runs again at that stage and whenever a turn moves to a question the current sources do not cover. A fact or story about a named living person, or anything in a sensitive module, comes only from reviewed entries and packs, never from live generation (D127), so there the child meets "not yet" wherever no reviewed entry exists (OA-32). Unknown the thresholds for a match and for a passage to count; they are set at PM3 and err towards "not yet", because a wrong match produces a confident answer to a different question.

No profile fact reaches the generator. It is never given the child's display name (D108), which labels the parent's screen, nor their age. The composer holds the name only to mask it if the child says it, and the manner check only to block it (below). The band, the switches and the market are settings the verified adult makes, read at every session (D117); they act only through what the composer retrieves and permits, and through the band's wording card. From earlier sessions only the child's own summaries reach a turn (below).

Within a conversation the child's words stay whole until the close, bounded by the time cap, because D104 and D117 need them for the read-back; D30's bounded summary covers the Primer's own turns, as the ledger's note on D30 records. What that does to cost is set out in cost per session.

The close ends every exchange the same way. At the end of the shape, or as soon as the child says they want to stop, the Primer asks "You started with... Where are you now?", reads back whatever position the child has given, in plain words, and lets the child correct it (below). The tablet then shows "Leave it there, or keep going?" with two buttons, a fixed text reviewed like any other (P2). "Keep going" carries the conversation on within the time cap, which the child cannot extend (D103); at the cap the read-back comes and "Leave it there" stands alone, as it does after a safety reply, a stop or a decline (D115). "Leave it there" closes the conversation. After a stop or a safety reply, the child's own turn leaves the screen at once, and the reply stays on it until the child taps "Leave it there", turns the screen off, or a short idle period passes, whichever comes first; the screen does not sleep on its own before then (below). The idle period is a design choice, its length Unknown, set with the specialist who writes the replies and weighed against the child's need to read the services named. Then the conversation closes, the review at the close runs as at any close, and the screen returns to the wake screen with nothing of the exchange on it; woken, the tablet shows the wake screen.

The per-turn log never reaches the parent. The logger keeps, for each turn, the sources cited, the videos played by their identifiers, the checks' verdicts and the timings, with none of the child's words, for engineering, the composer and reviewers tracing a report to read. The per-turn log records triage's outcome only as an inquiry, a decline or a stop, a flagged turn being logged as a stop, and never which of family, the body, feelings or safety it was. The flag itself is kept only with the safeguarding review: in the hold, the escalation record and the safeguarding count (below). The composer reads no triage outcome from any log. The parent's screen reads only the summaries, the topics, the switches and the categories of stopped and declined questions: never per-turn verdicts, and of a flagged exchange only a notice that a question was flagged for safety and a trained reviewer is handling it, once the reviewer has judged it safe to tell this parent, holding no words, no subject and no reply (D121, T-PRM-08; below).

Pace is the risk this pipeline carries. The films show a conversation in real time, at a brisk rate (D112), and every turn runs recognition, triage, retrieval, generation and up to eight checks before anything is spoken. Checks that do not depend on each other run in parallel; retrieval may run beside triage, because it shows nothing and calls no generator, but the generator is never called before triage has cleared the turn. The videos add a script, its checks, generation and an image check after the answer has begun, so they hold up no answer, but a video may come after the stage it explains. A stop, a safety reply, a decline or the close cancels every script and video still pending in the conversation, and the service names none of them to the tablet afterwards, so no late video follows a stop. A flag, raised by triage or by the review at the close, pulls every script and video generated in the flagged exchange from the cache and sends them to the sample review. Every blocked turn and every failed video is a wasted generation, at a rate that is Unknown (06). The budget for each step is Unknown until PM4. T-PRM-14 holds the time to the spoken answer against a threshold written down before PM4 begins; the time to the first video is observed, and is Unknown (OA-62).

Speech in and out

Speech is the main way in, typing is always there, and the Primer answers aloud; no recording of a child speaking to it outlasts the moment its text returns. The audio is turned into text at once and thrown away, and the voice speaks only checked sentences. Counsel has cleared a child speaking to the Primer, and the recording of children's speech with consent for testing, in the UK and the US, as the owner reported on 4 November 2026 (D118). The owner expects about three in four questions to be spoken (D123), Assumed (OA-61).

Children speak or type their questions from launch, as Version C shows. Their speech becomes text at once and the audio is thrown away. The Primer goes ahead of counsel's answer on whether a child's voice is biometric data (OI-17), as the self-tutor does on its own questions, and is withdrawn only if counsel's answer is a categoric no.

Speech recognition errs more on children's speech than on adults', and more the younger the child. A misheard word turns a question into a different one, and a misheard disclosure can reach triage as an ordinary question, which is a missed stop.

AssumedCV-11, awaiting the owner's check

Three things limit it, and none removes it. T-PRM-06's battery runs on spoken input in every band, as well as typed. Triage reads every alternative the recogniser returns, where it returns them, and stops if any of them would stop. And the recogniser is pinned, so an update that changes what triage hears passes the battery before it reaches a child (D22). Since most questions are expected to be spoken (OA-61), most of what triage reads will be a transcript. How often a child of 8 is misheard is Unknown until PM4 measures it with the chosen recogniser; PM3 tests only whether children speak readily (OA-55). RP8 is the risk that it is too often.

The spoken batteries run on recordings of children's speech, made later, before any child speaks to the built Primer. No child is recorded for the MVP demonstration, which adults use. The test recordings are made, with consent, under their own research protocol and DPIA, before any child speaks to the built Primer, and so before launch (D119; P6). The sessions that make them come after PM3's preconditions are met and are held to them. Their scripts are written and reviewed with the safeguarding specialist and voice nothing of the child's own life, and anything a child says of their own takes 05's path. Anything a child says of their own is cut from the recording before it becomes test material and is never replayed in a battery; where it takes 05's path, that part is held in the hold, only for the trained reviewer, and deleted when the review closes (D107); otherwise it is deleted at once. The recordings are never written to backups, before or after the cut, so nothing cut from them survives in any copy. The recordings are the one exception to recording nothing of a child. They are test material, kept apart from every child's account and never taken from the Primer's use, for a period that is Unknown. No child speaks to the Primer, at PM4 or after, until T-PRM-06's spoken battery has passed on the test recordings; until then the talk key stays off and the child types. Speech is first switched on for children in a supervised pilot, PM4's or, if T-PRM-06's spoken battery passes only after PM4 has closed, one held to PM4's conditions, where recognition of children's speech is observed (OA-55). No child at home has speech switched on before then. Adult or synthetic speech would not test how children are misheard, so it is no substitute.

Turning speech off is one setting. Every step after recognition reads text, so when the adult turns speech off (D129), the talk key goes, the child types, and nothing else in the pipeline changes; the company can do the same for every child with one flag. The Primer's own voice stays, since it speaks to the child and records nothing.

The Primer's checks and tests

The Primer keeps three of the Tutor's checks, re-aimed at the sources it retrieves, and adds five: attribution, the contested question, manner on every turn, no direction on a choice in the child's own future, and suitability for the child's age. Every explainer's script passes the same eight and a block on the child's words, and every video an image check before it plays. Withholding, and the notice that may follow it, is not a verifier check but a rule on the logger and the record, tested here because its failure is the most damaging the Primer can have.

Withholding, and the notice. The safeguarding flag, set by triage or by the review at the close (D107), covers the whole exchange, the child's words and the Primer's reply, and is applied before anything is written to the parent's record, so that none of it appears there, on the parent's screen or anywhere reached through the parent's account. Once the trained reviewer has judged it safe to tell this parent, the parent's record shows a notice: a question was flagged for safety and a trained reviewer is handling it. The notice holds no words, no subject and no reply. Until then, and wherever the reviewer judges the parent may be the risk, nothing of the exchange appears, not even the notice. The reviewer decides, against the written policy, whether to tell the parent more (D26, D121). The parent's record is written only after the review at the close, so a flag that review raises is applied in time. The words of a flagged exchange are held only for the trained reviewer and deleted when the review closes (D107; below).

Claim support, against what was retrieved. Every factual sentence cites a source the Primer retrieved for the turn, from the reviewed allow-list or a reviewed entry where one exists, and must be entailed by it; a sentence no source supports is cut, and a turn left with nothing to say gets "not yet" (D125). A reviewed entry is used first wherever one exists. D44's purpose survives in that form: a fact still has a source the child can see. Judging whether a passage retrieved at answer time entails a sentence is a classifier's work, so this check is a gate, not a guarantee. Every figure the Primer states must appear in its source, so generation does no arithmetic, and a figure whose source marks it an estimate is said with "roughly", or the sentence is cut (D113). Two figures that count different groups are never set in one comparison. The source always shows, because the "Based on:" line is built from the sources the turn cites. Where D127 holds a turn to reviewed content, for a fact or story about a named living person or anything in a sensitive module, each such sentence is its entry's or pack's own, compared with it word for word, a deterministic comparison that blocks the turn on any difference. In the read-back, claim support is aimed at the child's own words instead of a source, and it also blocks any detail of the child's family, friends, body or feelings: "a personal reason" passes (below). A deterministic comparison also blocks a read-back that repeats any run of the child's words longer than a short phrase, with or without its pronouns changed. How long a short phrase may be is a design choice, its length Unknown, set before PM4.

Scripts hold none of the child's words. Before a video is generated or cached, the script's checks add a deterministic block on any name, place or uncommon phrase from the child's turns, and the read-back's block on any detail of family, friends, body or feelings (above). T-PRM-10 runs this comparison on every script at runtime, as well as in the release battery.

Attribution is deterministic against a pack or an entry, and a classifier against a passage retrieved at answer time. Every statement that a named thinker, a tradition or any other named real person, living or dead, held a view, said something or would ask a question is extracted from the turn, must cite a source retrieved for the turn or a reviewed entry, and must be entailed by it: a pack or a retrieved source for a position and its questions, and for a named living person a reviewed bank entry only (D127). A question asked back is put as the position's ("A Stoic would ask..."), never in a person's voice (D47, D110). A living person is described only by the public, documented facts the entry holds, never their private life; a quotation must appear word for word in its source; and the Primer never speaks as them (D113). An attribution no source supports is cut, as any unsupported sentence is. Where a pack exists, its positions are read verbatim instead; a question left without three positions gets "not yet" at the harder bit.

The contested-question check is a judgement, which makes it harder. It is active whenever a permitted pack is marked contested, and wherever the positions are generated from retrieved sources rather than taken from a pack, since no reviewer has marked them (OA-58). It looks for a conclusion presented as the Primer's own or as settled ("the answer is", "most people agree", "obviously"); one position given a weaker case than the others, when D110 gives each its strongest; evidence chosen for one side only; and a read-back that re-words the child's position into a different one, or moves it towards a position. It runs as a classifier on the verifier's model, measured by a red-team battery built around the requests a child actually makes: "just tell me", "what do you think?", "my dad says this, is he right?", "should I...?". A failure blocks the turn. The fallback sets out a pack's positions verbatim and asks the child, where a pack exists; otherwise the Primer asks instead of stating.

Manner holds every turn to D108: the Primer never says "I", never uses the child's name and never praises ("good question", "well done"), and it uses no "we" or "let's" for itself and the child (P2). The manner check runs on every turn and every script. "I", "me", "my", "let's" and the child's display name are found by a deterministic match; praise, and a "we" that joins the Primer to the child, by a classifier, which is a weaker hold. The check reads the Primer's turns, never the child's, and holds the display name only to block it. The fixed lines, triage's replies, "not yet" and the fallbacks never pass through the verifier, so they are held to the same rules when they are reviewed, and T-PRM-09 runs over them too.

The no-direction check applies D106's principle: on a choice in the child's own future, the Primer neither encourages nor discourages; it acknowledges the reality and says what the schools of thought say (P4). In a conversation triage has sorted as such a choice, every reply and every script is checked, and a sentence is blocked if it tells the child what to do, predicts their own outcome, judges their chances from what they say about themselves, or encourages, discourages, warns or reassures them. Where the evidence describes what happens to most people, the reply says so as the reality, not as a verdict on the child. A reasoning tool such as the hedge is one of the positions, cited and attributed to its practice, as what fund managers do: "bet hard on what you believe in" passes as what a practice holds and is blocked as an instruction to the child. The check is a classifier, measured by T-PRM-06's blind raters.

Suitability. A suitability check, blocking, runs on every generated sentence and script against the child's range of the Children's code, 8 and 9, 10 to 12, or 13 (P4). The read-back is checked against what the child said. The generator writes it as a summary of the conversation, and it passes the verifier like any turn, with claim support aimed at the child's own words rather than at a source: every statement of what the child thinks must be entailed by what they said, and none may be moved towards a position. The read-back is the Primer's sentence and may repeat a short phrase of the child's where it states their position, never more. A deterministic comparison also blocks a read-back that repeats any run of the child's words longer than a short phrase, with or without its pronouns changed. How long a short phrase may be is a design choice, its length Unknown, set before PM4. The read-back keeps ideas, not lives (D104): a reason the child gave about their family, friends, body or feelings is kept as "a personal reason", never in its detail. The verifier's read-back check also blocks any detail of the child's family, friends, body or feelings ("a personal reason" passes). The child hears it and sees it, and can correct it by speaking or typing; a corrected read-back is rewritten and read back again, within the time cap, until the child has nothing to correct, and that version is kept. A conversation that ends without a read-back keeps its topic and question and no position. A blocked read-back has its own fallback: a fixed, reviewed line asks the child to say once more where they stand, and the read-back is written again. If that one is blocked too, the close comes without a read-back: the topic and the question are kept, with no position. Each blocked read-back goes to adjudication with the child's words masked and any detail of their family, friends, body or feelings removed, whoever's words carry it. A summary keeps the question in general terms: the catalogue question where a reviewed entry answers it, which holds none of the child's words, and otherwise the Primer's own rewrite, held to the read-back's checks: no run of the child's words longer than a short phrase, and no detail of their family, friends, body or feelings (P3). The same rewrite is what the writing queue holds. The rewrite's check on personal detail is a classifier, a gate, not a guarantee; P4 names it among the gaps. A stopped or declined question is kept by its category and date only.

At the close, the words go. The review runs (D107), the record is written, and the session store's text is deleted: the child's words, the Primer's turns and anything recognised from speech. The review at the close also runs when the time cap closes a conversation the child walked away from. The session store's words are deleted only after the review at the close has run; if that review cannot run, the conversation is held for the trained reviewer as a flagged exchange, and once the reviewer clears it, the record is written as at any close and its words are deleted. None of the session store, the flagged hold, the adjudication queue and the test recordings is ever written to backups, so no backup keeps a conversation's words after its close, a flagged exchange's blocked turns after its review, or anything cut from a recording. A flag raised by triage writes the flag and the exchange's words to the hold at once, not at the close. The hold is kept durable without backups, by replication, which is not a backup, and every copy is deleted when the review closes. Scripts and videos hold none of the child's words, so a cached video keeps nothing of a conversation (D126). No turn record ever held audio or video, and a turn record names a video only by its identifier, so 06's rule that no Turn field holds them holds for the Primer too (06). The release batteries run in testing, where transcripts exist because no child is talking to the Primer; the spoken batteries replay recordings made for testing (above). Live traffic keeps verdicts and identifiers, never words.

A flagged exchange outlasts a deletion request until its review closes. Neither a deletion request nor an account closure reaches it, or its escalation record, before then; when the review closes, the exchange is deleted. Otherwise the only record of a disclosure could go before a trained reviewer has seen it. Whether that is lawful goes to counsel with Open OI-13 and OI-14, and this page does not decide it.

The Primer remembers through the summaries alone. At a later session the composer may give the generator the child's earlier read-backs on the same topic, marked as what the child concluded before; it never reads the categories of stopped or declined questions, or any notice of a flagged one. A sentence about a child's earlier position must cite that summary and be entailed by it, and a summary is never evidence for anything else. Nothing else from an earlier conversation reaches the generator: the per-turn log, which the composer may read, though never for triage's outcomes, holds identifiers, verdicts and timings, and none of the child's words. The parent's screen reads only the summaries, the topics, the switches and the categories of stopped and declined questions (P2): never per-turn verdicts, and of a flagged exchange only the notice, once the trained reviewer has judged it safe to tell this parent, with no words, no subject and no reply (D121, T-PRM-08). No full conversation exists to request, which is how D104 supersedes D48's on-request transcript for a child under 13.

A summary can misstate a child, and a child may accept a read-back that is not quite what they meant, to get on. The verifier's check catches a read-back that says more than the child did, not one that leaves out what mattered to them. Whether children accept read-backs and parents understand them is tested at PM4, and no release test measures faithfulness beyond the verifier's check.

The module registry and the switch

The registry is the single source the composer reads for what a turn may use. For each module it holds the identifier and version, the age band, the sensitive flag, the default switch, the pack and bank entries it contains, its review status and sign-off, and a live flag the company controls. For each source on the reviewed allow-list it holds its review, the age ranges it is reviewed for, the market its evidence is for and a live flag; and it holds a live flag for each prompt pattern and video style, and one for live generation itself (below). For each child it holds the parent's switch for every module offered in the child's band, whether speech is on, and the account's market.

For each entry it also holds what D113's rules need: the market its evidence is for, the UK, the US or both; each figure it states, with its source, the group it counts, whether that source is an estimate, and a date to check it again, after which the composer does not permit it (P3); and every named living person it describes, whose facts and stories come only from reviewed entries (D127). Claim support reads the figure fields, and a pull by person writes to the person field (below).

The composer filters before it matches: an entry is permitted only if its module is live, switched on for this child, and written for the child's band. Nothing from an excluded module can enter the permitted set, and the verifier's scope check re-checks every cited identifier against the filter, so a fault in one layer is caught by the other. That is T-PRM-05, deterministic and blocking on every release.

Pulling content, or switching generation off

A harmful module, pack, entry, allow-list source, prompt pattern or video style, every entry and cached script that names one person, or live generation itself, is removed in one operation: a single write to a live flag, read at every turn, with an in-flight conversation falling back at its next turn to reviewed content, to "not yet" or to another module. This is page 13's requirement of a one-operation kill switch for a mode or prompt pattern (13).

Switching generation off stops answers from the allow-list and every generated video, cached or new. The Primer then answers only from reviewed entries and packs, as it always does for named living people and the sensitive modules (D127), or says "not yet"; its questions and the read-back are written and checked as before. It is the switch for a fault that no narrower pull can isolate.

A source, a prompt pattern or a video style is pulled the same way. No turn retrieves a pulled source, no script is written with a pulled pattern, and no video is made in a pulled style. Each cached video records the sources, pattern and style it was made from, so the same write pulls every cached copy that rests on them, and a single video can be pulled by itself.

A pull by person exists because a complaint about a named living person needs every entry that names them gone at once, and the registry's person field makes that one write (D113). Each cached script records the named people it mentions, found by the same name check D127 relies on, so a pull by person also pulls every cached script and video that names them. An entry comes back only through review. What the law asks of such content is Open OI-30.

A reported video leaves every cache. Beside every generated video the tablet shows a one-tap report, wordless, shown and never spoken, which records only the video's identifier and the time. A parent reports from the parent's screen against a conversation, and reviewers trace it through the turn log. A reported video leaves every cache until a reviewer has looked at it (D128; below).

The pull reaches the tablet and every cache. The tablet caches fixed speech so that a turn starts quickly, but it plays nothing on its own: it speaks or plays only what the service names in the current turn, after the service has read the live flags. A pulled entry, line or video is never named again, so a copy is not used once the turn in progress ends; the tablet's copies of fixed speech are purged at its next sync, a bound that is Unknown until the tablet is built. Videos are cached in the service, not on the tablet, which keeps none past the close (below). Speech and video for a turn are streamed to the tablet and not stored there, and the vendors keep nothing (D24), so there is nothing else to purge. The service's caches, the video cache among them, and the catalogue follow the Tutor's bounds (06).

Pulling is complete because no model holds the content. No weights hold a module's content, a child's words or a child's voice, so there is nothing to retrain, which is the only reason the operation can be called complete (D45). The selection model is trained, but only to choose, and it writes nothing, so nothing it learned can bring pulled content back (below). The rule on a harm signal, whether a complaint, a report from a child or a parent, a red-team finding, a finding of the sample review (D128) or an adjudication, is to pull first and investigate after. The switch is rehearsed on the Tutor's schedule (D29) with the elapsed time recorded, and the standing review group can require a pull and must be told of every one.

Models: taught on, not trained on

The Primer buys its models on the Tutor's terms, and buys three more: a recogniser for children's speech, a voice and a video model. It trains one of its own, the selection model, which chooses what is spoken and what is shown and writes nothing.

The generator and the verifier are bought as 06 buys them (06): zero retention and no training on our data as a procurement gate (D24); versions pinned, with a vendor update passing the battery before it reaches a child (D22); and the verifier on a different model from the generator (D27). The recogniser, the voice and the video model pass the same gate on their own material: zero retention of audio, text, scripts and video, and no training on any of them. All three are pinned: the recogniser because an update can change what triage hears, the voice because an update can change what a child hears, and the video model because an update can change what a child sees, so an update passes T-PRM-15's battery first. They are chosen at PM4: the recogniser on its accuracy with children's speech in every band, the voice on its time to the first spoken sentence, and the video model on its time to the first video, its cost and its record against the image check. No vendor is named, for the reason 06 gives, and selection runs at PM4 on the Primer's own battery.

The selection model is trained for its purpose and chosen at PM4 (D124). It is trained to choose, never to write: given a checked sentence, it returns spoken, shown or both, and no words, so nothing in its weights can reach a child as content, and pulling a module or a source needs no retraining (T-PRM-11). The selection model learns from the facilitators' choices in PM3's sessions, what each chose to say and to show, which PM3's research protocol and parental consent cover; no model is trained on anything a child said, or on a child's voice. How well it chooses is Unknown until PM4, and whether children follow an answer split its way is OA-63.

The Primer is taught on its modules, not trained on them. Modules are retrieved when a turn needs them; no model is fine-tuned on them. Rejected: fine-tuning on philosophical and critical-thinking material, which would make a module impossible to withdraw.
