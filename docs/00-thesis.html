Thesis

The differentiation in the 2025 submission no longer exists. Three consumer products now give it away for free. What is left that is worth building is narrower, harder and more defensible than what was originally proposed, and this document says what it is.

The claim, in four sentences

CINE sells a warranted answer, not a clever one. A student picks a named educator whose teaching they already recognise, and gets taught in that style from an item bank written to the published specification and checked by a working examiner, where every substantive statement the tutor makes is traceable to a validated item and blocked if it is not. The free tools are better general reasoners than anything we will build and we do not intend to compete with them on that. We compete on being accountable for correctness in a narrow, examined, high-stakes domain, and on letting a fifteen-year-old change how they are taught by naming someone rather than by writing a prompt.

What died between 2025 and now

Sections 4.1 and 5.1 of the 2025 submission described a product that teaches Socratically, adapts to the learner and produces diagrams on demand. Every one of those features is now a free toggle in a product with hundreds of millions of users.

  • OpenAI launched Study Mode in ChatGPT on 29 July 2025. Google launched Guided Learning in Gemini roughly a week later, built on LearnLM. Claude has an equivalent Learning Mode. All are free toggles.
  • Gemini's Guided Learning already produces scaffolded multimodal output including images, diagrams, videos and interactive quizzes, and generates flashcards and study guides.
  • Google offered students in the US, Japan, Indonesia, Korea and Brazil a free twelve-month AI Pro subscription, and committed $1bn over three years to AI education in the US.
  • Khanmigo is free to all US teachers; a consumer price point of $4/month has been reported.
VerifiedSeptember 2026

This is not a competitive setback to be worked around. It is the removal of the product's reason to exist as originally described, and pretending otherwise would waste the next two years. An LLM that teaches Socratically with diagrams is not a product in September 2026. It is a feature of three things a parent already has on their phone, two of which are free and one of which is being given to students for a year at a time at national scale.

The correct response is not to add features until the difference returns. It is to accept that general tutoring capability is now infrastructure, and to ask what is left that a well-capitalised general assistant is structurally unwilling to do.

Why a parent pays when Gemini is free

Three answers. The first is the only one with a durable technical basis; the second is the bet the venture is making; the third is the commercial frame that makes the first two sellable.

1. Accountability for correctness, which a general assistant will not offer

A general assistant is contractually and commercially prevented from telling a parent that its answer to a GCSE question is right. Its terms disclaim exactly that. It cannot warrant curriculum coverage, it cannot tell you which specification it is answering against, and when it is wrong it is wrong silently and fluently.

CINE can make the opposite commitment because it is built to a narrower job. Every substantive curricular statement that reaches a learner cites a node in an item bank that a qualified examiner has validated, and a verification layer blocks the turn if the statement is not supported by the cited node. The commitment is specified as a number with a test behind it and a defined behaviour when the number is missed, in 03 Curriculum and content and 06 Architecture.

This is a real difference and it is the moat, because it is expensive in exactly the way that model capability is not. The item bank and the examiner validation are a content and process cost that does not fall when the next model ships. Buy the model; own the bank and the verification layer.

That parents of examination candidates will pay for a correctness warranty they cannot themselves evaluate, and that they can tell the difference between a warranted answer and a confident one. Both are plausible and neither is established. This is the single most important untested premise in the venture and the roadmap tests it before the content cost is fully incurred; see 11 Roadmap, milestone M5.

Assumptionnot verified

2. A student can name a teacher but cannot specify a pedagogy

You can make a general assistant teach almost any way you like, if you can describe the way you like. A fifteen-year-old cannot. They cannot say "enter from a concrete instance, delay the formal statement until after three worked cases, and respond to my errors by asking me to predict rather than by correcting me". They can say the name of someone who teaches the way they like.

That is the whole of decision D4. The name is the handle; the style is the substance. The product turns a name into a point in a parameter space, gives the student the handle, and then lets them turn it, which is D10. The mechanism is specified in 02 Style engine.

The honest position is that this is a bet, not a finding. It is why the product ships named presets and unnamed archetypes simultaneously from day one and instruments return rate rather than satisfaction (D14), so that "the wedge is weak" and "the fidelity is short" can be told apart, because they have opposite fixes.

3. Substituting a purchase the household is already making

The comparator is not a free chatbot. It is a private tutor at prevailing rates.

29% of secondary pupils in England and Wales have had private tutoring at some point, up from 18% twenty years ago and 27% in 2019. Year 11 is the peak year at 25%. Prevailing 2026 rates are roughly £25 to £45 per hour at GCSE and £35 to £65 at A-level. An earlier Sutton Trust estimate put the UK private tuition market at up to £2bn.

VerifiedSutton Trust, Private Tutoring 2026, published February 2026

A household already spending on tutoring is not choosing between CINE and a free chatbot. It is choosing between CINE and an hour of a human, and the arithmetic of that substitution is the pricing section of 10 Economics. The free tools are a reason the market may not grow as fast as it otherwise would. They are not what the money is currently going to.

Where that argument does not hold

Three places. Stating them is the point of this section.

The specification-knowledge limb is weak on its own. "The free tools do not know your specification" is not durable, because a parent can paste the specification into Gemini and a competitor can ship specification alignment in a quarter. Knowledge of the specification is not the asset. Validated items, recorded provenance per item, and a gate that refuses to speak beyond them are the asset, because those are a rate-limited human process rather than a prompt. Any version of the pitch that rests on "we know the syllabus" should be treated as a sign that the real argument has been lost.

The persona wedge is unproven and the evidence to date is our own intuition. No data in this repository supports D4. It was retained against advice. If the D14 instrumentation shows named presets performing no better than unnamed archetypes on return rate, the wedge is dead and the venture is a correctness-warranted subject tutor with a style control, which is a smaller and more crowded business. The roadmap treats that as a live outcome rather than a tail risk.

Willingness to pay for correctness is untested in this market. There is good evidence that AI tutoring works and none in this repository that parents will pay for a warranty on it. Those are different claims and the second is the one the business depends on.

What the evidence actually supports

The case for AI tutoring as a mechanism strengthened over the same period that the case for our particular differentiation collapsed. These are the findings that survive.

  • Kestin, Miller, Klales et al., Scientific Reports, published 3 June 2025: a purpose-built AI tutor at Harvard produced median learning gains more than double those of an active-learning classroom group. The AI supplemented human teaching rather than replacing it; the correct comparison is AI-supported instruction against conventional instruction, not against a teacher.
  • ASSISTments: effect sizes of 0.18 to 0.29 SD across two large randomised controlled trials, ESSA Tier 1 evidence rating, at under £100 per student.
  • Carnegie Learning MATHia: 0.21 to 0.38 SD across more than 18,000 students in 147 schools.
  • Reported caveats: accuracy gaps at subject edges; students who default to the AI rather than working a problem can develop surface fluency that does not hold under examination conditions.
VerifiedSeptember 2026

Two things follow, and both constrain the design rather than decorating it.

The Harvard result is a result about supplementation. It is not evidence that an AI tutor replaces teaching, and the product must not be sold as though it were, both because that claim is unsupported and because it is the claim that makes schools hostile in Route B.

The second caveat is a design requirement, not a footnote. A tutor that answers is a tutor that produces surface fluency. The instrumentation in 11 Roadmap measures unaided performance on held-out items, not in-session success, precisely because in-session success is the metric that a surface-fluency failure would flatter.

The market this is aimed at

The demand is concentrated, and the concentration is the strategy.

  • Year 11 is the peak year at 25%, followed by Year 10 at 10%.
  • London 45%, rest of England 27%, Wales 24%. Urban 33%, rural 19%.
  • Worst-off households 23%, best-off 30%.
  • By ethnicity: Black pupils 64%, Asian 50%, White 20%; in deprived areas, 65%, 43% and 10% respectively.
  • 20% of pupils received one-to-one or small-group tutoring organised by their school, down from 22% in 2023; separate 2025 polling found 58% of schools had reduced their tutoring offer year on year.
VerifiedSutton Trust, Private Tutoring 2026, February 2026

Read together: the paying demand is in Year 11, in London, in urban households, and it is strikingly concentrated by ethnicity in a way that should inform where the first thousand customers come from. The school-funded channel is shrinking at the same time. That is the strongest single argument for going direct to parents rather than through schools, and it is stated as such in 09 Route comparison rather than being left implicit.

It is also the source of the business's worst structural feature. A Year 11 concentration means an annual cliff in June. 07 Route A confronts that directly instead of modelling it away.

Who is already here

UK competitors already in market: Third Space Learning's Skye (voice-based conversational maths tutoring), Century Tech (adaptive pathways), and Oak National Academy's Aila (a teacher lesson-planning assistant, reported to save teachers three to four hours per week, state-backed and free).

VerifiedSeptember 2026

Aila matters more than it looks. A state-backed free tool aimed at teachers is the thing that makes Route B harder to sell, because it occupies the budget-free part of a school's attention. Skye matters because it validates conversational maths tutoring as a category in the UK, and because it is the closest competitor to the launch scope chosen in 03 Curriculum and content.

What the venture is for

An AI tutor for UK secondary students at GCSE and A-level, built from Bengaluru, sold into the UK, whose distinguishing feature is that the student chooses how they are taught by naming an educator whose style they already know, and whose distinguishing commitment is that every substantive statement cites a validated item and is blocked when it cannot, and that the tutor refuses rather than guesses. That is the claim D21 says is supportable at the launch floor. The stronger version, that nothing ungrounded ever reaches a student, is not supportable yet and is not made.

Two things it is deliberately not:

  • Not a general study assistant. It refuses questions outside its declared scope rather than answering them worse than the free tools would. The scope is named in 03 and it is narrow at launch.
  • Not a companion. It does not claim feelings, does not initiate contact, and holds no social memory of the student beyond their academic state. That is a safety decision, an Online Safety Act positioning decision and a rights decision at the same time; see 05 Safety, privacy and regulatory.

The shelf life of the answer

The argument in this document has an expiry date and the plan should say so out loud.

The defensible claim rests on a validated item bank and a refusal to speak beyond it. Nothing stops a well-capitalised general assistant from buying the same examiner hours. Verified Google has committed $1bn over three years to AI education in the US and has given twelve-month subscriptions away at national scale in five countries, so the capital to do it plainly exists.

What is actually protective is narrower than "we have content": it is the combination of validated content, the outcome evidence that accumulates from using it, and the fact that neither is worth building for a general assistant whose product strategy is breadth. A specification-specific, examiner-validated, warranted tutor is a bad fit for a product that must work for every subject in every country at once.

The honest formulation, which belongs in front of an investor rather than only in a repository: this is a bet that the correctness warranty is defensible for long enough to accumulate outcome evidence, and that the outcome evidence is what a parent eventually buys. Unknown How long that window is. It cannot be estimated from anything in this repository, and it should be treated as a live strategic question reviewed at each milestone in 11 Roadmap rather than as a number someone invents now.
